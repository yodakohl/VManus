# GDT209 source audit

## Wolfenbüttel witness

The Herzog August Bibliothek catalogue identifies Cod. Guelf. 23.19 Aug. 4°
as a fourteenth/fifteenth-century alchemical miscellany whose fols. 34r–129v
contain John of Rupescissa's quintessence work; it explicitly records a small
pen drawing on fol. 40. The institutional viewer identifies image `00095` as
fol. 40r. Its permanent URL and retrieved-byte hash are frozen in the image
manifest.

Direct inspection shows two side-by-side pear-shaped vessels with capped tops,
narrow waists, shaded lower bodies, and two crossing connecting ducts. It does
not show human figures, colored pools, or a serial figure-stage array.

## Othmer witness

The Science History Institute/OPenn catalogue dates Othmer MS 7 to Venice,
1498 and identifies it as Pseudo-Lull's *Liber de secretis naturae seu de
quinta essentia*. The catalogue notes that the work incorporates large parts
of Rupescissa's earlier quintessence text. It independently identifies red-ink
vessel drawings on fol. 10r and a detailed *furnus Athanor* on fol. 36r.

Direct inspection of fol. 10r shows two pear-shaped vessel diagrams with
lower filled/shaded regions. One has an external return-loop; the other has
two internal descending ducts from the neck. Fol. 36r shows two domed furnace
sections, one enclosing a central vessel above a fire and the other enclosing
a central chamber with two side outlets. Neither image contains human figures
or a q13-like serial pool-stage array.

## Denison witness

The 2021 academic primary-manuscript study remains useful textual evidence for
the 1451 Denison copy: it describes the family apparatus as body-like and says
that its side tubes support repeated ascent and descent. The public thesis page
does not provide the cited manuscript figures as downloadable image assets;
its sole embedded JPEG is a modern stock image of books. Consequently GDT209
does not claim direct visual verification of the Denison figure.

## Provenance conclusion

The direct primary images establish a real late-medieval iconographic ecology
of paired vessels, return loops, side ducts, domed furnaces, and nested
chambers. They do not establish the much more specific q13 package of numerous
figures inside colored pools and channels. That asymmetry is the result, not a
reason to discard the content theory.
