## The important correction

The apparent semantic richness of the GDT180 output order was overstated.  If
the four quality states are already arranged as a cycle, then traversing all
four edges once necessarily yields the four classical element pairs once.
Inserting one repeated state necessarily creates one no-change boundary.

Exact enumeration gives:

| Condition | admissible six-state sequences |
|---|---:|
| all four-state strings | 4,096 |
| observed output mask `1,1,0,1,1` | 8 |
| one hold allowed at any boundary | 40 |

The eight fixed-mask solutions are just four rotations times two traversal
directions.  Consequently `EARTH–FIRE–AIR–WATER` is not a second independent
semantic success after the quality-state hypothesis; it is the edge list
forced by that hypothesis.  What remains unexplained is narrower: why the
source-derived local bits align with the four author-visible emissions and
the one non-emission on this one exposed folio.

## What the sources do establish

- Walters W.73 supplies the standard four element/quality incidence square.
- Medieval alchemical visualization linked written instructions to figures of
  furnaces and vessels and depicted stages of material transformation.
- Early-fifteenth-century Wellcome MS.140 joins medical and alchemical waters,
  practical recipes, distillation/sublimation, and apparatus drawings in one
  manuscript.
- A fourteenth-century apparatus can carry a directly readable process label,
  `Distilatio Aceti`.
- Arnald of Brussels' fifteenth-century diagram combines the four elements
  with four stages for separation of the stone.
- Edinburgh Cr. 2.3 contains a five-circle quality volvelle with a repeated
  compound quality state, showing that repeated state labels in a technical
  moving diagram are historically ordinary enough not to identify f77.
