# GDT214 — al-Jazari source audit

## Institutional source

The Metropolitan Museum of Art catalogue identifies object 451298 / 55.121.11
as a folio copied by Farrukh ibn Abd al-Latif in 715 AH / 1315 CE, probably in
Syria or Iraq, from al-Jazari's *Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical
Devices*.  The catalogue describes the donkey moving around a pole, wheels
raising a ladle from water, and discharge into an irrigation channel.

The exact API response and both official image hashes are recorded in
`gdt214_aljazari_source_manifest.tsv`.  The images are public domain according
to the Met and are referenced rather than copied into the repository.

## Direct observation

On one side, a quadruped in an upper bay connects through a shaft/gear system
to a lifting element over a painted water area.  On the other, four parallel
lifting bays occur beneath a shared gear train and above repeated water areas.
Both sides contain many isolated one- or few-mark inscriptions adjacent to
components.  These rows are visual observations only; no mark is read.

## Scholarly context

Balafrej (2022), DOI `10.11588/xxi.2022.4.91685`, documents the broader
mechanical-manuscript practice in which compact letter references link
depicted components with explanatory text.  The cited explicit example is a
related medieval Arabic mechanics manuscript, not a transcription of Met
55.121.11.  GDT214 therefore calls the visible marks `APPARENT_COMPONENT_INDEX`
rather than asserting their values or exact function.

## Limits

This is an engineering comparator, not a therapeutic source.  Its exposed
gears, shafts, ladles, animal drive, and irrigation outlet are not securely
identified in q13.  Visual resemblance cannot establish direct descent.
