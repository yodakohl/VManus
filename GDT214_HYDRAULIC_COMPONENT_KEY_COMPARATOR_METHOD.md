# GDT214 — hydraulic component-key comparator

## Question

Does a readable medieval hydraulic manuscript provide a concrete alternative
to treating every nearby q13 string as an object name?

The fixed mechanism is `COMPACT_DIAGRAM_KEY -> EXPLANATORY_TEXT_REFERENCE`.
It is tested only as document architecture.  No Arabic or Voynich sign is
transcribed, aligned, sounded, or translated.

## Source

The primary object is Metropolitan Museum of Art 55.121.11, object 451298, a
1315 copy of al-Jazari's *Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices*.
The Met identifies the two-sided folio as donkey-powered water-raising designs
using wheels and ladles to discharge water into an irrigation channel.

Both public-domain institutional images were inspected directly.  New rows
are marked `AI_DIRECT_VISUAL_OBSERVATION`.  The visual audit records only
basins, repeated components, shafts, a quadruped, and compact isolated marks.
Functional names come from the Met catalogue.  A scholarly article by Lamia
Balafrej supplies only the broader medieval Arabic mechanics convention that
compact letter references can link depicted components to explanatory text;
it is not used to read the marks on this folio.

## Comparison and falsifier

Seven fixed axes compare hydraulic architecture, embedded agent, repeated
parallel components, compact diagram marks, label-key reuse in prose, exact
topology, and therapeutic content.

The key prediction has already been tested independently in GDT187.  Across 23
nonsealed pages, exact PAGE_HOST reuse covers 57/215 label occurrences in
same-page prose and 22/215 on paragraph-opening lines, but is not globally
unusual (`max-ten p=.2963`).  GDT214 preserves that negative rather than
rerunning or selecting favorable q13 pages.

## Claim ceiling

The comparator can establish that a figure-bearing hydraulic diagram with
compact component references is historically attested.  It cannot establish
that q13 is a water-raising machine, that q13 labels are cross-reference keys,
or that any source group names a component.  It supplies no source descent,
language, plaintext, or translation.

No Voynich source or image is opened.  No f84 artifact is accessed.
