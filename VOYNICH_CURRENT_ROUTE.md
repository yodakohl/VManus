line-reset construction system with a mixed lexicon:

1. productive technical or address-like shells;
2. learned exact wholes and opaque name cores;
3. position- and context-bound renderer defaults;
4. separate descriptive and prescriptive pharmaceutical record channels as a
   historical architecture prior;
5. an H1–H4 record-role grid: H1/H2 entry-biased versus H3/H4 internal/final,
   crossed by two strong 24-body affinity pairs H1–H4 and H2–H3.

Physical lines, separators, entry/closure biases and repeated form families are
real formal structure. PAGE_HOST, DY, B3, workshop roots, exact tuple IDs and
H1–H4 are analyst variables. None is an authorial morpheme, sound, part of
speech or plaintext word unless separately grounded.

The old idea that recurrent forms can all be rendered as one universal process
sentence is retired. The historical comparators favor a mixture of learned

unknown. A consumed span renders once; punctuation attaches structurally.

## Corrected historical semantic bridge

### Category correction

EVA `p/s/r/l` are modern transcription labels. They are not medieval Latin
letters, manuscript glyph names, initials or phonetic readings. Therefore the
former mnemonic `p=pulvis`, `s=semen`, `r=radix`, `l=lignum` receives **zero**
letter, sound, glyph, relation or lexeme credit. It may not be used to seed a
translation.

The formal object survives independently: 96 unique head+body forms arrange as
24 complete bodies under four opaque contrastive classes H1–H4, with 1,166
cached occurrences and 875 alternate-reader exact occurrences. Every one of
the 24 assignments of *pulvis/semen/radix/lignum* to H1–H4 ties structurally.
The old EVA-initial mnemonic ranks only 20/24 in both zero-credit OCR mention
controls; different historical sources prefer different permutations. No
mapping is selected.
