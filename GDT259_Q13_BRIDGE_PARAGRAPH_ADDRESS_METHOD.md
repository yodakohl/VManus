# GDT259 — q13 bridge paragraph-address method

GDT247 found three complete source-native groups that occur both as isolated
graphical labels and as internal prose groups on f80r/f82r. GDT259 asks a
narrower architectural question: do those prose occurrences occupy a common
physical paragraph or HPR2-field address that could make the equality an
executable cross-reference rule?

The three bridges are frozen exactly as published by GDT247. Paragraph
coordinates come from the corrected five-paragraph f80r and three-paragraph
f82r inventories. A line's normalized paragraph coordinate is
`(line_ordinal - 1) / (paragraph_line_count - 1)`. HPR2 addresses are recovered
only where the already-published corrected field inventories contain the
prose locus and the exact surface once. No missing field is inferred.

The primary descriptive statistic is the mean normalized paragraph coordinate
of the three prose occurrences. Its local null selects two line-internal,
all-reading-aligned prose groups without replacement from f80r and one from
f82r, preserving the observed page composition. The inclusive one-sided tail
is enumerated exactly. A line-uniform sensitivity selects physical lines rather
than groups so that long lines do not receive extra weight. These are local,
exposed discovery diagnostics, not preregistered confirmation tests.

A common address requires all available bridges to agree on the exact
paragraph-relative coordinate or, where HPR2 exists, the same field ordinal,
within-field position, and closure. Mere occurrence in the later half of a
paragraph is not an address.

Only the published f80r/f82r discovery projection and corrected derivative
tables are read. They contain no f84r row. The prior GDT257 transient-access
breach is disclosed; GDT259 performs no new f84r access.
