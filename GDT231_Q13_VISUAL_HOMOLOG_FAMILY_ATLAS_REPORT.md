# GDT231 — q13 visual-homolog family atlas

## Outcome

**`F82R_PAIRED_WATERFALL_PREFIX_INTERESTING_EXPLORATORY_NOT_SEMANTICALLY_IDENTIFIED`.**

The paired left/right structures described by the human source as
“waterfalls” carry source-native family expressions `BACAB` and `BACACA`.
They share the four-family prefix `BACA`.  This is the strongest concrete
content-adjacent clue in the current q13 route, but it is one exposed pair on
one folio and both labels are proximity-owned.

## Transcription sensitivity

The family result is stable across ZL3b, IT2a, and RF1b.  At the lossy display
level the first label is `darol` in all three readings, whereas the second is
approximately `darary`, `daryry`, or `jarary`.  The stable claim is therefore
the shared family prefix, not an exact literal `dar` morpheme.

## Mandatory counterexample

The two unhedged f82v labels described as being “on” pools do **not** repeat
the waterfall prefix.  Their family expressions are `BAG|AQA` and `AQABBA`,
with leading common length zero.  Thus the f82r pattern cannot be generalized
to “water,” “pool,” or even a universal hydraulic-object class.

Other label arrays also contain stronger formal reuse.  The f75v nymph-label
array has an exact `BABBA` pair, and f80r has an exact `AQABA` pair despite one
label being between figures.  This demonstrates that same-unit register,
positional templates, or repeated label formulae can create equal or stronger
similarity without revealing an object name.

## Best working interpretation

`BACA-` is now a **local matched-apparatus/referent-class candidate** for the
two f82r positions.  That is narrower and more defensible than `dar = flow`:

- it may mark the same depicted component class;
- it may mark parallel left/right slots;
- it may be an array/register template;
- the differing final families may distinguish instance, side, state, or
  simply unrelated lexical material.

The current data cannot choose among those explanations.
