# GDT183 — f57 outside label is entry-like, not a translated word

## Result

The strongest defensible parse is:

`d[ENTRY_WRAPPER] + air/airol[OPAQUE PAGE_HOST or HOST+RIGHT realization]`

In the HPR2-covered prose inventory, BAFAB occurs four times and all four are
FIRST. Among 1,463 `d-`-wrapped groups, 372 are FIRST (25.43%); the raw
without-replacement probability for four FIRST draws is 0.00413. Among 83
family types having exactly four parser-covered occurrences, two are all-FIRST
(0.0241). This makes BAFAB an unusually entry-heavy construction in the
covered inventory.

That result does **not** license `dairal = start` or `dairol = title`.
The four prose occurrences span ENTRY_STATE, AL_STATE, and OL_STATE, while the
f57 label itself changes from `dairal` to `dairol` across readings. The stable
explanation is therefore the previously established entry bias of the outer
`d-` wrapper combined with an opaque address/rendering, not a stable whole-word
meaning.

## What this adds to the working theory

The f57 outside label is better modeled as a diagram-entry marker generated by
the same compiler layer that produces prose line entries. This is useful for a
future translation architecture: diagram titles and prose entries may share a
record-opening operation while carrying different opaque hosts. It also
prevents a seductive but unsupported lexical shortcut.

## Counterweight

- The family was selected after its f57 position was known.
- HPR2 coverage is a selected parseable subset, not a complete manuscript
  census.
- Two of 83 equally supported families are all-FIRST.
- No frozen, independently inspected diagram start target has yet predicted
  BAFAB.
