# GDT205 — Constantine creation-diagram homolog audit

## Purpose

Test the strongest newly located readable medieval ordered-system comparator
against the exact f77 topology retained after GDT202.  The comparator is
Constantine of Pisa's *Book of the Secrets of Alchemy*, compiled in 1257, as
described and edited by Barbara Obrist.  The principal manuscript witness is
Glasgow University Library MS Ferguson 104, fol. 45v (1361); a developed
fourteenth-century adaptation survives in Vienna, Österreichische
Nationalbibliothek MS 2372, fols. 46vb--47ra.

This is a source-only historical-homolog audit.  It uses the scholarly source
description and a direct visual check of the published comparator figure.  It
does not inspect a Voynich image or score a Voynich transcription.

## Frozen distinction

The source makes two different claims which must not be collapsed:

1. its explanatory argument links the creation of six metals with the six
   days of creation;
2. its diagram is rendered as a vertical sequence of seven planet/metal circle
   segments, followed below by segments naming earth, air, and the Dead Sea.

The six-day conceptual cardinality is therefore not a six-cell diagram.
The later Flemish adaptation adds a creator circle, personifications, and
sub-lunary figures rather than reducing the structure to six ordered states.

## Exact-homolog gate

The unchanged f77 gate requires one readable source diagram to document all of
the following together:

- six ordered states;
- five adjacent relations;
- four active outputs and one inactive/hold relation;
- readable labels assigned to the relevant states or relations.

Shared alchemical subject matter, an ordered vertical layout, six creation
days, seven planet/metal registers, or named metals cannot satisfy the gate by
themselves.  No external metal, planet, element, day, or operation name may be
assigned to a Voynich source group.

No f84 material is accessed.
