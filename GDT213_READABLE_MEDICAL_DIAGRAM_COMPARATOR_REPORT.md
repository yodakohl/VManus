# GDT213 — readable labelled-medical-diagram comparator

## Result

**MEDIEVAL_MEDICAL_SCHEMATIC_DOCUMENT_ARCHITECTURE_SUPPORTED_CONTENT_BRIDGE_ABSENT**

Ashmole 399 supplies the strongest readable precedent so far for q13's mixed
page architecture, but not for its content or exact topology.  A medieval
medical page can combine an abstract connected system, short inscriptions
owned by individual components, one embedded humanlike figure, and dense
longer practical text.  Another abstract-system page in the same manuscript
uses predominantly continuous prose instead, showing that caption and prose
layouts can vary within one technical codex.

## What matches

| Axis | Readable Ashmole evidence | q13 f80r/f82r | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abstract connected system | bilateral channels and component network | tubes, pools, enclosures, figures | architecture match |
| Local captions plus prose | component labels coexist with longer remedies | graphical labels coexist with paragraph prose | architecture match |
| Figure within system | one small figure in an upper oval | multiple figures in/beside connected structures | partial match |
| Conceptual rather than naturalistic rendering | source-supported for f13v/f22v | plausible for q13 hydraulic diagrams | broad format lead |

This makes a **hybrid medical-diagram plus practical-record format** more
historically concrete.  It also explains why attempting to force every q13
string into one homogeneous lexical class may be wrong: a readable analogue
can place short component captions and longer practical discourse on the same
diagram page.

## What does not transfer

The decisive asymmetry is ownership.  Ashmole's readable labels identify
components.  Of the 23 text-linked f80r/f82r rows in the frozen q13 inventory,
22 are only proximity-based and one—f82r.10—has connected-component evidence.
The comparator therefore cannot turn nearby q13 strings into object names.

The chronological distinction is also one-way.  Scholarship identifies
earlier labels and later remedy text in Ashmole 399.  No equivalent layering
has been established for q13, so “old diagram plus later recipes” remains a
speculation, not a result.

Finally, Ashmole 399 is anatomical and reproductive, not balneological.  It
strengthens a broad medical-technical document prior while leaving GDT210's
therapeutic bath-site theory as the closer page-genre hypothesis.  It does not
provide q13's repeated bathing arrays, long duct networks, or an exact f77
topology.

## Useful consequence

The next external target is now precise: a readable medieval bath, hydraulic,
or physiological-process diagram that combines q13-like repeated connected
units with **singular component labels**.  Another merely similar vessel or
another unowned nearby label is low-value.

No Voynich source group receives a body-part, bath, disease, process, place,
or material meaning.  No language, plaintext, translation, authorship, or
direct source relationship is claimed.  No f84 source, image, annotation, or
formal payload was accessed.
