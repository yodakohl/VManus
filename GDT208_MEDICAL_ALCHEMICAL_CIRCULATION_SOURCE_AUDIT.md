# GDT208 external source audit

## Authority-bound facts

- Wellcome Collection MS.708 is dated 1443 at Regensburg.  Its catalogue says
  that *De consideratione quintae essentiae* describes medicinal extraction by
  distillation from wine, plants, and minerals; book one treats distillation
  and book two medical applications.
- Bridget Koerwitz-Crosley's 2021 Denison thesis studies a 1451 Latin copy of
  the same work.  It records an hourglass/body-like vessel with side arms from
  top to bottom and translates the process as repeated ascent and descent
  through pipes until quintessence is separated from the four elements.  It
  also notes the text's head/foot/stomach anatomical analogy.
- The British Library catalogues Sloane MS 2560 as a Latin *Donum Dei* from
  Germany or Austria, 1450--1499, with eleven of the usual twelve painted
  process miniatures.  Folio 7 is explicitly “the chemical union of the red
  king and white queen”; later images include black/white flasks, a man, a
  flower, and white/red roses in flasks.
- Direct inspection of the exact BL-derived Sloane f7 image (SHA-256 recorded
  in the manifest) finds a rounded, internally divided vessel containing human
  figures/faces, a rising curved side spout, and branching green forms.  This
  is `AI_DIRECT_VISUAL_OBSERVATION`, not a catalogue transcription.

## What this newly supports

The combination “medical purpose + repeated fluid circulation + body/vessel
analogy + personified figures/plants inside a technical vessel” is historically
attested close to the Voynich date.  It gives the q13 imagery a coherent
candidate content ecology without reading any Voynich string.

## What it does not support

Neither external work reproduces the exact f77 six-cell, five-boundary,
four-output/one-hold topology.  The *Donum Dei* sequence has an explicit
black/white/red progression that is not documented in q13's blue/green pools.
No source assigns a readable value to a singular Voynich inscription.  Thus
the evidence cannot select a language, code, word, operation name, material,
or plaintext.
