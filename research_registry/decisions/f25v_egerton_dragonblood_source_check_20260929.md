# f25v dragon-blood alternative: bounded source check

29 September 2026. This is a **post-GDT1092 exploratory source comparison**,
not a new fixed test or a repair of GDT1092. No new Voynich pixels or text were
opened: the admitted f25v image and its fixed four-feature result remain the
target baseline.

An earlier independent illustrated herbal, British Library **Egerton MS 747**
(catalogue dates it circa 1280–1350), has on [f89r, official IIIF image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x0000b9/full/1500,/0/default.jpg)
two separately labelled drawings: upper `Saponaria`, lower `Sanguis draconis`
and `Arbor draconis` (visible abbreviated letters; exact expansion is not used
as a Voynich key). The lower drawing is a many-branched small-leaf shrub/tree
with scattered red dots or drops. It has **no little dragon**, no single huge
radial blade and no orange fibrous fan like f25v. The source comparison is
specific to this witness and cannot exclude another iconography of dragon's
blood. It does show that `sanguis draconis` cannot be inferred from a tiny
dragon alone, and the earliest inspected named image gives a different plant
architecture.

The same dated-source preflight has two rival historical mechanisms: [Sloane
4016 f38r](../../experiments/yolo/gdt1092_dragon_snakeroot_copy_anomaly/REPORT.md)
names a dragon/serpent-associated **herb** whose complete four-part image does
not match f25v; Egerton f89r names **dragon's blood** beside a red-dotted tree
whose structure also differs. A much later Veneto herbal, [TM 510, c.
1500–1520](https://www.textmanuscripts.com/medieval/herbal-60779), catalogues
dragon-like additions at three distinct plates (f24, f55, f58) with different
recorded names, but its labels include later hands and the catalogue itself
warns animals may be decorative. Those late descriptions are context only,
not independent f25v name witnesses.

Thus f25v's meaning remains open; `Dragontea`, `Sanguis draconis`, a generic
dragon mnemonic and decoration are **not** selected words or species. A new
candidate needs a prospectively fixed anatomical match plus a contemporaneous
written relation and separate witness. No f25v transcription, f84/f84r or
reserved folio was accessed. Source image downloaded at 1500 × 2149 pixels,
539280 bytes, SHA-256
`f119c86b93c44b8db5aedaba89b136009639d79a2652cf6530e5ac20db7fb04b`.
