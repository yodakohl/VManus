# f6v: Ricinus versus Eryngium in named source pictures

Registered 2026-09-29 before the four source images below are viewed for
this comparison. This is an exploratory source-side identity screen against
the already admitted and previously viewed Yale f6v canvas 1006087. The
target image and GDT623's weak `koary/korary` reproductive-drug hypothesis
are prior exposure, not independent confirmation. The older Ricinus/Palma
Christi visual guess conflicts with a new Eryngium suggestion; neither is
yet a taxon determination. The closed historical-image-matching family bars
any significance or lexical promotion from another resemblance.

Source owners to inspect, all institutionally captioned and pre-1500:
Morgan M.652 f.57r (*Eryngium campestre*), f.84v (*E. maritimum*), f.86v
(*Ricinus communis*, inscribed *kiki*); and Morgan M.873 f.74r (*Palma Christi*,
catalogued castor oil tree). Full pages must be checked so that traits from
adjacent separately captioned plants are not borrowed.

Fixed target features: (1) about six large blue/green, single globular
heads with many outward spines, (2) deeply palmate star-shaped, toothed
leaves, (3) a tall, narrow stalk with loose upper branching, and (4) a
thin, many-stranded root bundle. For each of the four source owners, record
all four classes, including contradictions. At least three compatible
classes without a decisive contradiction in head architecture are needed
to prioritize one **C0 picture identity**; a tie or weaker result leaves
both. This changes only which externally named plant to seek in a future
authorial entry/text relation. It cannot identify `koary` or any other
Voynich word, and no target text, new image, reserve, f84 or f84r will open.

Smallest adequate test: four complete official source pages and a four-class
table against f6v. Budget 35 minutes including retrieval, comparison,
validation, and publication; stop source expansion at that limit.

## Complete source-picture comparison

The four full Morgan pages were viewed. Their catalogue entries independently
identify [M.652 f.57r](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/99/143825)
as *E. campestre*, [f.84v](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/150/143825)
as *E. maritimum*, [f.86v](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/154/143825)
as *Ricinus communis*, and
[M.873 f.74r](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/147/159345)
as castor oil tree. On M.873 f.74r, the bottom *Palma Christi* is the
relevant owner; the two upper plants have other captions and contribute
no traits to it.

| Fixed class | f6v | M.652 f.57r Eryngium | M.652 f.84v Eryngium | M.652 f.86v Ricinus | M.873 f.74r Palma Christi |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heads | ~Six large blue-green single globes with radial spines | Several dark, sharply spiny heads of unequal form; none has f6v's regular green sphere | One small dark spiny globe plus lateral buds | A compact terminal spike/cluster, not separated globes | Small loose green beads, no large spiny globes |
| Leaves | Deep, star-like palmate blades with pointed tips | Small jagged leaves; partial pointed-lobe resemblance | Deeply cut serrated lobes but no flat star-shaped blades | Several unmistakably palmate star leaves, close broad shape | Flat, long pointed palmate star leaves, close broad shape |
| Stems | Narrow vertical axis, loosely branched in upper half | Branching and detached painted pieces, not one matching six-head architecture | Mainly one upright stalk | Tall branching stalks with separate fruiting parts | Sideways thin stem with separated star leaves and bead cluster |
| Roots | Long, many-stranded pale bundle | Thin bent stem/root and separately painted small fibrous bases | Several fine roots | Small fine rootlets | No root drawn; unassessable |

The Eryngium sources offer spiny heads and fine roots, while the Ricinus
sources offer the much closer palmate-star leaves. The decisive combination
of **large separate spherical heads plus palmate leaves** is absent from all
four. No source reaches three compatible classes without the head-architecture
contradiction; M.873 lacks a root observation rather than proving a mismatch.
The name decision is therefore **unselected** between these candidates and
other plants. The f6v heads and leaves may be stylized or composite. This
comparison does not contradict GDT623's weak reproductive-part category,
which was not a species identification. It does not identify `koary`,
`korary`, or any component; neither a source caption nor a whole-page first
group has a demonstrated Voynich word-to-plant rule. There is no statistical
claim or independent confirmation from the already exposed f6v image.
