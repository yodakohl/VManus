# An illustrated pre-1450 Viola witness does not identify f9v's first word

29 September 2026. Source-only exploratory check after the f9v Viola-like
image and `fochor` name/address rival were already known. No Voynich image,
text, reserve, or sealed f84/f84r content was newly opened.

The [British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002109150)
dates Add MS 11390, an illustrated copy of Jacob van Maerlant's *Der naturen
bloeme*, to about 1300–1325 and describes captioned medicinal-herb miniatures.
On its [f. 84v institutional image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100057739147.0x0000ae/full/1600,/0/default.jpg),
the right column begins a `Viola` entry under a red initial. Beside it a
boxed `violetten` caption accompanies a small rooted, coloured plant. Root
directly viewed the 1600-pixel image (SHA256
`1ae3fa97452bffc687bc6a8f5ede80d3507160d9a0716a6f78b6e7fe64966110`).
The drawing shows rounded/oval green leaves and a small dark-purplish flower,
but does not securely show a bilaterally arranged five-petal pansy face,
tricolour pattern, lower petal or diagnostic stipules.

This **adds an early written-name-plus-image Viola witness** to the Detmold
Mscr 70 f. 128r early written account, whose inspected folio has no plant
picture. It corrects any broad impression that pre-1450 illustrated *Viola*
is unavailable. It does not establish *Viola tricolor* or a close visual
homologue of f9v. The source's generic violet caption cannot choose whether
Voynich `fochor` is a plant name or an opaque entry address; neither source
word is aligned to a Voynich group. No translated word or independent target
confirmation follows. The existing need for a specific authorial text
relation or second independently bound target owner remains.
