# f35v / BnF Latin 6823 f60r: source-entry check

Date: 2026-09-28. Phase: exploratory source criticism; no Voynich target opened or scored. f84/f84r and reserves remain closed.

## Question and predecessors

Could the familiar visual f35v comparison to the right-hand plant of BnF Latin 6823 f60r supply an *author-written* two-subject relation such as “ivy on oak”, and thus a discriminating Voynich text consequence? This is a narrow source check, not a rerun of the closed historical-herbal plaintext-transducer family (GDT617/GDT618). `route-check` locates that family; no exact source-authored oak/ivy relation was found in its registration. The visual comparison itself is old, including Zandbergen's 2012 presentation; it is not new evidence.

## Source examined

- [BnF Latin 6823 f60r, BnF record](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b6000517p); [photographic derivative with BnF provenance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BnF_Latin_6823,_f.060r.jpg). The BnF image endpoint was unavailable in this session; the derivative shows the full folio and identifies its BnF source.
- The left painted plant and its lower-left initial/headword form one `Enula` entry. The right climbing/berry-bearing plant and its own lower initial/headword form a separate `Edera nigra` entry. The two illustrations occupy one page but are not depicted as ivy growing on the left plant. No oak host is labelled or described by the visible page division.
- Independent manuscript comparison: [BL Egerton 747 f37r](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x000051/full/1800,/0/default.jpg) likewise places `Ebulus` and `Edera nigra` as distinct entries; it supplies no named oak host for the ivy image. This is a check of entry layout, not a claimed direct exemplar.

## Decision

The written source supports an `Edera nigra` entry, not a written two-role oak-and-ivy relation. The latter cannot be transferred to f35v as a fixed semantic or word-order prediction. Even the right-hand picture's possible resemblance to f35v cannot identify a Voynich word without an independently owned target entry and a whole-text rule. No plant name, `ivy` word, or `oak` word is accepted. Reopen only with a historical source that explicitly writes a two-role relation and an independently justified target ownership; a generic tree-and-climber resemblance is insufficient.
