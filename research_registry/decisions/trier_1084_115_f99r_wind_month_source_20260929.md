# Trier MS 1084/115 f.99r: cardinal middle winds with months outside

29 September 2026. Source-only comparison for the historical f68v2 four-by-three analogy; no new Voynich page, image or text opened. Target f68v2 is not currently admitted.

The reproduced primary folio of Trier Stadtbibliothek MS 1084/115 4°, f.99r, in Barbara Obrist, [“Wind Diagrams and Medieval Cosmology,” fig. 18](https://pdf.danmalo.info/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Wind-Diagrams-and-Medieval-Cosmology.pdf), shows a central T-O world inside a square, surrounded by a circle with twelve textual wind sectors and month names at the perimeter. Obrist's reading (pp. 57–58 of the article) explicitly identifies the middle member of each three-sector quadrant as a cardinal wind and the twelve sector texts as Isidorian wind descriptions. The later [Wind Diagrams chapter](https://kartdok.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/kartdok_derivate_00000287/10_1515_9781501516016.pdf), p. 57, also names the exact manuscript and confirms the combination of wind passages and month names. The facsimile is black-and-white and small; an institutional folio image or complete diplomatic transcription was not recovered here. Thus the source's role map rests on its photographed topology plus published scholarly reading, not an independent word-by-word transcription by this project.

This differs materially from Laon MS 422 f.6v: Laon's four *season figures* span three month sectors each, and all twelve radial written sectors are months. Trier has four *cardinal wind sectors* as the middle of four triplets, eight collateral wind sectors on their sides, and months in a separate perimeter register. It is an independently sourced instance of **middle-versus-flank semantic roles within twelve radial written sectors**, and hence a closer class analogue to a putative four-center/eight-flank reading than the Laon source. The exact Trier wind/month combination is absent from the targeted local source/decision search as of this note; Walters W.73 f.2r, by contrast, was already the source of GDT952 and cannot count as new.

The analogy still lacks the necessary target contract. Historical Voynich f68v2 inventories describe four sparse star-bearing centers and eight radial titles in different visual layers, not twelve same-layer wind prose sectors with months outside. The accompanying same-folio f68r3 stellar payload has no Trier match. No independently fixed Voynich north, clockwise start, phase, cardinal word owner, or correspondence between target text layers and Trier's written wind/month layers exists. GDT353 found no exceptional ordered title alignment; its failed rotation cannot be repaired by choosing a favorable Trier origin. GDT1067/GDT1081 continue to block name/direction transfer on the admitted f69r wind-like wheel. GDT952's already tested Walters name network is unchanged.

Decision: retain a **positive source-role parallel, zero current word-test capacity**. A future lexical test requires a registered f68v2 access contract, independent target layer ownership and a predeclared invariant consequence that distinguishes wind-class text from month/star alternatives without choosing a post-hoc phase. No Voynich wind or month name is inferred, f84/f84r and reserves remained closed, and there is no confirmed translated word.
