# Trier MS 1084/115 f.99r: cardinal middle winds with months outside

29 September 2026. Source-only comparison for the historical f68v2 four-by-three analogy; no new Voynich page, image or text opened. Target f68v2 is not currently admitted.

The [institutional primary scan of fol. 99r](https://zimks68.uni-trier.de/stmatthias/T1084/T1084-00203-DOWNLOAD.pdf), linked as *Windkarte* from the [DFG viewer](https://dfg-viewer.de/show?cHash=5160a2b4624abd45b45237bb79f3a05d&tx_dlf%5Bdouble%5D=0&tx_dlf%5Bid%5D=http%3A%2F%2Fzimks68.uni-trier.de%2Fstmatthias%2FT1084%2FT1084-digitalisat.xml&tx_dlf%5Bpage%5D=203), was obtained and visually inspected (PDF SHA-256 `121c5431c8b4b33e0293d7e63c1cf289ddc551a38f37772de460800be99b152d`). It shows a central T-O world inside a square and twelve textual wind/month sectors. The middle sector of each three-sector side begins with a cardinal-wind formula: east/top Subsolanus with `August`, south/right Auster with `Novemb`, west/bottom Zephirus with `Februarius`, north/left Septentrio with `Maius`. The flanking sectors also contain a wind description and one of the remaining month names; the top three visibly read `Iulius` / `August` / `Septemb`. The compass headings *Oriens*, *Meridies*, *Occidens*, *Septentrio* sit outside the wheel. Barbara Obrist's [facsimile and reading, fig. 18](https://pdf.danmalo.info/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Wind-Diagrams-and-Medieval-Cosmology.pdf), identifies the middle member of each triplet as cardinal and the texts as Isidorian winds; the later [Wind Diagrams chapter](https://kartdok.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/kartdok_derivate_00000287/10_1515_9781501516016.pdf) independently identifies the manuscript and its wind/month combination. The exact word-by-word wind prose was not diplomatically transcribed by this project.

This differs materially from Laon MS 422 f.6v: Laon's four *season figures* span three month sectors each, and all twelve radial written sectors are months. Trier has four *cardinal wind sectors* as the middle of four triplets, eight collateral wind sectors on their sides, and **one month name in each of those same twelve sectors**. An earlier version of this note mistakenly placed the months in a separate perimeter register; the institutional image corrects that source error. It is an independently sourced instance of **middle-versus-flank wind roles within twelve radial written sectors**, alongside a complete month cycle, and hence a closer class analogue to a putative four-center/eight-flank reading than the Laon source. The exact Trier wind/month combination is absent from the targeted local source/decision search as of this note; Walters W.73 f.2r, by contrast, was already the source of GDT952 and cannot count as new.

The analogy still lacks the necessary target contract. Historical Voynich f68v2 inventories describe four sparse star-bearing centers and eight radial titles in different visual layers, not twelve same-layer wind/month prose sectors. The accompanying same-folio f68r3 stellar payload has no Trier match. No independently fixed Voynich north, clockwise start, phase, cardinal word owner, or correspondence between target text layers and Trier's written wind/month layer exists. GDT353 found no exceptional ordered title alignment; its failed rotation cannot be repaired by choosing a favorable Trier origin. GDT1067/GDT1081 continue to block name/direction transfer on the admitted f69r wind-like wheel. GDT952's already tested Walters name network is unchanged.

Decision: retain a **positive source-role parallel, zero current word-test capacity**. A future lexical test requires a registered f68v2 access contract, independent target layer ownership and a predeclared invariant consequence that distinguishes wind-class text from month/star alternatives without choosing a post-hoc phase. No Voynich wind or month name is inferred, f84/f84r and reserves remained closed, and there is no confirmed translated word.
