# Arsenal MS 2888 f. 197r: Trinité belongs to a trifoliate drawing

28 September 2026. Exploratory source-only comparison after the f9v
wild-pansy/Jacea ambiguity was known; no prospective target-word test. The
[Biblissima/Jonas catalogue record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/fr/ark:/43093/mdatae9907eafe1cf2fa8ee29eea0b9c99e947b4cab63)
dates Paris, Bibliothèque de l'Arsenal MS 2888 to the first half of the
fifteenth century (1400–1450) and describes the *Livre des simples médecines*.
The [institutional Gallica IIIF manifest](https://gallica.bnf.fr/iiif/ark:/12148/btv1b550098035/manifest.json)
maps **f. 197r** to image f405. Its [original image](https://gallica.bnf.fr/iiif/ark:/12148/btv1b550098035/f405/full/full/0/native.jpg)
was inspected directly. The 1500-pixel rendition inspected has SHA256
`a4d5909307b10956a3a5667bf18fad33867376ac2493b0e1c83c4760c55b5109`.

The page has two separately illustrated entries. The plant at right carries
several sets of three broad leaflets, fibrous roots and no visible open
five-petalled flower. A small `trinite` caption sits against its stems, and
the text starting directly under that drawing begins with `Trinite` in a
decorated initial. The plant at left has divided leaves and small pale/yellow
flower heads; it belongs to the neighboring entry and must not be combined
with the right-hand `Trinite` owner. This visible page boundary is the
decisive observation; the remaining French medical passage was not fully
transcribed and no modern species was assigned to either drawing.

This is a concrete early use of the *Trinité* name with a pictured
trifoliate referent, unlike f9v's five unequal blue/pale petals and mixed
simple/divided leaves. It cautions against transferring the later or modern
"Herba Trinitatis = pansy" gloss to a circa-1420 image on the basis of name
alone. It does **not** show that the f9v plant cannot be a pansy, nor that
`fochor` is or is not a plant name. No new Voynich target, reserve or sealed
material was opened; confirmed Voynich word meanings remain zero.
