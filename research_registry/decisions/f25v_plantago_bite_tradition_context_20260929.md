# f25v Plantago/bite tradition: source context, not a word reading

**Status:** C0 historical-content lead after GDT1093 and its append-only
Plantago shortlist correction; no new Voynich text or image access. This note
does not change the GDT1093 strict negative decision.

The earlier [Egerton 747 f77v](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x0000a2/full/1500,/0/default.jpg)
labels a flattened green fan and brown fine-root tuft `plantago`. Its
[f78r](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x0000a3/full/1500,/0/default.jpg)
continues Plantago prose, captions a similar illustration `plantago minor`,
and places a snake and scorpion in the prose margin. Both also have prominent
flower spikes absent from Voynich f25v. The GDT1093 correction documents the
direct comparison and image hashes.

An earlier Pseudo-Apuleius witness, Leiden UB VLQ 9 f20r, explicitly labels
its broad fan-shaped, fibrous-rooted illustration `Herba arnoglossa` in the
[manuscript image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MS_Voss._Q_9_fol._20r.jpg)
(1400px reproduction SHA-256
`c1be53129971e08cc81e24a9d10eb4cb390ab9344d9fc83f561e6400efff89ad`).
The [published transcription of the same Plantago unit](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9789048557745-017/pdf?licenseType=open-access)
includes separate treatments for snake bite, scorpion sting, and rabid dog
bite. The Leiden image has flower spikes and **no animal**. Egerton and Leiden
are different surviving witnesses, but source-family dependence and generic
plantain imagery prevent treating them as independent identification votes.

This combination permits one *testable concept*, not an English word:
Voynich f25v could depict a plant associated with an animal-bite remedy, with
the little quadruped a use cue rather than a rebus of the plant's name.
Alternatives remain: an animal unrelated to use, a generic decorative beast,
a different bite-remedy plant, or a differently coded drawing. Historical
comparisons to f25v Plantago and snake-bite imagery already existed in the
[pre-1960 tentative-identification table](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/herb_oldid.html)
and [2017 research discussion](https://www.voynich.ninja/printthread.php?page=3&tid=1546).
This lead is therefore neither a fresh independent prediction nor a
decipherment.

**Decision:** do not open f25v transcription on this basis alone. Plantago
appears in many remedies; selecting snake/dog bite *after* seeing f25v's
animal is not a unique lexical consequence. A future fixed test needs a
distinguishing Voynich-side prediction defined before reading f25v text,
including rival named bite plants and non-bite animal illustrations. More
source images of common fan/rosette plants without such contrast add no
meaning binding. f84/f84r and reserved data stay closed.
