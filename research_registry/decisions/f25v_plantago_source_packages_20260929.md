## Exact source and fixed alternatives

Primary text: *Pseudo-Apuleius, Herbarium*, Leiden UB MS VLQ 9, Plantago
entry, transcribed and translated by Claudine A. Chavannes-Mazel in
*The Green Middle Ages*, Appendix I, pp. 301–309. Chapter
[DOI 10.1515/9789048557745-017](https://doi.org/10.1515/9789048557745-017);

| SNAKE | VLQ 9 f21v; transcription p.303, heading `Ad morsum serpentis`; translation p.306, item 8. Local phrase `ex uino`. | A snake bite is the indication; Plantago herb is crushed with wine and taken as a drink; relief is asserted. |
| SCORPION | VLQ 9 f21v; transcription p.303, heading `Ad scorpionum percum`; translation p.306, item 9. Local phrase `radicem adligatam`. | A scorpion sting is the indication; the root of Plantago is bound to the affected place; benefit is reported as a belief. |
| DOG | VLQ 9 f22v; transcription p.304, heading `Ad canis ravidi morsum`; translation p.306, item 25. Local phrase `contusa et inposita`. | A rabid dog's bite is the indication; crushed Plantago herb is applied locally; healing is asserted. |

“Herb” preserves the source's distinction from an explicitly named root; it
does not assert that every physical organ is included. The snake transcription
has a problematic string before the wine phrase; crushing and oral use here
follow the edition's complete English translation. No silent Latin repair is
introduced. “Applied locally” follows the translation; the source does not
support turning the dog package into an oral medicine. These are historical
claims made by the text, not present medical advice.
