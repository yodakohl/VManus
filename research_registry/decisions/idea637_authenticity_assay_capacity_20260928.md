# IDEA637: genuine drug versus substitute, target-capacity screen

2026-09-28. Exploratory review before selecting a historical authenticity
passage or any Voynich reading. The proposal requires one physical assay
applied to two identified materials with opposite observed outcomes. A shared
plant appearance, neighboring drug names, or therapeutic use is not that
relation.

The admitted target baselines do not presently supply an independently owned
assay graph. [GDT735](../../experiments/yolo/gdt735_historical_semantic_bridge_atlas/REPORT.md)
establishes a historical descriptive/prescriptive *record architecture* while
leaving the four target head semantics unidentified; all 24 part-name
permutations fit the formal grid. It therefore cannot identify a genuine
material and its substitute. [W89](../proposals/translation_programs_20260912/work/W89/REPORT.md)
retains recipe and descriptive whole readings without independent content
selection. [GDT389](../../experiments/yolo/gdt389_connector_edge_census/REPORT.md)
and [GDT393](../../experiments/yolo/gdt393_remaining_page_connector_census/REPORT.md)
find zero eligible author-visible directed inscription edges in their fixed
permitted page frames. This is not a claim that no Voynich paragraph could
describe a test; it means their inspected visual links cannot own the
required material→operation→result connections. No nominated complete
admitted target currently binds two substances, one common test and opposite
results without assigning unconfirmed words first.

Decision: **not tested**. A dated historical source might provide the full
genuine/substitute assay, but mapping it to a target now would be arbitrary.
Reopen only with new admitted, independently owned target evidence for the
two materials, common operation and opposing results, followed by a frozen
whole-record comparison against the source and a two-material inventory
rival. The current four-head pattern, an image resemblance or a plausible
recipe paraphrase is insufficient. No source was selected; no word was
translated.
