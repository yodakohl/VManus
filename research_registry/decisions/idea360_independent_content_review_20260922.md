## Source boundary and native verification

I viewed both complete cached page renders of Boncompagni's 1857 *Liber abbaci*,
printed pp. 284–285, PDF one-based pages 290–291 (zero-based indices 289–290).
The bounded content starts `Qvatuor homines sunt` on p. 284 and ends `ergo
quartus habet denarios 20` on p. 285, before the five-man problem. The rabbit
material above and subsequent five-man material visible on the full pages are
not incorporated. Both four-man result tables on p. 284 are retained.

The source visibly has the four triple sums 27,31,34,37; their sum 129;
threefold counting; total 43; individual values 12,9,6,16 and their check 43.
It then uses the same four numbers for adjacent pairs, computes the two covers
61 and 68, and explicitly calls this impossible/insoluble. It explicitly
changes the fourth-plus-first amount to 30 to make a soluble problem, keeping
the others in order. On p. 285, `ad libitum` introduces a freely chosen first
holding from the shared 27; the illustrative choice 10 leads to 17,14,20.
The second table agrees. No conflicting value or false source claim was found.

Important distinctions remain: uniqueness is a mathematical consequence, not
a separately identified source word meaning UNIQUE. The prose does not spell
out the final addition 20+10=30; that is a modern closing check. The general
affine family and integer/positivity bounds below are modern formalization.
The witness does not state modern domain axioms or whether the endpoints 0
and 27 count as permitted holdings. Earlier OCR errors are not alternative
numerical models; the already-owned visible print supports the dossier's
129,16,6 and p. 285 initial 27. No new OCR or download was used.

## Exact multi-step content

Holdings are fixed within each expressly hypothetical case. Repeated ordinal
participants refer to the same participant inside that case; their holdings
must not be reset at each equation. The explicitly introduced alternative
cases may have different holdings. Identifying holdings across all three
hypothetical cases would misread the source.

| Complete case | Consequence under those identities |
|---|---|
| Triples 123=27, 234=31, 341=34, 412=37 | Every holding occurs three times: 3T=129, T=43. Complement subtraction uniquely gives (x1,x2,x3,x4)=(12,9,6,16). All four equations and printed total agree. |
| Pairs 12=27, 23=31, 34=34, 41=37 | Two covers of the same four people require T=27+34=61 and T=31+37=68. No solution, even over signed or fractional amounts. |
| Revised pairs 12=27, 23=31, 34=34, 41=30 | Covers agree at 61. Set x1=t: x2=27−t, x3=4+t, x4=30−t. The final equation holds for every t; it does not determine t. |

The triple incidence matrix has rank 4; the cyclic pair matrix has rank 3
and null direction (1,−1,1,−1). With nonnegative integer holdings the revised
case has t=0,…,27; with strictly positive integers, t=1,…,26. The source's
t=10 gives (10,17,14,20), one example rather than an identified answer.

**A precise invariant survives the free choice.** Subtracting consecutive pair
claims gives x3−x1=31−27=4 and x4−x2=34−31=3. The original closing amount 37
would also require x4−x2=37−27=10: the same 7-unit inconsistency appears as
3=10. The explicit replacement 30 instead requires x4−x2=30−27=3. Thus the
corrected case determines total 61 and these opposite-person differences even
though it leaves every absolute holding dependent on t. This is a useful
derived content obligation, not a newly observed manuscript result or a new
experiment. The proposal already contains the equivalent affine family.

General integer and positivity bounds must be stated if a future model uses
them. Merely quoting the source's equality criterion must not silently import
a universal modern domain theorem. For these exact amounts all reported
solutions and the positive example are valid.

## Strongest prospective written discriminator

A complete future account must jointly determine which participants belong to
each subset and which claims are asserted, rejected as impossible, newly
supposed, or freely chosen. Holding the four amounts fixed, triple versus pair
incidence changes the result from unique to impossible. Keeping the corrected
pair incidence fixed, claiming the equations force 10 would be false, whereas
the two opposite-person differences and total remain forced. These consequences
cannot be removed by choosing another t.

Accordingly a writer must preserve the whole argument: question, repeated
participant references, sum and counting justification, subtraction and check,
general solvability comparison, explicitly impossible example, explicitly
revised amount, and free choice followed by propagation. A contradiction cannot
be relabelled a negative example after a target fit; a fresh hypothetical case
cannot be inserted without a declared expression. Universal truth of every
embedded equation set would also be the wrong contract because the source
deliberately contains an impossible set.

Global participant renaming and equivalent clause orders remain symmetries.
Unbound units permit rescaling; none of these relations alone identifies men,
coins, the numeral 10 or historical wording. A complete hypothetical writing
proposal is allowed without any confirmed word, but it has to supply the
missing shared realization rather than substitute arithmetic consistency for it.
