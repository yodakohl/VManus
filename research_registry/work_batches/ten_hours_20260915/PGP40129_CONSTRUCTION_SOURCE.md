## Cross-record constraint architecture

The three selected records supply linked constraints while preserving their
boundaries. Supplemental examples are kept separate and are not counted in the
selected-record inventory:

1. **Article recurrence:** `אל` appears on both members of several adjacent
   noun–modifier candidates (`אלמעדה אלבארדה`, `אלמואד אלצפראויה`, `אלרמאן
   אלחלוא`). `אלמא אלבארד` is supplemental recto 2.
2. **Surface agreement variants:** related positions visibly vary in final
   morphology (`אלבארד` / `אלבארדה`, `אלחארתין`) while the article-bearing
   prefix is stable. The variation is evidence to preserve, not permission to
   normalize.
3. **Definite coordination:** `אלחרארה ואלברודה` occurs in the selected
   pomegranate record at recto 19 and in the selected quince record at verso
   12; its further verso 19 occurrence is supplemental. The conjunction is
   attached to the second word in the source.
4. **Construct/idāfa:** noun + article-bearing noun appears in
   `חב אלרמאן אלחאמץֹ` and `אצחאב אלמזאגֹ אלבארד`. `עקל אלטביעה` is kept
   separate as a likely verb–object construction.
5. **Suffix possession:** `פם מעדתהא` repeats exactly across verso 2 and 4
   within the selected pomegranate record; supplemental `ראיחתה` (verso 21)
   supplies a distinct noun-plus-suffix pattern outside the selected boundary.
6. **Predicate contrast:** `אלכוך. בארד רטב` (recto 5) and `אלספרגֹל
   בארד יאבס קאבץֹ` (verso 6) preserve unprefixed property sequences beside
   the prefixed agreement candidates.

This is a concrete grammar hypothesis because one frozen source packet offers
multiple recurring constraints: article placement, adjacency, coordination,
construct position, and possessive suffix recurrence. The repeated forms are
not independent historical replications. The hypothesis remains conditional.
A source-only extraction cannot establish that every `אל` is an Arabic definite
article, that every following word is an adjective, or that all article-bearing
pairs share one agreement rule.
