### IV — De suffocatione matricis

| ID | Complete source content |
|---|---|
| IV01 | The condition is defined by the matrix rising; stomach disturbance and weakened appetite are linked with weakness of the heart. |
| IV02 | Fainting and an imperceptible pulse can occur from the same cause. |
| IV03 | The woman may contract with head against knees, lose sight and speech, have a distorted nose, contracted lips, clenched teeth and an unusually raised chest. |
| IV04 | Galen's case: a woman lost pulse and voice and appeared dead; no external life sign was seen although a little heat remained near the heart. Some judged her dead. Galen placed well-carded wool at her nose and mouth and recognized life from its motion. This wool is a diagnostic object, not one of the burnt odor remedies. |
| IV05 | The text attributes the disease to excessive corrupted seed becoming poisonous. |
| IV06 | It singles out women without intercourse, especially widows formerly sexually active, and virgins of marriageable age unable to have intercourse; the author's account says seed accumulates and would naturally be discharged through a male. |
| IV07 | A cold vapor from this excessive corrupted seed ascends to parts called collaterales near heart, lung and voice organs, obstructing speech. |
| IV08 | Menstrual deficiency can also be a principal cause. When menstruation is absent and seed excessive, the disease is more troublesome and prolonged, especially in higher parts. |
| IV09 | Moderately rub hands and feet with laurel oil. |
| IV10 | Apply strong-smelling substances to the nose: galbanum, opoponacum, castoreum, pitch, burnt wool, burnt linen, burnt skin and asa fetida. |
| IV11 | Anoint the vulvae inside and outside with aromatic oils/ointments: oleum muscellinum, nard oil; the text ranks a zibethum ointment above them. |
| IV12 | These are said to attract and provoke **menstrua**. The grammatical object is not an explicitly stated womb attracted toward a pleasant smell. |
| IV13 | Apply cups in inguine and pectine. |
| IV14 | In the evening give diaciminum with juice of apium or nepita, or syrup of calamentum. |
| IV15 | Alternative: castoreum, white pepper, costus, mint and apium, one drachm each; grind and mix with white or sweet wine; give two drachms in the evening. |
| IV16 | A physician printed as Iustianus prescribes dried cumin: one drachm or two spoonfuls as a drink. Do not silently equate these measures. |
| IV17 | He also prescribes powder from a fox's or capriolus's penis, introduced by pessary. |
| IV18 | Boiled lovage root, ground with axungia and tied over the navel, is said to help the same condition. |

### V — De descensu matricis

| ID | Complete source content |
|---|---|
| V01 | If after childbirth the matrix descends too far from its place, apply previously moistened and heated oats in a bag. |
| V02 | It may move from its place, descend, or emerge outside through the vulva. These are distinguished descriptions, not a two-state toggle. |
| V03 | The author attributes this to softened nerves and abundant cold humors. |
| V04 | Softening/cooling is attributed to cold air entering the openings, for example direct exposure while uncovered, or sitting long on a cold stone. |
| V05 | A cold-water bath is another cause: it weakens the matrix, which leaves its place. |
| V06 | Straining in childbirth is another stated cause. The print's construction is awkward; do not repair it into a precise mechanism. |
| V07 | **If it descends and does not emerge**, apply aromatics to the nose: balsamum, muscus, ambra, spica, storax and the like. |
| V08 | In that branch, fumigate **below** with foul-smelling substances, for example burnt linen. The phrase says inferius; a precise application to the organ itself is an interpretation. |
| V09 | Foment the navel with wool soaked in wine and oil. |
| V10 | **If it has emerged**, mix aromatics with absinthium juice and anoint the belly using a feather. |
| V11 | Then take equal parts ruta, castoreum and arthemisia; boil in wine until two parts are consumed, then administer as a drink. The starting volume is not stated here. |
| V12 | Apply boiled grain in bags to belly and navel. |
| V13 | Then manually restore the emerged matrix to its own place; the text includes concussa, whose exact manipulation is not resolved here. |
| V14 | Then the woman enters water in which balaustiae, roses, pomegranate peel, galls, sumach, myrtilli, acorns, oak leaves/bark, cypress nuts and lentils have been boiled. |
| V15 | Then make a stupha; Galen prescribes boxwood in a pot over live coals, with the woman seated covered above it, receiving the smoke inside herself. This is distinct from the earlier foul-smell branch. |
| V16 | Diet is cold and astringent, without cumin, pepper or any pungency. |
| V17 | Fruits: coctana, mespila, sorba, sour apples and similar things. |
| V18 | Drink tempered wine participating in astringency. |
| V19 | An additional experimentum for an emerged matrix: powdered deer heart and laurel leaves, one drachm each, and myrrh, one scruple; grind, mix with wine and give to drink. The author claims the matrix will return to its place. This alternative is retained, not merged with manual replacement. |

### VI — displacement without ascent or descent

The neighboring complete chapter provides a retained counterexample to reducing
the source's entire anatomy to UP/DOWN. It was read before a target draft.

| ID | Complete source content |
|---|---|
| VI01 | The matrix can move from its place without rising toward the spiritualia, emerging through its opening, or descending. |
| VI02 | Signs: left-side pain, menstrual retention, distorted limbs, difficulty urinating, abdominal twisting and rumbling. |
| VI03 | Take apium and foenumgraecum, grind with wine and give as a drink. |
| VI04 | Alternative: powder agaricum, plantain seed and satureia; give with wine or cooked honey. |
| VI05 | To prevent movement and hardness: deer marrow, goose fat, red wax and butter, two ounces each; add foenumgraecum and linseed, cook in water over a slow fire until fully cooked, and introduce by pessary. |
| VI06 | The author calls this useful against several diseases of the mouth of the matrix. |

## What the proposed construction actually predicts

| Source condition | Nose | Lower application | Additional branch distinctions |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV upward/suffocation account | Strong odor, including burnt linen | Aromatic ointment, inside/outside vulvae | Diagnostic wool motion; semen/vapor/menstrual account; numerous separate remedies |
| V descent **without emergence** | Aromatics | Foul fumigation below, including burnt linen | Navel fomentation |
| V emerged organ | No nose instruction in this branch | Belly ointment; drink; grain bags; manual restoration; bath; later smoke | Diet and a separate return remedy |
| VI moved but neither up/down/out | No opposed nose/lower pair | Drinks and preventive pessary | Left-side pain and further symptoms |

The two odor qualities exchange sites across IV and one branch of V. They do
not alone encode the complete source. Attraction/avoidance of the organ is a
possible historical explanatory hypothesis, **not an explicit common causal
sentence recovered in these chapters**. IV12 explicitly says menstrua.

Before any Voynich comparison, the proposed source-based reading must preserve
the distinction between an application site's location and the organ's state;
descent versus emergence; recommendation versus observed transition; and the
diagnostic wool versus burnt wool. It must not convert all subsequent condition
branches into a single patient's uninterrupted journey. Old GDT985 functions
were conditional on exactly such shared-participant/current-source assumptions;
they are not automatically evidence for this different construction.

**Decision: source relation retained; no target binding for a complete reading.**
The exact source distinction has useful content: four illness branches, exchanged
odor sites in two of them, and an explicit no-ascent/no-descent countercase. The
two Voynich pages do not presently identify those branches or odors. Therefore
no ol/or=upper/lower or pleasant/foul assignment, no same-patient continuation,
and no automatic full tree-code search is selected. This is missing binding,
not a disproof of all gynecological content. A future draft must nominate and
account for a whole written paragraph with shared meanings and preserve the
source's branch distinctions; a newly noticed bowl or nude figure is insufficient.
