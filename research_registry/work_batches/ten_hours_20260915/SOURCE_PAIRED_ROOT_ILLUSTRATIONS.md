# Source-paired historical Orchis illustrations

**Bounded source-only search, 15 September 2026.** This dossier records only named historical illustrations in digital-library records. It does not open or use Voynich images, target text, or target observations. It does not infer a modern species from an ancient name. The relevant Dioscoridean name is retained as the historical label `orchis` / `ὄρχις` (and, where transmitted, `cynos orchis`); modern species names in catalog records are not used as evidence.

## Source assessment

- **Best usable pair:** Morgan MS M.652 f.121v supplies a named image, a pre-1450 manuscript, an exact folio, an institutional image viewer, and the explicit wording “inscribed orchis.”
- **Early corroboration:** BnF Grec 2179 supplies two 8th-century folio-face records in a Dioscorides manuscript, each catalogued as `orchis`, with Gallica image access. They corroborate the existence of an early named Orchis image, but the machine-readable records do not by themselves prove that the label is visually legible or give a diplomatic transcription.
- **Independence:** A and B must not be counted as independent witnesses; they are f.58 and f.58v of the same codex. C is a separate manuscript witness.
- **Chapter control:** These records identify the plant as `orchis` in a Dioscorides *De materia medica* witness. This dossier does not silently equate a folio to a modern species or claim a chapter number without checking the actual page sequence. `III.126` is therefore a source-navigation hypothesis for later philological checking, not an image-derived result here.
- **No third independent early witness was accepted within the bound.** Later illustrated editions and records using modern species names were not substituted for a securely historical, pre-1450 named witness.

## What this can and cannot support

The records support a concrete, reviewable source pair: an early named Orchis image (BnF, with catalogue-level label evidence) and a separate mid-10th-century named/inscribed Orchis image (Morgan). They can motivate a source comparison of root depiction, plant-part layout, and label placement after independent chapter and image verification. They do **not** establish that the same plant morphology, root anatomy, or semantics maps to any Voynich form, nor do they provide a decipherment anchor.
