## A. Fiore: marks identify roles, while prose binds the inherited procedure

Source: Fiore dei Liberi, *Fior di Battaglia*, Getty Ms. Ludwig XV13,
83.MR.183. Getty's [institutional catalogue](https://www.getty.edu/cona/CONAFullSubject.aspx?subid=700002326)
dates this manuscript **around1410**. This is not the dated1409 Pisani Dossi
witness. The bounded primary set is Getty2r,6v,16v, all actually viewed natively.
Wiktenauer's page transcriptions were used to navigate and collate the original
Italian, not as independent English evidence. Their status says proofread,
not validated. No English translation is copied into the proposed code.

Fiore explicitly uses the same crown for an initial guard master and a remedy
master, a leg band for a dependent scholar, and crown plus band for a counter;
the written context determines which master and inherited action are meant.

### Complete explanatory rule block on2r

The block follows the initial discussion of grappling and ends at the page's
instruction to attend to the painted figures, their plays and words. Its
content is preserved here as an ordered inventory rather than disguised as
a fixed-length historical serialization:

1. Four initial crowned figures show the grappling guards: Posta longa and
   dente di cengiaro oppose each other, as do porta di ferro and posta frontale.
   The text says these guards support the previously listed grappling actions
   in and out of armour. The earlier list is a prerequisite, not reproduced
   as a newly invented set of opcodes.
2. The reader must distinguish masters, players and scholars, and remedy from
   counter. The counter follows its remedy, sometimes immediately and sometimes
   after its plays. This statement is not a universal immediate-adjacency rule.
3. The initial masters wait in guards facing one another without contact.
   They wear crowns. The text explains guard/posta through defence and placing
   oneself to act against the opponent; this prose explanation is part of the
   source, not an additional recovered target distinction.
4. The following master responds to an opponent's action from those guards.
   He also wears a crown, but is called the second or remedy master.
5. His following players perform actions available to that master, starting
   from the same cover or hold. They wear a band below the knee. This family
   continues until a master countering the remedy and its scholars appears.
6. That counter bears both the master's crown and the scholars' band. The
   author calls it the third master and contrario.
7. Some passages contain a fourth master, contra contrario, countering the
   third. The text says few plays go beyond the third and warns of danger.
   The page does not license an unlimited recursive counter hierarchy.
8. The same master/scholar scheme applies to other weapon sections and mounted
   as well as foot combat, in and out of armour. The source ends by saying
   rubrics, figures, plays and words together make the art understandable.
   This is an explicitly multimodal reference scheme, not a claim that one
   small icon contains every motion or outcome.

Key source wording at2r, with abbreviations expanded for readability, is:

> E questo segondo zoe rimedio si a algui zugadori sotto di si i'quali zugano quelli zogi che poria zugare lo Magistro ch'e d'avanti zoe lo rimedio piglando quella coverta overo presa che fa lo ditto rimedio. E questi zugadori portarano una divisa sotto lo zinochio. E sarano questi zugadori tutti li zoghi delo rimedio in fin tanto che si trovara un altro Magistro che fara lu contrario delo rimedio e di tutti suoi zugadori.

This paragraph owns both the common starting action and the end of its normal
family scope. Crown does not mean a particular physical person; band does not
mean a fixed side of the picture. On6v the first acting figure wears a crown
and the other three acting figures wear bands. On16v the marked actor appears
on different sides in different scenes; its lower-left scene's actor wears
both crown and band. These are direct observations of those three source pages,
not a claim of error-free mark assignment throughout every Fiore witness.

### Connected play-family reference inventory on 6v

The four full scene paragraphs retain the following dependency structure:

- **6v-a:** identifies the first grappling play, says it can be reached from
  the grappling guards, describes the starting hold, and directs continuation
  into the second play. It names alternative outcomes of a fall or arm injury.
- **6v-b:** calls the speaker the first master's scholar and states the same
  ordinary outcome family. It adds a conditional branch: if the opponent
  releases the specified shoulder contact for another defence, the substitute
  scholar changes his hold and proceeds to the depicted third play.
- **6v-c:** expressly confirms the preceding scholar's account and says the
  latter's hold leads into this one. It repeats the conditional release and
  third-play reference. It distinguishes the first/second play's face-down
  endpoint from the third play's back-down endpoint. The first and second are
  called one proper play, not two independent actors with unrelated starts.
- **6v-d:** gives the fourth play and retains an explicit failure branch:
  if this route does not produce a fall, seek other plays/holds shown later.
  It says plays and holds vary and urges obtaining an advantageous hold.
  Those later alternatives are an unresolved external call in this bounded
  extract, not invisible successful continuation inserted into a model.

The body-action details are present in the linked original and corresponding
full transcription; this inventory records the reference claims and branches. It does not
replace the full paragraphs with a claim of verbatim completeness. It is not a reconstructed biomechanical
simulation, an assertion that the moves work, or a verbatim translation.

The source-owned dependency matters: the scholar's word "I" substitutes for
the master in an inherited role. It need not name the same pictured individual.
The branch retains the opponent and a specific condition, but leads to a
different endpoint. An unordered set of "master / scholar / action" labels
would lose precisely this constraint.

### The source itself rejects a nearest-master-only rule

The complete16v-d paragraph, again with abbreviation expansion and no spelling
modernization beyond word spacing, reads:

> Ben che sia posto dredo lo contrario del Sesto zogo io vo per rasone denançi de luii, per che io son so scolaro e questo zogo si e suo zoe del Magistro Sexto. E vale piu questo zogo in arme che senç'arme, pero fiero costuii in la mano, per che in quello logo non si po ben armare. Per che se uno e disarmato çercheria de ferirlo in lo volto o in lo petto. Overo in logo che pezo gl'avenisse.

Its opening explicitly says that although placed after the counter, this
scholar belongs logically before it and to the sixth master. Its remainder
compares armour conditions and names different intended actions under them.
Thus the placement correction is genuinely written, not an analyst's repair
to recover a desired family. The depicted figure has a band without crown.
The full paragraph remains part of the source; it is not reduced to an isolated
ordinal or used as a numeral-code example.

### Raw reference mechanism and distinct rivals

A minimal semantic form is `Family(master, shared_start, variants, counter)`.
Each variant keeps the master's named starting cover/hold and changes only
its expressly described continuation or conditional branch. A marked counter
targets the referenced family. An expressly named cross-reference can override
the normal displayed scope; arbitrary per-occurrence overrides are forbidden.
This is a finite set of typed reference operations, with unresolved concrete
actions retained as such, not a free causal graph whose edges can be chosen
after reading the desired outcome.

Three different consequences are available before any manuscript adaptation:

1. A sign-only dictionary cannot tell initial guard master from remedy master:
   both have crowns, but different discourse roles.
2. A nearest-preceding-master policy misassigns16v-d. The source's explicit
   named ownership preserves the sixth master's family despite placement.
3. A policy allowing each scholar a fresh initial hold loses the common-start
   condition and the paired conditional reference on6v. It would no longer
   represent the stated content, even if every figure received a plausible role.

These constraints still leave symmetries: renaming masters consistently or
swapping anonymous participant identities can preserve the abstract graph.
The icon inventory does not provide a text cipher. A prospective Voynich
application needs a complete paragraph and an actual finite realization of
shared reference and branch conditions; no such owner is supplied here.
GDT947's rejected olshedy/olshey overt-core duties are not reopened. No ol,
q-, or inherited renderer value is assigned to these source operations.

## B. Counterchanged tinctures: one reference acts differently by region

**The readable witness in this task is1486, not before1450.** The anonymous
heraldic section of *The Boke of Saint Albans* is read through the Blades
facsimile edition represented by [Project Gutenberg71266](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/71266/pg71266-images.html#Page_195).
This is a primary-text reproduction, not evidence that the exact wording was
already used at the date of the Voynich manuscript. The older Laud Misc.733
comparison was navigated from the existing dossier, but requested further
images timed out; their contents are unassessed, neither absent nor confirmed.

Two complete adjacent descriptions on page195 use the same backward reference,
`de dictis coloribus transmutatis`, after declaring different field divisions
and color pairs. The phrase determines the charge's color from the region in
which it lies instead of giving the whole charge one context-free color.

The first complete item introduces unusual chevrons whose transposed colors
cause difficulty in blazoning, points to the shield, then supplies Latin,
French and English descriptions. The Latin reads:

> Portat arma quarteriata de nigro & argento cum vno signo capitali de dictis coloribus transmutatis.

The French gives a quartered sable/silver field and a chevron `chaungee lung
de laultre`; the English repeats that complete content. There is no additional
social or genealogical claim in this bounded item. The accompanying image
was actually viewed: its quartered black/white field has a chevron whose
segments exchange those two values across the field division.

The second complete item says the chevron example is divided along the long
direction, points to its figure, and again supplies all three languages:

> Portat arma partita secundum longum de coloribus aureo & rubeo cum vno signo capitali de dictis coloribus transmutatis.

Here the declared pair is gold/red. Its French and English versions name
that pair again and the same color-exchange relation. Its image was actually
viewed and confirms a vertical partition and opposed chevron regions, but
this reproduction is monochrome: it does **not** independently verify gold
versus red. Those names are source-written textual assertions. The next
heading begins a different item about disagreements among heralds; it is
not appended as an invisible clause to either complete chevron item.

The short source content is not just a repeated color list. A finite modern
semantic expansion uses `Field(partition,c0,c1)`, `Charge(chevron)`, and
`Counterchange(charge,that_field)`. For each region i, its field has c_i and
the portion of the charge in that region has the other member of the same
two-color pair. Partition identity and color-pair identity are shared; they
cannot be selected anew for each segment.

This predicts something a context-free charge-color label does not: the
charge changes color when it crosses the partition. Copying the field color
onto the charge erases the displayed contrast. Giving the whole chevron one
uniform color fails on the opposite field region. Merely swapping all color
names globally, however, preserves the relative relation; anonymous color
classes cannot identify "sable", "silver", "gold", or "red" by themselves.
The two source examples differ in both partition and palette, so they are
not represented as an isolated experimental manipulation of only one feature.

This is a source-attested content constructor with a backward color reference,
not a licence for arbitrary descriptions of any coloured shape. It supplies
no token-count equality, alphabet, one-word-per-operation rule, or invisible
argument. The complete Latin/French/English descriptions are alternative
realizations of the same armorial claim; if a future model claims the whole
expository paragraph, its instruction, illustration call and language labels
must be included too. A narrower armorial-content language must declare that
projection explicitly.
