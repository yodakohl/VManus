## Does f17r discriminate them?

Not under the currently exposed evidence. Within the local P3 paragraph, the
only repeated whole token is `y` (twice). Page-wide, `otchol`, `chol`, and
`chor` recur across the three prose paragraphs, but none has an independently
established content identity. The tempting line
`otchol cthar okaiin chol daiiin` has singleton forms in this paragraph; the
earlier GDT813 readings of them as “this / root / powder-or-is / dry / high”
are explicitly provisional alternatives, not anchors. Assigning those forms to
upper/lower, tender/dry, route, or reproductive polarity would simply choose R1
because it fits the source.

The smallest fair page-wide consequence would therefore need two independently
bound target signals: (1) a repeated or explicitly contrasted form pair that marks
the two root states, and (2) a reused route/effect relation whose polarity
changes between the state branches. A qualifying R1 reading would predict the
same state relation across both branches while preserving distinct outcomes;
R2 predicts no such cross-branch relation. The exposed page supplies neither
signal. The native image supplies uncertain shapes, not those content
relations, and the Morgan Orchis comparator is a historical label, not a
Voynich binding.

A six-line local trace could still be recorded with all 29 tokens and explicit
UNKNOWN fields, but it would not test the whole f17r page. A page-wide trace
would have to retain all 78 prose tokens and the separate f17r.13 inscription;
its current outcome would still be `UNKNOWN/CAPACITY`, not a semantic win. A
failure after assigning free forms would be a failure of that assignment, not
a refutation of Orchis. Running another whole-form solver would add no
information and would reproduce the GDT963 identifiability problem. The fair
decision is to keep R1 as a conditional local source hypothesis and not select
a page-wide Orchis content experiment until the two state and route/polarity
signals are bound by already admitted evidence.
