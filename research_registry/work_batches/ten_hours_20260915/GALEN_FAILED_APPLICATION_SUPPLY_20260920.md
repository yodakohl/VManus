## Complete owned content

[Galen, De locis affectis I.6](https://www.graeco-arabic-studies.org/view/gal.delocisaffectis-orig-gr1) gives a complete reported clinical episode. A patient reports thirty days of finger sensory loss with motion preserved and no benefit from applied medicines. Galen asks the prior doctor which medicines were used and judges them appropriate. He asks the patient about preceding symptoms, receives an initial denial of inflammation/cold/blows, asks again about a higher site, and elicits a fall from a vehicle on the way to Rome shortly before onset. He conjectures injury at a nerve origin, explains the bundled fibres and their skin distribution, and uses involvement of only half the middle finger as a further reason. He removes/discards the finger application and applies treatment at the spinal origin; the narrative claims recovery and observers’ surprise. Its authorial opening and anatomical explanation are included, not dropped into an opaque cause marker.

[III.14](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Galien-Oeuvres_anatomiques_physiologiques_et_médicales_(trad._Daremberg)/Tome_II/X/3) repeats the case briefly, namesPausanias and his left hand, retains worsening under earlier care, elicits the earlier upper-back injury, states that this site had healed while finger sensation worsened, and commands the previously used medicines at that site. The final criticism distinguishes sensory skin-nerve roots from roots serving muscle movement. This is the same author’s reprise, not an independent confirmation or an instruction to merge all details into one literal paragraph.

## Actual contextual reference, with a material limitation

The Greek relative-correlative **ἃ...φάρμακα, ταῦτ’...** in III.14 binds the old medicines to the revised application. Demonstratives and relative clauses also identify the previously injured site and the anatomically indicated fingers. The dependency is not merely repetition of a drug name: the *remedy argument stays linked while the location argument changes*. The patient and the impaired sensory function persist; motion remains a separate variable.

The longer Greek account says **ἀποῤῥῖψαι** of the finger application. The French wording *je le disposai* could misleadingly suggest moving the identical portion. Neither that wording nor the reprise licenses lossless physical dose identity. Ingredients and dosage are not supplied. The source-owned relation is at least the same kind of treatment/medicament, not conserved material stock.

This is ordinary relative/anaphoric Greek, not a discovered cipher alphabet or special scribal symbol. It licenses a concrete reference dependency, not an entire compact writer. ## Different consequence and retained predecessors

A new-drug fallback reading can preserve the same symptom and eventual cure but contradicts the written judgment that the prior drugs were appropriate and the reprise’s explicit material reference. An unchanged-local-site reading loses the causal relocation. A motion-cure reading contradicts preserved motion and lost sensation. Treating the first denial as an omniscient no-injury proposition conflicts with the later reported back injury; preserving report attribution avoids silently repairing it.
