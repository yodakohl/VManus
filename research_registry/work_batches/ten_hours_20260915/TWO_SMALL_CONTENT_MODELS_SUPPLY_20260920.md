## A. Justinian Institutes III.22–23: consent, delivery, risk, and exchange

**Primary.** Justinian, *Institutes* III.22–23, the complete consensual-contract
and purchase/sale section in the Moyle translation at
<https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Anglica/just3_Moyle.htm>, especially
the sale clauses on agreement, writing, earnest, third-party price, risk,
delivery, ownership, conditional sale, and exchange. The primary says that
consensual contracts are bilateral; the sale section then separates agreement
from later delivery and risk. This is not the existing IDEA395 span (II.1.25–28
on physical transformation and ownership), IDEA372's target loan nomination,
IDEA333's charter chain, or IDEA400's Pacioli postings.

### Typed finite constructor

Declare `BUYER`, `VENDOR`, `OBJECT`, `PRICE`, `CONSENT`, `WRITING`, `EARNEST`,
`DELIVERY`, `RISK`, `TITLE`, `CONDITION`, and `ACTION`. The whole source account
reduces as follows:

1. `AGREE(buyer,vendor,object,price)` creates a bilateral sale once the price
   is fixed. A consensual sale does not require writing, presence, earnest, or
   delivery in the ordinary case.
2. A written sale has a formal gate: until the required writing/signature or
   notarial execution is complete, either party may withdraw without loss if
   no earnest was given. If earnest exists, withdrawal produces the source's
   asymmetric consequence: the buyer forfeits it or the vendor restores double.
3. If a named third party is to fix the price, `PRICE_FIXED` closes the sale
   and requires payment plus delivery; if that person cannot or will not fix
   it, the sale is void. This is a conditional price dependency, not a free
   price value.
4. Once an unwritten sale is concluded by price agreement, accidental risk
   shifts to the buyer even before delivery. The vendor may remain owner before
   delivery and must transfer recovery actions; an express safe-keeping
   promise changes the loss assignment.
5. A conditional sale suspends completion until its future condition is met;
   a known sacred/public object or fraudulent description changes the remedy
   rather than being silently treated as an ordinary object.
6. The source distinguishes barter from sale: exchange may be a separate
   contract, so the direction `object-for-object` cannot be collapsed into
   `price-for-object` without losing which party is buyer or seller.

### Distinguishing state prediction

The source writer predicts a state sequence in which `PRICE_AGREED` can coexist
with `NOT_DELIVERED`, `RISK=BUYER`, and `TITLE=VENDOR`; later delivery transfers
the vendor's remaining owner/action relation. An explicit custody promise
instead leaves `RISK=VENDOR` for the specified loss. A second mechanism, **R-S**,
would make delivery the event that perfects the sale and would keep accidental
risk with the vendor before delivery. A third, **R-X**, treats every exchange as
a sale and therefore forces an arbitrary buyer/price direction. The source's
later loss, action, and title fields distinguish these mechanisms without a
target gloss or quantity arithmetic.

**Strongest failure.** A future target passage may have no independently bound
price/consent/delivery/risk relation. Then the constructor is untestable; no
unknown group may be assigned a legal field merely to make the state close.

## B. Cato *De Agri Cultura* 133: attached layering, rooting, cut, and transplant

**Primary.** Cato, *De Agri Cultura* 133, complete layering and on-tree basket
instructions in the proofread public-domain text at
<https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cato/De_Agricultura/H%2A.html>
(lines 32–34 in the accessed page). The source first presses a scion into
ground while its tip remains out, waits for roots, then digs and transplants
vertically. It separately sends a shoot through a perforated pot or basket,
packs earth, leaves it attached on the tree, and cuts below it after two years
before planting with the basket; vines use a one-year interval. This is a
concrete propagation state machine, distinct from IDEA321's donor/stock graft
compatibility, IDEA368's mature-seed extraction, and IDEA189's uninstantiated
harvest-versus-living-plant proposal.

### Typed finite constructor

Declare `PARENT`, `SCION`, `SHOOT`, `TIP`, `SOIL`, `ROOTED`, `CONTAINER`, `AGE`,
`CUT_BOUNDARY`, `ORIENTATION`, and `PLANTED`. The source-owned sequence is:

1. `ATTACH(scion,parent)` and `PRESS_INTO_SOIL(scion,soil)` while
   `TIP=OUTSIDE`; the parent/scion relation persists.
2. `ROOT(scion)` changes the scion state to rooted without yet making it a
   detached plant.
3. `DIG(scion)` at the proper time and `TRANSPLANT_VERTICAL(scion)` yields a
   new planted individual. The source lists the propagated tree kinds, but no
   new species semantics are inferred.
4. For the more careful branch,
   `THREAD(shoot,perforated_container)`, `PACK(container,soil)`, and
   `LEAVE_ATTACHED(shoot,parent,age)` keep the shoot on the tree while roots
   form in the container.
5. At the declared age, `CUT_BELOW(shoot)` closes the parent relation and
   `PLANT_WITH_CONTAINER(shoot,container)` preserves the container as part of
   the planted package. The vine branch changes only the waiting age; it does
   not erase the common attachment/root/cut constructor.

### Distinguishing state prediction

The constructor predicts that a later reference to the same propagule can
occur in two states—`ATTACHED_AND_ROOTING` before the cut and
`DETACHED_AND_PLANTED` after it—while the parent remains a separate living
participant. The named rival **R-CUT** detaches first and roots later; it
predicts no on-tree waiting state and no source-preserved parent relation.
**R-GRAFT** treats the process as donor/stock fusion and predicts a merged
material identity rather than a shoot retaining its container through planting.
Changing the written age branch changes the cut event timing, not the identity
of the rooted shoot. This is a multi-step cultivation consequence beyond a
plant-part list.

**Strongest failure.** A target reading would need a globally reusable
attachment, root-state, age, cut, and container grammar. If those relations are
not independently bound, the model cannot discriminate R-CUT or R-GRAFT and
must remain raw.

## E. Two formal-observation semantic mechanisms (IDEA427/IDEA428)

The retained composition reports support two distinct raw mechanisms without
supporting a target lexicon. GDT608 transfers directed component boundary
profiles but retains a stronger exact-merge residual; GDT437 shows that written
component order changes formal transition signatures. Separately, GDT282,
GDT286, and GDT318 transfer opaque wrapper and entry-state profiles, while
GDT563 shows active action/argument carry in repeated context cards. The two
new cards use these observations in different semantic roles.

### IDEA427 — residual phase over an ordered frame

`FRAME(L,R,entry)` preserves two ordered opaque argument slots. The exact whole
residual selects one of three finite operators: `OPEN` creates an event record,
`UPDATE` changes its state while preserving its participant and slots, and
`CLOSE` emits the final result and clears the record. A complete three-clause
reading with `OPEN → UPDATE → CLOSE` therefore predicts one multistep event;
`CLOSE` before `OPEN` is invalid and cannot be repaired with an unseen reset.
Reversing the written components swaps the two argument dependencies.

Its strongest failure is that GDT608's residual is only a formal merge identity;
no event owner, state, or result is independently observed. The named rivals
are the existing ordered-reciprocity card (inverse relation semantics) and the
carrier-chain card (left opener/right closer); IDEA427 uses residual lifecycle
phase instead. Its exact falsifier is a complete passage where the same
residual/frame/context both opens and closes, or where update/close requires an
unwritten participant or reset.

### IDEA428 — wrapper-scoped participant continuity

`SCOPE(wrapper, line_start, previous_DY, paragraph_boundary)` returns either
`NEW_EVENT` or `CONTINUE`. A new event creates one opaque participant record;
continuation preserves that record while ordered payload components update it;
an explicit reset closes the old record before a new one opens. A complete
three-clause reading consequently predicts `OPEN → CONTINUE → CLOSE`, with the
same wrapper in uninterrupted context referring to one record and the same
payload after a declared reset referring to a new record.

This differs from GDT563's working microphrase carry because it predicts
participant-record continuity and reset, rather than merely filling an action
and argument. Its strongest failure is that GDT282/286/318 establish only
opaque formal channels; line and paragraph boundaries may be editorial and no
participant identity is independently known. The exact falsifier is a complete
passage where same-selector wrapper repetitions require distinct participants,
or where entry/reset context adds no stable record break beyond matched formal
frequencies.

Both cards remain `RAW_UNREVIEWED`; neither assigns a target word, image role,
source name, or confirmed meaning.

### IDEA429 — productive-family substitution and one state slot

A predeclared productive family frame fixes event, operation, participant, and
ordered argument slots. A licensed substitution inside that family changes one
state/property slot only; the exact whole-form residual remains an opaque record
of the complete form. A three-clause passage therefore has one event chain,
`state0 -> state1 -> state2`, while participant and argument order persist. A
written order reversal swaps argument slots rather than silently retaining the
forward relation.

The falsifier is a complete passage with at least two licensed substitutions: a
substitution that changes operation or participant without a written trigger,
incompatible effects for the same family/context, or continuity requiring an
unprinted event rejects the model. GDT608 and GDT437 support directed formal
order, while GDT754 explicitly warns that productive prose cards have no
component-exported meaning; that warning is the main failure risk. The card
therefore requires a written complete-passage state contrast before selection.

### IDEA430 — terminal r/l relation polarity

For one predeclared known stem pair only, the stem supplies an opaque relation
frame `R(A,B)`. A licensed terminal `r` is hypothesized to emit `EDGE(A,B)` and
`l` to emit `EDGE(B,A)`; operation, participant slots, and nonterminal order stay
fixed. A later clause consuming the edge then receives opposite source/recipient
roles. The named rival preserves `EDGE(A,B)` and changes only a relation-state

The falsifier is a complete passage containing both terminal variants of the
predeclared pair: inconsistent edge direction in one context, a fixed downstream
written role that preserves the old ordering, or any effect requiring an unseen
pair rejects polarity. GDT915 supplies only limited known-stem co-variation;

### IDEA431 — context-selected local versus carried payload scope

Each clause has an ordered payload `P(op,args,residual)`. A fixed entry-context
selector returns exactly `LOCAL` or `CARRY`. `LOCAL` consumes all arguments at
the clause boundary; `CARRY` leaves one typed active slot so the next compatible

one carried update and no leakage into the final local clause. `ALWAYS_LOCAL`
predicts independent payloads; `ALWAYS_CARRY` predicts a final-clause mutation.

model. GDT282/286/318/563 show formal wrapper/context and active-argument carry,

### IDEA434 — modal safety scope versus actual voyage state

[IDEA000434](../../proposals/raw_safety_modal_scope_algebra_20260920.json) supplies a different semantic construction: `ch` seeds a proposition frame, qok inside that frame selects a pair relation, `ched` supplies the absence condition, `qokchedy` supplies `UNATTENDED`, `chckhdy` supplies `WOULD_BE`, and `chety`/`checthy` close harmlessness and universal cargo scope. `chedain` remains the independent sequence function. The evaluator constructs the pair and possible-world condition before applying counterfactual force; actual voyage operations are evaluated against the current bank state separately.
