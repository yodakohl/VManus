# Whole-content supply without a fixed source length

2026-09-15. Source-only bounded supply; raw proposals, not experiment selection.

Three historical content architectures can express variable-length complete meanings: heraldic descriptions of a whole shield, coupled musical voices, and causal descriptions of a complete mechanism. The strongest new conditional architecture here is a typed causal clause program. None is nominated for an immediate Voynich test: this audit supplies source constraints but no concrete global writing rule with a nominated observable consequence on currently admitted material. That is missing design, not a failed manuscript test or a requirement to know a word beforehand.

Joint whole-reading hypotheses remain legitimate. A fixed conventional symbol-to-operation assignment is not a defect. The necessary distinction is between one reusable assignment whose executions can disagree with observed figures, and assignments or omitted clauses chosen separately for each passage. No source token count, clause count, figure-component count, or generated trace length has been matched to a Voynich paragraph. GDT969 is not repaired or inspected here.

## A. Heraldic descriptions: a real image semantics with variable wording

The Bodleian's [Laud Misc. 733 manifest](https://iiif.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/iiif/manifest/63b5bb09-e4d5-46ad-9616-e7407d754bb1.json) identifies a Middle English manuscript by Johannes de Bado Aureo, England, c.1440–1450. This range is not a strict terminus before 1450. The [digital object](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/63b5bb09-e4d5-46ad-9616-e7407d754bb1/) supplies the inspected images. The manuscript catalogue currently returned an access challenge; the usable primary metadata came from the manifest, not that failed response.

Both complete openings 11v–12r and 15v–16r were downloaded and actually viewed. They show English explanation and French blazon beside owned painted shields. The manuscript itself therefore supplies alternative linguistic realizations of related pictorial content; no modern fixed field count is needed.

On 15v, the first complete item explains a powdered border, describes a red rampant lion on silver with a border, and then gives the French blazon. A short diplomatic reading of the red clause is:

> Il porte dargente vn lyon rampand de gowlys & vne bordure de sabyl pouderie besauntz

The corresponding shield visibly has a light field, a red lion and a black border carrying gold roundels. This is a concrete composition of field, charge, attitude, border, tinctures and a distribution rule. It is not just a count of rings. On 12r, the plain quartered shield and the adjacent engrailed and indented examples distinguish partition color from partition-edge treatment. These are complete shield descriptions of different semantic complexity on the same source opening.

However, blazon denotes an equivalence class of depictions. The powdered-border instruction does not specify each roundel's exact location or total; the lion's brushstrokes are not a textual output. A model claiming to reproduce every mark would not be licensed. A model claiming the complete heraldic design must instead freeze which differences preserve that design. It cannot relabel arbitrary plant parts or pools as charges whenever convenient.

The full English paragraphs contain explanations beyond depiction: examples on 15v discuss signs of bastardy, while 16r discusses wells as a pilgrimage sign. A picture does not independently verify these social assertions. A whole source-copy reading must represent them; a hypothetical language of armorial descriptions may omit them only by declaring that narrower semantic language in advance. The French and English repetitions cannot be silently collapsed while claiming to transcribe every source clause.

The inspected opening also contains apparent color discrepancies requiring collation: in the lion-head item on 16r, the English description appears to say gold while the French names azure and the head appears blue. The eagle item on 15v likewise needs careful text/paint collation. These are not repaired into a clean training corpus here. The first lion-and-border item is the bounded clear illustration used above.

**Possible global rule.** One typed grammar composes fields, partition operators, named charges, tinctures, attitudes and positions, with a finite set of globally shared reference and coordination constructions. The syntax may have clause alternatives and optional explanatory subclauses, but their inventories and semantics are shared across all passages. The repeated components vary the text length; no source count is imposed on a target paragraph.

**Meaningful rival.** A fixed vocabulary that swaps field and charge tinctures, or places the powdering in the field instead of the border, yields a different complete heraldic design. Independently coded painted colors and containment reject that rival. A topology-only or count-only score would fail to distinguish it. Conversely, global color permutation remains unbound if the evaluator sees only anonymous color classes, and freely chosen charge identities would make the apparent fit permissive. No admitted Voynich whole shield, or equivalent restricted heraldic design, was nominated in this source task. A flexible description of any colored geometry is not a recommendation to test heraldry.

## B. Coupled voices: temporal relations beyond metre

The British Library's [own public image record](https://www.flickr.com/photos/britishlibrary/12458897473) identifies Harley 978 f.11v, mid-thirteenth-century England, as a whole folio. Its original-size image was downloaded from the linked static asset and actually viewed. The [Reading Museum exhibition](https://www.readingmuseum.org.uk/online-exhibitions/reading-abbeys-most-famous-manuscript/harley-978-sumer-icumen-and-music-reading), written by Anne Lawrence-Mathers and Helen Deeming, provides scholarly context; the operational account below was checked against the manuscript's boxed Latin instructions and the two red pes instructions, not against a modern performance.

The page contains the written melody, a marked entry cross, two written pes parts, and instructions. The boxed rule allows four singers for the rota; it says it should not be sung by fewer than three, or at least two, apart from those singing the pes. Thus a four-voice-only rule is not the full source instruction. One singer begins together with the pes while the others are silent. Another enters when the first reaches the first note after the cross, and the others follow in the same way. Each rests only at the written pauses for one long note. The upper pes repeats as often as required with its pause at the end. The lower pes pauses in the middle, not at the end, and immediately repeats the beginning.

This is an actual reusable temporal program rather than a metrical word list. Repeated copies of one melody share pitches and durations but have dependent entry offsets; the two pes voices have distinct rest phases. The event lattice is constrained simultaneously in each voice and across voices. Its realized performance length is deliberately open, because repetition is as required. The complete finite object is the written rule plus the owned notated parts, not an invented fixed-duration performance or fixed number of output fields.

**Possible global rule.** A shared grammar of start, wait-until-marker, copy-voice, rest, duration and repeat clauses can generate variable-length instructions and finite periods of a longer performance. It must preserve all source alternatives, both pes phase rules and all chosen pitch/duration conventions. If a proposed whole text is performance instruction rather than a lyric copy, that semantic scope is explicit; the song's words are not repurposed as decoded vocabulary. Full note-by-note diplomatic transcription and a mensural-duration policy were not completed in this supply task.

**Meaningful rivals.** Reversing a voice's order or moving its entry to another marker preserves many pitch counts but changes simultaneous intervals and written rest alignment. An independently bound score and entry marker can reject those alternatives. A global transposition preserves all relative intervals, so relative constraints alone cannot identify an absolute pitch vocabulary. A uniform time rescaling likewise does not identify an absolute tempo. Those symmetries must be retained as equivalence, or broken by independently observed clef/duration conventions. Arbitrary pitch relabeling need not preserve interval addition, but a model checking only voice recurrence would still allow it.

This supplies a concrete source-side consequence for IDEA000142. It does not establish that an admitted Voynich diagram is a score, that two writing bands are synchronized voices, or that any circular boundary marks an entry point. The original TML ANO11TRA and SALSCI URLs returned the same replacement page when fetched in this audit; they were not treated as recovered source texts. Their search excerpts did not substitute for a complete source read.

## C. Mechanisms: complete conditional clauses and a real rival actuator

The complete primary translation is Hero's *Pneumatics*, Greenwood/Woodcroft 1851, [§37, printed pp.57–58](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/77400/pg77400-images.html#Sec_37) and [§38, pp.59–60](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/77400/pg77400-images.html#Sec_38). Both whole sections, the editor's preface, and the bounded manuscript-description appendix were read. The two owned figures were downloaded and actually viewed. Gutenberg identifies its source as the 1851 publication and describes applied printed corrigenda; this is a later edition of ancient content, not a medieval image witness.

The editor explicitly says that drawings were made for this edition from consulted examples, and that §78's two drawings were supplied from its text because the consulted copies omitted them. The appendix dates Harley 5605 to the fifteenth century, but the current British Library catalogue instead dates that manuscript to the second–third quarter of the sixteenth century. The old appendix date is therefore not a pre-1450 witness license. A pre-1450 witness of the particular apparatus is not established here. Neither its ancient attribution nor an attractive later illustration proves access by the Voynich author.

### Complete §37 content inventory

The following retains the section's construction, operating assertions, explanation and optional material branch. It is a semantic inventory, not a fixed serialization.

1. Purpose: lighting a fire opens the temple doors; extinguishing it shuts them.
2. Put the temple on pedestal ABCD and a small altar ED on that support.
3. Insert tube FG with F inside the altar and G within globe H, reaching nearly its center; solder the tube into the globe.
4. Put bent siphon KLM in the globe.
5. Extend the door hinges downward so that they turn freely on pivots in the base.
6. Attach chains from the hinges, joining into one over a pulley, to suspended hollow vessel NX.
7. Attach the oppositely wound hinge chains, joining over a pulley, to a lead weight; its descent shuts the doors.
8. Lead the siphon's outer leg into the suspended vessel.
9. Through opening P fill the globe halfway with water; close P carefully afterward.
10. When the fire is hot, the heated altar air expands, passes through FG into the globe, and drives liquid through the siphon into NX.
11. NX gains weight and descends, tightening the chains and opening the doors.
12. When the fire is extinguished, the source says rarefied air escapes through the globe's pores.
13. With the siphon end immersed in NX's water, it draws that liquid back to fill the void left by the departing particles.
14. The vessel lightens; the suspended weight predominates and shuts the doors.
15. An alternative uses quicksilver instead of water; the source gives greater weight and easy separation by fire as its reasons.

Item 12 is the historical explanation in this translation. It is not silently replaced by a modern cooling/contraction account. Item 15 is preserved as a source claim, not endorsed as a modern physical recommendation. Masses, exact vessel dimensions, quantitative temperatures and a measured time interval are not written values. Their convenient inference must not become purported literal fields.

### Complete §38 content inventory and discriminating alternative

Section 38 gives the same opening/closing purpose through a different mechanism. It reuses temple/base/altar construction by an explicit reference to the preceding setup. Tube FGH enters airtight leather bag K. A small weight L below the bag is connected over a pulley to the hinge chains. When the bag folds together, L predominates and shuts the doors. Heating expands altar air into the bag and raises the bag with L; the doors open. The source explicitly allows the doors to open of themselves or through a counterbalancing opening weight. Extinguishing the sacrifice lets the air out; the descending bag and L tighten the chains and close the doors. The comparison to bath doors is explanatory content and is retained.

These two whole descriptions have the same gross endpoint, **fire on → open; fire off → shut**, but different intermediate consequences. In §37 the water-bearing actuator gains mass and descends during opening. In §38 the expanding bag and its closing weight rise during opening. Consequently the endpoint alone cannot select a complete reading. A model must predict the correct component type, coupling, motion direction, transfer medium and reset relation with one global interpretation.

### What the owned figures do and do not constrain

Figure 37 visibly supplies the altar/tube/globe/siphon/bucket/hinge-chain/pulley/counterweight arrangement. Figure 38 instead supplies the bag with its hanging weight and linkage. This is a meaningful graphical distinction between two causal programs. It is stronger than a pipe likeness or count of repeated components.

The figures depict apparatus structure, not both temporal execution states. Figure 37 includes a fire and frontal door panels without a separate open-state view. Neither figure depicts every pressure state, liquid fill amount, seal condition or causal assertion. Thus a demand that every source assertion be independently visible is false here. Conversely, a static component graph can still be a legitimate consequence of a whole program, and sufficiently fixed attachments plus initial conditions may constrain motion without a pictured before/after pair. Which semantic properties the image constrains must be specified before testing.

A bare existence proof of suitable masses is weak: if empty-bucket weight, transferred liquid mass and counterweight are all free, choosing the counterweight between the empty and filled loads can make opening and reset feasible. The source writes no numerical values for those masses. A test that merely asks whether some free parameters make the apparatus work may remain permissive. Whole predicted attachment relations and the opposite actuator-motion consequences are therefore essential; unrestricted state names or a common endpoint alone are insufficient.

### Strongest conditional architecture retained

A typed causal clause program is the best new architecture from this audit. It separates declarations of component type and attachment, initial conditions, conditional actions, asserted consequences, explanatory causes, cross-references and source alternatives. Repeated declarations and clauses make texts variable in length. References denote the same component across clauses; syntax choices and renderings are global. A complete hypothetical text may describe an apparatus different from a named source instance, provided its finite component/operation library and all syntax alternatives were fixed before target reading.

The semantic inventory is richer than an opaque state graph: a siphon must join specified liquid regions, opposite windings imply opposite hinge effects, transfer changes the suspended load, and the same attachments must permit the claimed reset. These consequences are invariant under renaming arbitrary component identifiers. They are not invariant under swapping gain/loss, ascent/descent or the two actuator types. A matched rival should preserve component counts and overall open/close endpoints while reversing the operative coupling or replacing the water-transfer actuator with the bag actuator. A global code must distinguish the full programs, not rescue both with local senses.

The surface language remains **unfrozen**. This dossier does not present a complete cipher, claim that each source inventory item occupies one group, or omit explanatory clauses while calling the result a complete source translation. A future proposed semantic language can be narrower than every historical prose genre, but its complete intended meanings and permitted syntax need to be stated. Source clauses only motivate that language; they do not license its writing format.

**Concrete unknown after GDT214/885/903:** can one small globally shared writing rule encode complete typed apparatus and conditional-operation statements such that the generated attachment graph and an operation-specific consequence agree with independently observed figures? GDT214 tested exact cross-reference keys; GDT885 tested a fixed three-state endpoint; GDT903 proved unrestricted compatibility. None answered this typed causal question. Its independence alone is insufficient for selection: this task has not supplied a nominated admitted whole figure/passage relation and an executable finite rule that would expose that contrast. Root may jointly hypothesize the ownership and vocabulary; previously confirmed words are not required.

**Selection recommendation:** retain this architecture as raw source supply, but start no decoder from this dossier. No currently admitted Voynich figure was inspected in this task, and no exact prospective consequence on such a figure has been specified. The outcome is an underdefined candidate, not a declaration that no such consequence exists. Do not start another unchanged missing-input audit or broaden a closed graph endpoint automatically. The next decision belongs with a concrete whole-reading proposal, using the existing admission and predecessor contracts.

### Bounded producer audit: RAW348 source distinction (2026-09-20)

The proposed distinction is already fully represented by `IDEA000348` and the
paired source inventory above, so no new raw card is added. Direct reinspection
of the owned Greenwood/Woodcroft 1851 e-text confirms two complete traces:

* **§37, pp.57–58:** the shared setup uses altar, globe, bent siphon, hinges,
  opposed chains and pulleys, suspended vessel NX, lead weight and a half-filled
  globe. Heating expands altar air; air entering the globe drives liquid through
  the siphon into NX. NX gains mass and **descends**, tightening the chains to
  open the doors. On extinguishing, the siphon draws liquid back, NX lightens,
  and the lead weight predominates and descends to shut the doors. The optional
  quicksilver substitution changes the transfer medium and weight, not the
  endpoint contract.
* **§38, pp.59–60:** the prior temple/base/altar and hinge-chain arrangement is
  explicitly reused, but the actuator is an airtight leather bag K with weight
  L. Heating sends expanded air through FGH into the bag; the bag rises with L
  and opens the doors. When the fire is extinguished, air leaves, the bag and L
  descend, and the chains close the doors. The source permits self-opening or a
  counterbalancing opening weight, an explicit branch that must remain separate
  from the main trace.

Thus a small shared inventory can be source-valid: condition (fire on/off),
altar/air expansion, hinge-chain/pulley coupling, actuator, motion, open/close
state and reset. Global renaming cannot erase the source difference because the
opening motion and transfer graph differ: §37 transfers liquid into a suspended
vessel that descends, while §38 inflates an elastic bag that rises with its
weight. Their reset operations also differ (liquid return versus air release).
The common endpoint alone would admit both; the typed actuator, transfer and
motion consequences separate them.

This is a source result, not a target result. The target-side fields that would
carry component identity, operation, motion sign and reset are independently
unknown. GDT214's hydraulic component-key comparison found historical format
support but no Voynich key prediction; GDT885 closed only a fixed three-state
common-endpoint machine; GDT903 left unrestricted reversible endpoint actions
compatible without a writing rule. GDT209 likewise supplied broad apparatus
ecology without an exact homolog. None exhausts the typed causal distinction,
but none supplies the missing target binding. Keep `IDEA000348` raw and
unselected; do not duplicate it as a generic UP/LOW or causal-chain proposal.

**IDEA000389 — Saint Albans counterchanged tinctures.** The two complete
adjacent 1486 items on page 195 reuse the small inventory `Field(partition,
color-pair)`, `Chevron`, and the backward reference `de dictis coloribus
transmutatis`. Trace A uses a quarterly sable/silver field; trace B uses a
per-pale gold/red field. In both, the same reference assigns the opposite
member of the declared pair to each charge region. The relation therefore
changes with the field region while a uniform charge color fails. Global color
renaming preserves the rule; arbitrary per-region colors destroy it. The
omissions are material: this is a 1486 witness later than the manuscript date
range, the second shield is monochrome so gold/red are text-only assertions,
and the two examples change both partition and palette. The source and exact
Latin traces are in `REFERENCE_ROLE_SOURCE_SUPPLY_20260920.md` and
`raw_counterchanged_tincture_reference_1486.json`. GDT980 closed a tautological

**IDEA000352 / IDEA000381 — Timaeus typed transformations.** The complete
54a–56e source uses a small inventory of material kinds, typed elementary
constituents, breaking, surrounding, reunion and transformation. One trace
states that water broken by fire or air yields one fire corpuscle plus two air
corpuscles; another states that dissolved air yields two fire corpuscles, while
earth fragments reunite as earth and cannot cross into another type. The
coproduct and the earth type guard change the relational consequence even when
scalar counts are preserved. Consistent renaming of abstract carriers leaves
the distinction intact; an untyped scalar reassignment erases it. The source
is a qualified probable account in Lamb 1925; surrounding/defeat conditions,
motion and reunion remain source obligations, and no specific pre-1450 witness

and `raw_f77r_timaeus_source_detail_20260920.md`. GDT964 rejected a uniform
single-edit element-class rule over a target family; that does not exhaust this
source typed-coproduct mechanism.

**IDEA000402 — Galen II.44 cabbage preparation.** The complete five-paragraph
entry reuses cabbage, juice/body, first cooking water, replacement hot water,
boiling and transfer. Trace A retains the first water with cabbage for the
purging outcome; trace B pours away the former water, immediately transfers
the cabbage into other hot water, and boils it again for the drying outcome.
The later passage repeats the former/replacement-water reference and says that
air or cold water defeats softening. The relational change is constituent
retention/replacement, not a free polarity word. Global renaming of material
roles preserves it; treating the two waters as one or independently relabeling
each recurrence breaks the trace. No amount, temperature, loss rate, or exact
second-water serving instruction is written; the two alternatives are not one
physical serving undergoing both treatments. The complete Greek source packet
and omissions are in `GALEN_CABBAGE_OPPOSED_EFFECTS_SUPPLY_20260920.md` and
`GALEN_CABBAGE_COMPLETE_SOURCE_20260920.json`. IDEA334, GDT625 and GDT768 were
checked as nearest predecessors; none binds a target meaning for this source
relation.

These three are the strongest current choices for later source review: Saint
