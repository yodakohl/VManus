## Actual complete content and contextual resolution

Question71 answers that angels exist and gives three arguments: relative God-likeness, bodily/intellectual versus intellectual/intellectual composition, and the ladder of difference/concordance. The first argument’s punctuation is contested by the comparison edition; all three are retained as source arguments rather than modern independent proofs.

Question72 asks both from what an angel is and whose it is. The same rule D has three species: primitive specification, specific constituents and ownership/part-whole relation. The answers are from itself; from spiritual correlatives; and God’s. The source explicitly writes `-tiuis`, `-bilibus`, `-are`, then explains active, receptive and act roles. The act exists within/between active and receptive correlatives. These are compressed references to whole families, not three fresh substance names. From itself does not mean temporal self-creation: the prerequisite definition also says the angel is created from nothing.

The continuous heaven section then answers six questions. It asserts self-motion through proper correlatives and constellations; circular motion in itself and inferior things but not outside itself; denies motion by an angel using active/receptive reversal and form/matter grounds; affirms a motive soul because the sensitive, vegetative and elements otherwise lack their described motion; and gives the two E answers. **E’s first species** answers why heaven exists by constitution from form and matter. **E’s second species** answers the same why-question by the purpose that inferior beings can move. The explicit closing sentence remains part of the owned source.

The formal resolution policy is small and source-attested:

| Written context | Shared sign/family | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
|rule plus first/second/third species|D|primitive specification / constituents / whose|
|rule plus first/second species|E|formal/constitutive cause / final purpose|
|correlative in the active family|`-tiuis`, `-tiuos`, `-tiui`|active aspect|
|correlative in the receptive family|`-bilibus`, `-bile`, `-biles`|receptive aspect|

The family labels in this table are our descriptions of the explicit Latin explanation. They do not identify Voynich morphemes or imply that every Latin-inflected variant has been modeled. Ordinary Latin supplies subject identities, syntax, negation, examples and reasons outside this small inventory. A whole passage cannot be written with D/E and three endings alone.

## Consequences, rivals and residual uncertainty

Exchanging E’s species changes the requested answer category: constitution becomes purpose and the inferior-motion purpose becomes material constitution. Flattening D to one fixed substance or origin relation loses the independently written God-ownership answer. Treating active and receptive suffixes as interchangeable loses the role inversion cited in question75. These consequences involve the same subjects across questions and the source’s specific inferential dependencies; they are not an unordered list of names or a fixed table-copy count.

The source’s negative answer to question75 must be retained, but the suffix rule alone does not logically prove it. The possessor of `suorum` and `suam` is not resolved here; the asserted physical/metaphysical counterfactual needs the source’s wider premises. Likewise `in inferioribus` together with `non extra se` is not automatically one modern trajectory. No new diagram, empirical cosmology or generic conservation law is invented.

Question80’s unexpanded referral to the second E species is separately preserved. It prevents a cheap rule that reads any mention of form as first-species E. A future writer would need the actual subtype context, not a best-fitting semantic choice after inspecting a word. The question71 punctuation issue and the known missing C alphabet sentence are neither hidden nor repaired.
