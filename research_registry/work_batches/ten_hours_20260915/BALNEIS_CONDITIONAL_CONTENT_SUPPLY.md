## Proposal A: XI water state chain

Complete entry XI (Sancta Anastasia, source lines 122–134) contains an
ordered state sequence:

- Illa recens in fonte suo sinthomata tollit,
- Fonte relicta suo nichil affert utilitatis;
- Hec eadem prodest frigida facta parum.
- Qui petit ergo sui bene de langore iuuari, / Senciet auxilium, si renouabit aquam.

Working content architecture: repeated water references may be one referent
whose operational state changes from fresh at the source, to removed from the
source, to cooled, and finally renewed. The source itself ties each state to a
different outcome or repeat-use condition. This is a whole-clause relation, not
a lexical claim about any target form.

Explicit roles and conditions:

- referent: the water of XI;
- state 1: recens in fonte suo;
- outcome 1: sinthomata tollit;
- state 2: Fonte relicta suo;
- outcome 2: nichil affert utilitatis;
- state 3: frigida facta;
- outcome 3: prodest ... parum;
- repeat condition: si renouabit aquam;
- outcome 4: Senciet auxilium.

Distinctive prediction: a complete paragraph adopting this architecture must
preserve the ordered state/outcome transitions and keep renewal as a condition
on renewed aid. A flat bath-praise reading may repeat water words but does not
require this state carry or the terminal renewal dependency.

Strongest failure: the clauses do not share one water referent, or source order
does not preserve fresh → removed → cooled → renewed dependency. Repeated water
forms alone would then be insufficient.

## Proposal B: source identity and recipient condition control sign

Three complete entries supply a signed contrast:

- VII (Foris Cripte, lines 74–85): Sed nocet ydropicis, cum sit dulcissima potu:
  / Vim consumendi non habet, unde nocet but later Pulmonem lesum sanat.
- XIX (Culma, lines 219–228): Culma nocet sanis, morbida membra iuuat.
- XXXIII (De Cruce, lines 393–404): Prodest ydropicis qui exfleumate grosso.
/ Consueuit eam splene tumente Jecur.

Working content architecture: sign is a relation of source identity and recipient
condition, rather than a fixed property of a body-term family. VII assigns harm
to hydrops under its stated sweet-water condition; XXXIII assigns help to
hydrops with the stated thick-phlegm condition; XIX contrasts harm to healthy
recipients with help to diseased bodies.

Explicit roles and conditions:

- source identity: the named bath entry and its water;
- recipient condition: ydropicis, sanis, or morbida membra;
- local condition: VII dulcissima potu and lack of consuming force; XXXIII
  exfleumate grosso; XIX healthy versus diseased recipient;
- signed outcome: nocet versus sanat/iuuat/prodest;
- body targets remain source clauses and are not collapsed into 13 semantic
  units.

Distinctive prediction: a complete reading using this architecture must permit
the same recipient/body family to change sign when the source identity or
condition changes. A uniform body-effect model predicts one stable sign for
hydrops or healthy/sick status and cannot represent VII versus XXXIII without
an exception.

Strongest failure: the source entries are merely independent bath claims with
no reusable recipient-condition relation, or all apparent sign changes are
editorial/genre effects. Then a signed source-by-recipient graph would be
overstructured.

## Why these are different

Proposal A is a state-transition dependency inside one complete entry, with a
terminal renewal condition. Proposal B is a cross-entry signed relation whose
edge sign depends jointly on source and recipient condition. Neither renames a
formal entry position, supplies a target gloss, or reuses the earlier PGP,
Dioscorides, astronomical, or decimal-register architecture.

Both remain exploratory raw hypotheses. No target endpoint, target word, or
medical truth claim is asserted.
