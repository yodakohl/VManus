**Status:** source-backed, `RAW_UNSCREENED`, 2026-09-15. This note deepens existing raw cards `IDEA000325` and `IDEA000321`; no new idea card, decoder, experiment, target text/image, reserve, or global file was opened or changed.

## 1. Celsus: diagnosis → conditional fallback → ordered polarity treatment (`IDEA000325`)

**Primary passage.** Celsus, *De medicina* IV.15.1–4, liver disease. The accessible Latin/English edition is [LacusCurtius, Book IV](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Celsus/4%2A.html), with the Latin locator [De medicina IV.15.1.1](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/836/2/260). Celsus first distinguishes acute from chronic/inveterate cases by signs: right-sided pain and fever/bile/hiccup for the acute picture; hard swollen praecordia, post-meal breathing difficulty, jaw paralysis, then abdominal/leg/foot swelling and wasting for the chronic progression. Treatment starts with bloodletting, then moves the bowel; if ordinary measures do not act, black hellebore is used. External plasters follow a fixed order: first repressing, then hot/dispersing, with iris or wormwood additions, and finally a malagma. The same case then receives hot, sparing food and drink that promote urination; cold things are specifically excluded. The later clause makes a seasonal route distinction for water affusion: hot in winter, tepid in summer.

**Reading A — staged clinical algorithm.** One record carries a patient/case through observation → acute/chronic branch → first intervention → conditional fallback → ordered opposite-direction external interventions → terminal preparation and regimen. A fixed condition selects the branch; the fallback is not a new independent item. The repressing→warming/drawing order changes the state of the same case and the final malagma closes it.

**Reading B — descriptive treatment catalogue.** The same paragraph may be a list of signs, remedies, ingredients, diet and seasonal advice. “If ordinary measures do not act” can be a local dosage remark; repressing and warming materials can be co-present properties; the malagma can be another preparation with no state transition.

**Smallest different consequence.** On the existing admitted complete-line endpoint named by `GDT625`, A requires a recurring condition-before-branch relation, a scoped fallback, and an ordered pair of opposed treatment states tied to one case before a terminal preparation. B permits the treatment fields independently and predicts no stable condition/fallback attachment or direction-sensitive pair. This is a relation/order claim, not a word gloss. `IDEA000034` and `IDEA000042` remain realistic exception/state rivals. The strongest failure is that no target condition, carrier, or terminal result can be fixed independently, leaving both readings relabellable.

This is a source refinement of `IDEA000325`, not a new medical-word proposal. It does not reopen any prior GDT625 decision or claim Celsus as the manuscript source.

## 2. Columella/Palladius: directed graft method → host/donor compatibility → altered product, with refusal exceptions (`IDEA000321`)

**Primary source set.** Columella, *De arboribus* 26.1.1 gives three grafting methods: a split stock receiving inserted shoots; slips inserted between inner bark and wood, with both methods assigned to spring; and budding with a piece of bark, assigned to summer. It also explicitly debates the older compatibility rule based on similarity of bark, inner bark and fruit. A readable source/translation is [ReadAncientTexts, Columella *De Arboribus*](https://readancienttexts.com/columella/de_arboribus/1); the primary-text locator is [Packard Humanities Institute 26.1.1](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/845/1/30/704-709). Palladius, *De insitione* XIV, gives directed host/donor examples in its graft poem: pear stock/donor combinations alter fruit size, taste, thorns, husk or flesh; quince refuses foreign grafting; pomegranate rejects foreign sap/foliage and exchanges its own germs. The readable Latin/French witness is [Palladius Book XIV](https://remacle.org/bloodwolf/erudits/palladius/livre14.htm).

**Reading A — directed transformation graph.** Each operation has ordered host and donor, one of the source-fixed methods, and a seasonal applicability condition. The downstream endpoint records the inherited or changed product trait. The refusal cases are genuine negative edges: an incompatible or self-only host does not produce the ordinary foreign-graft result.

**Reading B — paired botanical catalogue.** The neighboring tree names and product adjectives can be independent descriptions or a circular list. Spring/summer method statements may be general headings, while individual pairings do not carry direction or a causal output. Refusal language can be a local exception with no reusable graph role.

**Smallest different consequence.** On the already admitted f57v four-near/four-between source-bound object (`GDT389`/`IDEA000321`), A requires a repeated bridge to encode an ordered endpoint pair plus method/compatibility, and a downstream endpoint change; reversing a pair must change the relation or output, while a refusal bridge blocks the ordinary result. B predicts no direction-sensitive co-variation between bridge and endpoint. The four-pair scope may be too small, and absent leaders may prevent ownership; that is the principal failure.

This relation is distinct from name incidence and from a two-list layout fit because it demands direction, seasonal method class, output change, and explicit failure/refusal. It remains a source analogy until a fixed target relation schema exists.

## Selection note

These two source relations are materially different: Celsus is a bodily diagnostic algorithm with conditional polarity and fallback; Columella/Palladius is a plant transformation graph with seasonal method choice and negative compatibility edges. They can support exploratory whole readings without confirmed anchors, but neither source assigns a Voynich meaning or authorial provenance.
