**The useful closed consequence.** Let A, B, C denote the three stable grade types, with finenesses a, b, c, and let t denote the intended common grade. The two reported total-20 alternatives have weights

```
P = (5/2, 25/4, 45/4)
Q = (2,   7,    11)
```

Both total 20. Their common-grade assertion requires

```
(5/2)a + (25/4)b + (45/4)c = 20t
     2a +       7b +      11c = 20t.
```

Subtracting gives `2a - 3b + c = 0`, hence `c = 3b - 2a` and `t = 2b - a`. If the initial grades are distinct and ordered, the four levels A, B, target, C are equally spaced. Normalizing `a=0, b=1` gives `0,1,2,3`; the supplied source values are `3,4,5,6`. This identifies a relative numerical relation up to an affine grade calibration. It does not depend on independently naming silver. No separate written “100 units of silver” is needed: 100 is the computed fine-content total when the source grades are substituted.

This is stronger than merely assigning names to recurring formal roles. Keep the first source weights `(1,1,3)` and total 5, but move weight 3 from grade 6 to grade 3. Fine content changes from 25 to 19 and the output grade from 5 to 19/5. A mass-only interpretation cannot detect the error; the two-channel interpretation can, provided the written quantities and grade arguments retain their bindings. Likewise, a weighted intermediate from grades 3 and 4 in ratio 2:5 is 26/7, whereas an unweighted-average interpretation gives 7/2. These are differing content predictions, not changes of the English noun attached to one unchanged score.

Exact checks reproduce all five supplied weight/fine-content/grade triples: `5/25/5`, `16/80/5`, `20/100/5`, `80/400/5`, and `20/100/5`. The fine-content values are computations, not claims about five printed verification statements.

**What the proposed sharing must actually mean.** Stable material grade, recipe proportions, physical batch identity and final state equivalence are four different relations. Both source alternatives can produce mass 20 of grade 5 without being the same material portion or using the same component weights. A complete account may declare a batch, combine named inputs, scale a recipe, and assert that two resulting states have the same mass and fineness. Computed intermediate states need not each have a written numeric field. Every actual written group nevertheless needs a fixed role in the account's syntax; unexplained groups cannot become freely inserted commentary.

The minimum useful shared content kernel is therefore typed declarations, rational quantity expressions, mixture formation, recipe scaling and an explicit same-state assertion. The argument/reference grammar must distinguish these from physical reuse or a new independent example. It must bind the same rational components across fractions and computations: for example, 5/2 must be the quotient of the same numerical 5 and 2 used elsewhere, rather than another freely named scalar. A finite vocabulary of these constructors is possible, with variable numbers of clauses and nested expressions. This paragraph specifies its semantic obligations; it does **not** pretend that a historical realization rule, finite code family or complete target parse has thereby been supplied.

**A constructive countermodel for the remaining gap.** If the six weights in the two paths are independent numerical labels, repeated grade names plus a common total/grade do not fix the spacing above. For grades `(3,4,7)` and target 5, the two different triples

```
(1, 12,   7)
(5, 20/3, 25/3)
```

also each have mass 20 and fine content 100. All six weights are distinct, just as the six source weight expressions in P and Q are distinct. Thus even preserving that whole equality/inequality pattern of quantity labels does not select the source grade relation. This is a counterexample to the reduced free-label model, not to a grammar that really parses the source fractions or to the complete Liber abbaci text. More generally, for any three grades `(a,b,c)`, perturbing a positive weight vector by a sufficiently small multiple of `(b-c,c-a,a-b)` preserves both its total and fine content. The second path can therefore be manufactured whenever its quantities are left free.

Even with source grades 3,4,6 and total 20 fixed, the solutions satisfy `z=2x+y` and `3x+2y=20`. The positive rational family is continuous; the positive integer solutions include `(2,7,11)`, `(4,4,12)` and `(6,1,13)`. Conservation cannot select the source's particular procedure. Written ratio, scaling and alternative-method clauses must supply the extra conditions. The present draft explicitly has no grammar binding those clauses, so there is no concrete finite whole-writing hypothesis to select yet.

| Proposed distinction | What the two conservation laws do and do not determine |
|---|---|
| Free quantities | Binding the same actual rational expressions across both paths produces the closed relation above. Fresh scalar choices allow the explicit countermodel. A free mixing tree is also always satisfiable by positive leaf masses and recursively computed output grades. |
| Units and grade calibration | A global mass-unit rescaling preserves the physical equations. In the reduced model with weight ratios fixed and grades otherwise unknown, `g -> alpha*g + beta` preserves weighted averages, with nonzero alpha and the permitted range/order respected. Pure endpoints or a shared arithmetic numeral grammar may restrict that freedom; it is not claimed to survive every fully specified writing contract. Per-head units chosen after a conflict are extra parameters, not the same harmless global gauge. |
| Metal names | The arithmetic identifies at most a conserved constituent/proportion structure. Another binary constituent or commodity value can obey the same equations. Complementing the tracked constituent also preserves conservation with complemented grades. A full numeric convention may constrain that coordinate change, but it still does not independently identify the noun “silver.” This limits naming, not the mathematical content of a conditional reading. |
| Reversed operations | Reversing every mixture edge into a separation edge leaves both balance equations true. Conservation alone gives no direction of manufacture or physical separating mechanism. A fixed argument grammar and genuinely directional statements must carry any stronger claim. |
| Hidden losses | With every input and output mass fixed in one unit and `sum(inputs)=output`, a nonnegative unreported loss is forced to zero. If a free loss term is allowed, the account has changed: one unit at fineness 1/4 plus one at 3/4 can become one pure unit plus one unit of constituent-free loss. Both global balances hold, while the closed-mixture result would be two units at fineness 1/2. A no-loss assumption is therefore operative, not a discovered furnace property. |
