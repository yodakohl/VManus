## Euclid: positive witness and retained countercase

The [CMI I.1 page](https://www.claymath.org/euclid_index/triangles-parallels-and-area/?chapter=3)
provides the complete standard Greek text, English translation and manuscript
image. These are edition text and a linked witness, not a fresh diplomatic
collation. The [Bodleian manifest](https://iiif.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/iiif/manifest/d4a23501-0b98-4aff-acd6-fe06fe9b62e3.json)
identifies the image as D'Orville 301, f7v–f8r. The I.1 figure is on f8r.
Although the main manuscript is dated 888, [Heath's manuscript discussion](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0086%3Avolume%3D1&force=y)
dates replacement leaves 6–14 to the thirteenth century. The early propositions
are therefore a pre-1450 witness, without a claim that their hand is the 888 hand.
The claim-bearing Heath passage was retrieved directly, after web-view failure.

Complete I.1 operational projection, with distinct seed points A and B:

1. Retain the given segment AB.
2. Draw the circle with centre A through B.
3. Draw the circle with centre B through A.
4. Select one of their two intersections, C.
5. Join C to A and C to B.

The native f8r view shows both complete intersecting circles and the three
triangle segments. All these mathematical objects are visible. With a unit
seed segment, the selected intersection is at `(1/2, ±sqrt(3)/2)`; all triangle
sides have unit length. This is a metric consequence, not a count resemblance.
The unselected circle intersection remains geometrically present even though
it is not a new named program operand. Circle-name labels D/E, letter shapes,
overwritten strokes and marginal sketches are not additional construction
primitives. A mathematical-object inventory is not an inventory of every mark.

The complete proposition also proves the equalities. Its assertion clauses and
circle names are not naturally one-for-one instances of the four proposed
opcodes. The operational projection must remain labelled as a projection;
neither global symbolic serialization nor complete prose decoding is attested.

The [complete I.10 text](https://www.claymath.org/euclid_index/triangles-parallels-and-area/?chapter=12)
constructs an equilateral triangle by I.1 and bisects its apex angle by I.9 to
bisect the base. Native inspection of f12v–f13r shows the triangle/bisector
figure without I.1's two circles. Thus macro reuse need not retain the expanded
helper output. The absence is a countercase to blanket helper retention, not
a contradiction of the mathematical construction or proof.

## Digby 40: an owned astronomical construction

[PAL's manuscript catalogue](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236) dates Oxford,
Bodleian Library, Digby 40 to late twelfth/early thirteenth-century England.
Its first eight leaves contain Pseudo-Ptolemy's *Liber de compositione
universalis astrolabii*. [PAL's work entry](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/55)
records six chapters and a translated-work colophon dated 1147; this is not
proof of attribution to Ptolemy. David Juste supplies a primary transcription
with apparatus and corresponding facsimiles.

Chapter 1 runs from [f1v](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/1v)
through [f2r](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/2r)
to [f2v](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/2v).
It defines a round mater, cardinal labels A/I/E/O, centre V, crossed diameters,
rim and two inward circles, plus 360 equal divisions. The inward radial gaps
are related, rather than independently fitted. Its figure is explicitly
introduced by the preceding prose. Native viewing shows the labelled circles
and axes; the full prescribed degree divisions and suspension hardware are
not drawn. It is an explanatory construction stage, not a full finished mater.

The complete chapter 2 is on [f2v](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/2v),
[f3r](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/3r) and
[f3v](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/236/338/3v). It names its constructed
curves *almucantarat*, instructs both halves, and specifies one-, two-, three-
or other fixed division steps. The figure is owned by the chapter's closing
sentence: “Huius itaque artificium, hec est formula.” Native inspection shows
the labelled axes, curved arcs meeting at the two vertical boundary points,
and two diagonal ruler-position lines. The lower pole meets the leaf edge.
Only representative curves are drawn, not the complete one-degree grid.

The nonfree dependency, expressed in modern coordinates for clarity, is:

- Let the relevant inner circle have radius 1, with E=(0,1), O=(0,-1),
  I=(-1,0), A=(1,0), V=(0,0). These are source-defined roles, not target roles.
- For a selected division Q on the upper-left quarter, the ruler from O to Q
  marks P=(p,0) where it crosses the transverse diameter. Thus
  `p = Q.x / (1 + Q.y)`; it is not an independently chosen position.
- The instructed arc goes from O through P to E. Its supporting circle has
  centre `(c,0)`, where `c=(p*p-1)/(2*p)`, and radius `sqrt(c*c+1)` for `p!=0`.
  This formula is an analytic consequence of those three points, not a quoted
  medieval formula or a completed modern reconstruction of the entire plate.
- The opposite half is constructed analogously under the source's stated
  ruler placement. The limiting central member is the common diameter; no
  finite circle is inferred at `p=0`.

This is a real geometrical content constraint. Three non-collinear points fix
the supporting circle, and degree selection fixes the third point. A decoder
cannot independently move each arc centre or choose a new radius for each arc.
The source instructs bounded arcs between the two poles; it does not require
their entire supporting circles to be engraved. Some supporting centres may
lie beyond the plate. A complete modern reconstruction would still need a
careful reading of the compass-placement wording and the central limiting case.

| Object type | Source-side status | Consequence for a proposed model |
|---|---|---|
| Mater/inner boundary circles | Fixed frame with prescribed relations | Keep their role distinct from each constructed curve. |
| Almucantarat arcs | Final engraved members of this construction stage | Whole supporting circles outside the boundary are not silently required output. |
| Ruler positions and lightly marked points | Construction intermediates; representative diagonals appear in the figure | Their use is attested, but universal retention is not. |
| All degree divisions and repeated curves | Required in the instrument instructions; abbreviated in the illustration | Figure identity alone cannot certify a full execution inventory. |
| Labels and explanatory/proof prose | Identify roles and justify operations | Must not be discarded while claiming every word has been decoded. |

The fixed boundary and named arc endpoints could justify an explicitly different
final-output macro model. They do not retroactively repair IDEA000125's frozen
four-opcode, all-created-primitives contract. No target resemblance, latitude,
compass centre, input text or eligible target packet is asserted here.
