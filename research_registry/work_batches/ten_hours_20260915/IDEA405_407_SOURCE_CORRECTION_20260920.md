## Complete source example for IDEA000405: Rupescissa XII

The already owned source packet is
`research_registry/work_batches/ten_hours_20260915/RUPESCISSA_PORTION_RETURN_SOURCE_20260920.md`
with its complete inventory
`RUPESCISSA_XII_RETURN_CONTENT_20260920.json` (inventory SHA256
`8d43fa3d5a9fbe0694f5c36142da15cbe9324dbb3b6813396219bda4d8591337`). It
records Johannes de Rupescissa, *De consideratione quintae essentiae*,
complete Cap. XII in Basel 1561, pp.45–48, with the early return clause
directly checked against Wellcome MS708 f9v–10r, dated 1443. The source packet
retains the 1443/1561 wording difference and does not claim a full diplomatic
1443 transcription.

The actual written identity fields are unusually explicit:

1. The first distillation creates water that is received in a clean glass
   amphora. The next instruction says `aquam extractam`—the extracted water—
   is taken again.
2. The earlier sentence leaves `tria elementa`—the three elements—in the
   bottom of the original distillatory. The return places the extracted water
   over that retained residue and says to return it to `eodem balneo`, the same
   bath, for seven days, with mixing and sealing.
3. The returned mixture then yields a golden-oil appearance; the collected
   water/air pair is redistilled, clear water rises, and the air/oil fraction
   is set aside. Fire and earth from the original residue remain separately
   available for the later branch.

Thus the source fields carry output/input identity through explicit generated
output wording (`aquam extractam`), retained-residue reference (`tria
elementa`), a same-bath apparatus reference (`eodem balneo`), and later
fraction-specific set-aside/remaining statements. The discriminator is more
than “the same state remains consistent”: replacing the first output with fresh
same-type water breaks the actual-result reference, and treating the material
as one unchanged substrate cannot represent the concurrently retained residue,
returned water, set-aside air/oil, and remaining fire/earth. This is a source
content consequence, not evidence for any target gloss.

## Complete source example for IDEA000407: the same chapter's split/reunion

The same complete Rupescissa XII source is also a direct split-and-merge
example, with a different field contract. The first distillation splits one
starting material into two provenance branches:

- rising branch `UP(E0)`: water passes through the alembic tube into the named
  amphora, then is explicitly selected again as `aquam extractam`;
- retained branch `LOW(E0)`: air/fire/earth remain in the bottom of the
  distillatory as `tria elementa`.

The branches are not merely two adjacent mentions. The rising branch has a
receiver and return operation; the retained branch has a bottom-location and
component assertion. The later instruction reunites both by placing the
extracted water over the retained three-element residue, mixing them, sealing
the vessel, and holding them in the same bath. The source therefore supplies
the required actual fields: source event `E0`, branch provenance `UP(E0)` and
`LOW(E0)`, branch-specific receiver/retention roles, and a later `COMBINE`
consequence before the next heat stage.

Its discriminator is branch topology plus composition outcome. A nearest
neighbor or independent-singleton account can mention water, residue, and a
bath, but it cannot preserve both branch identities and require their reunion;
the source then predicts a mixed water/air output before the later separation.
The source does not license mass conservation, unchanged chemistry, or a
general merge rule: the later four-to-one water addition has unresolved
provenance and the printed black-water/black-earth wording conflict remains.

This second use of the same complete source is intentional: it isolates the
branch/reunion consequence from the three-step output carry. Both examples
remain source-only and unselected; neither binds a Voynich form or participant.
