## Result and exact ownership

The complete contiguous Book I chapters I–IX of *De diversis artibus* give
actual named compound preparations, explicit backward references, and later
modifications that retain an earlier recipe. Chapter VII names **posc** despite
intervening **rosa**, **lumina** and **veneda** preparations. Its phrase
`posc, de quo supra dictum est` is written in the source. The recipe reference
is not an inherited output of our parser. Chapters IV and V also explicitly
return to `simplici membrina`, instead of modifying the most recently named
compound. This is a source-attested writing mechanism, not a target decoder.

The full public-domain Latin below follows Robert Hendrie's 1847 edition,
[complete PDF](https://archive.org/download/theophiliquietru00theouoft/theophiliquietru00theouoft.pdf),
printed pages [2], 4, 6 and the top of 8 (PDF pages 68, 70, 72, 74). All four
were actually viewed natively. The university-hosted
[chapter index](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost12/Theophilus/the_0000.html)
and chapters I–IX were read and collated against those pages. Paragraph breaks
and line-end hyphens are normalized; lexical disagreements are recorded below.
The boundary starts at chapter I after the prologue and ends at `per omnia`
in chapter IX, before chapter X. It is a connected section, not one historical
paragraph and not a source-token count proposed for Voynich.

The work is medieval, conventionally dated to the early twelfth century.

## Full source text, without omitted intervening chapters

### I.1. De temperamento colorum in nudis corporibus.

> COLOR qui dicitur membrina, quo pinguntur facies et nuda corpora, sic componitur. Tolle cerosam, id est album, quod fit ex plumbo, et mitte eam non tritam, sed ita ut est siccam, in vas cupreum vel ferreum, et pone super prunas ardentes, et combure donec convertatur in flavum colorem. Deinde tere eum, et admisce ei albam cerosam et cenobrium, donec carni similis fiat. Quorum colorum mixtura in tuo sit arbitrio; ut si, verbi gratia, rubeas facies habere vis, plus adde cenobrii; si vero candidas, plus appone albi; si autem pallidas, pro cenobrio modicum prasini.

### I.2. De colore prasino.

> QUI prasinus, est confectio quaedam habens similitudinem viridi coloris et nigri, cujus natura talis est, quod non teritur super lapidem, sed missus in aquam resolvitur et per pannum diligenter colatur, cujus usus in recenti muro pro viridi colore satis utilis est.

### I.3. De posc primo.

> CUM vero membrinam miscueris inde facies et nuda corpora impleveris, admisce ei prasinum et rubeum, qui comburitur ex ogra, et modicum cenobrii, et confice posc, ex quo designabis supercilia et oculos, nares, et os, mentum, et fossulas circa nares, et tempora, rugas in fronte et collo, et rotunditatem faciei, barbas juvenum et articulos manuum et pedum, et omnia membra, quae distinguuntur in nudo corpore.

### I.4. De rosa prima.

> DEINDE misce cum simplici membrina modicum cenobrii et parum minii, et confice colorem, qui dicitur rosa, unde rubricabis maxillam utramque, os et mentum inferius, collum et rugas frontis modice, ipsam frontem super tempora ex utraque parte, nasum in longitudine et supernares ex utraque parte, articulos et caetera membra in nudo corpore.

### I.5. De lumina prima.

> POST haec misce cum simplici membrina cerosam tritam, et compone colorem, qui dicitur lumina, unde illuminabis supercilia, nasum in longitudine et super foramina narium ex utraque parte, subtiles tractus circa oculos et tempora inferius, et mentum superius, juxta nares et os ex utraque parte, frontem superius, inter rugas frontis modice, et collum in medio, et circa aures, ac articulos manuum et pedum exterius et omnem rotunditatem manuum, pedum et brachiorum in medio.

### I.6. De veneda in oculis ponenda.

> DEINDE commisce nigrum cum modico albo, qui color vocatur veneda, et imple pupillas oculorum. Adde ei etiam de albo amplius, et imple oculos ex utraque parte, et album simplex linies inter pupillam et ipsum colorem, et cum aqua lavabis.

### I.7. De posc secundo.

> POSTEA accipe posc, de quo supra dictum est, et admisce ei amplius de prasino et rubeo, ita ut umbra sit anterioris coloris, et imple medium spatium inter supercilia et oculos, et sub oculis medium, et juxta nasum, et inter os et mentum, granos seu barbulas adolescentum, et palmas dimidias versus pollicem, et pedes supra minores articulos, et facies puerorum et mulierum a mento usque ad tempora.

### I.8. De rosa secunda.

> DEINDE misce cum rosa cenobrium, et linies inde in medio oris, ita ut anterior superius inferiusque pareat, et fac subtiles tractus super rosam in facie, in collo et in fronte, et designabis inde articulos in palmis, et juncturas omnium membrorum et ungula.

### I.9. De lumina secunda.

> ET si facies tenebrosa fuerit ut ei non sufficiat una lumina, adde ei amplius de albo et super priorem linies subtiles tractus per omnia.

The printed footnote to `cenobrium` in I.1 reads **“Vel sinopidem, ex C. R.”**
It is retained as an editorial witness alternative, not added as an extra
ingredient to the main recipe and not silently selected. This dossier does
not resolve the editor's siglum through an assumed modern chemical identity.

## What is shared, and what must remain distinct

Six notions must not be collapsed: a named recipe/color type; a particular
batch or portion; its transformed material state; deposited paint on an image;
an application region; and a written mention. In particular:

- `posc` in VII refers to the earlier recipe/type. The text does not decide
  whether the painter saved a physical aliquot or makes an equivalent fresh
  preparation. No mass conservation or same-vessel claim is inferred.
- `simplici membrina` in IV/V refers to the unmodified base formulation,
  despite intervening derived preparations. A globally destructive update of
  the only current pot is not a faithful complete account unless it also
  accounts for obtaining the specified base again.
- `cum rosa` in VIII denotes input paint; `super rosam` denotes paint already
  on the image. The root name is shared and the Latin case differs, but they
  are not one individual material token. The deposited layer has its own
  spatial role and its relation to the changed paint must remain explicit.
- `anterior`, `anterioris coloris`, `super priorem` and `ipsum colorem` have
  local state/layer roles. Their antecedents are interpreted from the whole
  source context, not converted into one universal instruction to reuse the
  last noun. Some wording remains less explicit than the named posc reference.

Ordinary Latin case is an attested realization difference here: `membrina`
versus `membrinam`, and `rosa` versus `rosam`. These forms do **not** license an
EVA-to-Latin alphabet, a fixed suffix dictionary, or a claim that Voynich uses
Latin morphology. Nor does the same form necessarily have the same physical
referent in every clause.

## Concrete distinct consequences, with no invented writer

1. **Latest-preparation rival:** substituting the most recently introduced
   mixture at VII takes veneda (black/white) where the source writes posc.
   The full named recipe and its inherited materials are changed, even if
   both routes could produce some plausible dark paint.
2. **Fresh primitive-color rival:** treating the later posc/rosa/lumina as
   unrelated primitive colors loses their inherited base preparations and
   the stated additions. Matching final color names alone is insufficient.
3. **Flattened-layer rival:** laying the second rosa as an opaque replacement
   of the entire first application cannot also satisfy the written requirement
   that the earlier color remains visible above and below. That spatial
   consequence arises from the first application plus the second operation.
4. **Identical-name recurrence rival:** requiring the whole written form of
   every named object to recur unchanged ignores actually written case forms
   and compressed local references. This is a source counterexample to a
   universal literal-recurrence assumption, not a repair of any past test.

The source's own construction is the proposed mechanism: name a compound,
resume it explicitly, add stated components, and apply the result relative to
an earlier application. No new opcode alphabet, free modern program language,
fixed-count serializer, or variable-length target grammar is supplied. The
unresolved research debt is a finite shared realization compatible with a
whole admitted passage and with all its content, not just three color names.
No target owner or target vocabulary is nominated in this source-only task.

The source retains symmetries and freedoms. A consistent renaming of all color
families can preserve the abstract dependency structure. Flexible amounts and
complexion choices prevent exact compositional ratios. Prasinus remains a
named preparation rather than a chemically closed ingredient expansion.
The complete section therefore constrains reference/type/state relationships
more strongly than it identifies a unique literal painting or physical recipe.
