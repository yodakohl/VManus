# Historical lexical candidates for *musdel / musdeel / musmel / mus*

Source-only collection, 2026-09-15.  No Voynich image, transcription, f116v
material, or proposed decoder source was consulted.  These entries document
historical possibilities; they do not select a reading of any marginal text.
Quoted material is kept below 25 words per website source.

## 1. *musdel / musdeel*: legal widow-share term

The **Deutsches Rechtswörterbuch (DRW), s.v. Musteil** gives the headword as
“Musteil, m., n., Musteile, f.” and defines it as a share or division of food
stores.  Its first sense is the half of the food supply due to the wife at
dissolution of marriage or on the thirtieth day after the husband's death.

Source: [DRW, Musteil](https://drw.hadw-bw.de/drw-cgi/zeige?index=lemmata&term=musteil),
lines 55–60.  Short quotation: “die ... Hälfte des vorhandenen Speisevorrats”.

The same entry records **musdele** in the 1224/35 Sachsenspiegel material and
**musdel** in a 1429 Lübeck record: “musdel in all vnsen sloten vnde houen”.
These are genuinely attested legal spellings, primarily Middle Low German
record evidence, rather than proof of a Middle High German form.  The DRW
also records later *musteil / mußtheil* spellings and a general food-store
sense, so “widow's share” is a historically specific sense selected by legal
context, not the only lexical value.

An independent edited witness is **De Saksenspiegel in Nederland** (1888),
chapter III.74.  Its running text has *musdele*; the apparatus records the
variant **musdeel**: “ors musdeel an alle O. ende haer spise”.

Source: [DBNL, Sachsenspiegel III.74](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_sak001saks02_01/_sak001saks02_01_0202.php),
lines 93–100.  The edition is late, but the underlying legal material is
medieval and the variant is explicitly an apparatus reading, not the main
text.  Thus *musdel* and *musdeel* are genuinely attested orthographic
variants in the legal tradition; their presence does not establish the
language, date, or syntax of another text.

## 2. *musmel*: exact spelling versus related compounds

The strongest direct spelling found is **muͦsmel**, cited by the
Frühneuhochdeutsches Wörterbuch in a Schwabian 1414 source (Mollwo, *Rotes
Buch Ulm* 163,24): “daz [...] die muͥller schoͤn mel und muͦsmel ... mit dem
metzen uss messen”.  It occurs beside *schön mel*, oats and kernels in a
measuring context.  This is a real early New High German/late medieval
Swabian attestation of the order *muos + mel*; it is not a proof that an
earlier Middle High German text used the unmarked spelling **musmel**.

Source: [FWB, tuch](https://fwb-online.de/lemma/tuch.s.2n), lines 1922–1936.
The dictionary labels the witness “schwäb., 1414”; no independent gloss in
that quotation alone fixes whether the item means flour, flour meal, or a
prepared flour food.

The **Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Althochdeutschen (EWA)** separately
attests the ordered compound **melmuos**: “mhd. melmuos st.n. ‚farratum‘,
frühnhd. melmus ‚Mehlbrei‘”.  Its evidence includes glosses from the 13th–15th
centuries and later forms.  This is direct evidence for *melmuos / melmus*,
not for reversing the order in every period or dialect.

Source: [EWA, meloboum](https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/98910/de),
lines 32–35 and the *melomuos* discussion.  The exact compound status is
therefore: **melmuos** is Middle High German attested; **melmus** is early
New High German attested; **muͦsmel** is directly attested in a 1414 Swabian
witness; bare normalized *musmel* is a permissible citation normalization,
not an independently dated MHG lemma.

## 3. *mus / muos*: broad food noun

EWA's **muos** entry gives the Middle High German form as neuter and records
the meanings food, meal, prepared dish, and porridge-like food or vegetables:
“Mhd. muos st.n. (md. mûs) ‚Essen, Mahlzeit, (breiartige) Speise, Gemüse‘”.

Source: [EWA, muos](https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/103280/de), lines
26–39.  The entry also records Old High German and other West Germanic
correspondences.  The semantic range is wider than modern “porridge”: a
context-free gloss **Mus = Brei** is too narrow, while **food/meal/prepared
dish** remains compatible with the historical evidence.

## 4. What *der / den* would imply

The University of Bamberg's MHG grammar lists the definite article inventory
as “bestimmter Artikel: der, diu, daz” and states that pronouns follow the
article's inflection, with documented variation.  Source: [BABS MHG grammar,
PDF](https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/germ-philma/BABS/BABS_02.pdf),
pp. 122–128, especially PDF lines 3656–3705.

Under the ordinary MHG paradigm:

| form | ordinary singular/plural possibilities | implication here |
|---|---|---|
| **der** | masculine nominative singular; feminine genitive/dative singular; genitive plural | **der musdel** can be masculine nominative if *Musteil* is masculine; it is not evidence for the widow sense. If the noun is feminine *Musteile*, *der* would require genitive/dative context. |
| **den** | masculine accusative singular; dative plural | **den musmel** can be masculine accusative or plural dative. It is not the expected neuter singular article for the neuter *muos / melmuos* family. |
| **daz / diu** | neuter singular / feminine and some neuter plural cells | A singular neuter *muos* or *melmuos* would normally require the neuter form, subject to dialectal and editorial variation. |

These are grammatical consequences only.  A marginal heading may omit an
article, use a non-MHG variety, contain an inflectional error, or be
misread; **der** and **den** cannot independently choose between the legal
*musdel* and food-related *musmel* hypotheses.  In particular, the article
does not turn a reconstructed compound into a historical attestation.

## Date/dialect and evidential disposition

* **musdel / musdele / musdeel:** direct medieval legal attestations in the
  DRW evidence, including 1224/35 and 1429; the cited forms are Middle Low
  German or related legal witnesses, not securely Middle High German.
* **musmel / muͦsmel:** direct 1414 Swabian evidence in FWB; related ordered
  *melmuos* is Middle High German and *melmus* early New High German in EWA.
  The reversed order is real, but its date and dialect must be preserved.
* **muos / mûs:** MHG neuter food noun with broad meanings; “porridge” is one
  contextual specialization.
* **der / den:** article forms constrain a proposed noun's case, gender and
  number only under an MHG-like grammar.  They supply no lexical identity.

No lexical candidate is selected from these sources.
