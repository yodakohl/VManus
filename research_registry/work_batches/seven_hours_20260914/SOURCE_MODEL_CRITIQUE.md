# Cyranides Book I as a four-way source model: bounded critique

Date: 2026-09-15. Source-only review; no new Voynich page, image, mixed TSV,
or target label was opened.

The cached Book I has 24 chapters (`ΣΤΟΙΧΕΙΟΝ Α` through `Ω`) and each chapter
opens with four named entries: one botanical object, one bird, one stone, and
one fish or marine animal. That is a real source-level four-way association.
It is more specific than the generic late-medieval mixed record architecture
in GDT735. It also gives a plausible model family for a shared chapter/body
index: four category heads can be linked to one chapter key, while their
category-specific descriptions and effects remain separate.

The source does not support a fixed H1-H4 category order. Chapter 1 presents
plant, bird, stone, fish; chapter 10 presents plant, fish, stone, bird; chapter
14 presents plant, stone, bird, fish; chapter 9 begins with tree, stone, bird,
fish. A target model must therefore either observe category markers or allow
role assignment independently within each four-way unit. Assigning H1, H2,
H3, H4 to plant, bird, stone, fish by position would import an unsupported
order.

The shared index is also not equivalent to shared surface identity. Chapters
2, 5, 6, and many others use four different names with a common initial
letter, while chapter 10 uses `Κιναίδιος` for all four category entries and
chapter 14 uses `Ξίφιος` for all four. The same-name cases have different
category descriptions and properties; they are homonymous category records,
not evidence that one physical object fills all four roles. Conversely, a
common initial or chapter letter is only an observable index candidate if the
target model predeclares an equivalent marker. An opaque hidden index cannot
be scored after the fact.

The strongest genuinely falsifiable mechanism is therefore a **four-way
hyperedge model**:

```text
one body/chapter index k
  -> one plant head, one bird head, one stone head, one fish head
  -> category-specific attributes/effects, with optional cross-category links
```

The source predicts complete 4-way coverage for its 24 chapters, not merely a
convenient collection of four-head examples. It also predicts role separation:
the index association can be present even where no operation joins all four
objects. Chapters 19 and 20 give stronger cross-category constructions (a
bird/fish scene engraved on a stone with a plant root beneath); chapter 1 and
many ordinary entries mainly describe or prepare one category. A target model
that forces every four-head unit into one recipe or one output would therefore
be contradicted by the source's own asymmetry.

## Relation to the existing Voynich work

GDT635 exposed 760 body types and 24 bodies with all four historical
`p/s/r/l` heads. The equality of that count with Cyranides' 24 chapters is a
pattern-count coincidence until the remaining 736 bodies are included. GDT735
confirmed a historically plausible mixture of learned whole entries and bound
fields, but explicitly found no four interchangeable material heads in its
22-source atlas. GDT736 selected record-location and body-affinity working
roles; GDT737 transferred the location axis but rejected the full H1-H4
affinity transfer. None tested a source-fixed four-way hyperedge with a shared
index across all body occurrences. The proposed mechanism is consequently a
new structural hypothesis, but its apparent support currently comes from a
selected 24/760 subset.

The closest negative control is GDT635's broad head/body incidence: the four
heads occur over many bodies, and most bodies have only one head. That does
not refute a Cyranides-like register, but it prevents the 24 complete bodies
from being treated as the manuscript-wide writing rule. GDT736/737 also warn
that position and co-occurrence can be strong formal associations without
identifying head meanings.

## Smallest decisive next check

Use the already admitted full 760-body incidence table, with complete body
boundaries and all readings, and freeze before inspecting its result:

1. enumerate every body with each available head role;
2. require one shared index assignment for a complete four-way unit, allowing
   role permutation but preserving one-to-one body membership;
3. record head-count vectors, missing-role cells, repeated-index collisions,
   and whether cross-category neighbours are written as explicit operations;
4. compare the observed 24 complete units with a frequency-preserving
   selection baseline over all 760 bodies.

The meaningful outcome is not a high count. A source-like result requires a
predeclared index signal that recurs across all four roles, few role conflicts,
and additional complete units outside the selected training set. If the 24
units are the only completions, or the same opaque body can be assigned to
multiple indices by free repairs, the Cyranides analogy becomes a genre/count
lead and should not drive a decoder. If a stable four-way relation survives,
the next decision would be to inspect category-specific and cross-category
consequences separately, without assigning any word value.

The cached source therefore supports a concrete new **relation architecture**
and a finite future falsifier. It does not support H1/H4 meanings, a Greek
language hypothesis, a 24-chapter correspondence, or a manuscript reading.
