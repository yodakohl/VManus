# Is there a sixteenth generated geomantic figure?

This is a source-only review of Robert Turner’s 1655 translation of pseudo-Agrippa and the already cached Cod. Sang. 756 pp. 6–8. I did not inspect Voynich targets or candidate tables, and I did not use a modern figure-name normalization to fill the manuscript.

## Turner 1655

Turner states that “all Figures upon which this whole Art is founded are onely sixteen” and then proceeds to the figure-name table. This is a statement about the **alphabet of available figure types**, not a claim that a sixteenth output is generated after a fifteen-position judgment.

The common construction is explicit:

1. Four initial figures are *Matres*.
2. Four secondary figures, *Filiae*, are produced from the Matres.
3. The remaining four houses are formed from pairs of the first eight: 1+2 → 9, 3+4 → 10, 5+6 → 11, and 7+8 → 12.
4. “Out of the last foure Figures” (houses 9–12), two figures are made, called *Coadjutrices* or *Testes*.
5. “Out of which two is also one constituted,” called the *Iudex*.

Thus the count is 4 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 1 = **15 positions/figures**. The two witnesses are the parents of the single judge. Turner gives no sixteenth generated position, reconciler, or superjudge. The judge is the terminal result of the common procedure.

Turner also presents a preferred/“true” house arrangement. It changes where the four Filiae and cadent houses are placed and gives a different way to calculate the judge, including the instruction to count projected points, divide by twelve, and project the remainder from the Ascendant. It still refers to one *Iudex* and does not add a sixteenth output. The text’s “sixteen” therefore remains a type inventory; “fifteen” is the formed judgment register.

## Cod. Sang. 756 pp. 6–8

The page-7 red heading is visibly **“De xv. figura que iudex vocatur”**: the fifteenth figure is called the judge. The surrounding check prose discusses the right and left witnesses and their parity. It does not introduce a further numbered figure after XV. The page-8 prose visibly distinguishes “figure geomantie … xvj” (sixteen geomantic figures/types) from the formed fifteen-figure figure/register. Page 8’s lower diagram is headed with fixed/mobile figure classes; it is not a sixteenth-stage output.

The cached p6–p8 witness supports these bounded claims:

- point lines are reduced by pairing until one or two points remain;
- the formed question is described through XV, with XV the *iudex*;
- witness parity checks can diagnose an error above the judge;
- sixteen figure **types** are available even though the formed register uses fifteen figures.

The inspected pages contain no securely read term equivalent to *reconciliator*, *superjudge*, or an optional sixteenth position. They also do not provide a complete, diplomatically settled list of every construction edge. The witness cannot therefore be used to license an extra target slot merely because its figure alphabet has cardinality sixteen.

## Decision

The strongest source-grounded reading is:

```
16 = available figure types
15 = formed positions, ending in Iudex
13, 14 = two witnesses/Coadjutrices/Testes
15 = Iudex formed from 13 and 14
```

An experiment may test a fifteen-position register with a terminal judge, or separately test an explicitly declared alternative house arrangement. It should not call a sixteenth type an additional judge unless a different source supplies a parent rule and placement for that output. Any apparent sixteenth label in a diagram needs to be classified as a reused type, not as a new generated node, until such a rule is found.
