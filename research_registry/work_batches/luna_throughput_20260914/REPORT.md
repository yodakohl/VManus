## Concrete source candidate: IDEA000281

The written forms supply a crossed comparison of q-presence and fusion:

| | q absent | q present |
|---|---|---|
| Fused | `olkain`: f80v.30/.31/.33/.34 | `qolkain`: f80v.32 |
| Separate groups | `ol kain`: f80v.35 G009–010 | `qol kain`: f80v.35 G006–007 |

The full .35 line is `tol oltain olkar y qol qol kain okiin ol kain shey ldy`.
RF explicitly marks both inner split boundaries as definite spaces. ZL/IT word
arrays retain source IDs but lack separator flags. ZL additionally has `s olkain`
at .32; IT/RF have `solkain`, excluded from exact `olkain` counts.

All six ZL/IT paragraph records and all 30 RF lines were audited, including the
other two pages. Counts for `olkain / qolkain / ol kain / qol kain` are ZL
**5/1/1/1**, IT **4/1/1/1**, RF **4/1/1/1**. Every hit is on physical leaf80.
The three readers are alternative readings of the same manuscript. The second
page demanded by the raw proposal is absent in this packet.

This makes IDEA281 the most concrete source family for a next joint reading
draft, **not an experimentally selected semantic model**. A shared q-relation
and kain-head interpretation must specify an observable effect beyond merely
recovering these strings. A productive formal grammar can also generate the four
cells; a whole-word rival need not predict random positions. GDT629/GDT777 already
discuss split/fused constructions, and GDT944 covers local boundary variants.
The bounded review does not prove this rectangle historically novel or semantically
equivalent. No translation of q, ol or kain is selected.

The other retained raw ideas are IDEA282 (`otedy/qotedy` plus terminal forms) and
IDEA283 (a proposed qok frame). They are not selected. Their source claims still
need review: IDEA282's prose says three otedy despite four in its quoted lines;
IDEA283's qokol shorthand drops the ZL/RF terminal alternatives. Neither should
be run from the sketch without correction and a genuinely differing consequence.
