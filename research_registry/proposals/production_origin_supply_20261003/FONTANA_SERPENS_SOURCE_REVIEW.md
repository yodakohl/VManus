## Located device (indirect source account; not native proof)

Description: ff29v–30v; component drawing f30v; assembled drawing f31r.
A thin curved snake-shaped housing encloses closely spaced toothed wheels.
Each wheel carries an alphabet and exposes only one tooth outside the housing.
Each shaft protrudes through a hole so the operator can turn that wheel and
select a required letter. The exposed teeth resemble dorsal spines. The
component is reportedly labelled “Rotula que in dorso serpentis latet”; the
assembled drawing “Serpens”. The displayed spinal inscription is only partly
readable even in the cited edition. Its uncertain ending must not be repaired
from the modern article's suggested meaning. The construction account does
not prove that a working historical machine existed.

## Specific consequence and strongest countercase

Conditional on verification of the description, the machine distinguishes a
fixed physical sequence of readout sites from independently selectable letters.
A wheel's hidden alphabet supplies alternatives; its exposed tooth supplies the
current content. Turning one wheel changes one displayed letter while leaving
other selected letters unchanged. This is a local overwrite operation, not a
cyclic permutation of an already written sentence. The snake's outline is the
housing, not the referent of every displayed message.

Crucially, no rule here says that rotation preserves represented content, that
letters are numerical addresses, that a second ring decodes the first, or that
one must erase versus retain an old state. A drawing of a circle or a plant
would therefore be insufficient evidence for this operation in Voynich. The
source does not translate any target label and does not defeat GDT188.
