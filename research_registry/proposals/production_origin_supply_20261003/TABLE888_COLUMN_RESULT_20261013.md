## The one new comparison that can be retained

Root and both image-only readers agree on the harm cell **tarde descendit**.
The institutional transcription of BnF Latin9333,011r was rechecked at
[MediManus](https://medimanus.chs.harvard.edu/app/en/pharmantica/tacuinum_sanitatis):
its corresponding harm wording is **tarde descendit de stomacho**.
This agrees with the previously cached caption.

Deleting a heading or reordering the words of the read harm cell cannot generate
the additional de stomacho. Thus a literal per-cell copying account using only
heading deletion and reordering is incomplete for this pair of strings. A fuller
account would have to supply wording explicitly, perhaps by expanding an ellipsis,
using broader context, or preserving different wording between witnesses.

This is NOT proof that the prose adds a new medical concept: the stomach may be
implicit in the shorter statement. Nor does it prove copying direction, direct
descent, lossless semantic conversion, or that the two words are unavailable
elsewhere in the table or its surrounding prose. Those extents were not fully
collated. The disputed benefit and generatum readings do not furnish additional
claimed differences. In particular, no multum-omission result is retained.
