## Fixed formula versus substituted words

The text repeatedly points back with “eandem orationem”, “hanc orationem”,
and “eandem orationem memoratam”. These are actual written reuse directions,
not modern similarities inferred between different formulas. I find **no
instruction in this block to replace one displayed formula word** according
to illness, sex, prognosis, distance, pregnancy, or ignorance.

Three things must remain separate:

1. The displayed formula is reused.
2. The recipient and accompanying act can change: presence, a heard name,
   pulse contact, or an ignorance question.
3. The subsequent answer and interpretation branch. In the child/nonresponsive
   case the practitioner's first thought replaces the patient's spoken reply;
   that is not a replacement inside the prayer.

This is positive source evidence for **a stable opaque verbal object used
under several distinct conditions with context-dependent outputs**. It is not
evidence that small differences between opaque words encode those outputs.
If anything, this particular passage warns against inferring that every
different application must have a different formula token.

## Limits retained

The block combines response-based instructions with an explicit exception;
therefore a simplistic total decision tree “silence always means death”
misrepresents it. No priority rule beyond the stated child/inability exception
is invented here. The nescio clause remains qualified.

ear claim has been removed above. This correction does not change the

no complete transcription of the preceding opaque names is claimed.
