## 18v: what is actually visible

The right column first finishes Latin devotional prose. A red enlarged A
begins another prayer. The following instruction says this extra prayer is
to be said at both the beginning and end of each exercise of the art. Its
wording includes **“in principio et in fine”**. This is a whole-prayer reuse
instruction, not a translation assigned to individual opaque syllables.

Immediately below is a compact red/black sign with a central cross-like
construction and rounded lateral compartments. The next paragraph begins
**“Hec est nota ineffabilis theologie”**. It links the theological nota to
prayer reference material: one quoted beginning starts with O and later
contains totius pietatis; another reference clearly gives **“Nosel”**.
Earlier chapter context and a later explanation are invoked. I can read the
presence of prayer ordinals, but do not certify their values or expand the
abbreviated first incipit into a supposedly exact diplomatic transcription.

This directly supports reference by prayer beginning within commentary on a
nota. It does not complete the cross-reference: I have not located and read
the complete referred Nosel prayer in this bounded panel. Nor does proximity
alone prove every component of the adjacent sign's ownership or function.
No claim that each drawn compartment corresponds to one spoken word follows.

## 10r/v: inventory and layout, not a recovered alignment

10r contains long point-separated invocation sequences, several enlarged
initials, and short red terminal rubrics. At the bottom of the right column,
a distinct Latin inventory counts figures by subject. Philosophy, grammar,
dialectic, rhetoric, and theology are explicitly named; the inventory
continues at the upper left of 10v and gives a total. This note does not
certify every count or recalculate the complete inventory.

The remainder of 10v has a large circular construction of concentric red
bands containing text, interrupted by a narrow opening at the top. This is
native evidence that long invocation-like writing can occupy a figure's
bands. It does not demonstrate that the bands reproduce, abbreviate, or
reorder any complete 10r prayer. No beginning, direction or wordwise mapping
has been inferred from the circular shape.

## Consequence and boundary

The positive is concrete: ordinary Latin instructions can refer to an entire
prayer by its incipit and prescribe reusing a whole prayer at two procedural
positions, alongside notation/figure commentary. Literal formula reference
is therefore a historical operation available for source comparison.

The missing observation is equally concrete: this panel does not establish
an exact complete formula-to-diagram copy, a finite transformation of opaque
strings, or a unique relation between repeated strings and pictured objects.
A purported Voynich analogue would need its own independently bound unit and
instruction; shared circularity or repeated short forms cannot supply that.
No target route or decoder is selected by this source finding.
