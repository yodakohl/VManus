## Bounded comparison

The Latin daily-fever recipe (Macer lines463–466) places the patient condition
first, then plantain and betony quantities, preparation/drinking, and a timing
restriction. German moves the indication after ingredients/preparation, as the
complement of a benefit predicate, followed by conditional warm use and timing.
Both passages attach the single quantity to plantain and the double quantity
to betony. Reading German "eyn gewichte" and "zwi teil" as commensurate portions
yields the same1:2 ratio; that unit compatibility is an interpretation supported
by the Latin parallel, not independently explicit German ounce notation. No
absolute ounce scale is independently preserved.

This is not full equivalence. Latin specifies preceding chills; German names
the fever's onset. Nearby recipes differ in amounts, omission and wording.
The Latin comparison printed here starts at line460, although the editor names
the complete Betonica chapter as429–491. No claim of a complete two-language
chapter alignment is made.

## Relevance to the present conditional role-prior route

An indication's linear position is not its semantic role. A source-trained model
can encounter a real translation/redaction in which the patient-condition clause
is recast as the complement of “helps,” while ingredient ownership remains fixed.
The useful constraint is to explain both **local grammar and relational ownership**.
It is not permission to apply arbitrary clause permutations.

For a future explicitly hypothetical whole reading, the two limited alternatives
would need distinct written construction accounts: antecedent patient condition
versus efficacy predicate with an indication complement. A common label called
CONDITION cannot hide the difference. Nor does this comparison equate a prescription
with an efficacy assertion in all logical contexts; their speech-act commitments
may differ. The owned single/double quantities and timing restriction are additional checks,
not a recovered Voynich numeral or plant name.

GDT1160 establishes a supervised source-context ranking benefit, not portability
of these grammar labels to Voynich. Known directed composition and whole-form
residuals remain constraints; no suffix is named from the comparison.
