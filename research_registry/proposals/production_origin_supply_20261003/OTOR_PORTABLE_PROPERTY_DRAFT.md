## Actual partial content, assumptions, and cost

The smallest attempted local construction is the contiguous G4–G7 phrase
`cphol cheor opcheeol otor`. Under the property hypothesis its readable partial
rendering is:

> UNKNOWN[shokchy] UNKNOWN[chteey] UNKNOWN[choteey] —
> UNKNOWN[cphol] has potent power — UNKNOWN[choctheeey] UNKNOWN[okchoal].

The dashes separate readable and unread spans for this display, not manuscript
clauses. The phrase asserts possession of an attribute with a property; it
does not supply a speaker, a star identity, a comparison, or an event. The
English adjective needs no extra target copula under the proposed postnominal
modifier rule. The attached label would say **“potent”** of the pictured star;
its local attachment supplies that label's bearer. Nothing identifies this
star with UNKNOWN[cphol] in the upper line.

The degree/class alternative renders the same trial phrase:

> UNKNOWN[shokchy] UNKNOWN[chteey] UNKNOWN[choteey] —
> UNKNOWN[cphol] has power of [OTOR: UNKNOWN degree/class] —
> UNKNOWN[choctheeey] UNKNOWN[okchoal].

Its attached label supplies that class for the star, but neither the dimension,
the scale, nor a numeric value is identified. “Six” is not supplied by the
image's rays. This alternative describes a graded attribute rather than
asserting a sufficiently potent one. Without a written scale, threshold,
comparison, or downstream consumer, the two descriptions can be observational
renamings. **These are different intended partial contents, not an observed
manuscript discriminator.** RF permits only “UNKNOWN[cphol] has power
UNKNOWN[ot@221;r]” within the same conditional trial.

Exact paid burden: three provisional whole-form substitutions per alternative
(CHEOR, OPCHEEOL, OTOR); one binary subject–predicate–attribute construction;
one postnominal property/class attachment; one provisional subject role for
the otherwise unknown CPHOL; and one property/class label application to an
attached object. Six upper-line positions retain unknown meanings, five of
those retain wholly unknown attachment, and RF adds an uncertain property
position. No token segmentation, hidden earlier producer, silent subject
identity, unspecified unknown-span no-op, or narrow stellar name is supplied.
The unknown prefix can change whether the displayed possession is asserted,
queried, negated, or conditional. The unknown tail can change its qualification
or completeness. Thus even the four-group assertion is **conditional**.

## Bounded outcome

This attempt writes an actual partial alignment and a conditional four-group
property statement. It does **not** read the full nine-group line or establish
the statement's scope. The precise unresolved content is the three-group
opening, CPHOL's lexical identity/reference, the two-group continuation,
OPCHEEOL's noun value, CHEOR's standalone-label construction, and RF's marked
G7. Consequently it supplies no preference for STRONG/POTENT, ordinal/degree,
HAS, or POWER and no observed consequence selecting between the OTOR rivals.
The positive result retained from the primaries is exact label/running-text
reuse in two readers with genuine local star attachment. A complete reading
has not been produced; adding unmotivated senses to the six unknown positions
would conceal the present obstruction.
