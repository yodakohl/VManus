# f9v: `Jacea` is a late compatible name, not a period anchor

28 September 2026. This is a bounded **primary-edition supplement** to the
already recorded f9v chronology decision in
[the existing capacity dossier](../../decisions/f9v_jacea_full_entry_capacity_20260928.md)
and ledger rows `f9v_jacea_historical_name_chronology` and
`f9v_jacea_earlier_referent_contrast`. Those rows had already established
late pansy attestation and divergent earlier `Jacea` referents. The present
note supplies a directly accessible *Alphita* edition page; it is **not a
new research decision or independent confirmation**. It does not alter
GDT1063/1064, open a Voynich page or translate a group.

[GDT1064](../../../experiments/yolo/gdt1064_f9v_jacea_historical_synonym/REPORT.md)
correctly established that the 1485 *Gart der Gesundheit* prints
`yacea` beside `freyschem krut` under a pansy-like illustration. That proves
a historically attested pansy-compatible **use** of the name, but it does not
establish the use in the likely fifteenth-century setting of the Voynich text.

An older medico-botanical witness gives a concrete competing referent. The
[Wellcome digitization of Mowat's edition of *Alphita*, page 83](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/e45my5w6/items?canvas=147&page=25&shouldScrollToCanvas=true)
prints `Iacea nigra` with French `mathfelonna`/English `Bulwed` or `hardhaw`,
and `Iacea alba` with `scabiosa`/`scabwort`. The edited medieval glossary is
a primary textual witness for the older pair of names, though the linked
page is an 1887 edition, not a native manuscript image. The editorial note
identifies `matfelon` with knapweed; that botanical identification remains an
editorial interpretation rather than a labelled medieval plant image.

[Holtzegel's historical study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304142518_Viola_jacea_Zur_botanischen_Fachsprache_in_der_Renaissance)
surveys earlier *Circa instans* witnesses and the German *Macer* and finds no
pre-1485 illustrated or described pansy under `Jacea` in those checked
sources. This is a bounded literature report, not proof that no earlier use
existed anywhere. It also notes a 1478 `freysam` medicinal-water mention whose
plant was neither described nor pictured, so it cannot independently identify
a pansy. The exact pre-1485 referent of bare `Jacea` varies by source.

**Retained decision (already recorded):** GDT1064's narrow correction survives: the bare string
`Jacea` is not botanically impossible for a pansy. Its 1485 attestation is
too late and too polysemous to prefer `fochor=Jacea` for f9v. The public
decoder's specified `Jacea (knapweed)` still conflicts with
[GDT1063's](../../../experiments/yolo/gdt1063_f9v_viola_visual_name_exploration/REPORT.md)
pansy-like f9v drawing, while a generic entry-address reading of `fochor`
remains equally open. The only target-side C0 reading retained is
`fochor≈pansy-like Viola`; no lexical identity or confirmed word is added.

The source comparison uses public external material and the existing
GDT1063/1064 reports only. f84/f84r and reserved pages remain closed.
