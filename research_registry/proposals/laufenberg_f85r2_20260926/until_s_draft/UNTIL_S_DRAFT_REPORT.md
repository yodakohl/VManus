## Source and contrasting consequence

Galen, *On the Natural Faculties* III.1 (the complete opening chapter, pp. 223–225 in the displayed 1916 Loeb translation) argues that nutrition entails assimilation of nutriment to the receiving part; attraction culminates in presentation; presentation precedes adhesion, which precedes assimilation; adhesion and assimilation require time; continued departure prevents both; and a resident retentive faculty is needed. Its final inference depends on a premise of purposeful Nature. The webpage labels its transcription unproofread. [Primary chapter](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Galen/Natural_Faculties/3%2A.html).

The draft represents those stage and countercase relations only partially. It does not state each part's nutritive faculty, the juice's propriety, a residence duration, the need or location of a retentive faculty, or the purposeful-Nature inference. The full obligation table in DRAFT.json distinguishes these omissions; this is not a complete translation of Galen III.1.

The serious rival changes only `chey` to AFTER completion, leaving every other dictionary entry, binder and attachment fixed. UNTIL permits the adhesion and attracting events up to their named endpoints; AFTER places those same events after assimilation and presentation, respectively. That reverses the proposed event intervals and conflicts with the source's stage ordering and attraction-as-presentation-goal argument. It is a model-derived contrast, not an observed target temporal contrast: the source text does not independently reveal the target's event order.

The cessation-at-completion condition is a deliberate additional assumption. Galen says residence enables adhesion and assimilation and calls presentation the goal of attraction, but III.1 does not explicitly state that retention ceases exactly at assimilation completion. The strict UNTIL gloss therefore exceeds what this source entails.
