The unresolved vocabulary in lines 11–18 prevents a complete polished translation but does not conceal the opening, reversal examples, or final exhortation.

## 6. Sin — left column of canvas 60, 30 lines

The wide ship picture separates two verse columns. The left opens with an imperative addressed to a human being, not with a continuation of Death's final prayer. The ship's scroll reads `Vos et vestra ruunt`: “You and what is yours fall/perish.” The image shows the standing man holding a ring in a horizontal linked cord/chain, which is a visible thematic echo of the Death picture. This does not by itself establish a single literal cord running across physical pages.

| 11–12 | Sins are always accompanied by cares and are not hidden from God. |

| 15–16 | Sins turn against virtue [exact verb sense tentative] and are subject to the devil. |
| 17–18 | Sins bring [line 17's paired terms unresolved]. Avoid sins everywhere. |
| 19–20 | Do not forget your reason; [line 20 has an injunction involving great/grave sin, but its syntax is unresolved]. |

**Argument actually recoverable:** earthly transience supplies the reason to avoid sin; Christ's suffering, death, and judgment supply the objects of meditation; virtue leads to eternal life. This continues the moral *themes* of the preceding poems while starting a new thirty-line speech.

## 7. Elements and spheres — right column of canvas 60, 30 lines

This is the most consequential newly recovered source unit. It begins afresh: `Hie wil ich kundt thun und leren / Von Elementen und Speren`, followed by earth, water, air, and fire and the announcement of their properties. The four qualities below are read from the actual verses; they are not filled in from a standard schema.

| Lines | Provisional native reading or complete sense translation |
|---:|---|
| 1–4 | Here I wish to declare and teach about elements and spheres: earth, water, air, fire, and make their properties known. |
| 5–6 | `Das niderst element ertrich ist / Kalt und trucken zu aller frist`: the lowest element is earth, cold and dry at all times. |
| 7–9 | It has three regions [noun's exact spelling uncertain], named Asia, Africa, Europe; our part is Europe [the modifier of “part” is uncertain]. |
| 10 | `Im ertrich mitten die helle da`: hell is in the middle of the earth. |
| 11–13 | The low/deep places of the earth [line 11 syntax provisional] are water and sea, which are cold and moist. Anchor: `kalt und feücht`. |
| 14 | `Der lufft warm feücht als wir lesen`: air is warm and moist, as we read. |
| 15–16 | There are three regions of air; the upper is by the heaven [last word unresolved; no exact stellar boundary is imposed]. |
| 17 | The lowest, upon the earth, gives life. |
| 18–19 | In the middle the clouds float, with snow, rain, hail, and [last weather term unresolved]. |
| 20 | Devils are also said to occupy [this region: complement/verb reading provisional]. The evidential qualification `sagt man` is visible and retained. |
| 21 | `Hitzig und dürre das fewre element`: the fiery element is hot and dry. |

**Source argument in order:** name the elements → specify earth's position and cold/dry qualities → situate continents and hell → describe water as cold/moist → air as warm/moist → divide air into three regions with life below and weather in the middle → qualify the report about devils → fire as hot/dry → planetary, stellar, and crystalline heavens → motion carrying eight other spheres → direct desire toward God and heavenly joy.

This is an actual connected cosmological exposition, with a moral conclusion. It contains no prescription that a hot body must be cooled, no account of bodily mixture or predominant human complexion, and no explicit instruction to read the preceding physician as the speaker. Those different propositions cannot be imported into it from another work.

## 8. What the diagram itself labels

Canvas 59 was already in scope. For this check its retained derivative and then the official full image were rotated **180 degrees only**, without crop, recoloring, sharpening, or OCR. The official IIIF request for a 180-degree derivative failed with HTTP 500, so a local decoded-pixel transpose was used; hashes and operations are in the receipt. [Full official image](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00003708_00059/full/full/0/default.jpg).

**Native observation:** a legible `Aer` sits at roughly six o'clock in the rotated view, in the pale annular space **inside the orange flame ring and immediately outside the scalloped/cloud band**. The cloud band has blue hatching and a darker outlined scalloped edge. This establishes an explicitly air-labeled inner region associated spatially with that cloud border. It does **not** label every blue band “air”: a separate blue planetary ring outside the flames carries the Moon.

On the same bottom radial run, the named planetary bands read, outward, `Luna`, `Mercurius`, `Venus`, `Sol`, `Mars`, `Jupiter`, `Saturnus` (Latin spelling normalized). Beyond them is a black band with stars. `Celum Cristallinum` is written at the outer bottom; `Celum Empyreum` is identifiable at the outer upper left, with spelling not fully diplomatic here. The central landscape has a small terrestrial label whose full letters were not resolved in this bounded check. No missing `Aqua` or `Ignis` label is invented. The flame motif's identification as the fiery element is supported by its position in this explicitly cosmological diagram and the adjacent element poem, but that is a text-image inference, not a newly read `Ignis` inscription.

**Consequence:** the source supplies a better comparator than an unlabeled blue decoration: an actual air label, a cloud boundary, flames, and named planetary spheres in one ordered diagram. Its precise label placement must travel with the claim. A cloud-like outline elsewhere cannot acquire an AIR meaning merely by resemblance.

## 9. Transitions, limits, and decision

The source supports a compact **moral-cosmological sequence**, with thematic recurrence: unstable Fortune; temporal and bodily human misery; inevitable Death; rejection of Sin; ordered elements and heavens culminating in God. The complete openings, final prayers, and thirty-line divisions show separate poems, not one continuous grammatical argument. “Separate” here means formally delimited units; it does not assert independent composition, unrelated exemplars, or distinct origins.

The strongest local links are (a) the human-misery opening explicitly naming the three times depicted with the distaff, (b) Death's `strick` matching its depicted cord/chain, (c) the ship's Latin warning and repeated cord/chain motif extending the mortality theme, and (d) the element/sphere poem explicitly resuming the cosmos diagram's subject and ordering. No verse inspected here refers back to the physician, a urine examination, or a physician's advice. Because **14v was not admitted**, the stronger assertion “the physician has no relation to any neighboring text” remains unproved. What is established is no text on 15r and no discovered physician linkage in the complete selected later units.

The preregistered stronger outcome—an explicit physician-to-time-to-cosmos argument—is **not obtained**. Retain the source as an iconographic and substantive cosmological/moral comparison; do not promote the missing physician bridge into a historical fact. The cosmological verse is usable as an openly identified source argument in its own right, not as proof of a target reading.

Chronology remains restrictive: 1486 is the catalogue's witness date, not a newly established date for every underlying idea or drawing model. BSB's broader date is compatible but less precise. The inherited comparison with Tübingen Md 2 and Cgm 596 means the cosmography cannot count as an independently originated confirmation simply because this is another manuscript; no new lineage investigation was done here. A late witness may preserve older material, but that possibility does not establish priority, copying direction, geographical origin of a target, or an early-fifteenth-century exemplar. The catalogue's proposed wall-painting antecedent is not used as a proven dependency.

Remaining uncertainties are concrete: several words in the five poems; the ordinal/position in Elements 24; exact details of its upper-air and demon clauses; the physician's uninspected preceding neighbor; and final double-leaf foliation. These remain uncertainties rather than supplied bridges. Independent target-meaning confirmation added by this source continuation: **zero**.
