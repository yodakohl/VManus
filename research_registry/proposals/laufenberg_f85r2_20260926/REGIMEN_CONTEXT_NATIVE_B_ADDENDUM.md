# Native reading corrections and receipt qualification

This addendum preserves the original native packet and receipts unchanged.

On reinspection of the same whole scans only, I revise four readings:

* Scan 00173, verse 12: I now read **“von diser wandelbere”** (“of this changeable [thing/person]”) rather than my first-pass “von übel wandelbere.” The visible opening sequence is consistent with `diser`; the previous `übel` was an erroneous guess.
* Scan 00173, verse 27: I now favor **“Der sieche vnd der gesunde”** over my earlier “Der reiche und der gesunde.” The initial glyph looks closer to the long-s used in `sich`/`sicherlich` on this same page than to the r in `regieren`; the following letters look more compatible with `ie` than `ei`. This makes an ill/healthy contrast likely, though the word should remain marked as a reading rather than treated as an edited critical text. The adjacent lines still leave the exact scope of “ungleiche stunde” and the referent of “das” open.
* Scan 00171, speech verse 2: I withdraw my earlier gloss that God made the speaker a physician. The line appears closer to **“ich hab durch in vil me[n]sche[n] ernert”** (abbreviations/letters uncertain); it does not securely say “made a physician.”
* Scan 00175, speech verses 1–2: I now provisionally read **“Durch sich vnd auch die künste sein / die er manigem flüsset ein”** (the second line and inflection remain uncertain), rather than my former wording. Do not normalize this into an unseen claim. It does not change the broader ending's invocation of God's gift of arts/learning for body and soul.

These corrections leave the central source distinction intact but sharpen it: the text likely names sick and healthy recipients, rather than rich and healthy. “Das dem andern we thut” remains anaphoric and does not name a specific food/treatment; the stanza does not by itself prove a single invariant patient whose response changes only with time. Age/time and bodily change remain explicit and undirected.
