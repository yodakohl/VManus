# Oxford MS 17, fols. 7v and 40v: bounded source comparison

28 September 2026, before opening fol. 7v pixels. Exploratory source comparison, not a Voynich text test.

Question: does the original fol. 7v diagram supply an author-visible, explicitly named conjunction of the four seasons, ages, elements, qualities and twelve winds in the same historical witness as the already inspected fol. 40v wind rota? The fol. 40v source has a twelve-wind family resemblance to f69r, but no usable unique phase mark or text-item map; GDT1067 leaves source/destination polarity paired. GDT1042/1043 leave f85r2's season/quality reading provisional; GDT921's different four-person wheel also lacks exclusive second-register ownership. The secondary source *Earth in the Medieval World*, introduction pp. 10–12, describes MS 17 fol. 7v as a composite with these named systems and identifies fol. 40v as the same codex in the existing dossier. This note checks the original fol. 7v image before relying on that description.

Decision changed by outcomes: a readable fol. 7v conjunction strengthens the narrow common-tradition explanation of why seasonal/humoral and wind diagrams could coexist, while still supplying no target word. If the original lacks the described inscriptions, drop that conjunction. If fol. 7v also has a unique, readable placement scheme that fits f85r2 and f69r simultaneously, preserve it only as a new candidate for a separate owner/orientation test. A topical match alone cannot transfer labels or prove a direct copy.

Smallest test and budget: locate the institutional fol. 7v image or archived institutional facsimile, inspect one whole original, compare the named systems and their graphical owners with the already published fol. 40v and GDT1043 inventories, record source URL/hash and explicit differences, run repository checks, publish the bounded source finding. Maximum 25 minutes, including acquisition and publication; no Voynich page, cache-wide text query, reserve, or sealed material will be opened.

Dependencies: fol. 40v is an analogue, not an ancestor; both images in one codex are one historical witness; a copy from the twelfth century need not imply direct contact with the fifteenth-century Voynich workshop. Count and orientation correspondences require authorial devices, not imposed fourfold symmetry.

## Original-image result

The institutional [fol. 7v page](https://digital.library.mcgill.ca/ms-17/folio.php?p=7v&showitem=7r_2ComputusRelated_20ByrhtferthsDiagram) redirects to the [archived McGill page](https://wayback.archive-it.org/22619/20250723025321/https://digital.library.mcgill.ca/ms-17/folio.php?p=7v). Its embedded [archived Bodleian-credited fol. 7v image](https://wayback.archive-it.org/22619/20250723025321/https://digital.library.mcgill.ca/ms-17/pictures/high-res/aaa0026-M.jpg) returned HTTP 200, JPEG 2380×3502, 2,878,994 bytes, SHA256 `aeeabdceb8835d0f163b1e5c370b36bf2c264a412dfabe223dded2a9addf5f49`. This is one historical witness; the archive is a delivery route, not another manuscript.

The original visibly writes four element circles: **TERRA** at top, **IGNIS** at left, **AQUA** at right and **AER** at bottom. Each circle contains three wind names; e.g. Terra contains Subsolanus, Vulturnus and Eurus, while Ignis contains Septentrio, Circius and Aquilo. The four intervening circles explicitly pair season and life stage: Autumnus/Iuventus at upper right, Estas/Adolescentia at upper left, Ver/Puericia et infantia at lower left and Hiems/Senectus at lower right. The diamond itself carries directional labels, including ORIENS at top and OCCIDENS at bottom, and paired qualities such as *calida* and *humida* on its arms. The zodiac/month band surrounds the system. Exact expansion of every abbreviation is unnecessary to establish the visible conjunction. The [scholarly introduction](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/551699201/Earth_in_the_Medieval_World_Final_Introduction.pdf), pp. 10–12, independently explains the same system.

The already inspected fol. 40v in this **same codex** is a twelve-wind rota with named sectors and a different continuous cup ring; its image/source decision is [here](../../decisions/f69r_oxford_marker_source_decision_20260928.md). Thus one early computistical book really does place the season–age–element–wind conjunction and a separate wind diagram in one manuscript. This strengthens the **shared knowledge-program** explanation for the coexistence of f85r2-like and f69r-like topics. It does not demonstrate that either Voynich page derives from MS 17.

The graphical differences are decisive for lexical work. Fol. 7v has no four human personifications, raised flask, staff/linked strand or four feather-like corner objects; it puts season/age pairs between element circles rather than into four figure-owned paragraphs. Fol. 40v has twelve explicitly named wind sectors, whereas f69r's separately inventoried text has sixteen outer and twenty-two radial items. The two source diagrams have no author-visible cross-page marker identifying a particular Voynich sector or exact written form. Even a hypothetical four-season assignment to f85r2 remains one among viable medical/cosmological accounts, and the phase cannot be transferred from this diagram by rotation or reflection chosen after inspection.

**Decision: POSITIVE_SOURCE_CONJUNCTION, NO_TARGET_BINDING.** Retain the combined tradition as a historically attested prior, not a direct exemplar or reading key. The next useful observation would have to be an authorial, uniquely owned target-to-source relation with a fixed orientation and contrasting full-text consequences. The source conjunction by itself changes no Voynich word value, does not reopen GDT1067 or GDT1043, and supplies no independent translation confirmation. No new Voynich target or withheld material was opened.
