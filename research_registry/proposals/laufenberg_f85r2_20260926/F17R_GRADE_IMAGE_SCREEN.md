# f17r: visual check of the old hot-III/dry-IV reading

2026-09-27. Exploratory check of an already exposed page and an existing C0
reading; no new experiment, page admission, word value or reserved test.

The unchanged f17r.11 text is `otchol cthar okaiin chol daiiin` in the three
alternate transcriptions. GDT628 reads the middle as herbal material, hot
degree III, dry degree IV, but GDT813 supplies incompatible content-word roles
and GDT811 does not establish that the two quality fields describe the pictured
plant. This screen asked whether the picture could first identify a species
independently, making the old reading meaningfully testable.

Native inspection of the already cached admitted f17r photograph shows three
dark blue/white terminal heads and long smooth-looking, narrow leaves placed
up the stem in roughly opposite pairs. This permits only a broad resemblance,
not a species identification. A quick initial cornflower/Flockenblume idea is
weak; it was not a preselected identity. Another published identification
suggests *Catananche caerulea*, and other lists suggest *Aster* or
*Centaurea*, illustrating the unresolved visual ambiguity. These modern
suggestions were not treated as independent historical labels.

A source-directed search then found the striking exact pair in the edited
1415 Irish materia medica: its [Bitonica/betony entry](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600005/text677.html)
says hot in the third degree and dry in the fourth. This validates that the
**proposed kind of statement existed**, but does not identify a Voynich word.
Crucially, source selection used the pair predicted by GDT628; a resulting
species match could not be counted as blind confirmation. The [RHS botanical
description of betony](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/124649/betonica-officinalis/details)
describes a basal rosette, scalloped wrinkled veined leaves and dense flower
spikes. Those leaves do not match the straightforward native description of
f17r's smooth-looking narrow stem leaves. Stylization could explain a mismatch,
but doing so now would be a post-hoc rescue, not an identification.

**Decision:** f17r supplies no independently established species or known
temperature/degree value. The occurrence of betony in the historical source
is a genre comparator, not a Voynich plant name. Do not promote `okaiin=hot
III`, `chol=dry`, `daiiin=IV` or the five-word line on this basis. An exact
source-bound illustration with a readable contemporary plant label, identified
without the target grade pair, would change this decision; more modern visual
guesses or grade-first searching would not. Confirmed translated words: 0.
