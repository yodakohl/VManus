# Freudenthal cover diagram: source identification, with remaining foliation gap

The described diagram can be attributed, by a strong bibliographic match, to **Cava de’ Tirreni, Biblioteca/Archivio della Badia della Santissima Trinità, Cod. 3**, at **fol. 199r in Obrist’s published citation system**. This supplies a concrete shelfmark and published leaf citation. It does not establish why another record calls the corresponding subject 203r, nor verify the physical Oxford cover against that manuscript image.

## Attribution chain

**Publisher evidence.** Freudenthal’s cover note, p. viii, supplies the distinctive central Latin caption, four philosopher-style seasonal figures, peripheral qualities, and an eleventh-century southern Italian Bede manuscript. It supplies no shelfmark or folio. Its suggested ancient disc/mosaic ancestry is a historical interpretation, not manuscript provenance. The inspected edition metadata is 1999; its copyright page reports 1995 and a 2007 reprint. These dates concern the book. [Oxford cover note](https://academic.oup.com/book/26277/chapter-abstract/194504296), [copyright page](https://academic.oup.com/book/26277/chapter-abstract/194504025).

**Original scholarly identification.** Barbara Obrist, “Le diagramme isidorien des saisons, son contenu physique et les représentations figuratives,” *MEFRM* 108/1 (1996), pp. 158–160, explicitly assigns that caption to Cava ms. 3, fol. 199, figure 38. She dates the manuscript to the mid-eleventh century. The same leaf contains Abraham teaching astronomy and an astronomer accompanying the *Spera celi* incipit; the complete poem follows on 199v. Her 1994 study cites the sages illustration explicitly as **199r**, with Rotili’s 1976 reproduction. [1996 article, DOI 10.3406/mefr.1996.3481](https://www.persee.fr/doc/mefr_1123-9883_1996_num_108_1_3481), [1994 study, p. 76 n. 62](https://ethnologie.unistra.fr/websites/ethnologie/Documents/ASH/ASCH10.pdf).

**Institutional confirmation.** The BAdW PAL/Jordanus catalogue identifies Cava Cod. 3 as an eleventh-century Beneventan manuscript containing Bede, annals and a florilegium. Siena’s CORIMU record independently gives the shelfmark and mid-eleventh-century date, with Benevento as a questioned origin. The official Internet Culturale shelfmark index links Cod. 3 to object `CNMD0000204987`. None of these inspected catalogue texts directly equates fol. 199r and 203r. [PAL/Jordanus 8318](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/jordanus/ms/8318), [CORIMU 133](https://www.corimu.unisi.it/ms/133), [Internet Culturale shelfmarks](https://www.internetculturale.it/it/950/codici-per-provenienza-e-segnatura).

The exact caption plus manuscript date, region, subject and composition make the Cava attribution substantially stronger than generic resemblance. **This is an inference linking two published descriptions**, not a recovered Oxford credit line naming Cava. No named Roman philosopher has been established.

## Owners and argument of the identified diagram

The following is **Obrist’s reported reading**, not an independent transcription from the small thumbnail. She identifies identical masculine busts as the seasons. Her clockwise order starts at the upper right. [Obrist, pp. 159–160](https://www.persee.fr/doc/mefr_1123-9883_1996_num_108_1_3481).

| Position in her figure | Seasonal owner | Element | Direction | Elemental qualities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper right | hiemps | aqua | septentrio | moist, cold |
| Lower right | ver | terra | oriens | dry, cold |
| Lower left | aestas | ignis | meridies | hot, dry |
| Upper left | autumnus | aer | occidens | hot, moist |

The central medieval wording is **STIHIA ID EST TEMPORA VEL ELEMENTA**. The circumference links water–earth by cold, water–air by moisture, earth–fire by dryness and fire–air by heat. Thus spring–earth and autumn–air must be retained; a familiar alternative scheme would alter this witness. The four portrait slots have seasonal owners; individual philosopher identities remain unsupported.

Obrist interprets the composition as a **medieval assembly** using diplomatic *rotae* and antique philosopher/season imagery, rather than a direct ancient disc copy. That materially qualifies the cover note’s ancestry suggestion. [Obrist, pp. 160–161](https://www.persee.fr/doc/mefr_1123-9883_1996_num_108_1_3481).
