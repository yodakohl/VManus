## Every additional binder in the complete .20 assertion

Let e be the handle selected by keey. Assume e has one relevant material
theme m. The old WARM property applies to materials, while SLOW applies to an
event's rate. Therefore qoteedy keey requires a **new thermal lift**:
WARM(theme(e)), not WARM(e) with an event treated as physical liquid. No
numerical rate, flow amount, pressure law, temperature or speed is recovered.

The first clause asserts FLOW(e), WARM(m), and SLOW(e). The event's previous
written qokeedy does not already identify m. The fresh right-hand shedy binds
that m only under the following new cause/theme rule. This is not proof that
the earlier .18 or .16 sentence named the same material.

The proposed second-clause order is subject–object–relation–place. It yields
an aperture a with NARROW(a) and a fluid m2, and RESTRAINS_IN(a,m2,c) for the
channel c selected by shey dalom. The new identity clause sets m2=theme(e).
Without that explicit clause the restraint can concern another material.
The demonstrative c is an unresolved contextual channel variable; no drawing,
old chamber, old opening or source passage identifies it. Accommodating a
contextual demonstrative is another cost, not a recovered location.

qoty joins the true restraint claim and the true slow-flow claim by CAUSES.
There is no claimed universal law that narrow apertures always reduce fluid
velocity. Neither the identity nor causation is obtained from physical
plausibility. qoty's other written occurrence must retain the causal value,
but its two clause arguments and event participants are still unread.

Thus the costs beyond five whole-word choices are: event-handle introduction;
latest-exact-source reference; local pair morphology; the conditional RF
paragraph assumption; unique relevant theme and thermal lift; preposed narrow
modifier; the new subject/object/relation/place order; causal clause scope;
the cause/theme identity; and demonstrative accommodation. The .20 grouping
and ZL boundary choice are additional explicit parsing choices. No measured
compression gain is claimed.
