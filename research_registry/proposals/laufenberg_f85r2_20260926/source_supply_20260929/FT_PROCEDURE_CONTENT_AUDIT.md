## R's internal steps and what the signal does not prove

G07/A11 define: (1) close the nonselected origin; (2) release selected-origin and return gates; (3) place p; (4) wait Delta. CHEOL separately supplies the perceived-arrival assertion. G08 copies the full invoke/read prototype, preserving p,D,Delta and changing only origin A to B. Internal gate/place/wait actions are protocol expansion choices paid by the author; they are not separately sourced glyph readings. Invocation alone guarantees no positive result, which the frozen account correctly states.

A25 defines arrival as perception at D of a sensory marker associated with p. It does not assert transport of the entire portion, identify a smelled marker as the original specimen, or select garlic, semen, blood, named anatomy or an actual woman. Repositioning the same p in e2 is allowed by R's placement step. The source/marker identity distinction is meaningful and prevents CHEY's input-preservation claim from becoming a false material-identity assertion about the readout.

The four internal steps contain no clearing of D between e1 and e2, no signal-baseline check, no decay assumption and no origin-specific marker. P4 CHEEKY and P5 LSHEDY explicitly clear/reset D elsewhere, but Q1 does not import their runtime state and R does not include those resets. Hence the physical origin attribution of u2 is underdetermined: a marker perceived during e2 might include a surviving first-trial marker. Closing origin A prevents a new A contribution under a chosen mechanism; it does not establish removal of marker already at D. This is a limit on the diagnostic inference from two positive readouts to two separately demonstrated routes, not a contradiction of the textual assertions u1/u2 or a reason to insert an uncharged reset. An ineffective medieval procedure can still be plausible written content; modern assay validity is not a condition for translating a historical instruction. An adequate real mechanism could resolve it, but none is supplied here.
