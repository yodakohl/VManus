# I — Sloane MS 2435, complete age-regimen chapter: working reading

## Witness, boundaries and reading method

The British Library dates Sloane MS 2435 to **1265–1270**, northern France, possibly Lille, and identifies its *Régime du corps* as textual version B. The chapter begins beside the already selected four-age initial on **f31r**, after the preceding childcare conclusion, and ends in the **upper left column of f32v**, before the gold rubric introducing the second part. This is one complete local chapter, not a combination of the season and age chapters. [Official catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002114802).

All four complete page images were acquired and viewed: [31r](I_SLOANE2435_31r_FULL.jpg), [31v](I_SLOANE2435_31v_FULL.jpg), [32r](I_SLOANE2435_32r_FULL.jpg), [32v](I_SLOANE2435_32v_FULL.jpg). Their 2000-pixel height suffices for the main wording but does not settle every minim, abbreviation or drug spelling. The institution refused the requested 3000-pixel variants; none is substituted or treated as acquired. The original 31r hash equals the prior dossier's image hash.

This is a **complete provisional sense reading with local Old French anchor readings**, not a full diplomatic transcription. Every paragraph of the chapter is accounted for below, including the medicinal recipes, sexual/conduct advice and exceptions. The 1911 Landouzy–Pépin edition, already used in the predecessor, was consulted as an explicitly disclosed reading aid after whole-page inspection; it is based on other manuscripts. Its spelling or numbers are not silently installed in Sloane. Unsettled native readings are marked. There is no claim of independent blind collation.

The account reports historical medical assertions. It does not recommend those treatments.

## 1. Rubric and backward reference — f31r left, below childcare conclusion

The rubric names chapter20 and care in the **four ages**, including delaying old age and remaining young. Working reading of the central words:

> … comment l'on se doit garder es .iiii. eages et tarder de viellece et soi maintenir ione.

The initial paragraph says that the reader has already heard how to bring up/nourish a child in the first age and the second; it now explains how everyone should take care in the other ages. It introduces the physicians' common division into four: **adolescentia, iuventus, senectus, senium**. The narrow writing alongside the large initial was checked again in a column detail after the whole page; its backward reference is now readable, though this is still a normalized working reading. The four Latin age names are visible in the lower left column.

**Ownership:** an instructional narrator addresses a reader. These are not four first-person speeches by the pictured figures. The referents “first” and “second” in the childcare backward reference must be retained alongside the ensuing finer age division; they should not automatically be equated with the first two of the initial four broad ages.

## 2. Four broad ages and different bodily trajectories — f31r left foot → right top

The first is **hot and moist**. In it the body grows; it lasts to **25 or 30 years**. The second is **hot and dry**; the body remains in its strength and vigour, to **40 or 45 years**. The third is **cold and dry**; the body starts to decrease and weaken, to **60 or 65 years** in this witness's visible numeral sequence. The fourth is **cold and moist because cold humours abound through deficiency of natural heat**, and lasts until death. In that age the body continually tends toward nothing/declines.

Visible anchor readings include:

> en cest eage croist li cors … .xxv. ans ou .xxx.
>
> demeure li cors en se force et en se vigor … .xl. ans ou .xlv.
>
> li cors commence a decroistre et a afoiblir … .lx. ans ou .lxv.
>
> par le defaute de la calour naturel … dure iusques a la mort.

The quality list is therefore not the whole description. Growth, retained strength, decline, and decline ending in death have separate scopes. The final humid condition is specifically attributed to humours in the account; it is not a statement that innate heat increases.

## 3. A more detailed seven-age division — f31r right, middle

The text says that, speaking more precisely, there are **seven ages**. It describes the first from birth until the teeth begin to come, called **infantia**. The next is **dentium plantatura**, when the teeth have come, lasting to **seven years**. The third is **pueritia**, lasting to **fourteen years**. It explicitly places these three under the first broad age previously named **adolescentia**. Of the seven, the first two have already been discussed, and the text turns to what to do in the others.

Important completeness qualification: this paragraph does **not** separately name and define all seven stages. It explicitly announces seven, names three, and then supplies subsequent age-conditioned advice. Filling the remaining four names from a standard seven-age scheme would be an external repair.

## 4. From seven: character, emotional environment and daily routine — f31r right foot → f31v left

As soon as the child reaches **seven**, one should take pains that it acquire good habits. It should not encounter what causes excessive anger/distress or excessive wakefulness. What it asks for is to be given; what displeases it should not remain before it. The stated purpose is a well-constituted nature and good habits: this is the age at which children most readily retain and learn both good and bad habits, and good habits preserve bodily and spiritual health.

After this, the child should be bathed **twice or three times in a week**, especially on rising from sleep. After bathing it is fed, then allowed to play; on returning, it is fed again. This may be done **three times a day**. It receives wine mixed with water; the native line appears to specify twice for drinking, separately from the preceding three daily feeding occasions. This additional count remains a working reading. It must not drink cold water upon its food, because that cools it and could harm it greatly. Milk, fruit and cheese are to be avoided as much as possible because a stone may form in children.

These two frequencies have distinct owners: twice/thrice **weekly bathing** and thrice **daily feeding/routine**. The chapter does not turn them into a generic number repeated for every age.

## 5. Schooling without blows — f31v right top

At this age it is good to send the child to school and give it to a teacher who knows how to teach it in an orderly way **without beating**, and who does not force it to remain at school too long against its will. The text then contrasts teaching with good words and correction with sharper/harsh words. The exact final verbs are provisional, but the contrast between no beating and verbal instruction/correction is visible.

> … a maistre qui ordeneement le sace aprendre sans batre …
>
> … a bones paroles … a aspres …

This is conduct directed toward a child and a teacher, not an age personification performing medicine.

## 6. After fourteen: excessive humours, restrained sexuality, conditional purging — f31v right middle

After **fourteen**, one should see that humours do not become excessive through overeating and overdrinking. **If they do become excessive**, they are to be removed by bloodletting and mild medicines. The passage warns against excessive intercourse, on the stated ground that at this time it greatly weakens nature and complexion. It accordingly says not to take a wife before **20 or 25 years**.

The native verb for intercourse/using a woman has an unusual spelling; its exact form is unresolved here. Its subject and the following marriage restriction are clear enough for this sense reading. No prudential advice is inferred from the figure's gesture.

## 7. After twenty-five: stronger exercise and a contrasting diet — f31v right foot → f32r left top

Those who have passed **25** should undertake stronger exercise/work, in the manner previously stated. They should avoid foods that generate red bile, naming garlic, onions, leeks and other hot/dry things. They should use cold and moist foods, including fish and kid meat and **fruit** in the native list. They should drink wine mixed with water and avoid **old, strong wine**. Bloodletting and purging are allowed as the time/season and the person's nature require, because this is the age at which these can be done most safely.

The sentence spans the page boundary. The words after “old and strong” at31v's foot continue with bloodletting/purging at32r; they are not a new independent age paragraph.

## 8. After the next threshold: prefer medicinal purging over bloodletting — f32r left

A new decorated initial introduces the next age threshold. The native numeral reads **.xxxv. (35)**, followed by “an sunt passe”. The original detail resolves the earlier erroneous provisional 45 reading. At this age one should avoid bloodletting as far as possible **unless great need makes one do it**. Medicines are to be used to purge bad humours, because medicinal purging is more appropriate than bloodletting at this age. The passage again warns against intercourse, here because it especially hastens ageing.

For remaining young and delaying old age, avoid excessive work/exertion (native `travellier`, checked in the left-column detail), anger and anxious thoughts. Remain in joy and recreation; use foods that nourish well and make the blood clear, drink good wine, and bathe **once or twice a month** to remove and clean away bodily superfluities.

**Open correction:** the first whole-page reading tentatively gave .xlv. (45). A further source detail, after full-page and column viewing, shows three x forms: **.xxxv. (35)**. The earlier 45 suggestion was wrong; this is not a manuscript variant against the 1911 base text, which also has 35. The 35-year intervention threshold falls within the second broad age ending at 40/45, so treatment divisions are not identical to the four broad-age divisions. See `I_DETAIL_COLLATION_CORRECTIONS.md`.

## 9. Foods and a first preparation for delaying old age — f32r left foot → right top

The passage recommends foods described as fried, roasted/charcoal-cooked and otherwise cooked without water, for delaying old age. It then says to use preserved myrobalan, **two [unit not explicit at this point in the inspected native text] each morning fasting**, or to take some of the following electuary each fasting morning with a little hot water.

That preparation contains named myrobalan kinds: black myrobalans and kebles/chebulic myrobalans that have remained in milk, **long pepper**, ambleci/emblic myrobalans, and iron scale falling when a smith forges. They are put in sugar or honey; from these the previously named **trife/triphala-like preparation** is made. Exact botanical modern identities and the precise spelling of the preparation name are not adopted. “Iron scale falling when the smith forges” is an operation that owns the material; it is not a metallic metaphor.

## 10. A second, measured electuary — f32r right middle

The text calls the following electuary good and proven for delaying old age. The native visible list reads provisionally:

- kebles myrobalans: **ten drams**;
- iron scale: **two drams**;
- agaric: **two and a half drams**;
- ginger, black pepper and cloves: **one and a half drams of each**.

Make these into powder, mix with **honey or sugar**, and make an electuary. The source does not merely list the ingredients: the common “of each” owns the final three ingredients, followed by a preparation sequence. Spellings of the drug names are approximate normalized readings; amounts retain the native Roman numerals and half-quantity expressions in the image as their primary evidence.

## 11. Daily use, a fasting interval, and attributed authority — f32r right

Whoever wishes to remain young should use the named electuaries every day of the year, in the morning fasting, and drink afterward (no definite wine noun is established in this particular native clause). The person should abstain from eating **until midday**. The text says that these things delay old age above all others, **as Avicenna says**. This is a named textual authority, not a claim that the illustrated young figure is Avicenna.

## 12. Beyond sixty-five: exceptions, easier food and reversal of the wine advice — f32r right foot → f32v left top

But if one passes **65 years**, one should avoid **both bloodletting and purging unless great need requires them**. Use nourishing foods that cook/digest easily and drink **old red wine**. Avoid white wine and new wine, and do not let it be sweet, because sweet things harm this age-group according to the account.

This differs from the earlier young-adult instruction to avoid old strong wine. It is not a universal ban or permission for the substance “wine”. The age and wine qualifications must remain attached to their own clauses.

## 13. Final bodily and room care — f32v left, before the new gold rubric

Avoid too much exertion; bathe, and afterward be rubbed by hand or with cloths that are not too rough. Avoid cold. Have the bed and the chamber where one stays fumigated with **lin/aloes, myrrh, amber and storax calamit** [segmentation of the first name is uncertain; do not silently decide between lign-aloes and two materials]. Use named warming electuaries. The visible closing names include **diachiminum**, **diatrion piperon**, **diegenzibreos**, **dierod…** and **…dientas** [final name spellings unresolved], followed by the general phrase **“et trestous autres caus laituaires”**, all other warm electuaries.

This closes the chapter. The gold rubric immediately below begins the second part, on maintaining individual bodily members. Its hair-care initial and hair-washing scene in the right column are outside the selected age chapter. The narrow portions of facing leaves at the edges of these photographs were not used as additional pages.

## What is complete and what remains open

The complete local **semantic account and its physical chapter boundaries** are acquired. Nothing is omitted because it is inconvenient for an age-only, figure-only or quality-only interpretation: schooling, sexual restraint, frequency differences, two preparations, conditional interventions, fasting and room care are included. The remaining uncertainties are several minor verb endings, interpretation of the native intercourse verb, the additional drinking-frequency reading, the unstated unit of one myrobalan count, several drug-name spellings, and the first fumigant's segmentation. The numeral 35 and the work/exertion verb were resolved from detail images; the initial 45 reading is expressly withdrawn. These do not license substituting the 1911 main text as a diplomatic Sloane transcript.

No target passage is selected, no new target data is opened, and no meaning is assigned to a target word by this source reading. This is an improved reading of an already owned historical comparator.
