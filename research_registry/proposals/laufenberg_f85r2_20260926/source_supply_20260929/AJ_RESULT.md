# AJ: f68r2 lower disc remains Sun-like, with full Moon unresolved

The bounded native/source comparison does **not** establish a Sun versus
full-Moon identification. The upper Voynich medallion has a clear ochre
crescent. The lower has a complete face field with a segmented blue/ochre
border, whose short lobes are not unambiguously long solar rays. A Sun owner
remains a reasonable working interpretation; the full-Moon rival is neither

not new independent manuscript evidence or a translated word.

## Positive source content retained, with the correct subjects

The whole Morgan text explicitly distinguishes the Moon's transparent heaven
from its solid, non-transparent body; source light, the illuminated portion,
and changing appearance above Earth are separate. It next treats lunar
influences on humours, plants, animals and people. This supports a concrete
astronomical/medical subject connection without requiring each short caption
to be a celestial-body name. It identifies no target statement or lexeme.
The insecure eclipse condition and size antecedent stay insecure; neither
is completed from another witness. The agent's earlier stronger messages
were corrected before freeze and are preserved in AI_REVIEW.md.

This source-side distinction matters to hypothetical writing: a construction
for a body, its heaven, its illumination, its period and its effects has
different arguments. Interchanging those senses to rescue a paragraph is a
new assumption. It does not follow that the target encodes any of them.
