The decisive sentence is: “Quicksilver is matter of all metal, and therefore in respect of them it is a simple element.” Here `it` retains quicksilver and `them` picks out the preceding collective product class, all metal. The reference is supported by the explicit inferential ground, not a universal nearest-noun rule. Attaching `them` to water/earth/air, to observers, or to quicksilver and brimstone would leave no written premise that quicksilver is the matter of that different class. The source has said that those watery/earthly contributors compose quicksilver, not that quicksilver is their matter.

The source subject is a **generic material-kind**, not one measured physical sample. Same-denotation continuity is justified; a persistent post-treatment specimen with quantitatively unchanged composition is not. Quicksilver here is also distinct from planetary Mercury. The scope `in respect of` restricts the classification to its role in metals; it does not turn quicksilver into an absolutely constituent-free substance or a fifth member of the separate four-element list.

A minimal typed representation is:

```text
Mixed(Q, watery/earthly constituents)
MatterOf({Q, brimstone}, melting-metal class)
MatterOf(Q, all-metal class)
therefore ElementRelative(Q, all-metal class)
```

The joint subject and melting-metal class in the second line must remain distinct from the singular subject and all-metal wording in the third. They are consecutive claims, not two equivalent copies. The last `therefore` directly grounds relative classification in the third claim. The earlier inferential step also connects the material/congealing account with the joint matter claim; the full argument is not just three unrelated labels.

`RELATIVE_ELEMENT(A,x)` may return `Classification(subject=x, role=element/simple, referenceClass=A)`. Its inputs are a material-kind x and a product class A. `MatterOf(x,A)` supplies the source-stated ground. `Mixed(x,parts)` is an accompanying description that the relative classification can coexist with; it is not fuel consumed by a removal operation. This representation needs neither a newly produced material individual nor a physical state-change event. A curried modifier, scoped adjective or anaphoric predication could represent it equally well. The class must be retained somewhere in the semantic representation, even when not a separate surface word.

The complete96 argument contains other processes, so the card's “no transformation” needs precise scope. It means **no purification/transformation event is stated as the cause of this classification**. It cannot mean that96 contains no processes. The paragraph says quicksilver does not naturally curdle by itself without brimstone and that with brimstone and lead substance it congeals. Later it describes producing drops by heating minium and gives gilding/storage claims. None of these names constituent removal or says that a formerly mixed sample becomes absolutely simple at the classification sentence. An ineffective or disputed historical process can still be plausible written content; the objection to REMOVE_MIXTURE here is the absent written connection, not modern chemical impossibility.
