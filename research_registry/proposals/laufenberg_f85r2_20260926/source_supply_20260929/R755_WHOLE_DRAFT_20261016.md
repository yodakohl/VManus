## One shared content relation worth distinguishing

IDEA755 supplies a historical example of an intervention intended to prevent a later adverse change. The already owned source summary distinguishes this from later repainting of an already affected finish. It does **not** assert that the protective treatment is incapable of restoration. Its protect-after-drying, warming and rubbing steps are a multi-step prescription, not one observed native operation. The historical source is not identified as this manuscript's content. No paint, wax, wall, colour, anatomy or old recipe gloss is transferred.

The following is an explicitly invented, source-free comparison of two intervention laws. It demonstrates what a successful native binding would have to distinguish. It is not a word interpretation. Let the same unknown object x have an unknown Boolean property p and a separate protective condition g. Both conditions need their own binding; one must not identify protection with already having p.

| Hypothetical operation | Shared conditional law |
|---|---|
| Exposure E | (p,g) becomes (p AND g,g). This strong law assumes the specified exposure removes p when unprotected. |
| Preventive intervention I_P | (p,g) becomes (p,1): it changes future susceptibility while preserving present p. |
| Restorative intervention I_R | (p,g) becomes (1,g): it restores p but supplies no protection under this rival. |

For the explicitly stipulated initial condition (p,g)=(1,0), order has opposite consequences:

| Ordered hypothetical description | Preventive law | Restorative law |
|---|---|---|
| Intervention, then exposure | p remains1 | p becomes0 |
| Exposure, then intervention | p remains0 | p becomes1 |

This noncommutation is a substantive common relation, not a separate F_w for every token. However, its exclusive laws are additional hypotheses: the historical passage does not establish a universal inability to combine prevention and repair. A rival with both effects is outside this particular two-law comparison, not historically refuted.

An instruction means IF the specified intervention and exposure are performed on the same x under the stated initial condition, THEN the predicted result follows. An instruction is not evidence that either action actually occurred. A narrated result likewise needs its own written assertion and participant binding. No time ordering or intervention is inferred from the mere order of manuscript words.
