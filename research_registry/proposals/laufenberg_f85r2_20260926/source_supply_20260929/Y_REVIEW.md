## The precise C0 choices

There are two guessed names, one guessed relation and two guessed orientations:

* `ok = MOON`, `oko = SUN`, retaining the internal o;
* `LIGHTS(source, recipient)` is physical illumination;
* `B|ar X` describes LIGHTS(X,B);
* `B|aiin X` describes LIGHTS(B,X).

The domain is exactly the two proposed named bodies. The four unwrapped
outputs are okar, okoar, okaiin and okoaiin. **okoar is not present in these
owned units**; it is a schema product, not a newly discovered occurrence.
Neither free ar/aiin nor other apparent stems inherit these values.

The right argument is mandatory: the immediately following entire group in
the same complete ring or source-marked prose paragraph. Physical line
crossing inside that paragraph is allowed. There is no skipped word,
implicit/pictured argument, paragraph crossing or cyclic wrap around a ring.
The argument must have the required light-source or illuminable-entity type;
position alone does not establish that type or identify its denotation.

Application returns a RelationSpec, not a physical body or an assertion that
an event happened. Y also explicitly pays for applying that inner construction
before the outer q/ch wrapper. M_q and M_ch remain unknown operators on the
resulting RelationSpec. A positive assertion, negation, question or other
scope is not chosen. The entire surrounding passage remains present.

## Why this is different from the earlier relay

IDEA765-H had an opaque whole relation okoaiin. Its lower-ring source was the
preceding first word; its prose source was the preceding modified okol. Y
instead gets the source from the **written inner oko** in both occurrences.
The hypothesis thus predicts a different actor without first naming okol.
The separate ok|aiin instance gets a different source from its different
written host. This changes an argument, not only the English name of R.

Compared with W/IDEA780's BODY_TO_PERIOD, the predicate construction requires
a right participant and yields a RelationSpec rather than an interval. That
is a distinct written obligation. It has not been selected by the target,
and finding a possible neighbour does not identify LIGHTS. Reversing both
suffix orientations remains a semantic rival; the source motivates the
chosen orientation but is not a target observation.

The extra q/ch scope rule makes the immediate ykchol and the line-crossing
sar owed arguments. These consequences were authored after exposure to the
whole passage, not predicted on a holdout. No extra singleton meanings fill
them or the unknown surrounding predicates.

## Historical support, with its actual limits

The complete already-owned Hildegard passage is retained byte-for-byte as
Y_SOURCE_COMPLETE_PASSAGES.txt, copied from Q_OWNED_CELESTIAL_DRAW_PASSAGES.txt.
Its §XXXII says `Luna quoque prius defectum suum lumen de sole accipit`, then
describes the Moon helping by illuminating lower things. The same named Moon
therefore receives light and has an outgoing illumination role.

This is an electronic Latin text of *Liber divinorum operum* I.2, including
the entire §XXXII and following heading, not a new medieval transcription.
P_SOURCE_REVIEW.md records its [Clerus source](https://www.clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/it/fnk.htm)
and separates it from the independently dated thirteenth-century Lucca1942
f9r picture. Y acquired no image and did not collate this section against
the manuscript. The cached file also contains complete §VIII; it is not
silently attributed to XXXII.

The full source additionally says the Moon tempers solar heat with cold
humour, and other planets assist/moderate the Sun's fire. Those are different
relations. The text does not supply a modern theory of photon reflection,
energy conservation or an entirely causally independent self-luminous Sun.
No conservation of a particular light portion is imposed on the relay.
Hildegard is a source for this possible relation, not an identified plaintext;
the proposal does not claim to express every detail of the complete section.
