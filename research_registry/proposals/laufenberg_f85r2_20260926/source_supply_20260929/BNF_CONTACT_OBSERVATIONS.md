## Positive source contrast

The producer independently found BL Harley 4751 f14v; root then inspected the
entire saved official image. The official BL digitised-manuscript list dates
the codex late twelfth–early thirteenth century (other catalogues give narrower,
later dates; the institutional date is attributed rather than reconciled here).
The rubric is `Caprea`. A bowman faces an upper quadruped with an arrow in its
body. A lower arrow-bearing quadruped bends its mouth to a green plant.
The immediately following text includes, with abbreviation expanded:

> Herbas ruminat. vulnerata ad dictamnu[m] currit. qua tacta sanatur.

Working sense: it ruminates herbs; when wounded it runs to dittany, and is
healed by touching it. This is an actual written and pictorial wounded-animal
treatment relation. It does **not** independently show the weapon falling out,
and the touching clause must not be replaced with Pliny's ingestion clause.
The preceding generic rumination sentence does not eliminate that distinction.
The two painted animals may present successive states; they are not evidence
for two independent clinical observations.

Primary locators: [BL catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002050594),
[BL date listing](https://www.bl.uk/collection/digitised-manuscripts-archives),

## Consequence for the already exposed f25v

Root re-opened only the previously admitted 1500-pixel f25v image. Its small
quadruped's snout meets a green leaf point. At that supplied scale no separate
arrow, wound, hunter or opposing animal is identifiable. Species, swallowing
and temporal sequence remain uncertain. Therefore:

- animal-as-bite/danger cue is a concrete historical possibility (Verbena);
- animal-as-patient seeking the plant is also concrete (Harley Caprea);
- contact by itself cannot supply before/after, benefit/harm, species or plant
  name. The fuller Harley narrative has features currently absent on f25v.

This positively supplies alternative argument structures for exploratory
reading. It does not select either one on f25v, reopen GDT1092/1093's fixed
owner tests, or translate any word. Next, any proposed whole-passage reading
must make the actor/patient/danger distinction explicit and account for the
actual repeated forms; assigning an animal name to a convenient rare group
would not solve that problem.
