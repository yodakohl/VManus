# FT historical content: named parts, flow clauses and local reference

The useful source consequence is **content-preserving local rebinding**, not a
generic rule that short labels are names or instructions. On the same medieval
page, one short expression names a part, another sentence identifies a local
source and what flows from it, while the same short relational expression occurs
at multiple other places. A full account must specify what each occurrence
copies and which local argument replaces the earlier one. It cannot transfer
one arbitrary procedure everywhere merely because the short expression repeats.

No Voynich word value is supplied here. Figure/anatomy, apparatus and procedural
accounts remain rivals. Neither this historical graph nor familiar Latin
letters select one of them.

## Actual prose on that page: a diagnostic sequence, not a connector legend

The detached left prose block next to the small-circle inscription was viewed
in its full extent. Its first self-contained test is readable as a complete
sequence: to determine whether a woman is pregnant, take garlic, have the woman
place it in the matrix overnight, and the following morning treat perceived
garlic odor in her mouth as a sign that she is not pregnant. The source asserts
this historical diagnostic relation; no physiological efficacy is endorsed.

Its minimal argument graph is:

```text
same woman W
garlic A -> overnight placement at bodily site M(W)
next-morning observation of odor of A at mouth(W)
perceived odor -> asserted nonpregnancy of W
```

The input site, output-observation site, overnight interval, next-morning
observation, conditional evidence and negative diagnosis all matter. Replacing
this with a word list of woman/garlic/womb/mouth or a generic preparation recipe
loses the instruction's discriminating claim. The prescribed action and the
diagnostic condition also cannot be made into a static part-name caption.

The same prose block continues with a **second** test. Overnight holding in the
mouth, morning inspection, a condition involving swollen eyes and a negative
conception conclusion are legible, but the held material/amount and some exact
words remain unresolved. They are retained in the JSON as unknowns. The first
test is a complete self-contained sentence; the **whole two-test block is not
a fully recovered diplomatic or semantic account**. No second route, inverse
result, missing material or modern Hippocratic wording is installed to repair
it. The readable first sentence is enough for its distinctive input/output
argument consequence; the second cannot silently become the same operation.

This matters for the proposed f83 work: physical adjacency of the prose to a
circle bearing local reference does not establish that the circle refers to
this diagnostic action. The original-diagram inscriptions and the surrounding
prose have visibly different writing/layout. Dating their relative production
or proving authorial ownership requires more evidence; this audit supplies
neither. A physiological diagram may share a page with practical tests without
the prose explaining every drawn path or every label narrating that prose.

## Explicit joint graph hypotheses and their different consequences

Let Rcap/Lcap be the outer cap places; Rnode/Lnode the small circles; and
Rband/Lband the upper red-band places. The natural paired reading, fixed here
as a **source interpretation hypothesis**, is:

```text
Ccap = PartType(Rcap, U)                   [U: unresolved full historical term]
Cnode = FlowFrom(seed, Rnode) + LocalSourceKind(Rnode, T)
Cband = P(Rband, state)                   [P and full state relation unread]

HereLikewise(Lcap, Ccap)  -> PartType(Lcap, U)
HereLikewise(Lnode,Cnode) -> FlowFrom(seed,Lnode) + LocalSourceKind(Lnode,T)
HereLikewise(Lband,Cband) -> P(Lband,state)
```

This does **not** identify Rcap with Lcap or Rnode with Lnode as one physical
object. It preserves the supplied description/relation while changing the
local place argument. Nor does it identify U, T, seed and blood with each
other. The same relational expression has a common semantic function with
different contextual content arguments, rather than three unrelated lexical
meanings. Cband remains a hole in the source graph and cannot license a new
state-transition story.

The graph exposes a distinctive multitem constraint: **a transferred flow
description must retain the content argument and flow relation, whereas a
transferred nominal description supplies a part type without asserting that
flow.** A rival treating every short expression as a fresh component name
cannot produce that same source graph without adding the missing relational
construction. A rival treating every recurrence as the same copied diagnostic
procedure likewise fails to preserve the three differing contextual arguments.
These are falsifiers of those particular *historical interpretations*, not
new Voynich falsifications.

For any complete exploratory Voynich account claiming this sort of mixed
caption/prose practice, a meaningful construction would explicitly state, for
each purported local reference, its antecedent content, preserved predicate
and nonlocal arguments, rebound local argument and actual prose consumer.
That account may hypothesize unconfirmed values. It must nevertheless expose
its own complete relations instead of citing Ashmole as a license to move any
unread operation across text. This packet assigns no target caption or prose
group to one of the source roles.

## Existing circuit examples and tempting matches rejected

The complete existing Vitruvius IX.8.9 clock paragraph has water raising a
float, counterweight descent, axle/wheel transmission and a distinct month-index
description. It proves that an intelligible systems account can require
several parts and different event relations. Its ancient work/later English
translation, inherited apparatus declarations and uncollated Latin prevent
it from supplying this task's native medieval mixed-caption example. The
source does not make each month-hole a different machine component, and it
does not independently fix the calendar index actuator. Its old C0 word
assignments are excluded from this packet.

GDT214's al-Jazari folio is a real medieval mechanical design, with repeated
lifting bays under shared drive and apparent compact indices. Its source audit
read no actual mark value; GDT1032 deliberately left the changed-hash images
closed. Neither report supplies a complete native caption/prose relation, so
their modern apparatus description is not substituted for one here. This audit
did not reacquire those images or reopen that fixed experiment.

Rejected: copying the three Ashmole relational expressions into three target
labels by count; giving every rounded shape the same anatomical name; taking
the top human as proof that all following prose concerns a pregnant case;
using seed/blood/route vocabulary to fill target words; treating region symmetry
as an explicit directed-flow arrow; reading a nominal part label as a procedure
heading; importing the garlic test into the diagram's relational inscriptions;
or assuming the Voynich must reproduce this page's Latin, glyphs or content.

The exact source limit remains useful: two natural mixed-function pairs can be
written as distinct conditional graphs, but the upper-band predicate and parts
of the second prose test are unresolved. A complete page-wide explanatory graph
has **not** been recovered. This limit is not a claim that no historical example
exists. No new generic label corpus or decoder follows. The strongest retained
consequence is argument-preserving local rebinding; target meanings and domain
selection remain open, with0 confirmed words.
