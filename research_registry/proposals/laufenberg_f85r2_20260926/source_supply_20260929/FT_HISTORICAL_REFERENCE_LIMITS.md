## One complete C0 construction template

This is an explicitly invented exploratory construction, not a transcription,
word assignment, parser or recovered historical convention. Its purpose is to
make a later clause require genuinely different arguments under three caption
interpretations. No letters, target words or physical reading order are fixed.

The entire schematic context has five contributions, all retained:

1. A prior complete clause states a conveyance from source-place A, of material
   kind M, along route K to recipient D. It introduces an actual example portion
   m:M. Denote its content by `C_A = Convey(A,M,K,D)`.
2. A second source-place B is explicitly introduced, with `B != A`.
3. A caption X is explicitly owned by B. This ownership is a paid construction
   assumption; source proximity alone does not establish it for any target.
4. A later clause explicitly introduces a portion y of material kind N at B,
   with `N != M`. Kind incompatibility is written in the hypothetical account,
   not inferred from the drawing. Mere inequality of two portions would not
   suffice, since two different portions can have the same kind.
5. A complete later consumer cites X and states an actual conveyance of y from
   B along K to D. Its construction must say whether X supplies the physical
   source, copied content, or an invoked procedure. No additional source,
   material, route, recipient or operation is inserted.

The three full construction interpretations are:

| Caption interpretation | Explicit caption construction | Later consumer's argument obligation | Outcome with the written N≠M |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal component | `Name(X,B)` | The X occurrence resolves to physical source B. `Convey(e,B,y:N,K,D)` uses the explicitly introduced y. Caption naming alone contributes no preserved M argument. | Compatible. It must retain B as the actual source; an event name cannot silently occupy that source argument. |
| Relational caption preserving the prior material kind | `HereLikewise(B,C_A)` copies `Convey(B,M,K,D)` | The consumer realizes that copied description: its material argument must be of kind M, while B replaces A and K/D remain. This is a paid realization construction; descriptive reference alone does not create an event. | Contradicted by y:N with N≠M. No new portion-identity assumption is needed. |
| Named conveyance procedure | `ProcedureName(X,H)` with explicit `H(s,x,k,d) = convey x from s along k to d` | The consumer must invoke H with separately written arguments `s=B, x=y:N, k=K, d=D`; H is a method, not a physical source. The signature abstracts the material parameter instead of copying fixed M. | Compatible if that parameterized method introduction and invocation are paid. A title alone does not execute H or supply y. |

For the nominal construction, X is an argument selecting a component. For the
relational construction, X supplies a proposition whose fixed kind argument
must survive. For the procedure construction, X selects a parameterized method
whose invocation must consume the new material argument. These are different
semantic obligations even when the compatible nominal/procedure accounts
describe the same final transport event.

The procedure abstraction, local-reference realization and component-selection
construction are each additional assumptions of their respective C0 account.
None is free historical support. In particular, Ashmole's likewise caption
does not prove that all contextual arguments are immutable: a different
predeclared reference that transfers only the **relation schema**, abstracting
its material, would survive here like the method account. It is a distinct
rival requiring explicit scope, not a repair allowed after observing N≠M.

This template is complete at the stated conceptual scope, not a complete f83
reading. A future target account must assign every actual group or mark it
unpaid and instantiate the five contributions without invented word values
from this packet. If it never expresses an incompatible new kind, then the
template's distinguishing prediction is not tested. If the final clause never
binds its cited caption to a physical source, a copied proposition or an invoked
method, the consumer remains missing; proximity does not close it.

## What the template can and cannot decide

The decision-changing observation would be a fully derived later consumer that
introduces N≠M while claiming to realize the fixed-kind copied content. That
rejects the particular fixed-kind reference construction. A consumer requiring
a physical source constrains the nominal referent; one explicitly invoking a
method with a separately written input constrains the procedure interpretation.
These require reading the whole account, not counting captions.

The template does **not** automatically choose nominal component over procedure:
both compatible versions above produce the same transport history. Their
distinct typed consumer obligations must themselves be constrained by the
writing to rank them. Nor does it identify anatomy over apparatus: either
domain can supply a source, route, material and recipient. It does not establish
that any existing f83 clause supplies M, N, K, D or the needed inequality.

Source-supported knowledge is limited to mixed nominal/relational captions,
explicit flow/content roles in a provisional source clause, and the need to
retain unresolved antecedent choice. The five-part construction and its
fixed-kind transfer/parameterized-method distinction are new explicit C0
hypotheses for potential testing. No target selection, confirmed word,
independent meaning result or reopening of GDT1032/1043 follows.
