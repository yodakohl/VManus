## 2. Plant: recurrent phase versus life-stage termination

The complete **Pliny, Naturalis historia XIII.107–110** herb/root account was acquired in Latin from the [electronic edition](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Pliny_the_Elder/13*.html#107). This is ancient source text in a modern electronic edition. No dated medieval illustration, manuscript collation or modern taxon identification was obtained.

The entry itself distinguishes the preceding same-named tree from the herb; bean-like stem/leaves, poppy-like heads and millet-like grains are comparisons, not three literal species. Its full sequence is retained:

1. Egyptian wet-ground growth after the Nile recedes; stem/leaves, head and internal grain morphology.
2. Heads rot in heaps; grains are separated by washing, dried, pounded and used for bread.
3. At sunset the heads close and are covered by leaves; at sunrise they open **until maturation and the white flower's fall**.
4. A separately attributed **Euphrates** report adds sinking of head and flower until midnight, gradual rising and sunrise emergence/opening. It is not silently imposed on every Egyptian plant.
5. The root has a dark rind and white interior; it is eaten raw or cooked in water/embers, and its peelings feed pigs. Root, seed and flower are distinct roles.

The content mechanism is more specific than an unspecified state change: the narrated daily return cycle is bounded by maturation **and** white-flower fall. A writer must declare the carrier across successive phases and separately represent the life-stage boundary. The source uses plural heads; it does not count all later heads or generations. A strict rule denying every opening after maturity alone would be an additional C0 assumption, not a direct source result. If the specific complete Pliny account is proposed, head-to-grain processing and root food also become obligations; **they are not necessary consequences of a generic phase-cycle hypothesis**.
