## One concrete alternative: persistence after release

The preserved typed text has `teneeant` and `af fici`; these are not silently promoted into a fresh diplomatic transcription. The source's report frame, “some say,” must remain attached.

This is a general attributed conditional claim, not a witnessed patient's clinical timeline or proof of efficacy. Its important content is the difference between temporary suppression and completed cure. It does not depend on deciding schor, identifying a target plant, or translating a target word as pain.

**The sole proposed minimal test** is a release-consequence contrast on one complete already admitted target unit, if that unit independently supplies the required patient, herb/contact relation and pain state. Freeze the identity of the scorpion-injured patient P, the herb H and the symptom D across both conditions. Do not merge the ingestion recipe with the holding claim.

| Fixed competing meaning | While H is held | After H is released, with no new sting or other new cause |
|---|---|---|
| R: relief maintained by continuing contact | P does not feel D | D returns for the same P |
| C: completed durable cure from the contact event | P does not feel D | P remains free of D; release alone does not restore it |

The decisive recorded observation would be **the same patient's pain returning specifically on release of the same herb**. That is actually asserted in the external source: the `deposita ... dolore af fici` clause opposes durable-cure C for this reported source claim. It is more informative than mere co-occurrence of a plant and illness, and unlike an arbitrary dictionary fit, the losing prediction is fixed before any target lexical assignment. No English/German labels, temporal transitions or variable bindings may be hidden in a one-word phrase wrapper: P, H, the sting condition, contact, release, pain polarity, same-participant binding and the attribution scope are separate semantic obligations.
