## Three registered relations:actual words and consequences

1. **Operation reprise before rationale.** Page216 sequence is `deinde iterum
   in illa decoquimus ut tener fiat:non excoct[us?] [quum?] subductionis gratia
   sumitur:volumus enim non deponere...`. There is **no separate quoted-operation
   reprise between this prohibition/condition and its immediate volumus enim
   rationale**. The later `sicut dixi in instanti` belongs to the expanded
   vegetables/replacement-water passage, not this local position. Latin
   `coctus`, `decoquimus`, `non excoct...`, `bis coctus`, `elixata`, `bis lixatur`
   and `decoxeris` are distinct written forms; treating them as one recurring
   targetword or moving the later reprise is an additional author choice.

2. **Former/replacement water.** The actual local wording is `priorem aquam
   effundentes immittimus mox alteri calide deinde iterum in illa decoquimus`.
   The new water is grammatically elliptical after `alteri calide`; do not
   silently add another printed aquam. The later instruction actually writes
   `aquam calidam preparatam extrahentes ex priori transmutare mox in illa`.
   This is a concrete same-source former/other/pronominal chain, not freely
   exchangeable whole-word labels. Which Voynich native forms, if any, produce
   both water objects and carry their later consumers remains unbound in1138.

3. **Intrinsic-juice ownership/inability/comparative loss.** The source writes
   `omnem proprium ipsius succum` then `conservare quantum possibile est maxime`,
   followed by `conservare proprium succum nullum la[cha?]norum potest magis autem
   abiciunt:que usque ad plurimum elixata`. The owner is explicit ipsius in the
   first local occurrence, proprium recurs in the inability clause, and the
   subsequent comparative loss inherits rather than renames that object.
   The uncertain vegetable noun expansion does not authorize stripping the
   actual succum/proprium repetition or inventing new ownership.
