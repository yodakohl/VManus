## II.162, Ptarmike — complete critical entry

162 πταρμικὴ θαμνίον ἐστί, κλῶνας ἔχον μικρούς, πολλούς, περιφερεῖς, ὁμοίους ἁβροτόνῳ, περὶ οὓς φύλλα ἐπιμήκη, ἐοικότα τοῖς τῆς ἐλαίας, πολλά· ἐπ᾿ ἄκρῳ δὲ ὥσπερ ἀνθεμίδος κεφάλιον μικρόν, περιφερές, δριμὺ κατὰ τὴν ὀσμήν, ἐρεθιστικὸν πταρμοῦ, ὅθεν καὶ ὠνόμασται.

δύναμιν δὲ ἔχει καταπλασσόμενα τὰ φύλλα μετὰ τῶν ἀνθῶν ὑπώπια αἴρειν. τὰ δὲ ἄνθη πταρμοὺς ἐνεργέστατα κινεῖ, φύεται δὲ ἐν ὀρεινοῖς καὶ πετρώδεσι τόποις.

### Working translation

Ptarmike is a small shrub, with many small, round branches like abrotonon; around them are many elongated leaves like those of elaia. At the top is a small, round head like that of anthemis, sharp in smell and provoking sneezing, whence its name. Its leaves, applied as a poultice with the flowers, remove bruising below the eyes. The flowers provoke sneezing very effectively. It grows in mountainous and rocky places. This translates the historical account; it is not a present-day treatment claim.

### Entry boundary and apparatus

The source XML incorrectly nests the chapter164 element and the following sections2,3,4 inside a chapter112 element. Selecting only chapter164 would retain just its first paragraph. The extraction script explicitly includes all four blocks and stops before chapter165, Kyklaminos hetera. Its final storage, burning/pastille and habitat clauses are retained. The XML page/section numerals and apparent transcription defects remain as supplied; no silent spelling emendation is made. Eight apparatus notes, including the RV synonym list, are separate from the four main paragraphs in the JSON. The complete text is consequently larger than the opening morphological description.

### Complete passage synopsis, without conflating relations

Section1 describes leaves, stalk, flowers and root, then assigns drinking, local application, stepping across or wearing the root, and wine-mixture uses. Section2 gives a measured drink and warm-room/wrapping/sweating sequence, then several different applications of the juice. Section3 explains obtaining and thickening the juice, and additional external uses and decoction/oil preparations. Section4 hollows the root, fills it with oil and warms it on hot ashes, with optional wax; it then describes storing the cut root as squill is stored, burning and forming pastilles, and its shaded habitat. These historical uses are not inferred from resemblance to ivy, rose or turnip.
