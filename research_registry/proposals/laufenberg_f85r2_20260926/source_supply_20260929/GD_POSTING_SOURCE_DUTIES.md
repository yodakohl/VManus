## What the marks mean

**One role-parameterized POSTED predicate is fair shorthand** for the common
Journal→Ledger transfer tracked by both marks. Both are strokes on the **Journal**:
the debit stroke records transfer to Ledger debit; the credit stroke records
transfer to Ledger credit. They are not respectively Journal-entry and Ledger-
entry stages. Their distinct arguments are the transaction's debit and credit
roles, not two independent scalar values.

Nevertheless, writing a Ledger entry and marking the Journal are separate
acts. A faithful action reading must not let a marking instruction automatically
create an absent Ledger entry. A literal marker proves that the marker is
present; its intended certification of a completed transfer presupposes correct
execution. A model may use POSTED as a recorded completion flag only if it states
that convention and distinguishes a false/misapplied certificate from a correct
posting. No single global DONE flag substitutes for the two obligations.

6. After Ledger entries and Journal marks, make the index entry immediately:
   stones under G or Z according to Gioie pronunciation (Tuscany/Venice).
   These are alternative indexing initials, not another paired debit/credit act.

Chapter14 permits credit transfer at the same time or later. Close placement
is advice rather than an invariant; the source supplies order without a fixed
interval. The full Journal description licenses abbreviated Ledger prose.
Date ditto needs its stated owner, not an arbitrary nearest word or transaction.
Both Ledger `per` phrases introduce the counteraccount; the Journal's use of
`per` for debtor does not extend indiscriminately to both books/sides.

## Shared identity and strongest countercases

Correct completion preserves one Journal transaction, date and value, with
opposed host/side assignments: stones3/debit→capital2, capital2/credit→stones3.
The Journal3/2 and account index must resolve these same pages. Accounting
information identity does not entail physical stock transport or conservation.

* **Equal amounts, wrong account:** substituting another credited account for
  capital can preserve debit/credit totals40 yet break the written counteraccount.
  Balance is necessary under the rule, not sufficient for correct ownership.
* **Both marked, wrong reference:** two strokes can coexist with swapped Journal
 2/3, a wrong Ledger counterpage, or wrong index owner. Flags alone do not validate
  the reference network. A correct counteraccount name with a wrong address is
  still inconsistent with the declared example.
* **Only debit marked:** the credit posting remains unrecorded by its stroke;
  a temporary one-sided posting is permitted. Neither date/value agreement nor
  one common DONE flag supplies the second completed transfer.
* **Posting versus settlement:** cancelling the Journal item tracks copying into
  the Ledger, not cancelling a debt, paying an obligation, liquidating capital,
  selling stones or closing all accounts. None of those economic events is
  established by the stones posting example or its marks.

Thus a small future candidate can fairly reuse one POSTED contribution across
two explicit roles, but success would owe the original transaction owner,
separate acts, reciprocal references and any claimed index consumer. The source
does not supply manuscript account names, values, dates, boundaries or consumers.
Omitting the teaching prose while claiming to translate this entire source item
would also exceed the evidence. No meaning test or experiment was selected here.
