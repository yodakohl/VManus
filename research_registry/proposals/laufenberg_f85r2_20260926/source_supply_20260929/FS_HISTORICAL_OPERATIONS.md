## Actual source contexts

**Theophilus, I.1–9.** All nine complete Latin chapters were read in the cached
Hendrie1847 packet, including complexion alternatives, loci and the conditional
last highlight. This is an edition of a medieval work, not a new diplomatic
collation of a medieval witness. I.1 heats dry lead white until yellow, then
grinds and compounds flesh paint. I.2 dissolves/strains prasinus. I.3 makes posc
from membrina after the initial application; I.4 and I.5 explicitly use simple
membrina to make rosa and lumina. I.6 makes veneda, uses it on pupils, adds more
white to that color, uses it again around the eyes, adds plain white between
regions and washes. I.7 returns to the earlier posc. I.8 modifies rosa and
requires the earlier rosa to remain visible; I.9 conditionally modifies lumina
and draws fine strokes over its predecessor.

The strongest anchors are brief and distinguish different obligations:

- I.6: `Adde ei etiam de albo amplius` — addition follows the pupil application.
  [Complete chapter](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost12/Theophilus/the_1006.html).
- I.7: `posc, de quo supra dictum est` — the older named recipe is retrieved
  across intervening preparations.
  [Complete chapter](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost12/Theophilus/the_1007.html).
- I.8: `ita ut anterior superius inferiusque pareat` — the earlier application
  must still show above and below the later one.
  [Complete chapter](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost12/Theophilus/the_1008.html).

These are different material and reference constraints. Returning to a recipe
type does not prove return to the same physical vessel or a separately stored
portion. The chapters' explicit simple-membrina references defeat a universal
latest-mixture rule. Their named preparations do not specify all stock amounts,
binder details or storage vessels.

**Cennini, complete LXII.** The existing native1821 review covers the whole
chapter, pp49–53, and controls the OCR packet. Lapis is prepared in a pastello;
successive extractions go into separate bowls while the same pastello remains
available. Only after the ordered bowls have been individually re-stirred and
assessed are chosen fractions pooled. Sediment settling is expressly described.
The dried grades are stored separately; the maker later takes the required
amount and grinds it according to use. The optional color-correction branch
prepares another mixture before incorporating it into the blue. This is an
actual staged sequence with retained intermediate materials, rather than a
one-pot succession that consumes every preceding object.

The source's printed `non` in the18-bowl/3-grade/6-bowl sentence remains
unresolved. No exact18=3×6 partition, numerical quality scale or universal
mixing prohibition follows. See the complete existing
`CENNINI_LXII_NATIVE_SEPARATE_FRACTIONS_REVIEW_20260922.md` and the hash-bound
source offer; the existing GDT1005 literal-record writers remain closed.

**Medieval medicinal and alchemical contexts.** The complete Sloane73
gold-influence and gold/silver-separation spans were read from the existing
Furnivall packet and checked against its public edition. Successive quenched
waters must remain available for later pooling, then mixture with quintessence;
the florin itself persists. The silver/gold operation first employs one reagent
state, then optionally adds sal ammoniac to change the stated action on gold.
The sequence matters even without a depicted object. These are historical
assertions, not experimentally verified chemistry.
[Complete edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/17179/pg17179-images.html).

The whole existing Sloane2435 age chapter was read, not merely its four-age
picture or qualities. Its electuary instructions grind ingredients, combine
them with honey or sugar, make a named preparation and later take fasting
portions. Regimen, dose, conditional purging and subsequent daily routine are
different operations. The reading remains a provisional native sense account;
the1911 edition is a disclosed aid from other witnesses.
[Comparison edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_R%C3%A9gime_du_corps/Texte_entier).

The complete existing Kyklaminos critical entry similarly distinguishes root,
expressed/thickened juice, oil preparation and optional wax before topical use.
Its full text is a modern critical edition; the cached medieval image verifies
only the opening section, not the whole continuation. Neither electuary nor
Kyklaminos context supplies the earlier-layer visibility relation of I.8.
The Trotula IV15/V19 source packet supplies grinding, tempering and a later
dose, with explicit uncertainty in the dose referent/participial scope and no
proof that1544 wording was already present in1420. GDT1000 remains closed.

These bounded sources suffice for the operation question. They are not a survey
establishing what all medieval housebooks or medical recipes contain.

## Two content accounts and the present selection limit

A staged paint account predicts that L31 lacks the still-pending I26 whereas
later material from M27 can contain it. A one-pot account claiming all announced
ingredients are already combined before the first application predicts the
opposite composition for L31. Those accounts are genuinely different: the FR
annotation chooses the former by stipulation. The historical sources explain
why that difference matters, but no independent target fact in this audit tells
which composition or announcement interpretation is intended. The latter is
incompatible with FR's frozen rules, not thereby disproved as Voynich content.

A painted-image account with an earlier application deliberately left visible
differs from a medicinal topical account in which application is followed by a
therapeutic effect. The sampled medical sources establish that mixing, portions,
body loci and subsequent treatment are insufficient to choose between these
domains. Theophilus's explicit visibility relation is a potentially stronger
discriminator, but FR authored its target visibility meanings. Thus this audit
selects neither domain and proposes no newly glossed words.

Retain **C1 unresolved**: FR has no paid, bound return to a named earlier recipe
across intervening preparations. Retain **C2 unresolved**: FR's typed surface
owners and visibility claims are assumptions, not identified substrate/image
or earlier-layer meanings. A generic recipe need not describe a picture; these
are obligations only for the proposed particular painting account.

Exact outcome: the historical comparison supports testing material identity and
operation order, while leaving FR's lexical and domain selection unchanged.
No further historical corpus or decoder is justified by this audit alone. Any
next selection must first identify an existing target constraint that differs
between the two accounts without repairing the frozen unknown groups.
