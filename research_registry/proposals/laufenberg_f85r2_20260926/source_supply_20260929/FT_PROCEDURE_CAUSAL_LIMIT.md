# FT frozen B: causal limit of the paired assay

This is an explanatory audit of [frozen Author B](FT_AUTHOR_PROCEDURE.md), not a semantic repair. **The same p and an uncleared return observer do not establish two origin-specific arrivals.** Two distinct invocation records and two positive readout assertions can coexist with only one actual arrival. The exact frozen meanings, rules and hashes remain unchanged.

## Smallest persistence countercase

Frozen R closes the nonselected origin, releases the selected-origin and return gates, places p and waits Delta. CHEOL separately asserts perceived arrival of p's sensory marker at D. A25 distinguishes that marker from displacement of the whole physical portion. LOL reuses p, D and Delta, making e2 and its stated readout; R contains no step that clears a retained marker from D between these calls.

Start with D clear, an open A-to-D path, and an obstructed downstream B-to-D path. The B entrance can be opened while that downstream obstruction remains: opening an entrance does not guarantee complete route communication. Let the first marker remain perceptible through the second waiting interval. These are hypothetical world conditions permitted by the frozen protocol, not new meanings or a claim about the actual depicted apparatus.

| Call | Frozen actions | New arrival from selected origin | Marker perceived at D | Actual provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e1 at A | Close B, release A/D gates, place p, wait Delta | Yes | Yes | e1 via A |
| e2 at B | Close A, release B/D gates, place the same p, wait Delta | No; downstream B route remains obstructed | Yes; the first marker persists | e1 via A only |

The minimal relation is `PresentAfter(D) = ResidualBefore(D) OR NewArrival(D)`. For e2, residual is true and new arrival is false. Neither a second placement nor a second wait clears the residual. No removal effect is supplied by CHEOL. No washout, reset, origin-specific marker or event-tagging rule is being added.

## Event index is not physical provenance

B's exact assertion `u2 = ObservedArrival(e2,p,D)` is retained; this audit does not replace CHEOL with a new “presence” gloss. The observer in the countercase perceives MarkerOf(p) at both read times, but that signal was produced by e1. Assigning the second perception to an e2 record does not physically tag its source.

If Arrival entails a fresh arrival caused by e2, the assertion is false in this countercase. If the statement reports a perceived arrival attributed to the selected call, the attribution can be mistaken. The mere positive sensory observation establishes neither causal interpretation. Distinct attempts and reports remain distinct even when their signal provenance is shared.

Q1's DAIIIN/CHEDY and Q2's LDALOR/CTHOL therefore state an origin-pair conclusion under the guessed semantic story; they do not establish it through this observer. DAIN/CHEY preserve the source input identity/kind. The same p makes MarkerOf(p) less useful for distinguishing origins. Equal waiting intervals do not establish a new onset, and Q2's CHEOL refers to the same u2 rather than adding an independent observation. A first/second selector discriminates written event references, not causal signal sources.

This is a capacity limit of the proposed procedure. It is not, by itself, a lexical contradiction. A historical text could prescribe an unreliable test or assert an invalid inference. The [historical content packet](FT_HISTORICAL_CONTENT.md) itself describes an asserted garlic placement/odor/diagnosis relation and endorses no physiological efficacy. That example supplies no washout or provenance constraint for B. Requiring a sound diagnostic procedure would reject this frozen causal account; allowing an unvalidated historical instruction would leave it possible as an exploratory text hypothesis. Neither option identifies a word.
