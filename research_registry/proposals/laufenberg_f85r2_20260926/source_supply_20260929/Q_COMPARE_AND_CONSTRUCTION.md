### Does a precise DRAW/ATTRACT relation improve this?

It is a useful explicit rival to overbroad INFLUENCE, but the owned source
does not justify treating the two as synonyms. E Q1 says named stars give
influence to Gerapigra Galieni and **the drug draws away humours** from
head/neck/breast, not lower members. Its separate lower-region medicines have
different stellar associations. That complete text distinguishes two verbs
and participant structures; it does not say that the Sun draws that medicine.
E Q3 separately says burning water draws virtues out of gold. Selecting that
recipe would change the source account and incur its own disclosed duties.

A bounded check within the already acquired Hildegard HTML found explicit
drawing in complete §§VIII and XXXII, preserved in
`Q_OWNED_CELESTIAL_DRAW_PASSAGES.txt` with following headings marked. VIII
has certain stars drawing clouds up, then releasing them downward when the
stars descend. XXXII has the Sun attracting **cold** on its westward part of
the course, alongside a much longer account of solar/lunar regulation. These
are precise celestial relations, but neither is the desired unqualified
Sun-draws-water counterpart. No new external source was acquired and no
absence claim about medieval solar attraction follows.

The forward relay under DRAW would require the thing drawn in the ring to
become the drawer in the prose. Alternatively the passive rival could make
the same proposed fluid class patient of both an astral and a medicinal
actor. Both are intelligible C0 stories; neither is selected by the present
writing. Water, humour and general substance are distinct candidate values,
not interchangeable convenient glosses. DRAW must not mean illumination in
one place, generic influence in another and extraction in a third.
