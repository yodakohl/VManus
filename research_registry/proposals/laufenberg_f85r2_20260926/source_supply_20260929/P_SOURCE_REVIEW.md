## Complete local relationship inventory

The prose first specifies six adjacent concentric layers: lucid fire, black
fire, pure ether, watery air, strong white luminous air and thin air. Some
adjacent pairs visually appear as one; the statement is not simply six drawn
rings. A north-side line runs from the eastern to the western part and divides
the northern region. A globe and a human are contained within the wheel, which
is itself in the chest of a much larger figure. The human's extremities reach
the specified strong white luminous air circle.

Four main head occurrences are owned separately: leopard above the human's
head in pure ether; wolf below the feet in watery air; lion on the human's
right in lucid fire; bear on the human's left in black fire. Their mouths
produce paired subordinate head occurrences: leopard → crab/deer, wolf →
deer/crab, lion → snake/lamb, bear → lamb/snake. Right/left in these clauses
refers to each mouth and must not be silently converted into a modern compass
orientation. Secondary breaths reach inter-head midpoints, with explicit
meeting relations in the lion clauses. All heads blow into the wheel and
toward the human. Thus there are twelve occurrences of six animal types,
not twelve distinct animal names. XVI states that actual winds are not
literally animal-shaped; the depicted forms imitate the animals' powers.

## Three positive solar contacts, bear exclusion, and moral scope

§I explicitly distinguishes touching leopard/lion/wolf from not touching bear.
§XI describes the Sun's path as avoiding the northern region, then gives a
moral interpretation. §XVI identifies the heads as winds symbolized through
animal powers, then as situations confronting a person. These are not merely
modern physical diagrams supplied with zoological labels.

§XXXV explains the Sun as the spirit of fortitude, with its three positive
contacts tied to fear of God, divine judgment and infernal punishment. The
bear exclusion concerns bodily affliction that is not rightly for God,
including indiscreet self-affliction and affliction imposed by others. The
same complete paragraph allows disciplined, rightly intended bodily contrition
and says fortitude operates there. Thus the source does **not** prohibit all
bodily affliction or claim that fortitude can never accompany it. A writer
selecting the moral account inherits this conditional qualification; a writer
selecting only the physical §I account does not automatically inherit every
exegetical correspondence.
