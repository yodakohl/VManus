## Actual local text and uncertainty

The following is an expanded working reading of the **whole southern comparative pair**, not a quotation from a modern edition. Punctuation and capitalization are editorial; square brackets mark uncertain abbreviation expansion or letters. Cardinal direction and participant roles in this pair are readable. No missing operation argument has been supplied from a conventional wind table.

> Et orientalis refluxio transmi[ssa?] versus terram meridiei, quando maior fuit occidentali, violenter impellit occidentem, et sic collateralis fit allisio in terram meridiei a parte occidentis, et sic nascitur quidam ventus qui dicitur affricus. Si vero occidentalis fuerit maior, violenter impellit orientem, et sic collateralis fit allisio in terram meridiei a parte orientis, et sic nascitur quidam ventus qui dicitur not[h]us.

Translation of that reading: the eastern reflux, transmitted toward the southern land, when stronger than the western, violently pushes the western; a lateral impact occurs on the southern land on the western side, and a wind called Affricus arises. If the western is stronger, it violently pushes the eastern; the lateral impact is on the southern land on the eastern side, producing a wind called Nothus.

The immediately preceding f129ra context explicitly distinguishes eight collateral winds and says they arise from collateral impact of the previously described refluxes. Its recoverable sentence is:

> Sunt alii venti collaterales VIII qui ex collaterali allisione praedictarum refluxionum proveniunt.

The f128vb surrounding account identifies the material as sea/water reflux, compares its quantities, and describes the eastern and western refluxes and their division toward the southern and northern lands. Thus this is external wind generation, not the bodily flatus discussed elsewhere in the medical work. The local pair names its own southern owner and both operands; one need not infer an owner from image proximity. The wider cosmological opening has not been fully transcribed or critically established here; it must not be represented as a complete edition.
