The seven-line local prose block runs from the Ptarmike heading to the habitat clause just above the next Pelekinos drawing. The text above Ptarmike's picture is the previous Pepon entry. The visible local comparative wording and its ownership agree at the relation level with the critical entry. Minor word order and verb-form identity have **not** been diplomatically collated; quotations in the table are from the edition, not silently passed off as a manuscript transcription.

The rest of this short entry matters. It attributes one application to **leaves with flowers** and another effect to **flowers**; neither Abrotonon, Elaia nor Anthemis is an ingredient in the medicine. Its plant name is explained by the sneezing effect. The drawing also has a basal trunk/root formation although no root description appears in this entry. Thus illustration need not exhaustively encode only the explicit textual parts.

A second named source image provides a limited comparison: [M.652 f247r, Elaia](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/455/143825), [whole page](H_MORGAN_M652_247r_FULL.jpg). The upper prose on f247r belongs to the preceding Ebenos entry, not the pictured Elaia; it is not used as Elaia text evidence.

## Kyklaminos: named organ analogues do not ensure all analogous organs appear

Primary text: [complete II.164](H_COMPLETE_ENTRIES.md); dated image and beginning: [M.652 f72v](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/129/143825), [whole page](H_MORGAN_M652_072v_FULL_HI.jpg).

| Part owned by Kyklaminos | Textual relation | Whole-page observation |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves | like kissos/ivy; separately speckled above and below | No clearly broad, lobed ivy-like leaf blades are visible. Thin bent axes end in narrow reddish forms. Their identity as buds or reduced leaves is not forced. |
| Flowers | ῥοδοειδῆ, rose-like, and purplish | Three open purplish, lobed flower forms are visible. Calling these copied rose motifs would exceed the evidence. |
| Root | black, like gongylis/turnip, somewhat flattened | A large dark, horizontally broadened basal body with thin radiating rootlets is visible. Its agreement with black/flattened is clearer than any named turnip identity. |
| Cut root in storage, later in the entry | stored as skilla/squill | This is a processing analogy, not a fourth anatomical donor. No cutting/storage action is drawn on f72v. |

Here only ivy and turnip occur as independent named comparator nouns; rose is encoded by the compound adjective rose-like. It should not be counted as an identical syntactic construction. The complete entry has many additional preparation/application clauses. Neither a rose ingredient nor an ivy ingredient is licensed by the comparison.

A same-manuscript check is available: [M.652 f82v, lower Kissos](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/147/143825), [whole page](H_MORGAN_M652_082v_FULL.jpg), shows clearly broad, angular/lobed leaf blades on winding stems. The upper Katananke hetera picture belongs to a different entry. The distinctive lower leaf type is not plainly present in the Kyklaminos picture. This is a counterexample to the strong claim that all named organ analogues are visibly assembled in the illustration; it is not proof that the painter misunderstood the plant, and not an exclusion of every analogical herbal tradition.

The full medieval Kyklaminos text was **not** acquired. Its opening, including the comparisons, is on f72v; the text continues. A guessed f73r JPEG returned HTML, and its guessed ZIF URL redirected. Complete II.164 is supplied from the critical edition with this layer difference explicit.

## Consequences and retained alternatives

1. If one selects this exact source account, the described plant owns its branches/leaves/heads or leaves/flowers/root. The named comparison plants are reference objects, not automatically co-present ingredients or fragments to assemble.
2. Different comparisons do not transfer all properties of the reference plant. A resemblance relation neither equates the taxa nor transfers their medicinal uses.
3. A drawing may preserve some described forms, leave a described organ unclear or absent, and add an anatomically ordinary but undescribed root. No universal text-to-visible-feature bijection follows.
4. A matching organ type does not establish how the drawing was made. Direct observation, an inherited exemplar, a stock motif and a painter following prose remain genuine alternatives. H acquires no production instruction.
5. The later squill analogy is operational. Even within one complete entry, a named plant comparator need not name an anatomical shape.

These source facts do not select a target passage, identify a target plant, reopen a closed decoder or supply a lexical assignment. Selecting Ptarmike or Kyklaminos as a target account would require a separately declared whole-passage construction; the image observations alone do not supply it.
