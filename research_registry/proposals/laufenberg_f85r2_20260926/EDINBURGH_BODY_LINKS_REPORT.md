## What the accessible catalogue says

The KdiH record identifies `Edinburgh, The Royal Observatory, Cr. 4.6 (olim 9.14.5.14)`, chiefly dated around 1470 and localized to southwest Germany / Diocese of Constance. It lists f.121v among the drawings of its second illustrator. In the later schematic summary it describes f.121v as a macrocosm–microcosm connection, with a person in the center and red and black lines from bodily points to planets and zodiac signs. Exact catalogue wording: “von ihm werden mit roten und schwarzen Linien einzelne Punkte am Körper mit den Planeten und Sternzeichen verbunden.” [KdiH 87.2.5](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/87/2/5).

This is a concrete **catalogue-level J1 relation claim**: it assigns lines between bodily points and celestial referents on the same surface. That makes the page a potentially useful relational comparator, stronger than mere codex co-occurrence of a body, planets and zodiac signs. It remains a scholarly description, not my visual confirmation. The KdiH literature link identifies Blume/Haffner/Metzger (2016), *Sternbilder des Mittelalters*, but its public bibliographic record alone does not supply a page-specific visual check in this attempt.

The catalogue description does not enumerate all body points, signs, planets, crossings, or line endpoints. Without the page, I cannot independently tell whether all lines connect exactly as summarized, whether colors distinguish relation classes, or whether labels are readable. No particular planet–body mapping is asserted. Nor does the catalogue entry identify a season/person/physician at 121v; other manuscript sections mentioning these topics are outside this single-leaf check.
