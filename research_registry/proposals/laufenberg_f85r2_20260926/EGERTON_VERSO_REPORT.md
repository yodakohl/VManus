# Egerton2572 f51v: complete-composition native audit

2026-09-26. **A scientifically attributed full-composition reproduction was obtained and inspected.** All four figures, their principal attributes, central head and four banners are visible. The Alamy preview has watermarks and a clipped right edge; several letters cannot be certified. Status: **NATIVE_COMPOSITION_AUDITED; INSCRIPTION_TRANSCRIPTION_PARTIAL**. No Voynich page, text or reserve was accessed and no target meaning was assigned.

The concrete source result is four humoral persons with differentiated clothing, hair, purses, gestures and a support around a central haloed head. Their banners name humours/complexions and refer to four elements. The page does not depict the recto's saints or calendar instrument. Its supported figure is only one part of that fuller arrangement.

## Complete visible inscription inventory

This is a **conservative provisional transcription**, not a critical edition. `[...]` marks unread/lost letters, `(?)` an uncertain reading, square-bracket expansions are explicitly supplied. Line breaks below follow scroll folds or clearly separated text portions rather than invented sentences. A scholar's more complete reading is kept separate.

- Upper left, approximately x40–186,y65–97: visually `Malcolius(?)`; internal letters/possible contraction unresolved. Boxer prints **Malencolius**. The reproduction does not justify silently inserting the extra letters.
- Upper right, approximately x794–917,y56–101: `Sanguin[...]`; terminal letters are at/outside the crop. Scholarly identification: **Sanguinus**.
- Lower left, approximately x41–174,y708–754: `Colerius(?)` / `Colorius(?)`; vowel and internal abbreviation unresolved. A small mark is visible over the middle/end. Scholarly identification: **Colericus**.
- Lower right, approximately x759–880,y720–761: `Flemati[abbreviation?]`; the final raised mark is visible but its expansion is not certified. Scholarly identification: **Fleumaticus**.

The exact abbreviated forms need a better scan. These strings are not aliases or replacements for the scholarly forms.

### Four banners

1. **Upper-left banner:** `Ther / ar the / iiij humors` followed by punctuation. Fairly clear at this resolution. Cautious modern rendering: “There are the four humours.”
2. **Upper-right banner:** `þat / ar / od[er]wysse calde þe / iiij cōplecco / ns`. The expansion of the marked abbreviation in `od[er]wysse` is supplied; the bar in `cō...` is visible. Rendering: “which are otherwise called the four complexions.” The final `ns` lies on the folded end near the centre. Exact abbreviation practice remains provisional.
3. **Lower-left banner, read upward from its low end:** `Hafyng / þe / kynd / of / h[...?]s`. The final short word is unresolved; it looks close to `hus`, but the preview does not safely distinguish its last strokes. Rendering: “having the nature/kind of [unresolved].” Boxer reads **hus** and explicitly treats its interpretation as uncertain, tentatively “use.” That interpretation is **not** a native solution here.
4. **Lower-right banner, from the low/right segment toward the centre:** `þ[ey?] ar / re[...?]uid / un / to the / iiij / element[abbreviation?]`. The verb's internal letters and the final abbreviation are not secure. Rendering: “they are [unresolved verb] unto the four elements.” Boxer starts this unit with **þat**, prints **reservid**, and expands the final word as *elementis*. The pixels do not warrant choosing that verb over competing letter readings.

All four banner units are included; the uncertain word and verb have not been discarded to yield a cleaner account. The readable connection is **humours → alternative name complexions → uncertain kind clause → relation to four elements**. The global prose starts upper left then upper right; its remaining clauses occupy lower left and lower right. That row order is different from a continuous clockwise sequence. It remains unnecessary to invent a chronological narrative around the page.

## Whole-composition observations that constrain interpretation

- No visible spring/summer/autumn/winter names, hot/cold/moist/dry predicates, age numerals or chronological arrows were identified among the readable labels. This is not a claim about other pages or all unread strokes.
- No zodiac signs, calendar scale, concentric computational rings or movable pointer appear in this verso composition. The central nimbus is circular; curling banners are not the recto's volvelle.
- No urinal examination, medicine box, spatula, sickbed or patient consultation is depicted. Purses should not be redescribed as medicine containers solely to make the page medical.
- No separately held flower or plant branch appears. The nimbus has ornamental gold forms; those are not a botanical attribute held by one of the four men.
- The men differ in apparent maturity, hair and posture, but only the lower-right figure is visibly stooped on a support. A fixed four-age sequence or seasonal ordering is not written out by these observations.
- The page's medical/humoral interpretation is positively supported by its words and source context. The absence of diagnostic apparatus therefore does not make it nonmedical.

## Scholarly account and the recto/verso relationship

The complete directly relevant unit in [Carly B. Boxer,2021](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/dxxry-gb705), chapter1, begins under *Humoral Figures* on p89, continues through p90 and the opening paragraph of p91, and then turns to a broader humours/elements argument. It identifies the four people and scroll text, discusses external complexion, age, posture and colour, and contrasts the verso with diagnostic images and saints around the recto instrument. This audit checks the described composition but leaves her uncertain `hus` interpretation unresolved. Her row-order list and later clockwise age formulation must not be collapsed into one proven reading path.

A further complete Egerton discussion occupies Boxer's pp261–262. She interprets central Christ as mediating opposing qualities and medical healing. Crucially, she reports that the volvelle's matrix is physically linked through the part in Christ's hair on the reverse, and uses that position to argue for a bodily/cosmic relationship. **This is a concrete published claim of physical linkage, stronger than mere adjacent subject matter. It is not independently verified by this preview:** only a small dark mark at the hair part can be seen. No new recto image was acquired or remeasured. Her wider Christ-as-medicine account remains interpretation, not wording newly read from the banners.

Wragg's2021 introduction, illustration list and public appendix identify the medieval sequence and the f51v plate; the full plate/chapter was not accessed. Harrold's2007 p150 n408 describes the urine glass and medicine box of the named saints on f51r, as established in the predecessor packet. It supplies **no** reason to give these objects or saintly names to the four humoral men on f51v. The medieval core is late fifteenth century, conventionally1486; its dating does not establish dependence of any other manuscript.
