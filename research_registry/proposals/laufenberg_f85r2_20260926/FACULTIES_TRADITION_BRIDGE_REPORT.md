# Bounded source bridge check: faculties, qualities, and the owned cycles

## Finding

The bounded check found a concrete medieval physiology link from natural
faculties to elementary qualities, but did **not** find an explicit connection
from those faculties to the seasonal/age/complexion illustration programs in
the owned Laufenberg sources. The result supports a possible physiological
comparison at the level of operations and qualities; it does not support
assigning the Llull faculties to the four seasonal figures, to the complexion
figures, or to any target figure or word.

## Primary medieval physiology unit

In Albert the Great, *De anima*, II.2, chapter VIII, the chapter discusses
appetite alongside the operations serving nutrition. It describes
attraction in plants through natural heat and in animals through intestinal
villi; attraction is also aided by similarity and vacancy. Retention keeps food
in place until it receives the needed alteration and is aided by dissimilarity,
dryness, and (per accidens) cold, with villi as an additional animal
instrument. Digestion is aided by heat and moisture and is described through
several operations and four stages. Expulsion of excess can be aided by heat,
moisture, cold, and dryness in different ways; cold's role is qualified as
per accidens. Its opening four-member list names attraction, digestion,
expulsion, and appetite, while retention is then treated separately; the
chapter's internal counting and hierarchy are not a transparent four-box
scheme. It closes by calling natural powers bodily and instrumented, with
spirit as their vehicle. See
[the complete Latin chapter in the LombardPress reader](https://reader.lombardpress.org/text/albertusdeanima/admda-l2t2c8),
especially its full unit at lines 19–34.

This is a positive operation-to-quality relation, but it is not a four-to-four
assignment. Several qualities support more than one operation; some work
per accidens; and organ, body structure, food, and process stages matter. The
chapter keeps appetite distinct from attraction, so its vocabulary should not
be collapsed into an undifferentiated four-role chart. This is a scholastic
medieval physiology comparison, not a claim that Albert is quoting Galen
verbatim. It says nothing in
this unit about seasonal order, ages of life, complexion portraits, cosmic
rings, or a common illustrated program.

## Comparison with sources already owned

The GDT891 Vat. lat. 3468.pt.1 f.38r source report records a four-label
vegetative tree: *Expulsiua*, *digestiua*, *Retentiua*, *Appetitiua*. It records
caption ownership and spatial order, but says no directed inter-figure
process is drawn, and the functional attribute key remains partial. The
appetitive label is not silently substituted for *attractiva*. Thus this
diagram is a related four-role medical comparison, not a diagram of seasonal
figures or a full depiction of the process sequence in Albert's chapter.

The Augsburg 1491 *Regimen sanitatis* source reports preserve a separate
program. Its year-cycle unit names Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter and
contains first-person seasonal self-description plus variable advice to the
reader; its physician/regimen unit is separately framed as instruction on
conduct through time. The complete later element/complexion passage explicitly
compares the year's divisions with four human natures and discusses all humans
as mixed from the four elements, with different predominance and
individualized regimen. It does not name the natural faculties or connect
their actions to the season/complexion figures. The print therefore contains
an explicit year-to-human-nature analogy, while the faculty tree and the
quality-based nutritional physiology remain separate source programs in this
bounded comparison.

The fact that the seasonal and element/complexion units occur in one printed
work, and that the faculty tree occurs in a different work, is coexistence
across source units, not shared picture ownership. The physiological chapter
offers a plausible conceptual bridge through qualities, but no source statement
or diagram found here joins that mechanism to the owned illustration program.

## Scope and decision

This is a bounded screen, not an absence claim about medieval medicine. It
uses the fixed GDT891 report and the already owned complete Laufenberg source
reports, then checks one complete primary chapter of medieval physiology. No
new target query or image, new Voynich access, reserve, sealed material,
decoder, or external contact was used. Root's separate Galen III.1/9 check is
not replaced by the Albertus comparison.

**Decision:** retain the faculty-to-quality relation as a source-supported
possible content mechanism; do not merge it with the seasonal/age/complexion
image program absent an explicit cross-reference or co-owned diagram. A
future claim that one program mediates the other would need a written
cross-reference or a source image whose labels and relations jointly show
both systems, and must account for mixed/overlapping qualities and the
distinct role of appetite. This finding changes source-comparison
prioritization only; it confirms no target meaning or identification.
