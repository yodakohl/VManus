## 270v, full two-column unit: cautious content inventory

The opening is a Mercury section. I read its first lines as a positional/proximity statement involving Mercury and Venus (`... der Venus aller nächste` is the clear phrase; the initial words are less certain), then claims of strong influence in an earthly/body context. Exact grammar and referents in the first few lines remain uncertain.

The first column continues with a strong evaluative contrast around Mercury: `Mercurius ist gut bei den guten und böse mit den bösen` is my best reading, followed by a qualifying statement that it is good by nature. The precise referent of “the good/bad” is not fully explicit to me from this clause alone; surrounding astrological prose may mean planets or influences rather than moral persons. I do not treat it as a universal person-level law. The same column proceeds into effects/qualities attributed to Mercury, including readable references to speech/eloquence and memory or learning; several connecting words and the scope of those traits are uncertain. It then describes those born under Mercury and their disposition or capacities. Near the lower part of the column I can read a catalogue-like reference to natural matters and a `Meister`/mastery or skilled-arts phrase, but I cannot securely transcribe a specific trade or medicine term.

The second column continues the native/person description: it includes a likely bodily/appearance description (a long neck and a face/feature description are legible, though wording is partly uncertain), followed by behavior or aptitude claims. The column appears to contrast or qualify abilities by planetary company/condition; the text includes a phrase resembling “with the good …” and “when …,” but I cannot securely resolve each antecedent. A later clause appears to mention avoiding or opposing an enemy and a manner/measure of effect. I preserve these as visible but uncertain material rather than reconstructing a coherent astrological rule from context.

**Medicine versus arts:** I see language of skill/arts (`Kunst` or a closely resembling form) and a `Meister` phrase. I do **not** see an unambiguous word for medicine or a complete claim that Mercury governs medical practice on these two pages. Any stronger “medicine” claim needs exact source wording beyond my reading. There are explicit descriptions of people/natives under Mercury, so a person-level interpretive component is present; the conditional good/bad formulation is visible, but its argument (persons, planets, or influences) is uncertain.
