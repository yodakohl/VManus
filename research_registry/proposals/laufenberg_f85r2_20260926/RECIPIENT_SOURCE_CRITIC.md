The smallest source scope for a **bare suitability application** is the
opening organ account: the spleen attracts appropriate material; the same
assurance extends to liver, veins, arteries, heart and other organs. The later
necessity statement gives the nutritional setting. This is a generic account
of functions/powers, not an observed meal, transfer or successful assimilation.

There are two explicitly named general-case recipients, spleen and liver,
without invoking the final residue examples. If a writer requires bare uses
for gall-bladder or kidneys instead, it must instantiate the general organ
scope and expose that recipient's organ/nutritional membership and the common
context. The final clause makes those anatomical roles source-supported, but
does not introduce separate observed feeding cases. A repeated organ name
alone does not perform the required instantiation or bind material x.

For neutral A_c(b)(x)=PROPER_FOR(x,b,c), applying this atom inside an owned
generic condition can count as a real predicate application. It need not be
an unconditional assertion about a named portion. Merely mentioning the
predicate, placing it inside a faculty label, or only constructing A_c(b)
inside a modifier is not that additional application. A writer must specify
its generic/conditional force; the source does not supply a frequency, a
sample population or a quantified inventory of actual material tokens.

The general suitability claim must not acquire a liver-origin restriction
simply because liver residues appear later. Conversely, the source does not
positively exhibit a suitable material of non-liver origin. That difference
between scope and observed counterexample matters below.

## Case1: bare suitability differs from origin-restricted suitability

Fix recipient b, context c and D(x)=RESIDUE_FROM(liver,c)(x). Let
P(x)=PROPER_FOR(x,b,c). The following is a stipulated logical case, not source
data. All false entries are explicit countermodel assumptions, not an
inference from missing manuscript or medical observations.

| Material witness | D: liver origin | P: suitable for b | D AND P |
|---|---|---|---|
|m|false|true|false|
|n|true|true|true|

The bare predicate applies to both m and n; the restricted description applies
only to n. A source-compatible suitable liver-residue witness is still present
in n. The extra m is **permitted by this local logical account, not entailed
or historically observed in III.9**. This table isolates the output distinction
without claiming a modern physiological example. It is not a full model of
the paragraph's faculty and generic assertions; those would need their own
interpretations and are not computed here.

Thus a fixed writer which silently identifies P with D AND P changes the
scope of a bare claim. The table does not prove which target expression, if
any, realizes P or the restriction operator.

## Case2: one joint witness differs from two independent witnesses

Keep the SAME b,c,D,P, but consider a different logical case:

| Material witness | D: liver origin | P: suitable for b | D AND P |
|---|---|---|---|
|u|true|false|false|
|v|false|true|false|

`(exists x D(x)) AND (exists y P(y))` is true. In contrast,
`exists x [D(x) AND P(x)]` is false. An account with separately available
origin and suitability facts therefore does not necessarily supply an
appropriate liver residue. This case deliberately violates the intended
joint residue assertion while satisfying the weaker replacement. It is a
countermodel to that logical inference, **not a model asserted to satisfy the
complete historical source**.

The shared variable is local to one qualification. Applying the same schema
to another recipient does not license x to persist across both claims. A
later explicit reference to a qualified witness, however, must preserve that
witness's D and P properties; an unrelated new liver-origin fact cannot replace
the requested projection. No measured quantity or material conservation law
is involved in either example.

## These are weak rivals, not the strongest alternative

Case1 defeats collapse of a bare predicate into its origin-restricted version.
Case2 defeats replacing a joint witness with unrelated existentials. Neither
defeats the **separate-material-name rival**: it can introduce a description
M_b, bind a witness x of it, and assert BOTH D(x) and P(x) in ordinary clauses.
It can preserve that x in downstream reference and cover all source duties.
For example, choosing M_b with extension D intersect P is extensionally
equivalent to the proposed modified description. No loss of origin follows
merely from using an opaque material name.

The proposed factorization is consequently an additional compositional
writing hypothesis. Its possible advantage is repeated derivation of those
descriptions and actual bare predicates from fewer independently assigned
form meanings, under fixed argument and scope rules. The source does not
entail the factorization or make the equivalent rival false. A whole writer
must show that reuse and disclose its remaining aliases, reference rules and
free values. Two toy truth tables cannot supply that manuscript evidence.
