# II.25 seasonal-stage source limits (independent bounded review)

This is a source-only consequence review of the printed II.25 passage at 94.16–95.2 in the cached Pfeiffer/TITUS text. It is not a novelty review, a second witness, or a target-fit judgment. I read the complete source chapter, card 80, and `MEGENBERG_NEXT_CONTENT_ROOT.md`; I did not consult target material.

The opening question is framed as an anticipated reader question (“Nu maht dû frâgen”): if the vapour from which thunder and lightning arise goes up in winter and summer, why does it not thunder in winter as in summer? That is a staged question, not evidence of an actual dialogue. “Der dunst” names the vapour kind in the question; the passage does not follow one identified parcel persisting across seasons.

The generic winter answer gives two limitations on the heat: it cannot raise the “strong, firm” vapour associated with thunder, nor raise it high enough for it to fall with great violence. These are distinct requirements in the account, but the text supplies no measured threshold and does not establish that passing those two requirements alone is sufficient for thunder. The Sun still raises winter vapour for rain, snow, winds, and fires not called lightning. Autumn and spring are said to share this situation, “except very rarely.” Thus failed thunder stages do not mean no vapour or no weather, and the initial seasonal comparison is not a universal claim that winter never has thunder.

The local contrast that follows has explicit attribution and geography: “some say” that in lands by sunrise (eastern lands) there is no summer thunder but there is winter thunder. The offered reason is excessive summer heat dispersing vapour and preventing it from thickening into clouds; winter heat there is gentle, “just as it is in summer with us.” A final clause says lands toward sunset (western lands) are like us because their summers are not excessively hot. The opening winter explanation does not itself locate its climate in “our” place. Keep the comparison owners straight: eastern winter is compared to summer here, while the western comparison is to us generally. The regional account is reported, not presented as verified observation. The heat obstruction here (dispersion / failure to thicken into cloud) differs from the earlier winter failure to raise and elevate thunder-vapour.

A faithful account should preserve the question’s generic vapour, its two distinct winter limitations, the Sun’s residual weather outputs, the rare autumn/spring exception, the attributed eastern seasonal reversal and its cloud-thickening explanation, and the western comparison. Flattening deficient and excessive heat into one monotonic effect, moving the regional comparison to the generic opening, changing its place/season pairings, or dropping the residual outputs would alter the stated account. Conversely, a single persistent parcel, calibrated “optimum” temperature, universal regional law, or a necessary-and-sufficient numbered stage model adds claims not licensed by these lines. Such additions would need independent evidence; failure to see them in a later synopsis is not by itself a source contradiction unless that synopsis asserts them.
