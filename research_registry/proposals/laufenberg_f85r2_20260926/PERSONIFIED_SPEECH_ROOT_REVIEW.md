## What the images establish

The source distinguishes picture captions from the following teaching poems.
Spring's caption on162 names its first-person speaker, but163 begins by
speaking **about** Spring, followed by a teaching narrator addressing the
reader. Summer repeats this distinction between164's caption and165's
third-person opening; Autumn does so between168's two-line caption and its
large-initial third-person poem. The year overview already has an instructional
I/you before any season is introduced. Therefore the whole main poem cannot
be assigned to the pictured seasonal speaker from its caption alone.
The images do not prove that the different instructional I forms are different
people, or definitively exclude literary personification of the teacher.

## Meaning beyond a four-quality list

The complete Spring body combines timing, warm/moist season, blood in the
body, qualified conduct and foods, a young-maiden analogy, flowers/springs/
birdsong, and renewed earthly growth. It then explicitly locates something
already hidden **in the person**, which stirs at that time, whether health or
pain/illness, and concludes with protective advice. This is an existing hidden
condition stirring in a seasonal analogy. Hidden does not entail that it was
previously inactive. The passage does **not** explicitly name a
diagnostic observation, prove an outward symptom became visible, or say Spring
creates a previously absent disease. Nor does the earthly revival metaphor
assert resurrection of deceased human patients.

Summer's main body calls the season warm/dry, warns against heat-producing
conduct, then recommends things that cool and moisten **the addressee**. It
includes concrete food/preparation advice, a necessity qualification on letting,
and moving from sun to shade/a cool dwelling. This gives an explicit ambient
versus desired bodily-effect contrast in the source. It does not identify a
Voynich patient, effect word or imperative construction. No old537/532 target
ownership gap is filled by this source alone.

The shared I/you grammar thus coexists with distinct caption, narration,
explanation, permission and instruction roles. A target dialogue hypothesis
must preserve those distinctions; it cannot inherit one speaker per paragraph
or assume four consecutive seasonal speakers from these source pictures.
