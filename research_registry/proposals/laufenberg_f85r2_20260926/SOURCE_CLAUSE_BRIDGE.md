# Karlsruhe Cod. K 2790: the prose beside the physician

This note follows the **external source** only. The principal images are
Badische Landesbibliothek, Cod. K 2790, platform folios 91r and 91v. The
institution labels the physician image as 91v (canvas 5967406); the KdiH
manuscript record locates the urine-inspection doctor at 92v. I retain both
labels because the manuscript image shows more than one foliation. I do not
silently align them.

## Attestable observations

1. On the institutional image of platform 91r, two seasonal-age headings are
   legible in red: a third age with *Herbst* and a fourth age with *Winter*.
   The brief diplomatic readings are `Das dritt(e) alt … herbst` and
   `Das vierd(e) alt … winter`; the connecting articles and endings are
   uncertain. The upper, Autumn figure has one staff and a hanging beaded
   object; the lower, Winter figure is more stooped and has two supports.
   Thus this witness explicitly includes Winter, while the one-staff figure is
   the one labeled Autumn. [BLB platform 91r, canvas 5967405](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbhs/content/pageview/5967405)

2. The next institutional image, platform 91v / canvas 5967406, shows a
   standing, red-capped figure raising a narrow-necked flask to eye level; a
   second flask is drawn separately in the left margin. No season name or
   age label is visible beside this figure. [BLB platform 91v, canvas 5967406](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbhs/content/pageview/5967406)

3. Immediately above that figure is a red rubric. The readable words are
   `… mensch[en?] natur … vier complexion[?]`; the uncertain full-line reading
   is `[Wie?] d[es?] menschen natur ist nach d[en?] vier complexion[en?]`.
   I retain the uncertainty instead of normalizing the manuscript's shortened
   forms. The certain terms place the image in a passage about human nature and
   the four *complexions*; the heading does not name a season.

4. The rubric and raised flask are direct manuscript evidence. The statement
   that the urine inspection serves to determine a patient's temperament comes
   from the KdiH's work-level account, which describes the urine-inspection
   image as the entry to the temperament section. That is the cataloguer's
   structural interpretation, not a full transcription of the Karlsruhe
   verses. [KdiH, Laufenberg *Regimen*, chapter structure](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/untergruppe/87/3)

5. KdiH describes chapter four as treating four life ages in connection with
   the four seasons and the four temperaments; it says the urine-inspection
   figure is followed by temperament depictions. Its Karlsruhe record lists
   the life-age images at 90r–91r and the urine-inspection doctor at 92v, beside
   an extra urine flask. These are catalog folio references, not the
   institution's platform labels. [KdiH, Karlsruhe Cod. K 2790](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/87/3/4)

6. Taken together, the witness presents the physician at a transition into
   discussion of the four complexions/temperaments. The image is specifically
   an inspection of urine according to the catalog's identification; it is not
   a fifth season or another life-age personification. The visible page does
   not establish a diagnosis of a named disease, a seasonal prescription, or
   a one-to-one mapping between this physician and Autumn or Winter.

## Paraphrase of the relation

The illustrated text first associates life stages with seasons: on one page,
the third age is labeled Autumn and the fourth Winter. In the next platform
image, a man studies a urine flask beneath a heading about human nature and
four complexions. KdiH reads the wider section as moving from the four
season-linked ages to the temperaments, with urine inspection introducing the
temperament material by determining the patient's temperament. The immediate
link is therefore medical and humoral in the broad historical sense of
*complexion/temperament*. Nothing in these two pages makes the physician a
seasonal figure, identifies a particular patient's disease, or demonstrates
that the seasonal and complexion categories are identical.

## Limits and open points

- Only the short red heading fragments above are transcribed here. The prose
  body on the physician page is not transcribed: the hand and abbreviations do
  not support a reliable line-by-line reading in this bounded pass.
- The full patient-specific use of the flask is supplied by KdiH's work-level
  description. The local picture by itself establishes a flask held up for
  inspection, not the exact diagnostic procedure.
- KdiH's generic description mentions a doctor at a lectern, but no lectern is
  visible in this particular Karlsruhe image; the figure appears to stand
  between two small side objects. The generic description must not be used as
  a literal description of this copy.
- The 91v platform / 92v catalog foliation difference remains unresolved.
- This external evidence cannot assign meanings to Voynich forms, prove
  copying, or select a season reading for any Voynich panel or passage.
