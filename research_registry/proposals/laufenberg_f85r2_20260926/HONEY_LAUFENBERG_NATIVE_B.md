# Independent native reading: HAB 167-9-poet, scans 00303–00306

## First-pass frozen observations

I read only the four supplied full-page cached scans. Readings below are deliberately selective and retain uncertainty; they are not a critical-edition transcription. I did not inspect the locator report before making this record.

The rubric on 00303 reads approximately *Wie man die kindlein speisen soll / oder seügen in ir jungen* (“How one should feed or nurse the little children in their youth”). The following first-person lines read *Ich speis vnd seüg das kindlin mein / nach der natur vnd notturfft sein* (“I feed and nurse my little child according to its nature and need”). This makes the speaking voice a first-person caregiver/authorial voice in the poem; it does not prove that the pictured adult on the next scan is the speaker. A preceding passage on this page concerns rubbing/anointing and bodily care and is not itself the milk passage.

Scan 00304 shows an adult holding a child at the breast and a separate object on a table. I do not identify the object or its contents. The text below the picture continues with a direct address: approximately, “After this you may learn what their food should be … one should never feed them except with milk alone, for so long until the little teeth come through.” The final two lines are difficult to construe in this reproduction (*Was man im ander speise geit / den von milch zu dieser zeyt*); I would not use them to weaken the preceding milk-only-until-teeth statement.

Scan 00305 continues the feeding advice. It says no milk can strengthen the child as well as its mother’s milk, and recommends that the child suckle at its mother’s breast. It contrasts this with other women’s milk and refers to the child having been accustomed to the mother in the womb. The mother is told to be diligent about what she drinks while she has the child in her body, but the phrase is unclear in this image: *Das sy trincke alle tag vor t… / da sy das kind in den leib het*. The apparent word after *vor* is uncertain; I cannot confirm a specific wine attribution from these two lines. Later it says that if the child tolerates the milk, the mother should nurse it three times, or twice, in the day. The following phrase *So lang bis es erstecke sich* is uncertain to me; the page then says she should let the child suck often/daily. I do not infer a quantified strengthening endpoint from this unclear phrase.

Scan 00306 continues without a new rubric. It says the milk should be better tolerated/digested and gives a recommendation attributed to a “great master” (*ein maister groß*): that it is healthy for the child, at the same time it suckles, to have wine “gently/little” (*So es sauge das es auch wein / senfftiglich damit ein klein*). The grammar does not make the immediate recipient of the wine entirely certain, although the nearby text names the child; I record the attribution, not a modern interpretation. Elsewhere on this page, the speaker says one should give a little honey before the mother’s milk (*Das man wenig honig im gebe ein / vor der milch der mütter sein*). The exact force of *im* and the relation of this advice to the preceding wine sentence are not fully clear, but the sequence places honey before mother’s milk.

The same page says that each morning the mother should first let the “coarse milk” flow from her breasts before letting the child suck. At the end, the speaker qualifies the earlier milk advice: although he has said that no milk is suitable for the child unless it is the mother’s, “the mother should nevertheless [unclear: *an sett*] when she has just given birth” (*wenn sy erst geborn hett*). The final phrase is visibly uncertain; I cannot determine whether it instructs immediate nursing, waiting, or some other postpartum action. The selected scan window ends here. I did not view the next rubric or leaf.

## Correction to the post-freeze comparison

I mischaracterized root’s locator reading of the final two lines on 00304. Root does not read the warning about other food as advice about introducing foods after dentition. The 00304 rule says milk alone until the teeth emerge; its following lines refer to other food during that preceding milk-only interval (“at this time”), and the warning of death or illness continues on 00305. The placement leaves a qualified tension within the advice, not a new-food-after-teething sequence. My earlier comparison sentence saying the lines “introduce other food at the dentition point” was inaccurate and is superseded by this correction; the original first-pass observation is preserved as recorded. The 00305 line root reads as `Das sy trincke esse als sy vor thet / da sy das kind in dem leib hett` (the mother should eat/drink as she did before she carried the child) explains the prenatal reference better than my uncertain first-pass reading. The first-pass record is selective native confirmation, not a complete independent transcription of every line.
