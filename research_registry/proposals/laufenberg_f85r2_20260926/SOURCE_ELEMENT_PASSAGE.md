# Karlsruhe Cod. K 2790: the elements passage beneath the physician rubric

This is a bounded external-source transcription note. It covers the verse
under the red rubric on Badische Landesbibliothek platform folio 91v through
the first red rubric on platform 92r, `Die erste complexion Sanguinea.` The
manuscript images are the primary witness. A 1491 Augsburg print was consulted
as a separate reading aid; its wording is not silently supplied to the
manuscript. No Voynich material was accessed.

## Coverage and transcription convention

On 91v, the body occupies six short lines to the physician's right and six
lines below the image. On 92r, I count 21 physical baselines from the first
continuation line at the top of the page through the last physical line before
the red `Die erste complexion Sanguinea.` heading. The next complexion
section is outside this transcription. For a complete diplomatic coverage
record, every baseline is represented below; square brackets mark uncertain
or unreadable writing. Spelling and abbreviations are not normalized. This is
my visual reading, not an edition, and some lines are only partly legible.

The following are secure visual readings in the Karlsruhe witness, with
context retained:

- Rubric fragments: `mensch[en?]`, `natur`, `vier`, `complexion[?]`.

  `den vier elementen`, `complexion`.

The 92r first line is a tentative best read, not in the secure-quotation set:
`Das wasser blut vñ für hitz[e?]. Erd[e?] fleisch vñ bein; luft …`. I can
make out `wasser`, `blut`, `hitz[e?]`, `erde`, `fleisch`, `bein`, and `luft`,
but the grammar, pairings, and final words remain uncertain.

These phrases establish that the local discussion names human nature, four
elements, and complexions, and then proceeds to a section headed for the first
complexion. They do not establish that every element has been assigned to a
particular season. The 92r opening appears to associate elements and bodily
materials, but the syntax and the air-related term are not secure enough here
for a complete normalized mapping.

## Paraphrase and the scale relation

The passage under the physician rubric moves from a general statement about
human nature and four complexions to explicit mention of four elements. On
92r, the first line appears to associate water with blood, fire/heat, earth
with flesh and bone, and air with a further bodily term. This is a tentative
micro-to-macro correspondence read from the Karlsruhe witness; the syntax,
precise pairings, and final air-related term are not secure enough here for a
normalized translation. The text proceeds toward the first complexion
section.

The separate Augsburg 1491 print (scan 00172) supplies a useful independent
parallel immediately after its doctor-and-flask image: `Das dz jare nach
yedem stat / vierlerley naturen hat / Darnach sich auch die leib hie /
verwandlen ire naturen ye`. In cautious paraphrase, the year has four kinds
of nature according to its state, and bodies likewise change their natures.
This printed passage supports a year/body analogy at the level of fourfold
natures. It is a different witness and is not used to repair unreadable
Karlsruhe words or prove that the Karlsruhe copy has identical wording.
Further along in the separate 1491 witness, scan 00176 explicitly gives an
element/body list: `Das wasser blut vnd feuchtigkeit / erde fleisch vn bein /
lufft … treit`, with nearby lines naming the four elements. This supports
reading the print's passage as an element-to-body-material analogy. The exact
air phrase is retained as seen in the print and is not used to complete the
Karlsruhe line. The Karlsruhe's own readable terms support the more limited
claim: the first continued line appears to associate elements and bodily
material, followed by a section on `Sanguinea`.

## Limits

- The 91v image and its local words establish a physician holding a flask and
  a rubric about human nature and four complexions; the exact heading and
  several physical lines remain uncertain.
- The Karlsruhe verse was visually followed across the entire block to the
  next red rubric, but only a small set of words is secure enough for direct
  quotation. The 21-line 92r transcription is a coverage inventory, not a
  claim that 21 lines have been fully deciphered.
- The tentative first-line element/body associations are reported as a best
  read, not a settled transcription. This note does not establish a
  four-element/season mapping for the Karlsruhe witness.
- The 1491 print is a separate witness. Its readable year/body statement is
  corroborative context, not a substitute for Karlsruhe manuscript reading.
- Nothing here assigns a meaning to Voynich forms, demonstrates copying, or
  identifies a disease or a specific diagnosis.
