## 13v: every diagram zone retained

The full page is occupied by a single circular diagram with broad blank margins, surrounded by four heraldic banners on long poles. Inside are twelve inward-facing wind heads with elongated, bulbous, bellows-like forms at their mouths pointing toward the common center. Four heads are crowned; the others wear varied caps. Four large colored fields group three heads apiece. Twelve peripheral inscription sectors, separated by red lines, align with those heads; their writing alternates red and brown. The center itself has no separate named actor or running paragraph.

The inscriptions include wind names or direction terms and descriptions of conditions/effects, rather than names alone. The upright top sector visibly includes moistness, bringing rain and making leaves/grass grow, with red *orient* near its inner edge. Its initial name is crossed through; it is not securely transcribed here. The top neighboring red sector describes a mixed origin from one side and another; exact labels are uncertain. The next brown sector concerns snow/rain effects, with wording not fixed. Other sectors contain dry/cold/warm and cloud/weather wording, but curved/inverted lettering and crossed labels prevent a dependable twelve-entry critical transcription within this bounded inspection. No rotation or crop was used to select convenient sectors.

All twelve sectors, heads, colored groupings and all four banners were viewed as part of the whole image. Uncertain readings remain uncertain; the written Latin names are not silently normalized from a standard wind list, and banner identities are not assigned. The diagram's top *orient* association with moist/rain/growth is in apparent tension with 14r's east wind described as hot/dry. This may concern labeling, correction, reference or source disagreement; none is decided here. It is not an invitation to move directions or substitute seasons.

The crowns and groups support a visual principal/companion distinction when read with 14r. The picture alone does not prove physical generation of lesser winds, causal descent, or that opposed positions have opposite qualities. No such claim is adopted.

## 14r: complete prose accounting and qualified native readings

The page contains an illuminated initial and two prose columns. The heading identifies the preceding twelve winds and introduces the chapter; the numeral is not securely transcribed. There is no separate wind picture on this face, apart from a narrow visible gutter fragment of 13v. Reading proceeds down the left column, then the right: the southern-wind account continues across that column boundary. The final lines give a general conclusion about recognizing winds. There is no visible unfinished sentence requiring another page, although the terminal abbreviation is not expanded as further unknown prose.

The following readings preserve source relations; spelling is lightly separated for readability, not a diplomatic edition. Brackets mark uncertainty. They do not replace the full-page receipts.

**Opening/east, left upper column.** The text begins approximately *Der ersten winde sint vier*. One is called *Oster* and comes *von oriente*, followed by *haiss vnd trucken*. It then says *vnd hat zu iglicher siten ainen / Als hienan in der figuren stet*. This is an explicit two-sided ownership statement and a written reference to the figure. The next plural sentence calls *Die* healthy/good and says they do not alter the body, with a following qualification whose exact phrasing is uncertain. The plural may include the east wind with its companions or refer more broadly; no universal scope is fixed from it. The source does not expressly say that every companion inherits every quality of its principal.

**South, left middle/lower column continuing at right top.** *Der ander* is named *Auster*, glossed south wind; *Der hat auch zwen*. It is warm and moist and goes through the desert, through lands described as warm/moist, and over much sea. A series attributes harm to human nature/body: sleepiness, headache and dizziness are readable; harm particularly to sick people and bodily alteration follow. The passage finishes at the top of the right column with doing no good and causing decay/rotting. The exact last bodily phrase is less certain than the preceding route and quality clauses. The south account lists route and effects; it does not contain the same explicit local because-clause used in the west account below.

**West, right upper/middle column.** The next paragraph begins *Der dritte heist wester*, from where the sun goes down, and again *Der hat auch zwen*. Its central sequence is particularly legible:

> Der ist kalt vnd fuchte / von siner nature Vnd / ist bi vns allewege warme / Das macht das er durch / warme lant gat

It then says approximately *Der dut vns nit wee*. Thus the same grammatical subject is (a) cold/moist by nature and (b) warm here, with *allewege* giving a local generalization rather than a pair of observed episodes; the passage explicitly makes travel through warm land the explanation for the latter. Nature, route, land warmth and locally experienced wind warmth are stated, not supplied from a modern meteorological account. Calling the second property an **arriving state** is our analytical paraphrase of *bi vns*, not a source term. Heat transfer mechanics, route length, temperature magnitude, time lag and behavior on an unmentioned different route are not stated. The clause does not say that the intrinsic nature itself has been permanently erased.

**North and ending, right lower column.** The fourth wind is named *Boreas* and comes from north land. The following *Das ist kalt vnd trucken* may attach cold/dry to that land rather than directly to the wind; the owner is not fixed here. It then says *hat auch zwen*, apparently returning to the wind. Then *Der eyne* passes through cold land and mountains, does much good and much harm, strengthens human powers, but also brings cough/[a flow or catarrh-like condition], harms old people and constricts the chest. The *Der eyne* attachment matters: it may select one companion rather than simply resume the principal. The present report does not collapse these owners. The last clauses say that one who knows the winds may order/direct oneself accordingly and heal, and that recognizing them is useful. Exact therapeutic actions, quantities and an algorithm from profile to treatment are not supplied.
