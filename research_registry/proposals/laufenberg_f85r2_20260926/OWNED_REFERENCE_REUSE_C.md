## Exact content and restriction

Paragraph96 begins:

> Quicksilver is a watery substance medlied strongly with subtle earthly things

Later in the same paragraph it states:

> And therefore it is said, that quicksilver and brimstone is the element, that is to wit matter, of which all melting metal is made. Quicksilver is matter of all metal, and therefore in respect of them it is a simple element.

The first quotation attributes mixed composition to quicksilver. The next
argument calls quicksilver and brimstone matter of melting metals, then
specifically calls quicksilver matter of all metal and gives that as the
reason for its relative simple-element classification. The singular it
retains quicksilver; them refers to the plural class of metals in this
argument. Removing that class restriction produces a different assertion.

The two consecutive claims must not be normalized into one: the joint
quicksilver/brimstone sentence says all **melting** metal; the following
quicksilver sentence says all metal. The source does not give a modern
elemental analysis or prove that its taxonomy is fully consistent.

Paragraph95 separately says:

> There are two manner of silvers, simple and compound. The simple is fleeting, and is called quicksilver

It goes on to describe compounded silver as made from quicksilver and white
brimstone. This supports a local compositional contrast but does not turn the
historical terms into modern chemical classifications.

Paragraph82 calls elements simple with an explicit qualification, rather
than saying they lack every kind of composition. It contrasts unlike parts
of mixed bodies with each part of fire being fire, describes first in
composition/last in undoing, and gives the four elements and their qualities.
The relative quicksilver statement must not be used to add a fifth absolute
member to that separate list. Nor does82 automatically settle every use of
simple in95–96; the restriction is preserved instead of claiming a perfectly
uniform ontology throughout an edited anthology.
