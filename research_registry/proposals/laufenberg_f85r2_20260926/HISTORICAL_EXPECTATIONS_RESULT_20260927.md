## A concrete layout consequence, checked at the images

The Iris heading and name list sit below its picture on f66v. Its prose
continues at the top of f67r, above the next plant, whose separate caption and
name block identify Viola. Assigning each block to the closest plant would
misassign this continuation. The red Iris caption also reads across the plant:
the drawing divides words between its left and right text areas. Root checked
these two layout facts on the same source images after receiving the source
reader's observation; this is an informed check, not a blind replication.

The practical consequence is specific: a proposed Voynich reading must state
which complete textual unit belongs to the pictured subject and why. Nearest
distance, a physical page edge or an illustration gap cannot alone establish
entry boundaries, clause boundaries or word boundaries. This source example
does not demonstrate that Voynich uses the same layout. It does not override
the established Voynich space/composition evidence or license joining words
after a failed test.

## What a historical comparison may predict

The three records support several possible text functions, not a single herbal
template. A particular proposed source must supply its own complete sequence
and dependencies: which part is used, for which purpose, under which condition,
with which other substance, and with what warning or qualification, when those
relations are actually written. A claim that a substance helps one condition
but harms another is more informative than the unrestricted guess that a
paragraph concerns medicine. Not every historical entry contains every field.

Depicted colour, leaf number, drawn length and position do not automatically
name textual qualities, numbers, dosage or order. Conversely, a written leaf
comparison can be an identification clause. A neighboring plant name can be
a comparison, alternative name, ingredient or corrective rather than the
entry's main subject. Modern botanical identifications and catalogue titles
remain distinct from names visibly written in a witness.

This changes candidate construction: freeze a source-grounded whole-content
hypothesis and its textual ownership before assigning target meanings. Account
for admitted word frequencies, dispersion, composition and retained countercases
before preferring a gloss. These source examples do not calibrate a semantic
probability, impose Latin, fix a part of speech, or make frequent *daiin* a
season. Exploratory readings remain allowed without a proven exact exemplar;
their source and layout assumptions must stay explicit.
