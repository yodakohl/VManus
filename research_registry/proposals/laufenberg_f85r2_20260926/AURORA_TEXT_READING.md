# Three humidities and a musical warning in the opening composite

29 September2026. **The owned inscription now supplies a specific source
relationship: body and spirit join through three humidities. The musical
language also explains the picture's bow/crab and piping bird.** This advances
the historical source reading beyond the previous unidentified composite.
It does not supply a Voynich translation, a four-element assignment, or a
complete unambiguous native transcription of every faded verse.

## Every inscription zone

The middle column preserves the **published reading of Rh/R**, including
obscure syntax. Bracketed discussion in the last column is ours. The Leiden
wording is not silently imported into Zürich.

| Zone | Complete published Latin reading | Native comparison and warranted meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Top, introductory pair | *Solubile corpus iunctum spiritui / Unionem sentiunt virtute triplicis humiditatis* | The assisted Zürich reading supports the body/spirit and threefold-humidity phrase. Leiden gives the corresponding German body/spirit union and explicitly threefold powers of humidity. Exact paleographic expansion of every German word is not claimed. |
| Top, three branches | *Prima aerea combustibilis mercurialis / Secunda aquea scilicet vapor lunaris / Tertia ignea scilicet liquabilitas solaris*; beside the bracket: *elementa humiditas* | Three numbered properties are clear, not a four-item list: airy/combustible/mercurial; watery, namely lunar vapour; fiery, namely solar fusibility. Leiden visibly distinguishes first/second/third and the lunar/solar character. The nouns are technical properties of the stated humidity, not labels demonstrably pointing to three particular animal limbs. |
| Right | *Fidibus tu patulas si cancri gesseris nostri / tria tua corisando mea sibi bursa retentat* | Zürich remains too faded for a secure complete independent collation. Leiden's corresponding first German line refers to open ears and the crab; its Latin second line appears to begin *Era tua*, not the edition's *tria tua*. Its German contains a purse retaining what is yours. Preserve this as a witness difference/reading uncertainty. A complete syntactically secure translation of the Latin couplet is not obtained. |
| Bottom | *Perfectam formam fatuorum practica cuique / infatuas norma contra naturam sensuum tibi quinque* | Leiden's Latin wording visibly differs; its German closing line invokes the rule of one's five senses. The practice of fools and senses belong to the frame, but the exact syntax and word-by-word rendering of the whole couplet remain unresolved. The edition reports R's *normam naturam* variant; that is not silently made the Zürich text. |
| Left | *Fistula dulce canit mihi si non crede kathoni / dum lira dulcissono carmine prodit aves* | Leiden repeats the flute's sweet song, a reference to Cato, the lyre and birds in Latin and German. This makes a musical reading of the depicted action concrete. Whether each final verb should be rendered as betraying, luring or deceiving is not settled by the faded Zürich letters alone. |

The main technical statement can therefore be paraphrased cautiously:
**a soluble body joined to spirit experiences union through a threefold
humidity, whose modes are mercurial/airy, lunar/watery-vaporous and
solar/fiery-fusible.** This is a source translation of the opening technical
block. It is not a translation of the surrounding obscurities or a Voynich line.

## What changes in the picture reading

The musical words and the corresponding Leiden picture jointly improve
the earlier object description. A red clawed crab-like object is held across
the body; the winding serpent is used across it as a bow, and the small bird
at the right plays a pipe. In Leiden these claws and the serpent's head are
clearer. This makes musical performance an owned reading of the composite,
rather than merely a viewer's analogy from harmonious colours. The old
Zürich observation's fish-shaped held object and water-like winding band
remain recorded as earlier visual impressions; they are no longer the best
interpretation of those particular attributes.

No inscription here maps every pictured part to an element, names the golden
head Sun, or proves that the lower pale face is Moon. Nor does it say that a
grain-shaped leg means a botanical ingredient. The technical union and the
musical/fool/senses warnings coexist in the complete frame; dropping either
would give an incomplete account. The purse wording favours a warning about
misleading performance, but its full syntax is uncertain and the general
allegorical interpretation is not uniquely established.

## Research decision and limits

Retain **a specific three-mode union plus musical warning**, replacing the
former source-side gap for that technical relationship. The missing four-
figure annular topology on f85 remains, as do GDT195/208's old limits. This
source cannot license four-element or four-humour names for those figures.
It can inform a later conditional account only if that account carries the
actual distinction between subject, joining relation, three modes and the
different rhetorical material. No such Voynich account is selected here.

All four margins and the complete later folio are accounted for. Nevertheless
the stated acquisition goal is only **partly achieved**: complete published
Latin readings are available, but two couplets remain textually/syntactically
uncertain and the faded original is not completely collated. Do not call
this a fully verified diplomatic edition or a complete translated historical
argument. Another unchanged native-view pass is not the next step; a genuinely
clearer corresponding text or an exact philological resolution would be new
input. No additional comparison witness was substituted after these limits.

text/image, new target admission, reserve, f84/f84r, f116v or outside contact
was involved. The manuscript's broader alchemical hypothesis remains C0;
