# Sloane MS 2435: seasons and ages as an internal source control

Source-only comparison, 2026-09-28. This is a new comparator in this dossier,
not a Voynich word reading or an independent confirmation panel. The f85r2
target attributes are those already fixed in GDT1043; no target image or text
was opened for this comparison. The search path included a public discussion,
but all source claims below were checked against the British Library catalogue
and its own digitised images.

The [British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002114802)
identifies Sloane MS 2435 as an illuminated *Régime du corps*. It explicitly
assigns f. 23r to care of the body through the **four seasons** and f. 31r to
care through the **four ages**. Thus one manuscript places both fourfold
programmes within a medical regimen, without making them the same chapter.
The catalogue dates the manuscript to 1265–1270 and places its origin in
northern France, earlier and in a different milieu than Laufenberg's surviving
Southwest German witnesses; common subject matter does not establish copying.

I viewed both complete official canvases, then only their miniature regions.
The [f. 23r full image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100162150552.0x00002d/full/full/0/default.jpg)
has SHA256 `335c04bf6be6ffd6ae1d6be08e07684316e4a0e0a38d9c5d5dd7f5fed413a9ed`;
its [miniature region](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100162150552.0x00002d/1800,2800,1600,1600/full/0/default.jpg)
shows four clothed figures in a divided initial. The upper-right figure holds
a small branching or sprig-like object; the lower-right is heavily hooded.
Gestures occur in several cells, not as a unique seasonal key. The
[f. 31r full image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100162150552.0x00003d/full/full/0/default.jpg)
has SHA256 `0ab2913dfd217650a5b0c6ea4ddf6197dd132c645b2dc0865574a3c473106340`;
its [miniature region](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100162150552.0x00003d/400,2000,1800,1700/full/0/default.jpg)
again has four figures. The upper-right young man holds a narrow plant or
flower-like sprig, the lower-right older man leans on one substantial staff,
and several figures raise or extend a hand. The visible drawing does not show
a linked strand hanging from the staff-holder or a physician inspecting a
flask. At this image resolution, botanical identity is not asserted.

The **positive** comparison is the controlled coexistence of two different
fourfold medical themes in one illustrated source. The pictures also provide a
within-source warning: sprig, gesture and a clothed older figure are not
exclusive markers of either season or age. The source does **not** reproduce
GDT1043's harder south staff-plus-linked-strand and east raised-vessel
conjunction, its circular annulus, or its four textual sectors. Combining
Sloane's age miniature with Laufenberg's separate doctor would be an invented
composite, not an observed exemplar.

Decision: retain Sloane as an independent historical control supporting a
general medical seasons/ages programme. It does not choose the four f85r2
referents, their order, the paragraph contents, or a single Voynich meaning.
Any proposed age-versus-season test still needs a written proposition with
target ownership and an outcome the rival cannot also explain. GDT1042's
complete four-block surface inventory and GDT1043's native target observations
remain unchanged; no further f85r2 word-frequency or fourfold-count test follows
automatically. No reserve, f84/f84r or unadmitted Voynich image was opened.

## Related text: what would actually distinguish the chapters?

The [1911 Landouzy–Pépin edition of Aldebrandin's *Régime du corps*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_R%C3%A9gime_du_corps/Texte_entier)
is based on Bibliothèque nationale and Arsenal witnesses, **not a transcription
of Sloane MS 2435**. It can specify this textual tradition's content, but it
cannot certify the exact wording or page layout of the Sloane witness. Its
seasons chapter (printed pp. 62–63, digital text around lines 371–376)
assigns spring hot/moist, summer hot/dry, autumn cold/dry, winter cold/moist.
Its ages chapter (printed p. 79, digital text around lines 442–444) gives the
**same ordered four quality-pairs** to the first through fourth ages. A future
four-block match to that cycle would therefore be compatible with both chapter
types; it cannot choose an exclusive *season* or *age* reading.

| Written proposition in the related edition | Season chapter | Age chapter | Present f85r2 decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four ordered hot/cold and moist/dry qualities | Yes | Yes, same order | Non-discriminating even if quality signs were independently established. |
| Solar passage through zodiac signs; flowering, falling leaves, annual cooling | Explicit | No corresponding four-age anchor in this passage | Potential discriminator only with a fixed target referent and textual owner; neither is known. |
| Four ages with ends around 25/30, 40/45, 60 years and death; finer seven-age partition | No corresponding age limits in this passage | Explicit | Potential discriminator only with independently bound years/numbers and a textual owner; neither is known. |
| Medical regimen for each phase | Yes | Yes | Generic care vocabulary is nonexclusive. |

This is a **source-side discrimination audit**, not a target test. GDT1042
already inventories all four f85r2 paragraphs: their starts differ, only
`aiin` occurs as the same whole form in all four, and there is no common
reader-stable repeated two- or three-token construction. GDT1043 fixes the
visible figures but finds no word-to-object connector or prose order. No
Voynich form can presently be designated as a year threshold, zodiac entry,
flowering event or quality term merely because this source contains one.
The diagram might combine seasons and ages in a correspondence rather than
choose either chapter; that is a working possibility, not a reading. No new
target pixels, sealed leaves or reserves were opened; independent target
confirmation capacity remains zero and confirmed translated words remain zero.
