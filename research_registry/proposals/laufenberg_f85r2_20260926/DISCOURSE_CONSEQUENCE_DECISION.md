# Discourse-architecture consequence decision for f85r2

## Decision

**No content-bearing consequence that discriminates the two source architectures is presently identifiable from the owned GDT1042 f85r2 surface rows without adding a word meaning, presuming a speaker, or assuming a four-part target mapping.** The two source cases are also not a clean binary: each combines collective and local layers in a different way. This result keeps both as source comparators; it makes no translation claim.

## What the source contrast actually supplies

- **Egerton2572 f51v:** four separate red rubrics identify the four neighbouring personifications, while the four spatially separated banner portions form a connected plural definition of humours/complexions, with lower continuations relating that collective to the four elements. Figure, label referent, banner subject, and banner position are distinct roles. The report does not license four first-person monologues. See `EGERTON_SENTENCE_OWNERSHIP.md` and `EGERTON_VERSO_REPORT.md`.
- **Arundel 83 II f.126v:** four outer banners name four age categories; ten annular texts belong to ten inner life-stage scenes; a separate central text has a first-person divine speaker. Some age-stage inscriptions also speak in first person. The physician is an actor local to the illness episode, not a fifth age-label. This is a layered program (four macro labels, ten episode inscriptions, separate centre), not a simple “one speaker versus four speakers” contrast. See `ARUNDEL_WHOLE_LABELS_REPORT.md`.

Thus the smallest potentially discriminating content consequence would concern **speaker/label ownership**, not cardinality: does a written self-indexical keep one central speaker across the spatial text blocks, or does a fixed naming/speech construction bind it to different locally introduced figures while a separate collective assertion ranges across them? The source facts alone do not say that the target has either construction.

## What the complete owned surface can and cannot decide

GDT1042 contains all rows for the four fixed spatial paragraphs and the two outside loci, for all three transcriptions. Its report records 108/107/109 groups in the four blocks. `aiin` is the only exact whole form shared by all four blocks; it occurs 5/4/5 times in ZL3b/IT2a/RF1b. In every transcription, the listed occurrences are interior to their lines rather than at a block boundary. That makes it a concrete candidate for a future role test, but neither its type nor referent is known. The same form in each block is compatible with a recurring discourse particle, a shared content term, one speaker's indexical, or a block-local speaker's indexical; the surface inventory does not choose among those. ZL/RF `or aiin` repeats, but not IT, and the ZL spaces are partly uncertain. No reader has a stable repeated two- or three-group sequence across all four blocks.

The block labels N/E/S/W are the experiment's spatial scopes. They do not identify the text as four seasons, four speakers, four ages, or four separate propositions. The common `aiin` is not itself a written name, speech verb, person marker, or cross-reference until a fixed role binding establishes that. No count of figures or text groups supplies that missing content.

## Exact missing binding and smallest feasible next acquisition

The missing evidence is one **fixed, written argument/voice binding** for an existing f85r2 whole form: an independently justified target-semantic type that distinguishes (a) a speaker self-reference from (b) a category/participant reference, plus the written antecedent that binds its speaker or referent. To compare an Egerton-like collective banner with an Arundel-like center/local-episode program, the same complete reading would also need explicit clause force/ownership (label, collective assertion, local assertion, or instruction). A plausible candidate such as `aiin` cannot be called “I” or a speaker marker to create this contrast.

The smallest feasible acquisition from the already admitted work is a **coverage/role crosswalk**, not another census: take the full GDT1042 rows as fixed and check the already frozen target-side meaning records for a complete f85r2 mapping that assigns the same written form a speaker-indexical or category-reference type with explicit antecedents across these four blocks and .1/.24. If such an unchanged mapping exists, test it against every assigned occurrence and leave all other groups open. The checked records do not supply one: GDT1042 is descriptive, GDT809 is other loci, and IDEA534/538 remain proposals. So there is no current acquisition path within those records; the next admissible input would have to be an independently grounded complete f85r2 binding, not a new synonym invented for this comparison. No additional target data, image, decoder, or peripheral-ring order can supply this semantic argument role by itself.

**Reopening condition:** an already admissible whole-block binding that names the exact written form(s), antecedent(s), clause ownership and force. Until then, neither a season-speaker reading nor a central-narrator/collective-label reading has a distinct f85r2 content consequence.
