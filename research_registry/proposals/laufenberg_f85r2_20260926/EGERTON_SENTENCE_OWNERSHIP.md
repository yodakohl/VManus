### Which particles and letters are editorially marked?

The crucial particle is **`(Th)a(t)`**, occurring at the starts of **both upper-right and lower-right clauses**. The resulting word *that* produces the relative-clause connection. In that rendering, `Th` and final `t` are parenthesized, while `a` is not. The retained native preview left the final banner's initial form unresolved, provisionally near `þ[ey?]`. This task has not reinspected pixels and does not adjudicate between those readings.

Other parenthesized material is `(h)` in *humors*, `(er)` in *oderwysse*, `(th)` in both occurrences of the article *the*, `(m)` in *compleccons*, and `(is)` in *elementis*. The inspected passage does not supply a general key distinguishing abbreviation expansions from uncertain/restored letters. It would be inaccurate to call every parenthesis a freely invented word. Conversely, the edited expansions should not be presented as fully legible native letters. No extra *and*, *if*, singular owner or time shift is written into the quotation.

If the final form were instead **they**, the last banner could begin a new finite clause with a plural anaphoric subject. That would weaken the claim of one relative-clause chain but still allow a continuous collective definition. It would not by itself justify assigning the statement exclusively to the single adjacent phlegmatic figure. Thus the sentence-count issue and the shared-reference issue have different evidential strength.
