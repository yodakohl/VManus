# Endpoint and extent correction to the bounded transmission note

This addendum supplements, and does not alter, the frozen `GALEN_MEDIEVAL_TRANSMISSION_B.md` (SHA-256 `53f1cbb594e3d2ce6dae2abc21467295c7866e3adf02682f13796ef44b0a4ecd`).

## New endpoint evidence

The Galeno Latino record for [Burgundio da Pisa's *De facultatibus naturalibus* translation](https://galenolatino.com/traduzioni.php?id=22) explicitly labels the translation complete, dates it to the second half of the twelfth century, and gives its incipit and explicit. The explicit is “*vel nequaquam universaliter vel omnifariam evellunt breve*.” It names Vatican, BAV Barb. lat. 179 as the source for its translation record, and says Burgundio's translation depends on Laur. plut. 74, 5, a Greek codex written by Ioannikios in the first half of the twelfth century and annotated by Burgundio. It also reports that Bartolomeo da Salerno cites the translation in his *Ars medica* commentary. These are claims of this specific scholarly catalogue/edition project, not a direct collation made here.

The Wellcome MS.6013 record's f.22r explicit is the same short endpoint, and the Wellcome description itself identifies the ff.9r–22r item as *De facultatibus naturalibus* translated by Burgundio. The NLM catalogue record for [Wrocław MS IV F.26](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nlmcatalog/101089270) separately locates *De naturalibus facultatibus* at ff.66vb–83rb and gives a longer explicit mentioning the distensions of humours contained in the stomach and intestines before the `...vel neque ... universaliter vel omnifariam evellunt breve` close. This supports the identification of that ending as a work-level ending, rather than a reliable boundary at book I. The supplied source-side comparison places the longer wording at the end of book III; I have not inspected manuscript images or performed a line-by-line Latin collation.

## Revised inference and limits

The first report's inference that the Wellcome MS.6013 rubric “Liber primus” limits its catalogued item to book I was too strong. That phrase occurs in the opening formula; by itself it does not set the item's terminal extent. In combination, (a) the complete-translation record with matching Burgundio incipit/explicit, (b) the same endpoint in the Wellcome ff.9r–22r record, and (c) the independent Wrocław catalog's later-book-ending wording make it likely that the Wellcome item's text extends through the full work, notwithstanding the initial `Liber primus` rubric.

This is strong work-level and likely full-work evidence, but still not a chapter-13 collation of the particular fasting liver-vein versus organ-body argument. The proposed Galen III.13 content is likely inside a complete version of the work if the Wellcome text is indeed complete; direct ownership in that codex remains unverified from catalogue metadata alone. Translation existence and a medieval witness are established at a higher confidence than before; the first-report phrasing should be read with this extent correction.

The Wellcome catalogue's “Closed stores MS.6013” describes its location/access category, not image availability. I did not infer that digitization is impossible, and did not fetch or inspect any manuscript image. No new manuscript witness beyond the two supplied catalogued records was investigated; no target data, author draft, or Voynich image was accessed.
