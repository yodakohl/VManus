The best lead is **München, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Cgm 38**, Konrad von Megenberg's *Buch der Natur*, a medieval Middle High German parchment manuscript. It was not identified in the local Megenberg thunder-source receipts checked at preflight, which document the TITUS transcription of Pfeiffer 1861 and an optional Wellcome copy of that same edition, but no native manuscript collation. This locate-only result does not claim Cgm 38 is independent of, or genealogically separate from, Pfeiffer's edition.

The BSB catalog data surfaced by its official Bavarikon record dates the codex to the **second half of the 14th century** and gives the place as **`[S.l.] Raum Regensburg`**; the uncertainty and wording are retained. The scholarly *Katalog der deutschsprachigen illustrierten Handschriften des Mittelalters* (KdiH 22.1.13) independently gives **second half of the 14th century** and localization **Bavaria**. These are catalogue claims, not an origin inference for any other manuscript. KdiH describes the *Buch der Natur* as occupying **1ra–5rb (register with folio references) and 9ra–209vb**, with a Prolog at 9ra; 5va–8v contain later genealogical notes. The register therefore provides the smallest native navigational start for locating II.25 without first reading unrelated body text.
