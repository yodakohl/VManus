# Additional regional source locator report

Catalogue-only scan on 2026-09-27. This records bibliographic triage and exact public catalogue loci; it does not identify a Voynich source or support any meaning. Prior dossier/project exposure is disclosed. No target data or target images were opened.

The strongest new lead is the catalogued seven-page planetary/life-age cycle in shelfmark **M III 36**, fols. **236r–239r**. The catalogue describes four medallions per page: a personified planet, two zodiac-sign scenes, and a pair of human figures representing a life age, with a time-of-day label. Across the sequence it names *Infancia*, *Pueritia*, *Virilitas*, *Adolescentia*, *Juventus*, *Senium*, and *Senectus*. Its page-by-page structure explicitly relates age to celestial categories. The cycle includes a Sun page and zodiacal circles/medallions, but the catalogue does not describe four seasons, the target-like flower/staff/flask attributes, or attached humoral advice. It is a partial age/celestial comparator. The catalogue labels its record `AT7400-MIII36`; exact record: [manuscripta.at catalogue PDF](https://manuscripta.at/_scripts/php/cat2pdf.php?ID=8469&cat=SBUB). The catalogue excerpt dates the illustrations to the fifteenth century (its excerpt says “Hälfte 15. Jh.”); no more precise dating or localization is claimed here.

The Innsbruck **ULBT Cod. 433**, fol. **239r**, is retained as a near-miss only. Its catalogue describes an adjacent seasonal account: year divided into four seasons, followed by a Spring text stating that Spring is warm and moist, affects human blood/strength, and prescribing moderation. The codex also contains separate astronomical material, but it is a composite manuscript and the catalogue does not identify the seasons passage and celestial diagrams as one illustrated unit. It offers independent season/medical prose, not a joined source for the target-like diagram bundle. Exact record: [Innsbruck ULBT catalogue](https://manuscripta.at/_scripts/php/msDescription2_m1_NEU.php?ID=7518&IDinitia=). This catalogue identifies the manuscript as 249 leaves assembled from seven parts, with parts dated across the late fourteenth and fifteenth centuries and provenance at Schnals Charterhouse; those codicological statements do not date the 239r text independently.
