# Ein weiterer Alters-/Medizin-Bildverbund: begrenzter Quellenvergleich

27. September 2026. Bounded source-only follow-up after GDT1042/1043 and the Laufenberg/Arundel dossier. No Voynich image, new transcription selector, reserve or sealed page was opened. The public [Cambridge University Library exhibition record](https://exhibitions.lib.cam.ac.uk/vesalius/artifacts/uroscopy-and-blood-letting/) identifies a woodcut in the 1496 Westminster English *De proprietatibus rerum* (Wynkyn de Worde, mii recto, Inc.3.J.1.2[3559]). Its institutional description puts successive human ages in the upper image and, below, a physician examining a patient's urine while a surgeon performs bloodletting. It explains the medical acts as care across life stages. The description also says the woodcuts draw on earlier printed designs from Lyon 1482 onward. This comparison was obtained from the institution's indexed complete item description; the page itself timed out in the web reader, and its pixels were **not** natively inspected here.

This is a second work family in which ages and uroscopy share an image program; the older [Karlsruhe Laufenberg witness](EXTERNAL_SOURCES.md) places a separate physician after the four seasons/ages, while [Arundel 126v](ARUNDEL_WHOLE_LABELS_ROOT_REVIEW.md) distinguishes outer age banners, inner life scenes and local medical actors. GDT1043's native f85r2 audit instead observes north pointing, west plantlike object, south staff/strand and east raised vessel around a central disk, with no word-to-object connector. These are **different arrangements**. The Cambridge print is later than the proposed Voynich production period and provides no claim of a shared exemplar, copying, geography, wording, authorial order or Voynich meaning.

Decision: retain only the already viable *mixed age/medical program* as a broad source-side rival to a literal four-season label list. Do not elevate a doctor to a fourth/fifth season or assign the east paragraph a physician/urine word on this basis. The new record adds no target-owned relation capable of distinguishing an age narrative from a medical/regimen description; a fixed semantic test using it would merely rescore an image analogy. No new target experiment, significance claim or confirmed word. Reconsider only if an independently readable and topologically matched inscription or a target word-to-figure connector becomes available.
