## The reused constructions and their actual constraints

The limited component proposal is:

```text
qod : ContainerKind → LocativeExpression = IN
ar   = RECIPIENT
aiin = BODY
ain  = FOOD

qod|ar   → IN(RECIPIENT)   E.10 G002
qod|aiin → IN(BODY)        S.16 G002; W.21 G005
qod|ain  → IN(FOOD)        W.19 G002
```

The suffix values are the same as their independently assigned free wholes. This is one component operator with genuinely different container arguments. The bodily recipient and the food input are different roles in the case structure; the operation cannot change FOOD into BODY when a context becomes inconvenient. Exactly these three cuts are licensed. `oraiin`, `sodaiiin`, `qotedar`, `oldar` and other lookalikes remain separately costed wholes. There is no unrestricted deletion of prefixes, or a claim that all visible `aiin` endings denote bodies.

The case supplies the particular container through a declared role rule: BODY/RECIPIENT resolve to the written recipient, FOOD to the written input portion. A fresh generic case with `IN(BODY)` obtains its recipient description from the written BODY component. This contextual binding is an assumption, not zero-cost morphology. In particular, the operator is not evidence that the target actually distinguishes body and food.

The second construction uses `shedy=PRODUCT` with two declared orders: `input FORMS output` and `input [modifiers] output FORMS`. Both denote a positive product relation. These are two syntax rules, not an assertion of free word order.

## The selective-release rival survives the outcome contrast

Keep the positive honey/bile and honey/blood cases and the separately asserted difference. A disclosed countermodel can give each hypothetical input portion both constituent kinds and let a single recipient-dependent selector release bile in W and blood in O without changing those selected constituents. Its invented stock and selector are rival assumptions, not observations. It fits the outcome claims and their difference without bodily generation.

Let P be the preformed-bile assumption, U the separately written uniform-result bridge, and D the asserted contrast. The draft reconstructs the author's use of `P ∧ U → ¬D`. D can oppose that conjunction; it does not refute P independently of U. A hypothetical text can contain this historical inference even when its causal bridge is unjustified. No modern causal validity is required to retain the content hypothesis.

The more precise discriminator is S2's named blood lineage. `KindChange(zC,yC,cC)` and `NoKindChange(zC,yC,cC)` cannot both hold on that same written lineage. Switching to a different precursor would change the compared claim. However, W1's bile lineage and S2's blood lineage are different: preformed bile plus altered blood remains a coherent **mixed account**. A pre-existing constituent elsewhere does not negate this one change claim, and this one change claim does not eliminate release elsewhere. The draft contains no blanket absence statement and no argument that all bile must be newly generated.
