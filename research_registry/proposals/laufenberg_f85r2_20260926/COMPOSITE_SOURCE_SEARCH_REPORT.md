# Composite source search: eight manuscript programs

2026-09-26. Source-only, bounded exploratory search; no target data or images, lexical assignments, reserve access or contacts. Read the live route, pictures context, bounded idea search/route-check and the retained Laufenberg source report before selection. The fixed decision permits three query families, eight independently catalogued manuscripts and two separately selected source images. All eight candidates remain below. Known Laufenberg and Cava programs are predecessors, not discoveries here.

**Concrete result:** Arundel83 II f126v combines an age wheel, an elderly person using supports, and a sickbed physician examining urine within one pictured cycle. Egerton2572 presents a different joint medical program: four named saints, including a urinal-bearing Cosmas, surround the circular instrument on f51r; four humoral personifications occupy f51v. These are useful alternatives with different owners and structures. They do not establish four seasonal identities, any target correspondence, textual dependence or translated words.

## Image1: Arundel83 II f126v

**Attribution and source distinction.** The [BL child catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-002405144) identifies f126v as the ten-age wheel, and distinguishes f126r's twelve-stage wheel. Helen F. Smith's [2016 Edinburgh thesis](https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/8f425cda-3361-4cfd-b2dc-ee0c32016e31/content), printedp34 and figure1 on p36, identifies the eighth episode as a sick man visited by a physician examining urine. The general season–age analogy discussed in the following prose is not a transcription of this leaf's labels.

**Direct pixels, reproduction666×693.** Ten circular scenes form the circumference of a wheel with alternating decorated radial sectors. A bearded frontal head occupies the centre. Four larger figures lie outside the wheel at the corners; some are clipped by the reproduction. On the right-hand descending side, one elderly figure carries a long support; the next bends toward a smaller assisting person. The sickbed scene below these contains a standing figure raising a small rounded vessel near his face. The vessel and physician belong to that bed scene. Other scenes visibly include a child with a seated adult, a mounted person, a crowned seated person, and a bottom tomb. This records the complete visible organization without transcribing every small circular inscription.

The identification of the vessel as a urinal and standing man as physician is supported by Smith; the small reproduction alone does not provide a complete diagnostic reading. The central head's religious identity and all four outer age labels require fuller caption/text audit if used. No source season words or humour names were newly read from these pixels. A doctor embedded in the decline/illness phase of a ten-stage life is a concrete joint comparator, with a relation different from four interchangeable seasonal portraits.

## Image2: Egerton2572 f51r

**Attribution.** [Wellcome work vfk32zzb](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/vfk32zzb) explicitly assigns photograph M0005104 to Egerton2572 f51r; its creation date7January1937 is the photograph's date. The [official full image](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/M0005104/full/full/0/default.jpg) is1223×1741, monochrome. It shows the complete illustrated composition with page edges partly cropped. Wragg's study describes the medieval sequence and Boxer identifies both faces of f51. Jillian Harrold's [*Saintly Doctors* (Warwick PhD,2007)](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/38150/1/WRAP_THESIS_Harrold_2007.pdf), p150 n408, explicitly identifies Cosmas's urine glass and Damian's medicine box/spatula in this manuscript. Neither colour nor hidden mechanisms are recoverable from the photograph.

|---|---|---|
| Upper left | Scroll reads Johannes/Baptista in recognizable abbreviated forms. Bearded figure holds a lamb/book grouping with a cross-staff. | Outside the instrument; not a season label. |
| Upper right | Scroll identifies Johannes/Evangelista. Figure holds a chalice with a small emerging form. | Outside the instrument; distinct from the urinal owner. |
| Lower left | Scroll identifies Sancte Cosme. Figure holds a raised bulbous, narrow-necked, flared-mouth vessel. | Explicitly named medical saint beside the instrument; scholarly attribution supports urinal. |
| Lower right | Scroll identifies Sancte Damiane. Figure holds an open compartmented box and long thin implement. | The paired medical saint; box/instrument, not mobility support. |

The instrument has multiple concentric labelled rings, month names, zodiac figures, numbered scales and an overlapping pointer/index; its large central annulus is dark in this photograph. Thus **four named persons plus a medical vessel plus a circular celestial/calendar instrument** genuinely share one illustrated leaf. The names prohibit relabelling these four as temperaments merely because another page contains four temperaments. The long cross-staff at upper left also has a different owner/function from a crutch.

**Verso remains scholarly, not newly seen.** Carly B. Boxer, [*The Visual Culture of English Medicine,1348–1500* (2021)](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/dxxry-gb705), pp89–91, describes f51v as four humoral figures around Christ. Her coordinate descriptions put Malencolius upper left, Sanguinus upper right, Colericus lower left and Fleumaticus lower right; the latter is hunched, with mittened hands on a cane. She also discusses age and appearance. Her stated scroll-reading order and later clockwise characterization are not silently harmonized here; direction remains unverified without that image. Do not transfer its cane to recto Cosmas or its age associations to four seasons. Wragg's introduction treats the volvelle and temperaments as successive parts of the medieval decorative program, followed by medical/astrological tracts; it does not make the two faces one scene.

## What these sources distinguish

The most useful source distinction is ownership. A support can mark a life stage in Arundel, a phlegmatic person in Egerton's scholarly-described verso, or an injured patient in the Fitzwilliam catalogue. A vessel can be a physician's diagnostic object within a sickbed scene or Cosmas's saintly attribute. A flower can be heraldic background. The recurring object does not carry a unique figure label across these programs.

Two positive joint arrangements are retained: a circular life sequence with a medical episode, and an adjacent medical-saint/temperament program whose recto contains a circular instrument. Worcester supplies a third, nonfigural arrangement explicitly connecting ages, seasons, qualities and humours. None of the eight has been established here to combine **all** the requested features in one medieval scene. This is an exact limit of the inspected evidence, not an exhaustive absence claim. Further work, if chosen, should audit the full written ownership/caption structure of a selected source rather than accumulate more isolated motifs.
