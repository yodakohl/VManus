## Follow-up: layout and family-width sensitivity

The tempting rescue after seeing the table was to shift every text block one
figure clockwise: East `tch` could then be made to refer to the southern older
figure, and West `ksh` to the northern pointing figure. I checked the already
admitted [GDT1043 official final panel](../../../experiments/yolo/gdt1043_f85r2_native_attribute_binding/artifacts/SOURCE_FINAL.json)
at native full-panel resolution. Four diagonal boundaries delimit radial
sectors, with each of the four figures at the inner end of its same-direction
paragraph sector. No arrow, leader or other authorial device sends the East
paragraph to the South figure or West to North. A cross-sector prose reference
remains possible, but the proposed uniform one-sector shift is **not licensed
by the visible layout** and was chosen only after inspecting the lexical hits.
It cannot turn this table into a seasonal match.

As a family-width sensitivity check, I intersected all four blocks and outside
lines with the **21 observed complete forms** in the earlier
[GDT646 form ladder](../../../experiments/yolo/gdt646_tcheey_surface_completion/artifacts/FORM_FAMILY_ATLAS.tsv),
without using GDT624's `o`/`qo` wrappers. Only East `.9 tchedy` matches, once
per reading; North, South, West and OUTSIDE match zero. Thus the East–West
opposition depends on GDT624's inherited assumption that wrapper-bearing
forms retain a common core value. It is a useful formal candidate under that
model, but **not a model-independent text fact about two medical qualities**.
This follow-up used already admitted images and cached source projections;
the same leaf remains previously exposed. No significance or word meaning is
derived from either post hoc check.
