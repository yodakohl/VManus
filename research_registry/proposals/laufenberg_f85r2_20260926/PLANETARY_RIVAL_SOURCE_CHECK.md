# Planetary-program rival: bounded source check

2026-09-26. Catalog-led comparison, with no Voynich target pixels or text opened and no new external images fetched. This is a source-side check of a possible historical program, not a target identification or a word-meaning proposal.

## Finding

Two specific planetary cycles show why a mixed planetary reading remains a genuine iconographic rival, but neither source supplies the full proposed bundle (flower-holder, supported elder with bead/chain object, urine-inspecting physician, and figure pointing around a Sun) as one coherent planetary program.

KdiH describes Munich, BSB Cgm 5185, **Text 2, “Die Planeten und Planetenkinder,” 2v–6v**, as a seven-image planetary sequence. Its Saturn holds a crutch and sickle; Venus, a flower bouquet and mirror; Mercury, a moneybag and entwined snakes. Jupiter, Mars, Sol, and Luna each have different listed attributes. KdiH separately describes a urine-examining doctor at 15r and marks that image “without text,” between the zodiac unit and Fortune. [KdiH Cgm 5185](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/11/4/32).

**Inference:** this is a single named program where Saturn’s support and Venus’s flowers co-occur, but it does not attest Saturn’s beads/rosary, a physician as Mercury, or the physician and Sun-pointer as members of that planetary sequence. The separate 15r image cannot be folded into Text 2 just because both occur in one codex. The cited cycle has seven members; a hypothetical subset account would need an explicit selection principle and an account of omitted members.

KdiH describes Tübingen UB Md 2’s text “Von den sieben Planeten und ihren Kindern” (266va–272r) with six surviving planet images and associated occupations. At 271r Mercury is a physician holding a urine vessel, astrolabe, and open book. The entry describes Saturn as a farmer with a shovel, Venus with mirror and torch, Jupiter with scepter and globe, Mars with martial equipment, and Luna with horn and torch. [KdiH Tübingen Md 2](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/11/4/43).

**Inference:** this different witness gives a physician embedded in a planetary program, but is not evidence of an independent origin or of the full proposed bundle. Its catalogued members do not provide the flower-holder or Saturn’s beads/staff combination. The cited cycle is seven-membered; any subset hypothesis needs to state why it selects some figures and omits others.

The Tübingen codex also contains separate programs that should not be conflated with that planetary unit: a four-part life-ages/seasons image at 23r and a microcosm image at 37va described in the prior bounded source report. The latter’s pointing elder is not part of the planetary sequence. Likewise Cgm 5185’s late sphere diagram and physician belong to different catalogued sections. These examples show that a single manuscript can juxtapose relevant-looking motifs while preserving distinct text-image programs; physical co-presence in one codex is not proof of one argument.

## Rival strength and unresolved members

The planetary alternative earns consideration because these sources show planetary cycles with varied attributes and explanatory text. Cgm 5185 gives Saturn-with-crutch and Venus-with-flowers in one sequence; Md 2 gives Mercury-with-urinal in another witness. These examples cannot be assembled across manuscripts into one attested model, and the shared manuscript category does not establish independent origins. Cgm 5185 supplies the strongest single-witness overlap; neither comparator yields the full combination.

The cited cycles each comprise seven planetary members; they do not establish that every hypothetical planetary subset must include Moon, Mars, or Jupiter. A subset proposal would need a principled selection rule and a concrete account of omitted members rather than selecting only matching resemblances. No role-to-target-sector assignment is proposed here. This check found no evidence for a central-Sun pointing figure in the same Mercury/Saturn/Venus program.
