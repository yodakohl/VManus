## A continuous cautious account of the whole unit

The heaven is round, and the Sun and stars follow differing paths. Were their paths the same, the account says they would encounter one another and break. It compares Earth–Moon distance with Sun–stars distance, apparently threefold, then gives the stars greatest power because of their proximity to heaven. The Sun derives its power from them. Books call the twelve annual solar paths the twelve signs and relate them to the months. The passage describes change through that annual sequence and says that a Sun confined to one sign would remove the year's change and the day/night and summer/winter distinctions named there. A partially unread construction/purpose clause must remain in any full rendition.

It then asks why months differ in nature despite or within the described recurring solar course. The answer connects a sign's changing nature with the same planet to which that sign is assigned. A stellar referent's nature is further considered: that referent goes nearest to the planet and also runs in the sign. According to this, the final mon is apportioned. The ownership of the two relative-clause predicates is clearer than the final Moon/month interpretation. Nothing here establishes a physical model in which the Sun is literally inside another planet.

This paraphrase preserves the full chain but is not offered as a fully resolved historical translation. In particular, the exact creation/purpose clause, some path grammar, comparison scope, nearest-body domain and final output remain qualifications.

## Relation direction and scope: what can now be stated more precisely

**Assignment has a grammatical direction.** In R13–R14, the sign is the subject of the relative construction: the sign is assigned **to the same planet**. Thus the source supports a relation of the form `ASSIGNED_TO(sign,planet)`. It does not give the reverse ownership statement that a planet is assigned to one sign, a numerical lord table, or a one-to-one mapping. No actual sign/planet names occur in this tail to instantiate such a table.

**The final two predicates share one stellar referent.** In R14–R16, the antecedent is `das gestirn`; its relative clause says that it goes nearest to the planet **and also** runs in the sign. The same grammatical subject supplies both relations. A faithful exploratory account cannot satisfy nearness with one unnamed object and same-sign traversal with another object without adding a different parse. This is an actual relational ownership constraint, not an inference from the proximity of pictured figures.

**One referent need not be one physical star.** `Gestirn` can be collective, as it is elsewhere in this Item. Shared relative-clause ownership identifies one discourse referent; it does not prove that this referent is an individual celestial body rather than a constellation or collective. An individual-bearer model is an additional assumption. A collective model must say whether nearest and same-sign predicates apply to the group as a whole or distributively to its members. The grammatical conjunction alone does not decide that ontology.

**The repeated planet is an anaphoric link, not a new name each time.** R12 introduces the relevant planet; R13/R14 repeat “the same planet”; R15 returns to “the planet.” Maintaining that chain is the natural reading. No hidden succession of three unrelated planets is supplied by those repetitions. The source does not yet identify this planet as a particular one of the traditional seven.

**The phrase about the Sun is not yet a typed spatial law.** R12 reads as the Sun traveling “in whichever planet.” A coherent astronomical reading may interpret this through the planet's assigned sign, which the next clause explicitly mentions. That would be one metonymic/elliptical bridge: the planet stands for a sign assigned to it. The bridge is plausible but is not an extra written `sign` in R12. Alternatively the phrase may encode a broader astrological configuration. Neither option is silently converted to the Sun's literal physical containment in a planet or a modern aspect definition.

**Nearest among what remains open.** R15's superlative supports a nearest relation, but the comparison domain and metric are not stated. “Nearest overall, and also in the sign” and “nearest among bodies in the sign” need not choose the same bearer. The conjunction by itself does not settle that selection order. No angular distance, unique minimum or tie-break is supplied.

**Final mon remains a real two-way reading.** The same short spelling in V6 clearly denotes Moon because it is a distance endpoint. R4/R5/R11 use fuller month forms. That favors taking the final short form seriously as potentially lunar. The immediate unequal-month question, however, favors month as a discourse continuation. Orthographic reuse and discourse pull in different directions. This reading does not eliminate either possibility or manufacture a month-to-Moon identity. The verb of apportionment also needs more secure reading before a temporal subdivision can be claimed.

A useful **prospective** constructor is a restricted celestial-bearer reference, not a sentence macro:

```text
STELLAR_BEARER(p,s,c) selects q only under both
    NEAREST(q,p,c) and RUNS_IN(q,s,c).
The nature subsequently mentioned is NATURE(q,c).
ASSIGNED_TO(s,p) is a separate relation.
```

Here `c` is an explicitly declared situation, not an inferred date. Introducing that context, choosing a nearest comparison domain, choosing/denoting q where ties are possible, and resolving the Sun/planet phrase are added modeling costs. The source does not provide numerical nature values or a combining function from the two influences to a final month/Moon value. This constructor therefore preserves an attributable input and its ownership; it does not pretend to compute weather or duration.

Two concrete non-vacuous future contrasts follow from the **individual-bearer variant** of the proposed shared-reference interpretation. If q is nearest but lies outside the relevant sign while r lies in that sign, the two different objects do not jointly satisfy one bearer reference merely because each meets one condition. If a single q satisfies both, its nature is the relevant attributed input, rather than r's convenient nature. These are declared logical cases, not celestial observations or target cases. Under a restricted-nearest-domain variant, the first configuration must be reconsidered within that declared domain; it is not automatically a source contradiction.

The strongest rival is not an arity error. A **static per-sign configuration account** can preserve the assigned planet, neighboring stellar referent and their attributed natures as a fixed combined description of each sign. If different months within one year are the comparison, that can explain their different natures without any same-sign change of neighbor. A **collective-bearer account** can preserve the shared grammatical referent without making it one physical star. A **separate-witness account** would need a different syntactic or distributive analysis before assigning nearness and same-sign motion to distinct physical referents. The present unit gives written reasons to retain the assignment and shared relative subject; it does not alone prove occasion-dependent neighbor replacement, a deterministic month-output law, or two historically observed varying configurations.
