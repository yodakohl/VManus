The centered captions and surrounding verse need to be kept distinct. The verse on 68v names `glentz` (Spring), the surrounding verse on 69v names Summer, and the verse on 70v names Herbst (Autumn); these are direct page-context associations, not words in the figure captions. The fourth caption calls for an old woman by the fire; KdiH frames the four-age passage in connection with the four seasons, but the caption itself does not say “Winter.” The fire is the only explicit prop in the age sequence. The physician caption is the only one that explicitly names an action.

The catalog classifies 72v as *Urinschau* and its image-program description says a physician at a lectern holds up a urine vessel to determine a patient's temperament. That is useful catalog-level interpretation, but those details are not present in the native caption `ein artzt der wasser besicht`. The blank space remains unillustrated in this witness: instructions are evidence for an intended program, not evidence that the requested figures or objects were painted here.

The reading above is a bounded visual transcription, not a critical-edition text. In particular, the third caption's descriptor remains unresolved: on rereading image 153 it can be read as `bestanden`, while the first pass read `bescheiden`; both are recorded, and no age/character nuance is inferred from either. ## Clarification after rereading page context

The season association is stronger than the short caption alone suggests: page 68v's verse explicitly names `glentz`, page 69v names `Summer`, and page 70v names `herbst`. Those are surrounding verse rubrics attached to the age sequence; the centered illustration instructions still ask respectively for a pretty young woman, a young woman, and a woman with an uncertain descriptor. The fourth centered instruction is “an old woman by the fire.” I do not make the caption itself say Winter or supply the missing exact fourth season rubric from inference.

The physician caption on 72v (`ein artzt der wasser besicht`) sits above the lower verse on that same page, which gives the change-of-season formulation (approximately, “one season may be healthy … as each season requires”). The facing 72r provides the preceding transition, including `Hie merck wie sich ein mensche ye / nach dem zite sol halten` (approximate reading). The caption does not say that the doctor is an introduction to complexion theory. The 1491 print places a doctor-with-flask image on scan 172 with the verses introducing the year’s four natures/states, before the generic complexion introduction on print scan 175; scan 178 later begins the first named complexion, “Von der ersten complexion / Sanguineus.” Karlsruhe platform 91v instead places its physician drawing with the red rubric about human nature according to the four complexions. These are different witness placements. They caution against treating the doctor as a fifth season or assigning one fixed text-section boundary from a single witness. The manuscript instruction itself remains only “doctor inspecting the water”; no vessel or gesture is written into it.

The Cgm 377 seasonal sequence and its surrounding verse text are visible here, but the planned spaces remain unillustrated. The evidence describes a requested program, not executed figures.
