## Exact critical readings and semantic caution

Long-s is rendered s, and an abbreviation written dz is retained as dz.
Punctuation and modern clause division are not present as asserted here.

172, last six lines:

> Das dz jare nach yedem stat
> vierlerley naturen hat
> Darnach sich auch die leibe hie
> verwandlend ire naturen ye
> Dar an verstastu hie zestunde
> dz ein zeitte möchte sein gesunde

173, first four lines:

> Das ander zeitte schade were
> von diser wandelbere
> So mustu ye regieren dich
> als yedes zeitte heischet sich

The report's time-dependent benefit/harm interpretation is plausible. The
contrast carries modal forms möchte/were and two temporal expressions; it
does not identify any actual treatment or named food. The repeated dz/Das
allows a generic evaluated-thing reading with time changed, but the exact
clause attachment is less explicit than the later recipient contrast. A
reading about one time being wholesome and another harmful cannot simply
be excluded from this working transcript. Do not turn this ambiguity into
an independently confirmed same-dose experiment. Both time and body natures
are discussed, with plural bodies changing; fixed intrinsic constitution or
one persistent individual patient is not thereby established.

173, lines17–23:

> Der sieche vnd der gesunde
> hand vngeleiche stunde
> Da von so ist dem einen gütt
> das dem andern we thütt
> Der alte vnd der junge
> hand ander wandelunge
> An der kelte an der hitze

Here the same generic subject of being good for one and hurting the other
is considerably clearer: the relative das supplies the shared subject of
the second evaluation. The recipient changes, rather than the evaluated
thing being silently replaced. The preceding sick/healthy pair supplies
the relevant contrast, but the verse does not identify which concrete item,
which physical portion or which direction of treatment benefit applies to
each named class. In particular do not generalize that everything helpful
to sick people harms healthy people, or that the two recipient labels name
successive states of the same patient. A generic comparative assertion can
be retained without upgrading it to either such universal rule.

The old/young clause gives differing changes with cold and heat, but no
directional age-to-heat ranking.174 continues with the need for judgment,
experienced physicians and wise people. It does not retroactively give a
hidden polarity to every prior noun.
