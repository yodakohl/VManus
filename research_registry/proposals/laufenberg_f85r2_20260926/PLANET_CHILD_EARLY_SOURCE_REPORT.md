# Earlier planet-child conclusion: early witness lead, full unit unverified

27 September2026. **A precise pre1430 candidate was located, but no early
full conclusion was checked.** The bounded search therefore strengthens the
bibliographical route, not yet the primary attestation of the mixed-influence
paragraph at that date. It neither finds the paragraph absent nor dates the
already read Md2/Berlin wording retrospectively to1405.

## Early object and the remaining attribution gap

[KdiH11.4.2](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/11/4/2) identifies
**Augsburg, Universitätsbibliothek, Cod.III.1.4º1**, localized to Ulm and dated
1400–1405, with additions until about1414. It identifies Johannes Wissbier
among the scribes. The [official HSP resolver](https://resolver.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/HSP0003F3A100000000)
resolves to object **HSP-868b9e38-87d8-3140-bbab-995ed87910a4**; its public
metadata gives the same early range. These are catalogue findings, not a
fresh dating or an inspected page-specific colophon. The modern catalogues
also disagree in their displayed total-leaf accounting; none is used to infer
a paragraph boundary.

[Brévart1996](https://www.enotes.com/topics/calendar/criticism/criticism-calendars-and-culture/francois-b-brevart-essay-date-1996),
notes87–89, places the planetary tract at **22v–26v** of this codex. He links
the theoretical conclusion to VolkskalenderA and quotes it through
**Stegemann1944,p63**. His discussion dates the calendar's completion to
17January1405. That dates a textual section, not automatically every clause
of the planetary tract. The conclusion's quotation and the Augsburg folio
range are adjacent bibliographical evidence; without the edition's witness
attribution or the actual manuscript, their juxtaposition is not a checked
Augsburg transcription. The article also explains why Princeton Kane52,
despite its1435 colophon, is not a substitute: its copy ends with the calendar
and lacks the two astrological treatises.

Thus the strongest result is a named early physical candidate with an exact
tract range and a specific edition page to verify. **The exact conclusion
start/end within22v–26v remains unestablished.**26v is the reported tract end,
not a natively verified conclusion folio here.

## Rejected substitution and comparison scope

A discovery hit also identified [Fribourg Ms.L309](https://www.e-codices.ch/en/description/bcuf/L0309/Jurot),
with the planetary tract and a matching conclusion incipit at25v–28v. Its
catalogue dates the relevant part to the second third of the fifteenth century,
with1440s/1450s watermark comparisons. It therefore did not meet the preferred
early boundary and was not opened as a source image or substituted after the
Augsburg access gap. Its incipit alone is no full mixed-influence attestation.

The existing [Berlin result](PLANET_CHILD_EXTERNAL_PARALLEL_REPORT.md) remains
unchanged: another late physical witness, not independent textual origin.
The present early lead similarly does not demonstrate a stemma, direct ancestry
of Md2 or Berlin, common Voynich source, or any target meaning. A1405 context
would improve chronological plausibility only after the actual unit is checked.

## Smallest next source inspection, if separately chosen

Obtain either the full Augsburg conclusion with its neighbouring textual
boundary, or Stegemannp63 plus the edition's explicit witness identification.
The latter may identify a different witness; it must not be presumed to be
Augsburg. The source-specific question is whether the connected sequence of
power/birth-or-conception, multiple contributors, unequal properties and
predominant naming is present in the early witness, and which clauses differ.
Its presence, a shorter precursor or its absence would be distinct source-history
results. None would by itself select572's target grammar or alter its fixed
Md2 source unit. No broader planetary image search is needed for this question.
