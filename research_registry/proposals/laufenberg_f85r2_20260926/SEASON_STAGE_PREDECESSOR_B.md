## Conditions for a genuinely shared operator test

The reports leave room for a new, carefully bounded composition hypothesis, but they do not provide its operator or meanings. Before any selection, a useful test would have to name the two complete, distinct written arguments and the exact repeated internal substring/cut prospectively; assign that substring one typed operation with an explicit input/output signature; and show the same unchanged operation being used in both full derivations. Each argument must be independently delimited by its local context, and every whole-form occurrence, unmatched residual, alternative reading, and surface-order assumption must remain accounted for. No typed cast, context-driven redefinition, owner supplied only from the source, or change of boundary between the two examples can be hidden in the shared-operator claim. A valid local result would still be a working hypothesis about two occurrences in one manuscript, not proof of a general prefix law or confirmation of Megenberg's historical model.

Thus GDT753 supplies the main retained counterexample to overclaiming components; GDT717 supplies a narrow exact-whole quality/stage success that must not be generalized; GDT699 supplies a bounded example of explicit, unique relation attachment; and GDT818 retains local-attachment debt. The relevant decision for RAW595 is not whether earlier reports already assign its terms. It is whether two written Megenberg arguments can share an unchanged, independently constrained internal operation while preserving their distinct meanings and all surrounding obligations. These predecessor results make that a testable condition, not an established outcome.
