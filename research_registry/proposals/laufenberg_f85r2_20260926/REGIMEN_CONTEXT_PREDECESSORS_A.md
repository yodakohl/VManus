## Exact difference from 558

The frozen 558 regimen formula is, schematically, `NOT FORALL person p, treatment t: Benefit(p,t)`. Its review explicitly rejects upgrading this to every person having an unhelpful treatment, and the relevant source-coverage entry still lacks the full person-specific criterion connection. It contains no automatic equation `Harm = NOT Benefit`.

A possible new contrast would instead require a source-appropriate binder for a shared input `x`, separately written contexts `c1` and `c2`, and positive predicates resembling `Benefit(x,c1)` and `Harm(x,c2)`. These are a schema for the unknown obligation, **not** a transcription, selected source quantifier, or already assigned target formula. The eventual source may be generic, modal, conditional, or distributive; that force must be retained rather than silently replaced by two actual events.

The distinction is real: a model with one unhelpful person/treatment pair, no harmful pair anywhere, and no shared-input positive contrast can satisfy 558's nonuniversality. It does not supply the proposed positive contrast. Conversely, a shared item can have opposite evaluations under different contexts without contradictory predicates in the same context. Harmful is stronger than simply unhelpful. Neither contrast alone entails a causal explanation of why the result changes.

There are also ownership debts independent of the quantifier distinction. A generic thing can be evaluated relative to two recipient classes without identifying one unchanged patient across those classes. A temporal contrast can concern a generic item without identifying one material token carried through time. A year with changing bodily conditions cannot be reduced to a constant intrinsic constitution merely because that was the old card's intended narrative. An age contrast does not supply a directed hot/cold or young/old advice table without the missing source assertions.

The 558 root review also matters for type claims: its disputed `Datum` attachment was not proven disjoint from propositions, and an explicit special production already licensed the relevant attachment. ## What a genuinely distinct construction would have to do

The source could motivate one shared evaluation relation whose explicitly written input remains fixed while a context argument changes. That is a meaningful content constraint if both uses actually bind the same input and the consequences distinguish benefit from harm. It becomes a compositional proposal only when some **written reusable operation** retains that input/owner while accepting different context arguments, with a consequence beyond replacing each clause by an unrelated English name.

At least two uses should expose the operation's argument behavior, not merely repeat an output label. Time and recipient can be different typed context inputs without being arbitrarily interchangeable; a common context record or lifting rule would itself be a new, counted commitment. A single clause saying that things vary is not the same as actually deriving the two applications. The author must expose binder versus reference, generic kind versus material witness, context versus owner, and modal versus actual application. No preconfirmed word is required for an exploratory proposal, but all these commitments remain hypotheses.

The strongest rivals are still serious:

1. **Separately qualified clauses:** two ordinary assertions bind a generic input and state their own contexts, without a shared word-part constructor. They can reproduce the entire source argument.
2. **Case-indexed response relation:** one relation directly maps input/context pairs to benefit or harm. A more articulated constructor may be only a notational factorization unless it predicts some additional written reuse or excludes a case.
3. **Contextual lexical sense or polysemy:** a surface form may behave differently in different grammatical or discourse environments. This cannot be accepted as unlimited repair, but it is not eliminated by recurring forms alone.
4. **Different-input or broad-regimen account:** if the source does not identify the evaluated thing across clauses, changing advice may concern different items. The shared-input hypothesis then loses its source bridge, while a broader medical account remains possible.

Neither smaller dictionary size nor a successfully stated relation inventory confirms a medieval reading. GDT540/551 already demonstrate why changed context and changed lexical meaning must be distinguished at the level of an explicit model. They do not provide a preauthorized context carry-over law for this source or target.
