## Object, dating and finding path

The [official HSP record](https://handschriftenportal.de/?id=HSP-842382e4-cb59-31a9-a310-b43a1a18015f)
identifies the codex as fifteenth-century, circa1458/circa1462, associated with
Augsburg/Regensburg. The [SBB manifest](https://content.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/dc/PPN785310398/manifest)
uses circa1462, records24 leaves and maps22v/23r to canvases48/49. The manuscript
is held under this shelfmark, not the unrelated Hamilton557.

The decisive search lead was Annett Klingner's2017 dissertation,
[*Die Macht der Sterne*](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/1833435a-7de9-4dfa-a0bc-4b4e19e4de2a/content),
appendix pp309–310, identifying the planetary tract at19v–23r and printing its
conclusion. This was navigation and prior textual exposure; the result below
comes from checking the official source images. A separate secondary lead
through Brévart's1996 Volkskalender discussion pointed to Stegemann1944,p63.
It was not independently acquired or counted as an additional witness.

The initial three exact-clause queries produced mostly irrelevant results;
the fifth, combining human/planet/east vocabulary, exposed the connected text.
The sixth located the official Berlin record. No further discovery search,
second witness acquisition or access-repair chain followed the success.

## Complete connected content, cautious paraphrase

After recapping seven planets and their children, the paragraph defines a
planet's child through birth or conception in the mother when the planet has
great power **or** rises from the east. Although many planets are powerful
and rising, the strongest then governs/influences and gives properties
according to its nature. One planet rarely has all power and lordship. Its
giving is qualified by a difficult capacity phrase; other planets also give
properties according to their strengths. A human consequently receives from
more than one: two, some three, some all, with little or much from each.
Nevertheless the human bears the name of the planet supplying the most
properties. The exposition then explicitly ends.

The difficult phrase at23rB1 is tentatively **[allsinl/allsint?] und er mag**;
B3's short word after the recipient is **[dan/din?]**. Neither is normalized
into a ratio or exact transfer law. The subsequent plural donor/strength
construction is clearer. These uncertainties remain despite the printed
transcription having suggested readings beforehand.

## Material comparison, without repairing Md2

The [unchanged Md2 transcript](MERCURY_LOCAL_TEXT_ROOT.md) ends with an extra
birth/day/hour qualification absent before Berlin's explicit ending. Md2's
shorter power/rising wording also differs from the explicit disjunction and
multi-planet qualification above. Around Md2B18–20 its singular/pronominal
giving clause lacks the fully expressed plural donor statement checked here.

Those differences matter for572's proposed scope contracts. They support
preserving an abbreviated or divergent witness account instead of treating
every clause as a universally fixed tradition. They do **not** select a
birth-versus-conception procedure, establish equal power/share rankings or
supply a numerical weighting algorithm. The clearer pronouns of another
witness cannot silently replace an uncertain pronoun in Md2. Nor can the
shorter Berlin ending delete Md2's written tail.

The combination and sequence of clauses support a relationship at the level
of transmitted text, beyond generic planet iconography. A common ancestor,
intermediate exemplar or direct dependency has not been reconstructed. The
Volkskalender connection is a bibliographical lead, not a newly proved stemma.
Regional cataloguing is not a Voynich provenance argument.
