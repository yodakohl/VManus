# Bounded Latin-witness access check: BAV Vat. lat. 2383

## Result

The official Vatican catalogue establishes that Vat. lat. 2383 contains *De facultatibus naturalibus*, books I–III, in Burgundio of Pisa's Latin translation, on ff. 1r–21r. This corrects the earlier uncertainty about whether that named witness represents the full work. I could not retrieve a verified manuscript image through the official viewer within the five-minute access limit, so I did **not** inspect or transcribe the Latin wording of book III, chapter 13. Whether the particular fasting liver-vein/body distinction is present in this codex remains unverified by native text inspection.

## Official records and attempted image access

The [official Vatican Digital Library viewer](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Vat.lat.2383) links to its `READ MORE` catalogue detail. That official record identifies manuscript Vat. lat. 2383, date range 1301–1400, locus 1r–21r, title *De facultatibus naturalibus*, and summary “Lib. I-III; interpretatio latina Burgundionis Pisani.” It identifies the Vatican Library as holding institution. The standard browse link redirects to the viewer.

The viewer displayed a field for entering a IIIF manifest URI, but no manifest URI was exposed as a page link in the ordinary viewer/detail navigation. I made one standard manifest lookup at `https://digi.vatlib.it/iiif/MSS_Vat.lat.2383/manifest.json`; it was inaccessible through the available browser tool. I stopped there rather than guessing alternate API paths or attempting a workaround. No canvas/image was loaded, so there is no image hash or native reading to report.

## What follows and what does not

The catalogue directly supports a complete three-book Latin text in this fourteenth-century Vatican witness; the earlier rubric/extent caveat for Wellcome MS.6013 is therefore materially narrowed by a second, clearly itemized witness. The specific III.13 argument is expected to fall within this complete text, but catalogue metadata does not itself collate or quote the fasting liver-vein versus organ-body paragraph. Exact manuscript wording, clause sequence, and local Latin uncertainty remain an access gap. This result bears on historical source transmission only, not on Voynich language or word assignment.

No Voynich target, author draft, reserve, other manuscript witness, or outside contact was used.
