# Schermar Ms. Med. 8: source comparator check

2026-09-26. Bounded source-only review. No Voynich target image or text was accessed. A source-selection decision note preceded the further scan search. No exact public scan was identified, so this report is catalogue-only; this is an access result, not a claim that reproductions do not exist.

## Primary catalogue facts

KdiH identifies the former Ulm Schermar-Bibliothek, Ms. Med. 8 (now private property), as an East Swabian manuscript from the mid to later fifteenth century, formerly in the Schermar family library. It is a bound, multi-part astrological-medical collection. Its first described unit is a calendar poem at 49r–50v; the next is a large *Planetenbuch* beginning 50v. KdiH describes 49v as a full-page quartered roundel with inscriptions in its frame: the quarters show saints whose feast days mark the beginning of the four seasons, and the corresponding outer figures are winged evangelist symbols. For 58v, within the *Planetenbuch*, the catalogue describes a four-element earth encircled by a planetary sphere and held by two “Amtmänner,” shown from the hips emerging from cloud bands, one naked and the other armored. (Catalogue-derived summary: 106 words.) [KdiH 11.4.44](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/11/4/44), contents and image descriptions.

## Relational reading and limits

The primary catalogue distinguishes the two relevant layouts by content unit and function. The 49v seasonal opening belongs to the calendar poem, whereas 58v is inside the *Planetenbuch*. This is not one page or an explicitly continuous argument linking saints, winged evangelist emblems, elemental quarters, planetary spheres, and cloud-emerging figures. The codex’s composite character makes adjacency insufficient to join the units.

The source is nevertheless an unusually compact comparator for several forms together: an inscribed round frame, quartered central imagery, outside winged forms, and another inscribed circle with cloud bands and paired half-figures. The wings are specifically catalogued as evangelist symbols associated with the saints, so this source gives a documented alternative to identifying a winged/feathered perimeter figure as a wind. That does not determine any target’s identity. The cloud figures are the two planetary-office figures in an explicitly planetary unit, but they are not the four season-opening saints; nor does the catalogue call them wind figures or point to a central sun. The paired layouts therefore create multiple source analogies but leave the proposed cross-layout relationship unproved.
