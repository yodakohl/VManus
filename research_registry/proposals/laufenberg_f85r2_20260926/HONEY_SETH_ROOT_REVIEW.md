# Root native review of Seth's complete honey entry

26September2026. Root read the entire already-rendered Greek printed p69 of Langkavel1868, the same scan recorded in HONEY_SETH_PROVENANCE_RECEIPTS.json, including its heading, full entry and visible apparatus. This is a second informed inspection of one edition, not an independent textual witness or collation. Root had read both agent reports and Noble's full entry before viewing the Greek image. No target material was opened.

The recipient clauses are legible: `γέρουσι` and `ψυχραῖς κράσεσι` occur with suitability, while `τοῖς δὲ ἀκμάζουσι καὶ θερμοῖς` occurs with provoking bile. The old/cold and prime/hot historical comparison therefore has an actual identified edition behind it. It remains separate from Laufenberg and supplies no manuscript word meaning.

Two corrections to the earlier translation-based summary should remain visible:

1. I agree with B that `ἑψηθῇ` and the foam-removal instruction support **boiling and skimming**, not Noble's fermentation process. A processing parameter cannot be imported from that English sentence as if verified by the Greek.
2. The Greek says spring honey is better, then winter honey unusable, then separately says it is readily changed into yellow bile, particularly in warmer bellies. There is no explicit causal connective equivalent to the English translation's **because** between winter unusability and transformation. The subject of the continuing honey description may be broader than winter honey. Retain the adjacency and the general transformation qualification; do not assert a uniquely winter-caused bile mechanism from this punctuation or translation alone.

The quality term `δριμύ` is pungent/acrid; it must not automatically become Galen's bitter endpoint. The passage says honey boiled by itself becomes milder in that quality and more nourishing. This does not refute Galen's claim about excessive heating; the two units do not fix the same heating duration, dilution or endpoint. Conversely, do not invent a numerical threshold to force harmony. `εὐαναδοτώτερον` and the later `δύσπεπτα` are distinct written terms; this review does not equate distribution/assimilation and digestion. The precise bodily sense of `κόλπων` remains unresolved and is not silently filled as a named organ from Noble.

The rest of the complete entry includes preservation, an ancient funerary use, comparison with wine for epilepsy, appetite in a phlegmatic case, sputum, poorly prepared honey products, liver/spleen obstruction and excess-related itching. No omitted detail is claimed to be mapped into RAW565. Historical advice remains attributed to the source. The apparatus shows alternate readings; no stemma or particular medieval witness was independently examined.

Decision: retain the recipient-conditioned honey comparison as a historically sourced content option; correct the preparation and causal-link overreadings. Its provenance is stronger than an unattributed modern translation, but neither an Upper Rhine borrowing nor any Voynich meaning has been established.
