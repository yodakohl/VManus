## One consequence beyond new names: preserve the contrast's common argument

The connected source argument requires more than two lists of useful and harmful things. Within each contrast, the beneficial and harmful assessments concern the **same kind of conduct/thing**, with different cases. It does not thereby concern the same physical administration, dosage or patient. A disciplined semantic skeleton is:

| Source contrast | Preserved argument | Distinct written conditions needed in a target reading | Positive assertions that must remain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seasonal contrast | One conduct-kind A | Two different times/seasonal situations, with the associated bodily conditions | A is beneficial in case U; A can be harmful in case V. |
| Sick/healthy contrast | One conduct-kind B | Sick versus healthy recipients/cases | B helps in one case and can hurt in the other. |

**A=B is not supplied.** Neither is an identification of either seasonal case with either health case. The old/young cold/heat comparison adds a difference requiring judgment but does not supply its signed response directions. There is therefore no source-given four-cell treatment table, no selected summer/winter pair, and no claim that health or season is the only causal variable.

Formally, the reading should own claims of the form `Beneficial(A,U)` and `CanHarm(A,V)` with U and V distinguished, and an independently tracked B for the second pair. The possibility force in the public source account must remain; this is not `every administration in V causes harm`. These are historical contextual assessment claims, not measured clinical outcomes. They are not a total single-valued response function: the source does not state that benefit and harm are exhaustive, that neutrality is impossible, or that simultaneous mixed effects on different dimensions are excluded. Positive harmful-response content is not silently replaced by NOT-BENEFICIAL.

The decision-changing consequence is a **restriction on transferring the assertion**: the assessment must retain its conduct-kind and case arguments. Forgetting the case, switching the conduct-kind between the two halves, or turning advice into an accomplished treatment changes the expressed content. This is a concrete relational consequence even before the conduct is given a name. It remains an additional target-binding hypothesis until written forms and scope actually own those arguments.

This formulation does not require body state to remain unchanged between seasons. The source explicitly permits bodily change.532's persistent intrinsic-constitution field can remain a declared hypothesis while current bodily state varies, but the source does not independently establish the persistence of any selected patient's constitution. No hidden state update is inserted.
