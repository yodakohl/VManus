## Main finding

Karlsruhe **Cod. K 2790** supplies a real, closely spaced sequence of Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter, then a urine-inspecting physician. Crucially, **the one-staff figure with hanging beads is explicitly Autumn; Winter is a separate, more stooped figure with two supports on the same page**. This qualifies any exclusive Winter attribution without making staff-bearing old age exclusive to Autumn. Spring also holds a flower, so a flower alone does not uniquely identify Summer.

Laufenberg composed the *Regimen* in Strasbourg in 1429; that is the work's date, not the date of the surviving illustrated copies. Its chapter on life ages associates them with seasons; the physician introduces a different medical topic. The cycle varies among witnesses. These distinctions follow the [KdiH work-level account](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/untergruppe/87/3). The observed images below establish the particular attributes.

**Foliation discrepancy retained:** KdiH lists the Karlsruhe urine physician at **92v**, whereas the institution's manifest labels the physician canvas **91v**. The inspected rectos display two numerations, with the lower pencil number one greater than the upper number (e.g. platform 91r shows upper 91 and lower 92). This may be relevant, but it does not by itself reconcile all catalog references: KdiH's life-age range agrees with the platform labels. Cite **canvas/page 5967406** with “platform 91v; catalog 92v” until resolved. Do not silently report either as an unproblematic single foliation.

The sharper [91r institutional rendition](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/i3f/v20/5967405/full/2200,/0/default.jpg) was inspected specifically to distinguish the two rubrics and supports. Rubric readings here are selective, not a critical transcription or a claim of specialist palaeographic authority.

## Tübingen Md 2: distinct diagram programs

The institutional record places it in fifteenth-century Württemberg; KdiH specifies mid-century, Ulm/Urach. This note does not replace those with a more precise popular date. Its health-text component includes Konrad von Eichstätt; it must not simply be called another Laufenberg *Regimen* witness.

## Broader housebook controls, catalog only

**St. Gallen, Stiftsbibliothek, Cod. Sang. 760**, c.1450, southern Germany/Switzerland: the [institutional record](https://www.e-codices.ch/de/searchresult/list/one/csg/0760) explicitly combines zodiac, planets, four temperaments and four seasons with health regimens. Its [digitization/manifest](https://www.e-codices.ch/metadata/iiif/csg-0760/manifest.json) is available; no pixels or exact season pages were inspected here. This is a concrete non-Laufenberg housebook lead, not an established motif-by-motif match.

**Zürich, Zentralbibliothek, Ms. C 54**, the Schürstab codex, c.1470, **Nuremberg**: [KdiH](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/87/1/18), [institutional digitization](https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/de/list/one/zbz/C0054). The catalog places its iatromathematical housebook at3r–55v, with calendrical/medical illustration. No pixels inspected. This supplies a concrete caution against treating the wider housebook genre as exclusively Upper Rhine. It does not exclude an Upper Rhine origin for any particular work or manuscript.

## Limits and usable next comparison

The source-side bundle supports a **testable iconographic analogy**, not demonstrated copying, common authorship, a geographic attribution of the Voynich manuscript, or any word meaning. Native inspection of the target under its own registered scope would have to compare exact posture, number of supports, object geometry, arrangement and accompanying text independently of the desired season assignments. A three-seasons-plus-doctor selection, if proposed, must openly account for omitted Winter; neither Karlsruhe nor the general tradition supplies that omission automatically. The source physician belongs to a separate topic, which also constrains any presumed text order.
