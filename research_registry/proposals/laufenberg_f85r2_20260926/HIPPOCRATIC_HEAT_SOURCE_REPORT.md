# Internal heat, seasons, age and food: two source arguments kept distinct

2026-09-26. Independent source-only audit under [HIPPOCRATIC_HEAT_SOURCE_DECISION.md](HIPPOCRATIC_HEAT_SOURCE_DECISION.md), written before image inspection. No Voynich transcription or image file was opened, no IDEA551 lexicon was read, and no new source image was fetched. The requested predecessor cards contained report-level target examples; those were not interpreted or used as inputs. This concerns historical claims in the sources, not medical advice.

**Result: the proposed strong Laufenberg–Hippocrates mechanism is not established.** Hippocrates I.13–15 explicitly connects innate bodily heat with food need. Laufenberg's selected pages supply more than generic quality labels: a complete four-season regimen, age analogies, and the beginning of a human-constitution account. They distinguish a person from the season and recommend different food, shelter and thermal conduct. However, the decisive winter food/warmth wording is partly unresolved; it does not securely yield the full proposition “winter increases internal bodily heat, therefore more food is needed.” Retain the positive seasonal-regimen evidence and the separate Hippocratic hypothesis. Do not treat this limited reading as proof that Laufenberg lacks such a mechanism elsewhere.

## 2. All three Hippocratic aphorisms

The following compact account retains every substantive clause of the fixed unit:

| Unit | Complete content retained |
|---|---|
| I.13 | Fasting is easiest for old people, next adults, harder for young people, and hardest for infants—especially particularly lively infants. |
| I.14 | Growing bodies possess the greatest innate heat and therefore need the most food; otherwise they waste away. Old people have weak heat and need little fuel for that flame: too much would extinguish it. Their fevers are also less acute because their bodies are cold. |
| I.15 | In **Winter and Spring**, the bowels are naturally hottest and sleep longest. Most sustenance is prescribed then because the belly's greater innate heat needs most food. Young people and athletes are cited as supporting examples. |

The heat owner is explicitly the growing/old **body** or the **bowels/belly**, not the ambient season. Sleep is a retained accompanying statement; the text's stated reason for greater food is innate heat, not an invented causal effect of sleep. “Less acute” retains the translation's comparative without supplying a newly quantified reference class. I.13 gives a ranking; connecting that ranking causally to I.14 is a plausible reading across consecutive aphorisms, not a separately written “because” in I.13. [Complete I.13–15](https://classics.mit.edu/Hippocrates/aphorisms.1.i.html).

## 4. Decisive native wording and exact uncertainties

Readings are provisional manuscript transcriptions; brackets mark unresolved words or endings. German capitalization and occasional abbreviation expansion are editorial. Their value here is to locate the claims, not to provide an edition.

**Summer, 91r upper continuation:**

> `Die sunnen soltu fliehen / Vn an schatten ziehen`  
> `Eine küle wonung suche dir`

The person addressed is instructed to leave solar exposure and seek shade/cool lodging. That explicitly distinguishes the human actor from the environment he should choose. It does **not** say the body's actual innate heat equals the temperature of its surroundings, or that the body is currently cold. Other summer diet/drink instructions surround these lines; no isolated universal cooling rule is exported from them. [Whole 91r](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/i3f/v20/5967405/full/2200,/0/default.jpg).

**Winter, 91v, opening food and warmth passage:**

The following successive physical rows are the critical unresolved stretch, not an omitted qualification:

| Position within the top winter continuation | Best recoverable wording and limit |
|---|---|
| Row 1 | Contains `complexion` and `kalt`; surrounding words and the second quality are not resolved confidently enough to normalize. |
| Row 2 | Approximately `Merck nu d[e] speiß bruchet wol / Me dann uff die and[ern?] zal`. A food/use comparison is plausible; the exact subject, verb sense and comparison scope remain uncertain. |
| Row 3 | `Alle vernunftloſe dyer / [Grebet?] in dem winter schier`. Animals and winter are legible, but the action is not secure. This row prevents silently assigning the next warmth phrase to a human stomach. |
| Row 4 | `[Der der?] wermi bringen mag / Dz man drymal essen deſ tags`. Warmth and a possible three-times-daily eating instruction are readable candidates. The first clause's subject/reference is unresolved; neither an internal bodily location nor causal direction is securely recovered. The meal-frequency reading remains provisional. |
| Rows 5–6 | Spice/drink/need instructions follow; several words remain uncertain. A word resembling `Vaste` before `tranck` is **not** converted into a fasting statement. |
| Rows 7–11 | Air, foods including approximately `rinds fleisch` and `schwin...`, moderation and warmth occur. The clear conclusion is `Vn gar warm halte dich`. The complete exact food list and its imperative polarities are not reconstructed. |
| Rows 12–15 | The text likens the season to an old woman nearing death, with bodily deterioration. This is the conclusion of the winter analogy, not a new statement that all winter bodies have elderly physiology. |

The clear instruction “keep yourself warm” expresses a recommended conduct or resulting state. It does not state that innate body heat is already maximal in winter. Likewise, even if the possible food/warmth clause eventually proves to mean “food brings warmth,” that would be **food → warmth**, not automatically the Hippocratic **innate heat → greater food requirement**. The present reading does not choose either causal analysis. [Whole 91v](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/i3f/v20/5967406/full/2200,/0/default.jpg).

**Human-constitution opening at the bottom of the same page:**

The rubric reads approximately `Wie d[es] mensch[en] natur ist nach d[en] vier complexion`. Within the new unit, `menschlich`, `elemente`, `complexion`, water, fire, earth and air are visible. The final phrase is approximately:

> `Dz für dz kan die wermi / geben`

“Fire can give warmth” belongs to the beginning of the human-constitution discussion. Its human setting supports a constitutive-body interpretation; that ownership is contextual, not an independently transcribed internal-organ locative. The paragraph continues outside the sample. This is a useful distinction from ambient summer heat, but it supplies neither the seasonal increase in internal heat nor the required heat-to-food causal link. Do not combine two different sections into one unwritten sentence.

## 5. Source graphs and the missing bridge

For the Hippocratic unit, the secure explicit relations are: **growing body → high innate heat → high food need**; **old body → weak heat → little fuel**, with excess fuel and fever qualifications; and **Winter/Spring → hot belly → high food need**, with prolonged sleep and the youth/athlete comparison retained. The age-ranking of fasting endurance is an additional statement, not an omitted fourth season.

The safely recovered Laufenberg relations are narrower:

| Written source relation | Status |
|---|---|
| Year has four season divisions; regimen varies with the season | Present, with some linking words unresolved. |
| Summer situation → advice to avoid sun, enter shade, seek cool lodging | Explicit native instructions. |
| Winter situation → food/drink instructions and advice to keep warm | Present; precise food frequency and intervening warmth syntax provisional. |
| Seasons compared with human age/stage figures | Present as analogies, not literal identity or a rule transferring every physiological property. |
| Human constitution involves four elements; fire gives warmth | Present in the beginning of an incomplete next unit; exact owner link partly contextual. |
| Winter/Spring → increased heat **inside the belly/body** → more food | **Not securely recovered.** No complete readable clause in this audit establishes the necessary owner, comparative change and causal direction together. |
| Age-ranked fasting tolerance, lively infant, old-age fever, and youths/athletes as evidence | Not recovered from these Laufenberg pages; no claim of absence throughout the work. |

A simple “season HOT therefore human HOT” transfer is not licensed by either source argument. The opposite error would be to convert cooling advice into an assertion of achieved coldness. The positive Laufenberg evidence justifies separate environmental state, human constitution and regimen-action roles. It does not yet supply the particular Hippocratic mechanism connecting them.

## 6. Decision, predecessors and limits

**Keep:** Hippocrates I.13–15 as a complete, distinct source-content hypothesis with explicit internal heat and all its qualifications. Keep Laufenberg's season-sensitive regimen and its separation of environmental conditions, the addressed person and conduct. This improves on saying the witness offers only a fourfold quality table.

**Do not promote:** the stronger claim that these selected Laufenberg pages independently attest the same internal-heat/food mechanism, or that the Hippocratic unit explains the age/season images. The decisive winter stretch is partly unread; therefore the result is **PARTIAL_SOURCE_BRIDGE_WITH_UNRESOLVED_CAUSAL_CLAUSE**, not a fully positive bridge and not a demonstrated contradiction.
