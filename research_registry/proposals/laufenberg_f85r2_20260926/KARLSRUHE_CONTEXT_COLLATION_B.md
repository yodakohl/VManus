## Comparison with the printed physician speech

The printed 1491 physician speech states a generic reciprocal proposition of the form “for one [it is] good / that does the other harm,” surrounded by statements about varying times, bodies and conduct. In these two Karlsruhe images I did **not** locate a textually clear equivalent of that same one/another benefit-versus-harm clause. Nor did I find a phrase naming one shared food or treatment and explicitly saying that it benefits one recipient/time but harms another. I therefore report **no matching clause found in this fixed two-page range**, rather than claiming that such a clause is absent from the whole manuscript or tradition.

The visible Karlsruhe context does relate human nature to the four complexions and places the urine-inspecting physician immediately in that discussion. The first complexion heading `Die erste complexion Sanguinea` is an explicit sectional label. This is thematic medical/contextual proximity, not the same relational statement as the print's generic “one good / another harm” contrast. It may be that the comparison is phrased elsewhere, but the fixed-range evidence does not license an extension beyond these two pages.

No full diplomatic transcription was attempted. The 91v continuous prose, its lower text, and the 92r prose contain many visible words but not a sufficiently stable complete sentence transcription for quoting as an exact benefit/harm proposition. I do not back-repair these readings from the 1491 print.
