# Mercury native-reading B: reconciliation addendum

This addendum preserves `MERCURY_LOCAL_TEXT_B.md` and `MERCURY_LOCAL_TEXT_ROOT.md` unchanged. I compared the root's 116-baseline working transcript against the same full cached 270v and 271v photographs. The root transcription resolves several passages that my first whole-page pass had only loosely paraphrased. I withdraw the unsupported details below rather than preserving them as source claims.

## Corrections to my first-pass paraphrase

- On 270vA4–11 the opening is an astrological placement/dignity sequence: Mercury is nearest to Venus; Gemini is its house, Virgo its exaltation, and Sagittarius/Pisces are named as places where it has no power. My phrase suggesting a bodily/earthly-context claim here was unsupported and is withdrawn.
- The `gut by den guten ... böse mit den bösen` sentence is followed by the native description beginning `Syn kint ist ...`. The page does contain a good/bad conditional formulation, but my suggestion of an enemy clause or a separate “when” rule in 270vB was unsupported; those are withdrawn. The exact antecedent of “good/bad” remains open in this visual reading and should not be translated into a universal person-level conditional.
- The second Mercury column describes the Mercury child/person: appearance (brows/eyes/nose/face/teeth/chin/fingers), clothing, friends/favor, limbs and attributed further traits. This supports the cautious statement that a person-level character description is present. References to learned capacities and arts/crafts appear in the first column, but the source does not securely name medicine or give a treatment instruction.
- On 271v, the left column first treats Luna, then describes the Moon's child. The right column turns at B5 to an explicit general conclusion about the seven planets and their children, with birth or conception, rising/strong influence, multiple contributing planets, and naming by the strongest share. My first note's portrayal of the whole second column as continued Moon/sign interaction was wrong and is withdrawn.

## Numeral check

The first-pass note did not record an exact numeral transcription; its loose “about 28 days” wording should not be used as a reading. On direct comparison, the second glyph in 271vA5 after the initial `2` looks angular and may match the angular numeral at 271vB7 (`7 planeten`) more closely than the looped `8` in A7 (`38 minute`). I cannot resolve it confidently from this full-page view: retain `2[7/8?] tagen` rather than choose 27 or 28 by expected lunar period. A7's `2 tage 6 stunden 38 minute` is more visually distinct, though still a working numeral reading. Any multiplication or astronomical consistency check is secondary and cannot decide the glyph.

## Source conclusion

The main ownership inference stands more firmly with the transcript: 270v names Mercury and 271v opens a new Luna section; therefore 271r follows the Mercury text. The text provides Mercury-related natal traits and arts/crafts wording, but the composite image's individual scenes are not all explicitly captioned by a complete proposition. This remains a source-program comparison, not a Voynich identification. The two reports derive from the same photographs and are not independent witnesses.
