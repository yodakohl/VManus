# Galen *Natural Faculties* III.13: bounded medieval Latin transmission check

## Decision

There is an institutionally catalogued pre-1450 Latin manuscript witness to a Burgundio of Pisa Latin translation of Galen's *De facultatibus naturalibus*: Wellcome MS.6013, dated early fourteenth century. The catalogue locates the item at ff. 9r–22r, gives its rubric, incipit and explicit, and describes the codex as produced in Italy, perhaps Padua or Bologna. This establishes a Latin medieval witness at the level of a work/item and its attributed translator. It does **not** establish that this specific witness contains the target argument from book III, chapter 13: the rubric calls the text “Liber primus,” and the cataloged folio span has no chapter-by-chapter contents. Thus passage ownership in this witness remains unverified. Nor does one Italian manuscript establish wider regional circulation.

The medieval source tradition is therefore plausible in a narrow sense—at least a Latin Burgundio version of the work circulated in a fourteenth-century Italian codex—but this bounded check cannot trace the specific liver-vein/organ-body argument into that copy. It does not identify the Voynich's source language, Vorlage, or any target word, and it is not a prerequisite for exploring a working hypothesis.

## Witness and evidence

The sole witness chain used here is the official [Wellcome Collection catalogue record for MS.6013](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/fcz3mmm8), catalogue description transcribed from Richard Palmer's 1999 Wellcome Western Manuscripts catalogue. It dates the volume to the early 14th century; identifies ff. 9r–22r as Galen, *De facultatibus naturalibus*, translated by Burgundio of Pisa; records the rubric “Liber primus Galieni de naturalibus virtutibus a Burgundione cive pisano translatus”; supplies incipit “Quia sentire quidem et moveri secundum electionem…” and an explicit ending “…vel omnifariam evellunt breve”; and says the volume was produced in Italy, “perhaps at Padua or Bologna.” The record labels the language Latin. The manuscript is listed as closed stores, so no image or direct transcription was inspected.

The catalogue's object-level identification supports existence of a medieval Latin translation witness. But “Liber primus” limits what can be safely asserted from this item: no itemized book-II/III folios or chapter-13 locator is supplied. An incipit/explicit for this cataloged first book cannot, by itself, confirm that the target book-III passage is present. The exact argument in the modern English source (presentation/adhesion/assimilation, fasting liver-vein versus organ body, and reversed supply) is not quoted or paraphrased by the Wellcome record.

## Distinctions and bounded limits

- **Translation existence:** supported for a Latin translation attributed in the manuscript rubric/catalogue to Burgundio of Pisa; the catalogued title is the natural-faculties work.
- **Manuscript date:** early fourteenth century according to the institutional catalogue, hence before 1450; no narrower date is claimed.
- **Specific passage ownership:** not established. The described rubric is “Liber primus”; the catalog supplies no book-III/chapter-13 locator or passage incipit.
- **Regional circulation:** one copy is catalogued as Italian, perhaps Padua or Bologna. That is not evidence of either specific local use or circulation across a region.
- **Textual equivalence:** not tested. The medieval Latin version's wording, completeness, and alignment with the particular Natural Faculties III.13 argument remain unknown because the codex is closed stores and its described excerpt is not a diplomatic edition.
