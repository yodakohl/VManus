## 1r-3: image and writing zones

One large rectangular picture fills the central page area. It has a red field, a narrow greenish border and rocky or uneven dark ground at the bottom. In it I see:

- a large upright human figure near the center, with a gold/yellow ring or halo around the head and garments in muted green/brown and blue;
- a large black, sharply arched wing-like or bird-like form behind and above the figure’s right side;
- a blue winding band crossing in front of the dark form and the central figure, continuing down toward the lower-left area;
- a long yellow/brown handled object held diagonally downward by the central figure, with red flame- or brush-like strokes near the bottom;
- a smaller human form at right, holding a narrow horn/trumpet-shaped object toward the central group;
- a further head/face and partial body emerging at the lower-left edge of the ground, close to the blue band and the central figure’s lower limb.

These are visual shapes and relative placements only. In particular I do not identify the blue band as a fluid, the black form as a named animal, the yellow object as a tool of a specific process, or the people as personifications. No sun disc is clearly depicted in the main field. The colored square, border and ground are the clearest spatial framing; the visible lines do not make an unambiguous arrow or written connector between each object.

Writing is present in several separate zones: a faint multi-line band above the picture with red ruling/bracketing; a narrow vertical inscription area at the left of the image; another narrow vertical area at right; and a low-contrast line or lines below the picture. There are ink strokes and letter-like forms in each, but I cannot safely read a complete phrase in any zone from this full image. Some of the red marks may be rubric/ruling or delimiters. I do not see a reliably readable complete proposition that explicitly binds the central person, dark form, blue band, smaller figure, and lower ground figure together. That is a limit of my visual reading, not evidence that the inscriptions contain no such statement.

## 1v-4: later writing, no matching picture

The full verso shown is mostly blank parchment. A continuous block of several dark handwritten lines occupies the upper margin; below it is a more compact, separately set verse-like block with a large red initial/rubric and red underlining or ruling. These are distinct text zones. I can see individual letterforms but cannot safely transcribe a complete statement from either zone at this scale. There is no large illustration on this leaf corresponding to the 1r-3 picture, and no visible line or label linking the upper writing to the image across the opening.

The decision records the catalogue characterization of the 1v additions as later writing on a page originally left blank; the image alone does not date the two text zones relative to each other. This leaf supplies no visually inspected, legible proposition binding the 1r-3 picture’s components. Its adjacency in the same manuscript does not make its prose a caption for that picture. I do not use it to name the depicted elements or to turn the candidate catalogue description into a verified program reading.

## Bounded result

The recto contains a conjunction of striking visual objects in one framed field, with surrounding but mostly unread writing; the verso contains later-looking writing zones and no parallel composite image. I cannot establish from these two full images alone that the text states the relation among the objects or that the verso explains the recto. This native note supports the decision’s distinction between one composite picture and adjacent later text; it provides no Voynich correspondence, no four-element assignment, and no target-word evidence.
