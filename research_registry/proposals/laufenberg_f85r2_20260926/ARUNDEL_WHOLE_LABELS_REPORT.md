# Arundel 83 II f.126v: full wheel, labels and speakers

Source-only follow-up, 2026-09-26. Read with [the advance decision](ARUNDEL_WHOLE_LABELS_DECISION.md) and [access/image receipts](ARUNDEL_WHOLE_LABELS_RECEIPTS.json). Prior packets are unchanged.

**Result:** the British Library's larger public reproduction exposes the complete painted wheel and all four corner figures. Their banners identify ages, not named Fortune figures: **iuuentus, senectus, infantia, decrepitas**. Ten separate life-stage roundels surround a central divine head with its own first-person statement. Several circumferential statements use an age-person's first person; others have a different grammatical subject or remain partly unreadable. This is an organized representation of human life, not one continuous sentence spoken by four people. A complete folio and fully secure transcription of every early verse remain missing.

## Exact witness, access and limits

The [BL child catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-002405144) assigns the ten-age wheel to **Arundel MS 83, Part II, f.126v**. Its twelve-stage wheel is f.126r and was not viewed. The De Lisle prefatory diagrams belong to a manuscript dated by the catalogue c.1310–c.1339. The [official BL Flickr record](https://www.flickr.com/photos/britishlibrary/12458890495), identifier **C13385-26**, calls its reproduction the upper half of the folio. That boundary remains authoritative: an entire rectangular painting is not an entire leaf.

Path 1 supplied the directly declared original [JPEG](https://live.staticflickr.com/7445/12458890495_35467bb540_o.jpg), **1308 × 1365**, not a generated enlargement. It is the same composition as Smith's smaller figure, with more complete outer figures and much better letters. A narrow parchment margin and the entire painted rectangular frame are visible; the rest of the folio is outside the photograph. No lower-leaf writing can be declared absent.

Path 2 was the predeclared [PICRYL exact-folio record](https://picryl.com/de/media/wheel-of-the-ten-ages-of-man-from-bl-arundel-83-f-126v-9c1d58). Its indexed text names f.126v and BL but exposes only a truncated Europeana source link. Ordinary direct HTML access returned HTTP 403. The browser page supplied only a 320 × 468 display thumbnail URL, not the reported 1024 × 1500 original. That thumbnail was not inspected; no original URL was guessed or reconstructed. No other reproduction path, manuscript image, viewer service, adjacent folio or target data was accessed.

## Whole painted unit and ownership

Direct pixels show ten gold-ground roundels, linked by ten spokes to the central roundel, against alternating decorated blue and reddish fields. A separate age figure fills each corner. No arrowhead or numbered reading sequence is visible. Starting with the nursing scene at lower left and proceeding up the left side, over the top, then down the right gives the ten-stage sequence described by Smith and the BL caption. The start/direction is therefore a combined iconographic and scholarly identification, not a newly read arrow.

The central bearded head has a cross-shaped nimbus and a surrounding text of its own. Christ/Deity is also the BL catalogue-caption identification. Each outer age banner labels its nearby corner figure; each roundel's annular text encloses its own scene. Some scenes contain an additional actor: mother and infant; old person and child; patient and physician; bier and cleric. A scene's inscription does not automatically belong to every actor in it.

### Four corner figures: complete census

| Position | Readable banner, preserving medieval u/v convention | Direct physical attributes | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper left | **iuuentus.** (normalized *iuventus*) | Crowned, bearded person seated on a throne; long robe; upright slender sceptre on the viewer's left; other hand raised near the banner. | Youth is explicitly named even though the figure is a crowned bearded adult. The sceptre is a royal attribute, not evidence of impaired walking. |
| Upper right | **Senectus.** | White-haired, long-bearded standing person in a blue/grey robe; one hand raised; other hand beside/on a long vertical support at the right side. | Old age is explicitly named. This outer figure is separate from the hooded staff-user in roundel 6. |
| Lower left | **Infantia.** | Unbearded, curly-haired figure seated/reclining on the ground; one arm reaches up toward its banner, the other supports the body; long garment. | Infancy is the explicit label. The corner figure is not the nursing infant of roundel 1. |
| Lower right | **Decrepitas.** | White-haired and long-bearded reclining person; upper body partly uncovered, lower body draped; bent supporting arm; other arm extends toward the banner. | Decrepitude is explicitly named. The banner's ending reads **-tas**, not the **-tus** printed in Martín Criado's discussion. |

These are four named age personifications with rising, standing, seated and reclining poses. A Fortune-wheel resemblance is supported by the BL caption and scholarship as a compositional comparison. **No visible banner says Fortuna**, and the centre is not an observed woman turning a wheel. Absence here refers only to the complete photographed painting.

## All ten annular inscriptions, including unresolved words

The following is a working visual transcription, not a critical edition. Dots approximate visible stops, not modern sentence boundaries. Square brackets indicate uncertain reading or an unresolved stretch; parentheses supply an abbreviation expansion. The public-domain manuscript text is quoted directly; modern translations are paraphrased separately. Initial unaided misreadings were rejected when larger same-pixel views or the cited scholarly text resolved the letters. Such guided readings are identified rather than called independent confirmations.

| Order / position | Scene and local owner | Readable inscription | Translation / exact limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, lower left | Seated woman holds an infant beside a fire. The first-person state is attributed to the infant by the life-stage identification, not to the mother or fire. | **Mitis sum et humilis . lacte vivo puro.** | I am meek and lowly; I live on pure milk. The reading of the first words was guided by Martín Criado p.11 and checked against the pixels. His Spanish wording about being small/tender and milk alone is not used as a literal translation. The Latin adjective *puro* does not itself mean *only*. |
| 2, lower left side | Unbearded standing youth holds a round mirror-like object and a toothed comb-like object. | **N[unquam?] ero labilis . etatem [unresolved].** | The later enlarged view supports *ero labilis*, not the initial exploratory impression *ero ludens*. A complete reading/translation is withheld, particularly the last word(s). No exact numerical age is recovered. The initial false impression of a *quinquennis* word is rejected. |
| 3, upper left side | Unbearded youth holds a two-pan balance. | **[Vita?] carnis tenui[s?] . [s]peculo probatur.** | *carnis*, the *tenui-* sequence and *probatur* are visible; the first word, ending and opening of the second clause remain uncertain. This is not securely an *ego* statement. No complete Latin syntax or translation is supplied from the balance motif. |
| 4, upper left | Mounted young adult with bird on raised hand; other hand holds reins. | **Non imago speculi . set vita letatur.** | Approximately: not the mirror's image, but life rejoices. The *non … set* opposition is visible; *set* is the written form corresponding to *sed*. Martín Criado p.11 supplied the correct *imago speculi* reading, subsequently checked. An earlier unaided *timeo* impression was wrong. His interpretation that life makes the speaker happy is not silently inserted into the manuscript's third-person verb. |
| 5, top | Crowned king on throne, sceptre in one hand and other hand raised. | **Rex sum rego seculum . mundus meus totus.** | I am king, I rule the world; the whole world is mine. Pixels read *rego*, while Martín Criado prints *regno*. The painting supplies a royal role for the human peak stage; it does not name a historical king. |
| 6, upper right | Hooded older man with staff; other hand raised. | **Sumo michi baculum . morti fere notus.** | I take a staff for myself, almost acquainted with death. The staff belongs to this age figure. It is not the physician's instrument. |
| 7, right upper side | Bent white-haired older man with staff, alongside a child; old person's other arm reaches toward the child. | **Decrepitati deditus . mors erit michi [unclear final word].** | Given over to decrepitude; death will be for me […]. The last word is not securely resolved from these pixels. Smith/Dove paraphrase death as the person's coming condition; that translation is not used to restore the unread letters. |
| 8, right lower side | Reclining patient in bed, with a standing attendant holding a flask above eye level. | **Infirmitati deditus . incipio deficere.** | Given over to sickness, I begin to fail. The state's speaker is the patient under the scholarly stage identification. The diagnostic attendant is identified as physician examining urine by Smith and BL; the vessel is directly visible. The text does not say that the physician causes, cures, or prevents the decline. |
| 9, lower right | Covered raised bier/bed with a standing cleric reading an open book; only limited bodily detail visible under/at the covering. | **Putavi q(uod) viverem . vita me decepit.** | I thought that I would live; life deceived me. Smith calls this the dying stage; BL describes a funeral in its visual sequence. Those descriptions are retained, not harmonized by inventing a currently breathing patient. No separate *forever* word was read in the Latin. |
| 10, bottom | Decorated tomb with a recumbent figure on top. | **Versus sum in cinerem . vita me decepit.** | I have been turned to ash; life deceived me. Native text supports ash; Smith/Dove's expanded English reference to both dust and ashes is not a second material noun read from these pixels. |

The account is complete as a census of all ten inscription locations. It is **incomplete as a fully resolved transcription**: roundels 2, 3 and the ending of 7 remain open. No uninscribed labels such as *Infans*, *Boy*, or *Perfect-Aged Man* are passed off as words in these rings. Those English stage names come from Smith's interpretive list.

### Central statement

The centre reads **Omnia simul cerno . totum racione guberno.** My translation: I see everything together; I govern the whole by reason. The manuscript spelling is *racione*. The cross-nimbed head is the pictorial owner; the first-person verbs differ from the human age-voices. The annulus is visibly separate from every spoke-end annulus.

This central governance statement and the king's worldly claim produce a concrete theological contrast when read with the two concluding *vita me decepit* statements. Calling that a contrast is an interpretation of the separately owned texts, not a newly read causal connector. No inscription has been recovered that equates the king with the central divine speaker.

## Scholarly context and the complete relevant units checked

**Smith:** [Helen F. Smith, Edinburgh PhD, 2016](https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/8f425cda-3361-4cfd-b2dc-ee0c32016e31/content), *Disability, Impairment and Embodied Difference in Late-Medieval Drama: Constructions, Representations, and the Spectrum of Signification*, printed pp.34–36 and 41–43, was read as text. P.34 gives all ten stage names and identifies the physician/uroscopy scene. Its subsequent seasonal discussion draws on other texts and does not quote this leaf. P.42 supplies the five late-stage translations from Dove p.80, tying staff use, decrepitude, sickness, disappointed expectation and death into a life-course reading. It then compares a **different** poem, *ʒing and tender child I am*, cited to Dove p.92 and continuing onto p.43. That poem's eye, crutch and earth/pit lines are not f.126v's wording. Smith also cautions in the surrounding discussion that literary ageing can be shaped by rhetoric and does not simply document normative biological experience.

**Martín Criado:** Arturo Martín Criado, “La escala de las Edades del Hombre, una alegoría del camino hacia la muerte. Primera parte: de los orígenes al siglo xviii,” [*Revista de Folklore* 465, November 2020](https://funjdiaz.net/folklore/pdf/rf465.pdf), pp.4–35, especially the complete f.126v discussion on pp.10–11. P.10 links its figure directly to the same BL Flickr record. P.11 prints selected Latin texts for infant, mounted youth, king, dying stage and tomb, then turns explicitly to other works. It does not transcribe roundels 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 or the centre. Its footnote 24 cites Dove p.80. Its own transcription has the noted *regno* and *decrepitus* discrepancies. Its content assisted reading but is not an independent witness to the manuscript.

**Dove:** Mary Dove, *The Perfect Age of Man's Life* (Cambridge University Press, 1986), p.80, is the common cited predecessor. The [Google Books bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&id=Do_deIerv9UC&newbks=0) was found, but its p.80 text was not accessible in the single direct attempt. No complete Dove transcription has been inspected. Two later writers citing the same page do not supply two independent readings.

Only the admitted f.126v reproduction was viewed. Other images in the scholarly PDFs were not rendered or extracted in this task.

## Source argument and exact residual gap

The useful positive model is **a human life course presented through distinct episodes and stage-person voices under a separately speaking divine centre**. The physician's ownership is local to an illness episode. The supports belong to elderly people; a child's assistance and a royal sceptre are separate attributes. Four outer age nouns summarize a different level of the composition from the ten episodes. A reader can understand a representative human life passing through these roles without asserting that each portrait depicts one identifiable historical person.

The source does not authorize merging all subjects. The nursing woman's action, the physician's inspection, the patient's decline, the royal claim and divine governance have different owners. Repeated *vita me decepit* connects two late-stage statements rhetorically; it does not itself prove that medical attendance caused death. The photographed unit supplies no recovered seasons, humour names, regimen, weather-to-body causal rule, botanical attribute, or planetary labels. Flame occurs locally beside the infant, not as a labelled seasonal or elemental band.

**Stop:** complete painting gained; complete folio unavailable; three annular readings partly unresolved; direct Dove p.80 still uninspected. These are source gaps, not negative evidence that the missing text does not exist. The task has produced a concrete ownership/voice distinction and leaves no basis for an automatic four-person paragraph mapping or any confirmed target meaning.
