## Referent and scope census

These are counts of the plan's roles, not token counts or quantities inferred
from a diagram:

- **Zero named individual humans and zero individually named planets** occur
  inside the unit. There is one explicitly counted collective of **seven
  planets**. Its members can be variables without inventing seven proper names.
- **One generic human role** is reused in birth/conception, child attribution,
  receipt and naming. `Some` introduces **three quantified classes** (two,
  three, all contributors); it does not introduce three concrete people.
- **One mother role** belongs to the conception relation. It neither donates
  planetary properties nor supplies another horoscope in this source.
- **Two event kinds**, BIRTH and CONCEPTION, concern the same generic human.
  At most two event references are needed for a human in contract B below;
  contract A uses one selected reference plus the explicitly recalled birth.
- **Three planetary roles** must remain distinguished: eligible/power-leading
  planet, property contributor, and predominant name source. A planet may fill
  more than one role; the role distinction does not assert three different
  physical planets.
- **Two ordinal comparison respects** are explicit: greatest power and most
  received properties. C07 adds **one strength-dependency relation**, whose
  bearer is uncertain; it is not a third secretly calibrated measurement scale.
- **Two temporal-rule types**, planetary day and planetary hour, qualify the
  final birth. EAST is **one direction**, not an invented observer identity.
- **Three principal scopes** suffice: event-qualified power/attribution;
  the same human's contributor profile; naming with its natal qualification.
  Nature-qualified transfer is nested inside the second. Qualitative rarity,
  generic mixture and existential class examples have different quantifier
  force and cannot be merged into a single ALL.

The source does not identify the power-bearing support with the contributor
support. A rare sole-power case is admissible in both contracts. Neither
contract excludes four-, five- or six-planet contributor sets:2,3,all is not
treated as an exhaustive allowed-count list. Nor does either import570's
two-to-four complexion bound or558's all-material-elements premise.

## Shared finite content machinery

This section specifies what a hypothetical reading would say, not how any
script writes it. There are **six content schemata**:

1. `EVENT_OF(person, kind)` supplies the person's birth or conception reference.
2. `POWER_FRAME(person, event, q)` keeps event, planet, east-rise qualification
   and power comparison together. The comparison set is declared as all
   planets; the top planet must additionally satisfy the rise/great-power
   guard. Selecting the top only among rising planets would be a different,
   unchosen scope. There is no astronomical calculation.
3. `CHILD_CLAIM(person, event, q)` is produced by that qualified leading role.
   It is a classification/attribution, not biological planetary parenthood.
4. `CONTRIBUTION(person, profile_scope, q, bundle, degree, nature_ref)` preserves
   provenance and the C05/C07 nature/strength restrictions. Degree remains
   ordinal/qualitative; the source supplies no actual bundle enumeration.
5. `TOP(profile, respect, q)` is the **same relational comparison pattern**
   when respect is POWER or RECEIVED_PROPERTIES. It never identifies the two
   orderings. No total ranking is required. Tied tops are left tied; the
   passage's singular name is a generic assertion, not a manufactured tie-break.
6. `NAME_FROM(person, q, profile, birth)` requires that q contribute to that
   same profile, have a greatest received share there, and satisfy the chosen
   final birth/day-or-hour attachment. `BornUnder(person,q,birth)` is not defined
   as sole power, greatest power, or biological parenthood.

For both contracts the final attachment is fixed conservatively as **all of
C13, C14 and C15 concerning the same name-source planet**. In logical form,
an asserted `NAME_FROM(p,q,F,b)` carries:

`q in contributors(F); TOP(F,RECEIVED_PROPERTIES,q); BornUnder(p,q,b);`
`PlanetDay(q,b) OR PlanetHour(q,b).`

This is an assertion-ownership contract, not an inference that these
conditions are sufficient to name every possible person. Making the converse
rule or guaranteeing a unique successful name would add a sufficiency or
uniqueness premise. The complete source's generic naming assertion remains
written; the plan does not delete it merely because no named case is given.

The source's qualitative `rarely` and generic claims are retained as attributed
quantified assertions. No percentage semantics is fitted. C09–11 are claims
about some humans, with the same receipt relation. They are not three empty
conditional examples invented by the analyst. The two contracts differ in
event/profile scope, not by changing these quantifiers to gain consistency.

## Contract A — alternative reference convention, one attribution profile

**Commitment A1:** choose one event kind, BIRTH or CONCEPTION, once for the
whole reading. This treats the source OR as alternative reference procedures,
not as a per-person switch chosen to obtain a favorable answer. The plan
retains both possible global settings; a later selected author must fix one.

For every generic person in the account, the selected event supplies the
POWER_FRAME, conditional CHILD_CLAIM and one corresponding CONTRIBUTION
profile. That profile supplies the repeated comparisons, contributor counts,
little/much assertions and predominant naming. The initial winning planet
gives its nature-qualified properties; the subsequent multiple-donor clauses
extend receipt to other planets without changing the human owner.

The final naming still explicitly refers to the person's **birth**. In the
CONCEPTION setting, the received-profile comparison and the final birth
qualification therefore concern different time references. They may happen
to favor the same planet, but no equality is imposed. A proposed name must
meet both written conditions; if it does not, that proposed attribution does
not satisfy A. This is not repaired by silently switching to the birth profile.

**Added scope costs:** one global event selector with two possible settings;
one event-to-received-profile indexing assumption; one cross-reference from
that profile to the person's birth; one fixed final-conjunction attachment.
The source does not independently verify this convention. No aggregation of
birth and conception or power/share equality is introduced.

**Complete source output:** a generic exposition of event-qualified child
attribution, nature/strength-qualified giving, rare sole power, mixed receipts
including the2/3/all classes, variable degrees, and a qualified predominant
name. Its retrospective seven-planet recap and trailing `etc.` remain part
of the discourse. Every C01–18 obligation survives.

## Contract B — separate event claims, natal naming profile

**Commitment B1:** read BIRTH OR CONCEPTION distributively as two conditional
applications of the same event-qualified rule. This is an explicit added
scope choice; the source does not say to merge two charts. A human may have
one child claim at conception and another at birth, each bearing its event.

The conditional giving relation is indexed to the same event as its child
claim. The generic source gives no rule for adding, averaging or accumulating
the two event profiles. **Commitment B2:** the mixed-receipt discussion and
final naming use the natal profile, because birth is the explicit final
reference. The conception-conditioned giving assertion is retained, but it
does not silently enlarge the natal contributor set. The narrowing to a natal
profile is an interpretive cost, not an independently observed distinction.

Consequently a different conception leader and birth leader can coexist as
event-indexed claims. The final name uses the natal received-property
comparison and the same-planet birth/day-or-hour qualification. Neither leader
is automatically the largest contributor. A change of reference from the
conception claim to natal naming must be expressed, rather than supplied by
an invisible pronoun or a global person label.

**Added scope costs:** one distributive two-event reading of OR; two event-indexed
profiles under one schema; one explicit natal focus switch before the mixed
profile/naming discussion; one fixed final-conjunction attachment. No event
pooling, cross-event conservation or chronological change in the person's
traits is asserted. Conception and birth dates need not be numerically known.

**Complete source output:** the same C01–18 inventory as A, with the extra
event-indexed claims kept distinct and the single public naming statement
given an explicit natal reference. It is a coherent stronger articulation,
not an assertion that the source itself contains two observed case histories.

## Strongest compatible rival and exact outcomes

The serious rival is a **single conventional classification head**: the
exposition's strongest planet, largest property source and name all express
one conventional type under one relevant event, and the final day/hour wording
merely explains that convention. This can be compatible with the source.
It requires explicit alignment of event scope and the two ordinal maxima;
it does not require a numerical common unit or linear ratio. A weak
order-preserving bridge may suffice, so rejecting it solely for lacking
arithmetic would be a straw rival.

Another compatible reading leaves received profiles generic rather than
event-indexed. Neither A nor B is then forced: their indexing is the added
authoring assumption. A third treats C12 as restricted to the immediately
preceding all-planets class; the broader EACH-donor reading must not be
claimed certain. Both A/B choose broader scope and count that choice once.

Concrete decision-changing outcomes of later whole authoring would be:

1. A repeated written event marker separates two same-person claims and
   the later natal naming, with every other occurrence retaining that role:
   B then has content that a single unindexed head loses.
2. A uniform convention binds every relevant claim to one event and needs
   no extra focus switch: A may be the smaller whole contract. This is not
   a retrospective permission to remove an inconvenient conception clause.
3. The written account requires both comparisons but makes their maxima
   differ under the same stated scope: the aligned-head rival fails **under
   those guessed meanings and bindings**, not by independent source truth.
4. The account names a planet outside its own contributor support, changes
   the generic person, or supplies no written birth/day/hour ownership:
   that particular full reading remains incomplete or inconsistent. Filling
   all words with glosses does not make it complete.
5. All supplied consequences remain shared by A, B and the aligned-head
   rival: report no discrimination and do not build a further simulator.
