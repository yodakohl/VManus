# Native comparison: Karlsruhe heaven diagrams and band/plume descriptions

2026-09-26. Bounded external-source image check. I inspected only the official
Badische Landesbibliothek full-page images for platform 87v and 88r, then the
already cached Tübingen Md 2, 322v image for a controlled visual comparison.
The image labels in the Karlsruhe manifest were checked against the canvases;
both identify the expected platform folios. GDT1043's report was used only as
the written description of its fixed target observations; I opened no new
Voynich target pixels.

## What the Karlsruhe pages show

The page headings identify two successive, specific heavens, rather than four
figures: platform 87v is headed **“Der achte himel der firmament”** (eighth
heaven / firmament); platform 88r has the heading **“Der nunde himel”** (ninth
heaven). A further word follows the latter heading, but I cannot read it
securely from this inspection and do not supply it. The labels are read from
the page images, not inferred from the number of marks in the diagrams.

On 87v, a circular diagram has several concentric outlines and a ring populated
by many small red star symbols. The enclosed center is mostly blank. The star
ring is a positive sky/firmament feature, strengthened by the written caption.
Its bands are unfilled parchment/ink contours, not a blue or visibly wavy
band. There are no cloud plumes, winged heads, faces, mouths, or jets in the
diagram.

On 88r, the circle again has nested outlines, but the most visible marks are
curved red strokes/ticks running around the bands; it does not repeat the
star-filled ring of 87v. These strokes give a circular, potentially revolving
appearance, though the picture alone does not prove physical rotation. The
diagram is not painted blue, and no blue wave or cloud band is present. The
irregular red ink area on the lower arc is not a separate body of water or a
depicted plume. Neither page gives a direct picture match for GDT1043's four
nested curl/plume forms.

## Comparison with Tübingen 322v and the registered target description

Tübingen Md 2, 322v presents a different and more explicitly layered cosmic
diagram: broad colored concentric bands surround a central area labelled for
Earth/hell, and labels occupy the rings; upper divine figures appear above the
outer bands. A muted blue-grey annulus is one colored band within this labeled
concentric scheme. It is therefore a positive example of blue used in a cosmic
diagram. It does not by itself establish that every blue or wavy shape in
another image means a heaven or water. The fine red wave-like strokes visible
near the bottom belong to the central region, not to the blue annulus. The
diagram has no four-corner wind-head/plume set.

The external Karlsruhe pages thus support a bounded distinction: their heaven
diagrams use circular outlines, a red star ring for the eighth heaven, and
curved marks in the ninth-heaven circle; Tübingen 322v uses labeled, colored
cosmic annuli. None supplies a generic blue-water identification. Neither
Karlsruhe diagram contains the specific plume/wing anatomy described for
Tübingen 43r in the existing source dossier. GDT1043's registered report says
the target's outer forms are nested curling fan/plume strokes, partly blue,
without clear faces, mouths, or jets, and that its whole panel has a sunlike
face within a swirling blue-white disk with an inner written annulus. This
comparison can note superficial circular/color overlap, but the external
images do not supply a whole-panel match or semantics for those target forms.

No inference here uses fourfold count to name winds. This is image/source
categorization only: it establishes no word meaning, copied source, date,
language, authorship, or geographic origin for the target.
