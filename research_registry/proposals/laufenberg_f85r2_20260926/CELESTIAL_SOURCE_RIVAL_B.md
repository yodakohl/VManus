# RAW569 source and rival review B

Review status: source-only, pre-author-draft freeze. I read the root authoring decision, complete RAW569 card, complete C1–C8 source inventory, and the cited normalized electronic text receipt. I have not read an author decision, target draft/result, or target pixels. This is a review of the historical content contract and its rival, not a target interpretation or a new source collation.

## Source content the reading must retain

The bounded unit is the complete headed subsection *Quod terra sit rotunda* in the electronic 1478 *De sphaera* text, from the selected heading through its final apparent-flatness sentence, before *Quod acqua sit rotunda*. Its reported C1–C8 set is coherent as a sequence of observations and arguments, but it is not one undifferentiated “viewpoint changes the answer” proposition:

- C1 compares eastern and local rise/set reports, with the eastern report earlier, and gives curvature as explanation.
- C2 keeps one numerically identical lunar eclipse while reporting it at our first night-hour and approximately the third night-hour in the east; the eastern sunset is earlier. “Approximately” and the local-night frame matter. The text does not name an eclipse contact or prove that both reports refer to the same instant of an extended eclipse.
- C3 states a northern class of stars perpetually visible and the opposite-polar class perpetually hidden.
- C4 gives a qualified southward journey: stars formerly perpetually visible tend to set after sufficient travel, and the tendency strengthens farther south.
- C5 concerns the same traveller seeing stars previously hidden, and states the converse for travel in the opposite direction. It must not be forced to use the exact same star as C4 absent an explicit reference link.
- C6 and C7 are conditional flatness arguments, each with its premise and claimed consequence rejected. C6 is the east–west case. C7 is the north–south/converse-travel visibility case. Both the counterfactual and its scope are content; neither is interchangeable with an unqualified cosmological assertion.
- C8 attributes apparent flatness to the great size of Earth. It is a perceptual qualification, separate from the two report channels.

The inventory’s distinction between event identity, local phase, cyclic visibility and instantaneous sight is necessary. In particular, the text licenses neither converting the first/third local hour labels into elapsed duration or a calibrated two-hour offset, nor treating perpetual visibility as a still photograph. It gives no numerical trajectory, threshold or geometry.

## What the proposed construction explains, and what it adds

A typed `VIEW(referent, station, scope)` is a useful explicit representation for the content: one world referent can be carried into reports indexed by different stations, while a channel determines whether the result is a local night-phase assertion or a cycle-relative visibility assertion. The source provides concrete identity/reuse constraints: the eclipse remains one event in C2; C4/C5 explicitly preserve a traveller across a conditional journey; and the relevant stellar references remain objects of visibility claims while the station changes. These facts make referent preservation a real obligation, not merely an attractive metaphor.

The source does **not** itself state a general `VIEW` constructor, a `REFERENT` operation, or the algebraic laws for repeated `REFRAME`. In particular, preservation of scope through every frame replacement, associativity of successive reframings, and reuse of one uniform frame sort for event and star reports are formal modeling choices. Ordinary anaphora and an explicit contextual relation could represent the same content. A future author must show the actual written argument owners and typed uses before claiming that the source has selected this notation.

The two channels cannot be collapsed without losing a distinction: the first returns approximate locally framed hour descriptions of an event; the second reports a star’s cycle-relative visibility regime under a compatible horizon/motion scope. Nor does the first entail that the eclipse reports concern a common stage. The optional common-stage bridge is properly costed as an assumption. Event identity alone does not imply that two local descriptions are contradictory: an extended eclipse can be reported at distinct times. If a later model claims a unique common-stage comparison, it must write that bridge and preserve the approximation. Conversely, a complete account that declines the bridge must not advertise the familiar intrinsic-time contradiction as a consequence of this source alone.

C4 and C5 similarly do not entail that all stars change regimes, that every change is immediate, or that arbitrary station substitutions preserve one universal cycle scope. The sufficient-distance condition, tendency language, further-south comparison, and converse clause are limits on what may be generalized. Daily celestial motion can coexist with station-dependent reports; “changing world” is not refuted merely because the traveller’s station changes.

## Strongest rival and what would discriminate it

The strongest rival is a set of direct contextual relations: for example, `reported_phase(event, station, scope, phase)` and `visibility(star, station, cycle_scope, regime)`, with explicit anaphora for the same event/traveller/referent. This can state every C1–C8 claim without an intermediate VIEW object and can preserve exactly the same arguments. It is therefore a content-equivalent rival unless a complete written reading shows a repeated construction whose ownership and composition favor the shared frame operation. Merely fitting both channels, preserving an event, or using the same English explanation does not discriminate.

A physical-motion account is a weaker rival only if stated too simply. Successive celestial motion can explain changing instantaneous appearance generally, but to cover this selected unit it still must preserve C2’s one eclipse, C4/C5’s traveller-owned conditional station changes, the distinct local/cyclic report kinds, and C6/C7’s rejected conditional claims. It need not be excluded wholesale: celestial rotation can coexist with a reporting-frame account. A fixed intrinsic label rival has a conditional failure only where a common stage is explicitly fixed; without that bridge, C2 alone does not defeat it. An observer-free visibility label also needs qualification for C4/C5, but that does not establish VIEW specifically.

A future whole reading should therefore be assessed on two separate axes: (1) whether its written arguments cover the full C1–C8 content with the correct identities, quantifiers, directions, conditions and output distinctions; and (2) whether a shared frame construction is actually written in both typed uses, rather than reconstructed from the English gloss. If direct predicates and VIEW are equally explicit and compositionally equivalent, the correct outcome is non-identifiability between notations, not a preference based on the historical source.

## Constraints and limits for the later replay

The source inventory is an adequate complete content checklist for this selected electronic unit, subject to its stated normalized-edition limits; the native 1478 print was not inspected and no regional provenance follows. I found no reason in the supplied inventory to drop or merge any C1–C8 item. I also found no source basis for making the C4/C5 stars identical, for identifying the eastern observers with the northern traveller, or for forcing a common eclipse phase. Those remain choices only if explicitly written and charged.

A later literal/type replay can legitimately reject one fixed descendant for an explicit type/identity/scope conflict or report exact incompleteness. It cannot treat unbound source details as target contradictions, or call a source-compatible target synopsis confirmation. The RAW card’s own warning is apt: success can establish at most a complete exploratory reading and compatibility; the direct-relations rival remains serious.
