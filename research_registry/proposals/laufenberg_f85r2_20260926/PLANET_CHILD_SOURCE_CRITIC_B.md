# Independent source critique of the RAW572 whole-unit plan

27 September 2026. Bounded source-only review. I read the live route and
composition excerpt; `PLANET_CHILD_WHOLE_SOURCE_PLAN.md`; the complete
RAW572 card; the complete 271vB transcription and subsequent reconciliation;
and the complete 558 root review. I did not inspect a target page, a current
572 author draft, or any target values. The Tübingen reviews share the same
photographs and are not independent witnesses.

## Source-unit coverage

The chosen unit is appropriately bounded at 271vB baselines 5–32: 28 physical
lines, from the seven-planet recap through `etc.`. The assertion inventory
does cover each line: C01 lines 5–7; C02–05 lines 8–16; C06–08 lines 16–23;
C09–12 lines 23–26; C13–16 lines 27–32. C17 and C18 restate consequential
links already assigned to lines 18/21 and 27; they are analytical overlap,
not omitted extra clauses. The report appropriately keeps the uncertain
strength pronoun at line 20, the generic/existential force of `ettliche`,
the backward scope of `in alle sammet`, and the trailing `etc.` visible.

The summary should continue to call the counting set “all seven” a reading of
`in alle sammet` by reference to line 7, not a separately enumerated donor
list. The individual line inventory preserves that qualification. Likewise,
`gar selten` is a qualitative rarity claim; it neither bans sole-power cases
nor gives a frequency. The two/three/all clauses introduce classes/examples,
not a measured distribution or an exhaustive set of allowed contributor
counts. The 558 review is relevant here: as it explains, a class-level or
universal statement does not by itself supply a person-specific ownership
bridge. The present plan correctly keeps the recipient as the same generic
person through the receipt and naming clauses.

## The consequential scope issue: the source's OR

The native text says, in substance, that the human is a planet's child who is
born **or** comes forth in the mother's body at the time when the planet has
great power and rises in the east. The nearest source-faithful minimum is one
event reference `e` for the same person, with `kind(e) ∈ {BIRTH, CONCEPTION}`;
the event time is the time qualified by the eastern-rise/power clause. The
written OR remains a disjunction over those event kinds. It does not say that
both events must be evaluated, that their records are pooled, or that one
globally selected convention governs every generic person.

Contract A exposes two possible global settings, but any realized A reading
chooses just one and thereby omits the other disjunct from that reading. A's
conception setting also needs its disclosed extra link from the conception-
indexed profile to the later explicit birth qualification. Preserving the
unselected setting in commentary does not make the fixed contract itself
express the source's OR.

Contract B retains both alternatives as two conditional applications, but
that is stronger than the OR: it permits two child-attribution claims and
event-indexed profiles for the same human. The source gives no observed pair
of birth/conception records and does not require both claims to be asserted.
Its added natal-profile focus for the mixed-receipt/name passage is candidly
reported, but remains an added scope switch.

The smallest source-faithful contract is therefore a **single disjunctive
event-kind variable**, not a third general framework:

```text
e : EventOf(p)
kind(e) = BIRTH OR kind(e) = CONCEPTION
t = time(e)
PowerAt(q,t) AND RisesInEast(q,t) -> ChildAttribution(p,q,e)
```

Keep the source's singular event-reference in this initial clause. Do not
quantify it into two simultaneously required applications or merge two
profiles. This representation leaves whether the disjunct is a per-person
instantiation or a general alternative convention open; that is more faithful
than deciding a global selector now. The subsequent text explicitly says the
leading planet gives the same person properties, so retain that contribution
by `q`; however, do not infer that the set of planets holding power is
identical to the full set of property contributors. The later two/three/all
clauses add plural donors but give no complete rule mapping power-support to
receipt-support or indexing every later share to `e`.

The final naming clause should remain separately anchored to its explicit
birth/day-or-hour wording: the person is named after the planet from which
that person has most properties, and under that planet the person was born
in that planet's day or hour. The plan's same-name-source binding is a
reasonable conservative coreference. Do not make that planet identical to
the earlier event's greatest-power planet without a written bridge. Nor does
the day/hour OR license combining one planet's day with another planet's
hour. A and B both handle this qualifier more carefully than a generic
planet label would, but their added profile-to-birth scope needs to remain
marked as a hypothesis choice.

## Content-role assessment

The plan's three role distinctions are useful and source-motivated: the
event-time power leader, the property donor(s), and the planet with the
largest received-property share/name. They are roles, not a claim that the
source names three distinct planets. One relation is explicit: the leading
planet “gives” the person its properties according to its nature. Plural
contributions follow in the same paragraph. This makes the leading planet a
donor in that assertion; it does **not** establish that every powerful
planet is a donor, that every donor was a power leader, or that the support
sets coincide.

The plan correctly avoids identifying the greatest-power comparison with
the largest-received-share comparison. It also avoids treating a source
dependence on `Kraft` as a calibrated share function: the strength bearer
remains uncertain, and no ratio, scale, tie-break, or unique maximum is
provided. The dominant-name claim is not exclusive-source language: it
coexists with multiple contributors. The day/hour phrase is attached to
birth under the name-source planet; the source does not provide a table or
astronomical algorithm.

## Decision

The whole-unit source is worth preserving as a bounded content hypothesis:
it has real conditional obligations across event-qualified planetary
attribution, multi-donor property receipt, and predominant naming. The
plan's C01–18 inventory is materially complete, and its two candidate
contracts are useful *scope contrasts*. Neither is the least source-faithful
contract as currently fixed: A resolves the source OR globally; B expands it
to two applications. If a later whole reading is attempted, retain one
birth-or-conception event variable and the expressly separate birth/day/hour
name attachment, while keeping the profile's exact temporal indexing open
where the source does not settle it.

This is source-content analysis only. It supplies no f85 word meaning,
transcription, semantic confirmation, astronomical computation, or evidence
that any of these roles is written on the target.
