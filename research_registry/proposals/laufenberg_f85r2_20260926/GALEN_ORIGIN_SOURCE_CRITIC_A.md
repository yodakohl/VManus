The bodily definition on311 includes the liver's flesh and its vessels. The
contrast with their cavity contents therefore cannot be reduced to “inside
liver” versus “not inside liver.” It concerns material already appropriated to

latter outside the liver would erase the expressly nested anatomy. No modern
histological reconstruction is needed or claimed.

## Surrounding chapter prevents a simpler reading

The earlier13.1 opening compares shared passages of stomach, bladder by the
liver and uterus. The uterine example itself has different incoming/outgoing
materials. It supports a channel relation, not invariant material identity.
On291–295, feeding, fasting, purgatives and relative need explain changes in
which participant draws. Natural relative strength and current demand are
expressly distinguished, including the food-snatching analogy.

The same run also permits a needy stomach to receive waste fluids rather than
a plentiful favourable supply. The source lists biliary, phlegmatic and serous
materials; this is an adverse qualification to a blanket “everything drawn is
proper nourishment” inference. The exact pronoun/need wording there is not
used to assign an unambiguous new supplier-state variable in this critique.

On295–303, neighbouring parts can pass surplus matter onward under weakness,
burden, obstruction or irritation. Cold can make previously tolerable nearby
fluid burdensome. Purgation, disease and reverse expulsion are not silently
converted into nutritional appropriateness. These clauses make condition and
process relevant; they do not define universal material-origin categories.

On303–307, the account rejects simultaneity as the intended sense of opposite
traffic: the same organ/channel acts in different directions at different
times. In the final313–315 paragraph, arterial/heart movement, incomplete closure
and reflux qualify the general model. A reversal claim must therefore retain
its timing and anatomical qualifications rather than describe a modern closed
circulation system.

## Relations that must remain separate

| Relation | What the source can support | What it does not automatically mean |
|---|---|---|
|Transfer source|Material is drawn from liver-vein cavities or from spleen toward stomach under stated conditions.|The supplying organ created that material or is its exclusive ultimate origin.|
|Containment|Juice lies in a vessel cavity; vessels lie within an organ.|Every item spatially inside liver is already part of liver tissue's assimilative appropriation.|
|Presentation/adhesion/assimilation|One named organ stands at a particular nutritional phase with respect to material.|A static location label, or successful nutrition whenever a suitable item is mentioned.|
|Working-up/transformation|Spleen processes thick-leaning liver material into more useful matter.|Unchanged kind, a uniquely identified physical product token, or deletion of its earlier liver-related provenance.|
|Appropriateness|The chapter's general nutritional account concerns material appropriate to its recipient.|Anything transferred to, contained in, or attracted by an organ is therefore proper for that organ.|
|Residual origin|III.9 supplied a particular liver-residue description.|All III.13 food, venous juice, spleen product and surplus are instances of that same residual-origin predicate.|

“From liver” in the311 discussion names the immediate compartment of withdrawal;
“from spleen” on313 follows an explicit account of processing liver-supplied
material. The latter could retain a liver-related history while acquiring a
spleen-processing relation. Neither the equality nor the exclusivity of those
possible provenance descriptions is defined here.

## Candidate pairs and the actual capacity limit

**Pair A: ordinary stomach aliment versus fasting venous juice.** Both concern
the stomach's nourishment, and both have meaningful descriptions. Their source-
stated difference is feeding/fasting, location and nutritional phase. They occur
under different conditions/times. Reusing one recipient constant is therefore
not enough to claim the same fully fixed predicate/context input: a context-
dependent modifier that ignores D could change its constant output with the
case. This pair is useful for a conditional transfer account, but is not already
a second RESIDUE_FROM argument.

**Pair B: venous-cavity nutriment versus spleen-worked matter potentially supplied
to stomach.** This is the more promising descriptive variation within the later
account. One description marks available luminal supply; the other marks
processing and an immediate supplier. The second is expressed as a possibility
that should not surprise, and “some of this” points back to the processed supply
without tracking a particular physical portion. Its being more useful and
potentially conveyed to stomach does not alone instantiate the exact III.9
PROPER_FOR predicate. A link to that predicate is a plausible additional
interpretation of the general nutritional account, not a free equality of
USEFUL, TRANSFERRED, NOURISHING and PROPER.

**Pair C: liver-body appropriated material versus vein-cavity material.** This
has the clearest explicit negative: the fasting withdrawal is not from the
former. But the negative is about the withdrawal relation in that phase. It
is not NOT PROPER_FOR(stomach), and it is not a negative ultimate-origin claim.
This could constrain a compartment/availability description, but does not turn
both descriptions into positive applications of one suitability modifier.

Thus there are multiple legitimate description candidates. They could support
a new, openly assumed account of compartment-sensitive or processing-sensitive
qualification. There is no need to demand confirmed words before considering
such an account. What is missing for the stronger proposed discriminator is
an owned pair of distinct D inputs with the **same** fixed recipient predicate
and compatible context, plus a consequence that a constant-in-D output cannot
also satisfy. The chapter does not supply the required cross-membership or
nonmembership facts merely by naming two suppliers. Its cases also do not
identify particular witnesses across the alternative supplies.

This is a limitation of the proposed source binding, not proof that all
predicate/description modifiers are impossible. It also does not erase568's
actual local applications and same-witness projections. Those remain distinct
from a test of dependence on a varying D argument.

## Dependencies, rivals and next decision

A future choice would have to specify whether its D records current location,
immediate transfer source, prior processing, residual generation or nutritional
phase. Those are different additional content commitments. It would also have
to retain case time/need, the modal status of the spleen continuation, and the
body-versus-cavity distinction. A changed supplier name cannot stand in for a
changed material identity or a proven origin relation.

The serious rivals remain: separate descriptions plus ordinary same-witness
relations; a modifier constant in D but indexed by case/context; and a transfer/
processing account in which the observed distinction belongs to source, phase
or event rather than suitability. The whole chapter makes the last rival
substantive, not a straw man. A uniform one-way vessel explanation is opposed
by the source, but rejecting it does not establish the desired material-
description modifier.

**Park an unchanged568 RESIDUE_FROM extension.** Retain the positive source
inventory and the sharp compartment/appropriation contrast as possible input
to a separately selected content construction. Do not start another whole-word
writer on the claim that311–313 already supplies an independent second residual
origin with all other arguments controlled. No new acquisition is required to
state this limit; no next writer or target meaning is selected here.
