## Source ownership and new content

The retained [source search](COMPOSITE_SOURCE_SEARCH_REPORT.md) and
[informed root review](COMPOSITE_SOURCE_ROOT_REVIEW.md) distinguish Arundel's
ten-scene life wheel from its four larger outer figures. The sickbed physician
belongs to one episode; supports and a helper occur in other scenes. The centre
is not established as the Sun. The full outer inscriptions are not available
in the cropped reproduction. The separate spheres table on f123v cannot supply
a cosmic frame for f126v. The institutional catalogue identifies the leaf;
the physician/urinal interpretation also uses Smith's source study, as recorded
in the source report. This task did not fetch or view either source again.
([BL catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-002405144),
[Smith thesis, printed p34 and figure1 p36](https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/8f425cda-3361-4cfd-b2dc-ee0c32016e31/content))

The possible content is not ten age titles shortened to four. It is a nested
argument: a life trajectory has a subject; episodes concern that subject;
within the illness episode another person visits the subject and examines a
vessel. A later terminal assertion, if actually written, would concern that
same trajectory subject. Neither the examination nor circular placement
implies death, cure, failed treatment, numerical age or rebirth. The supplied
source is a depicted program, not a complete transcription of its ten scene
labels. Unread stages, the outer figures and the theological frame remain
obligations before claiming an explanation of the entire source.

The new cost is explicit identity across episodes. A generic life-cycle image
can use different exemplars of ages. Source relevance does not decide whether
the Voynich text says SAME person, each person of an age class, or no such
thing. Nor does a physician's possession of a vessel prove that the specimen
came from the pictured patient.

## A finite model with open written arguments

The card declares five sorts and seven linking relations: SUBJECT, PART, AT,
BEFORE, BEARER, VISITS and EXAMINES. Its minimal case uses two persons, one
trajectory, two episodes, two times and one vessel. All eight variables remain
unbound in the target. Age, dependency, illness and terminal predicates are
additional open content, not hidden sentence meanings of PART or BEARER.

Two stage episodes share the explicitly introduced trajectory subject `p`.
In the illness episode, `d` visits `p` and examines `v`, with `d != p`.
The stage's bearer therefore remains the patient when the grammatical agent
of a local action becomes the physician. The proposed model would require a
written construction that manages this distinction across complete units.
It supplies no invisible anaphora rule and no lexical assignment to `aiin`
or any other recurring form.

The genuine conditional contrast is whose earlier state and later outcome
are asserted. An independent-exemplar model can retain the same sickbed
episode but assign its patient a different identity from the earlier elder.
An explicit fixed reference across those whole statements would distinguish
the two restricted models. A generic word for person, patient or age would
not. Broader typological accounts can also instantiate one person's life, so
the models are not universally exclusive. A bare, freely inserted `p=p`
assertion would not earn a new executor.

The complete future obligation is all already owned f85r2.1–24: the four whole
blocks plus both outside loci, keeping the latter as loci rather than invented
paragraphs. No allocation or ordering has been selected. Counts inherited
from1042 are156/157/160 groups and115/115/123 exact types for ZL/IT/RF;
assigned meanings and assigned positions are both0. A care-only E reading
would repeat533's local question. A new whole account must also own another
episode and a written frame or reference relationship. No large dictionary
or parser is proposed to fill this gap now.

## Egerton: a strong countermodel, but no new protective procedure yet

Egerton2572 f51r supplies names belonging to four persons around a calendar
and zodiac instrument. Cosmas owns the urinal; the Evangelist owns a different
chalice; Damian owns a box/implement; the Baptist's cross-staff is not an
old-age support. The source report and root follow-up establish these
relationships, not a patient-specific diagnostic episode. The humours on
f51v remain a different surface and were not inspected in this task.
([Wellcome photograph catalogue](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/vfk32zzb))

A possible *further* hypothesis would combine a request to a named saint
for some beneficiary with a separate calendar operation. But the current
evidence does not provide a complete request, its beneficiary, protected
condition, fulfilled outcome, or an instruction connecting an instrument
setting to that request. The visible names and saintly attributes do not
provide those clauses. Counting them as four protective roles would merely
relabel people; adding REQUEST, beneficiary, condition, calendar input and
output would create the entire new content without source ownership.

Consequently there is **no separate Egerton RAW** here. Its present gain is
the serious R_SAINT_ATTRIBUTE alternative to an automatic urinalysis reading.
A saint can also be a physician and act in a narrative; those identities are
not contradictory types. The evidence neither forces a procedural care
paragraph nor rules one out. Future work would need a complete already-owned
written relation joining the named figures to an action or instrument use;
this task does not authorize a new source/image search to obtain it.
