## Three consequences worth preserving before interpretation

1. **Keep the proposed whole-form meaning fixed across the ring and prose.** Literal `SUN`, a named plant, and an operation such as `EXPOSE-TO-SUN` are different hypotheses. Under literal `SUN`, the prose may discuss exposure, avoidance, direction or a name involving the Sun while retaining the same referent. Under a whole plant-name hypothesis, the solar ring must actually concern that plant; the picture alone does not supply this extra relation. Under a whole exposure-operation hypothesis, the ring must support that operation rather than silently converting the group into a noun. The immediate useful check is a complete accounting of the lower ring, its differently worded upper partner, and the whole outside paragraph, recording each required role and every proposed sense change. Only the lower ring contains the particular `okoaiin` at issue; no identical Moon counterpart is assumed. A fit requiring occurrence-specific reassignment rejects that **fixed** candidate. Mere presence in Pharma does not.

2. **Separate light treatment from its direction and from its duration.** Positive exposure, restricted light and timed action are competing relations, not alternative translations to choose after seeing difficult neighbours. The source examples show why an operation claim needs a patient plus a light/duration relation; a timing claim needs a temporal condition; neither can be manufactured from `okoaiin` alone. A small working reading could bind one relation-bearing construction consistently while leaving other groups unknown, but must state those bindings before comparison and retain complete context. Repetition of the same written construction elsewhere with an incompatible fixed relation would change the decision. If no relation has any independent or jointly testable binding, these remain equally open; prose length or an unexplained neighbouring group cannot count as support. No particular hidden predicate is supplied here.

3. **A solar term does not identify HOT, SUMMER or even SUN rather than DAY.** The selected entries explicitly separate cold/moist or cold/dry qualities from solar references. Duration in days also occurs beside solar exposure. Thus `SUN` and `DAY/daytime` are useful distinct cross-context rivals, while a default HOT quality is not licensed by the medallion. A future fixed reading must keep a celestial entity, counted interval and material property distinct. A written construction independently constrained as duration could favour DAY over SUN; a relation requiring an illumination source could favour SUN. At present no Voynich numeral, temporal marker or such predicate is identified by this source work. Merely making all three sound plausible changes no candidate ranking.

These are conditional semantic obligations, not claims that unknown Voynich grammar must use Latin/German case endings or a particular adjacent-word order. An encoded construction or inflected/multivalent system remains possible, but its finite rule is an additional assumption. No deletion of the extra `o` in `okoaiin`, inheritance of `okaiin` meanings, or newly assigned prefix rule is permitted by this note.
