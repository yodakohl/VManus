# Source-reading qualification

Append-only clarification to `MEDIATED_COLD_SOURCE_RIVAL_B.md` (2026-09-27): its first rival row compressed two predicates. In the Latin, the **retentive power** keeps food in one place until it receives its due alteration. Cold’s specific contribution is separately described as holding the nutritive material in its essence and figure against heat-driven dissolution and loss of power, incidentally. The “until due alteration” duration belongs to retentive action; it should not be attributed to cold alone.

The closing phrase *frigiditas spiritum a motu suo impellit vel impedit* means cold drives the spirit away/from its own motion or impedes it from that motion. It does not assert that cold positively accelerates or promotes spirit motion; the following *ideo ... per se officit* explicitly characterizes its effect on the powers as direct obstruction. The relative *quae* in the wind sentence most plausibly refers to nearby *ventositas*, but *frigiditas* is also grammatically feminine; the rival report’s reading remains a qualified interpretation, not a settled reference.

The original note remains unchanged; these qualifications narrow its wording without changing the source-compatible rival or decision.
