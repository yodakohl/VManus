### Complete selected source unit

Printed-page references are the electronic editor's labels; no native image was inspected. Martins's normalized electronic edition attributes the un-imprinted print cautiously to Venice/Adam de Rottweil/circa1478. It expands abbreviations and changes spelling; it is not diplomatic.

Complete source-claim inventory (C1–C8):

1. C1: Eastern people receive rising/setting earlier; curvature explains the difference.
2. C2: One numerically identical lunar eclipse appears at our first night-hour and approximately the eastern third; sunset was earlier there.
3. C3: Northern observers have perpetually visible polar stars and perpetually hidden opposite-polar stars.
4. C4: Travelling sufficiently south makes formerly perpetually visible stars tend to set; further south strengthens this tendency.
5. C5: That same traveller can see formerly hidden stars; converse travel reverses the stated direction. Curvature explains these cases.
6. C6: If Earth were flat east–west, stars would rise as soon for western as for eastern observers; this is declared false.
7. C7: If flat north–south and conversely, stars perpetually visible to a person would remain visible wherever that person travelled; this is declared false.
8. C8: Earth's great size makes it appear flat.

### Proposed content construction and the actual consequence

This construction is a new analytical hypothesis, not an attributed Latin grammatical rule.

Two typed channels are required: a local night-hour description of an event, and a visibility regime of a stellar referent. Their outputs have different types. A visibility regime concerns a horizon and an observation cycle, not instantaneous visibility; a local hour label is not an elapsed duration or a global event order. No label is a new event, star or physical trajectory.
