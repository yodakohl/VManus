# Complete Winter boundaries in the Augsburg 1491 print, scans 00169–00171

2026-09-26. Separate source-only extension under [HEAT_WINTER_EXTENSION_DECISION.md](HEAT_WINTER_EXTENSION_DECISION.md). The preceding [HEAT_PRINT_PARALLEL_REPORT.md](HEAT_PRINT_PARALLEL_REPORT.md) remains frozen and correctly incomplete for its original 00171–00175 scope. This report uses only full official HAB scans 00169, 00170 and 00171 of item 167-9 Poet. The Augsburg 1491 identification is retained from the existing witness dossier; the readings below are native-image observations, not catalogue assertions.

**The complete Winter boundary is recovered. Its print text connects winter's cold/moist complexion with permission to eat more, and separately permits things that warm the reader. It does not express an increased-innate-body-heat mechanism or three daily meals.** The animal clause says that apparently “imperfect/incomplete” animals die in winter; it does not describe animals generating or conserving internal heat. Several individual readings remain uncertain, especially one food adjective and the scope of a prohibition. Those gaps are preserved below.

This supports a real seasonal-regimen argument, including warming consumables and health/necessity qualifications. It does not supply the specific Hippocratic `cold season → greater heat inside the body → greater food requirement` chain. The printed witness cannot settle uncertain Karlsruhe letters by substitution.

## 3. Complete meaning account, including qualifications

| Lines | Meaning supported by the print | Limits retained |
|---|---|---|
| V1–V2 | Winter names itself. Whatever is fruitful apparently rests within it. | The exact vowel/word form in V2 remains uncertain; “rests” is provisional. No food-metabolism proposition is extracted from it. |
| W1–W4 | Winter comes promptly and lasts until spring returns. W2 appears to name Saint Clement as bringing it. | The saint reading is visually plausible, but no feast date or calendar calculation is imported. This clause sets season duration, not a physiological mechanism. |
| W5–W8 | The season has cold and moisture in its complexion; **“and therefore”** one may consume more food than at another time. | The topic and rubric support winter as the owner of this complexion; no human organ/body has been introduced as its owner. `Magstu` is permissive: “you may/can,” not an explicit assertion of increased physiological need. The text gives a connection but does not spell out its bodily intermediate mechanism. |
| W9–W10 | The animals called `vn volkomen` die soon/in winter. | This is apparently “imperfect/incomplete animals,” with a separated negative prefix. The exact zoological category is not identified. The written verb is `sterbent`, not a digging, digesting or heat-producing verb. The passage must not be paraphrased as “all animals” or “all irrational animals” without retaining its actual modifier. |
| W11–W13 | Whatever can bring **you** warmth may be enjoyed/consumed during these days, with fine spices as an example. | The recipient of warmth is explicit `dir`, the human addressee. The consumed thing is the potential cause of warmth. This does not say winter has already increased the body's innate heat. `die tag` supplies days/time, not a meal count. |
| W14–W16 | A prohibition qualifies the advice, with an exception when necessity calls for it. The prohibited wording includes taking many drinks/draughts and an uncertain initial `[Lassen?]`. | A likely reading allows “letting [blood]” and taking draughts as prohibited interventions, but **blood is not written here** and that interpretation is not established in this packet. The coordination and exact scope remain uncertain. The necessity exception and prohibition are retained even though the object is not fully resolved. No fasting injunction is inferred. |
| W17–W18 | Water and air are then in a murky/hazy condition. | This concerns the named water/air. It does not locate heat inside the body. Exact modern force of `trübem tuffte` is approximate. |
| W19–W24 | Here one may consume, if available, the qualified kind of food: beef/pork, apparently hare and deer, and similar items, **provided one is healthy and otherwise has no ailment/deficiency**. | W20's adjective is not secure; food that lies heavily/is difficult for one is a plausible sense. A particular digestion mechanism, greater digestive power or rate is not stated. The availability and health conditions must accompany the permission. Meat names retain minor orthographic uncertainty. |
| W25–W26 | Bathe moderately and keep yourself very warm. | This is practical conduct directed at the addressee. It is not evidence that the body is naturally hottest in winter. Moderate bathing must not be expanded into unrestricted heating. |
| W27–W34 | The master's comparison makes this season like an old woman: withered/bent with age, wrestling with death for life, burdened with age and its harms, naked/bare and without help. | Some morphology and the final `sigeloß` remain uncertain; a provisional force is powerless/without victory. The adverse age/death conclusion is part of the complete unit. It does not assert the Hippocratic old-person fever exception, fasting tolerance or an internal-heat value. |

The resemblance of Winter to an old woman belongs to an explicit literary comparison. It does not turn every literal Winter proposition into a proposition about an old human body.

## 4. What the causal argument actually licenses

The following graph uses separate owners. These are analytical summaries of the native passage, not proposed target-language predicates.

1. `WINTER has COLD + MOIST complexion` → via W6's `vnd dauon` → `human addressee MAY consume more food than at another time` (W5–W8).
2. `vn volkomen animals` → `die in winter` (W9–W10). There is no explicit “because” joining this death clause to the reader's food intake, nor a bodily-heat intermediate.
3. `consumable x CAN bring warmth to addressee` → `addressee MAY consume x in these days`; spices instantiate x (W11–W13).
4. `prohibited intervention(s), exact object partly unresolved` → `exception when necessity requires` (W14–W16).
5. `water and air` → `murky/hazy state then` (W17–W18).
6. `food available AND addressee healthy/no other defect` → `permission for the specified foods` (W19–W24), alongside `moderate bathing` and `keep oneself warm` (W25–W26).
7. `WINTER resembles aged woman` → the written old-age/death/helplessness characterization (W27–W34); this is a comparison, not an identity equation.

Three distinctions matter. **More food is licensed, not explained through an explicit digestive-heat mechanism.** **Warming consumables cause or may cause warmth in the reader; that is a different direction from innate heat causing a need for food.** **The text says `die tag`, not `drymal` or “three meals.”** No visible word in this complete unit identifies a belly, stomach, internal heat, innate heat, sleep, fasting tolerance, young athlete or spring as co-owner of winter's food permission. W20 may concern difficulty of food for the eater, so the cautious result is an unresolved food-quality/digestibility qualification, not a claim that the text has no connection whatever with digestion.

The print's cold/moist season and warming prescription can sustain an explicit exploratory **season/regimen** model. They do **not** by themselves sustain the narrower **outside cold / increased inside heat / increased food need** model. The complete Hippocratic I13–15 argument remains a separate source hypothesis with its own wording and qualifications.

## 5. Witness separation and decision

This is a 1491 printed witness according to the existing dossier, later than the Karlsruhe manuscript. Shared content is evidence of a related tradition, not an independent origin or a demonstrated direct copying relation. No uncertain Karlsruhe word has been silently changed to match `sterbent`, `vn volkomen`, `die tag`, the Saint Clement reading, the drinks prohibition or W20. The two witnesses would require an explicit collation to establish an exact variant; this packet does not perform a new manuscript reading.

The apparent contrast with the earlier tentative “three meals” reading is therefore a warning about that **inference**, not a declared established manuscript/print variant. The print itself gives a different, directly readable formulation.

**Decision: COMPLETE_BOUNDARIES_PARTIAL_NATIVE_READING_NO_INTERNAL_HEAT_BRIDGE.** The extension succeeds at recovering the whole Winter unit and a substantive seasonal argument. It does not confirm the requested innate-body-heat mechanism; several subordinate words remain unresolved. Preserve the permissive food relation, warming-consumable relation, animal death clause, necessity/health/moderation conditions and adverse old-age ending together. Neither this result nor the first packet confirms any target meaning. No further neighboring page or acquisition is needed or authorized by this extension.
