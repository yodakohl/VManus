# Symeon Seth honey-entry provenance check

## Edition and scope

A verifiable Greek edition is available: Symeon Seth, *Simeonis Sethi Syntagma de alimentorum facultatibus*, ed. Bernhard August Langkavel (Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1868). The scanned edition is catalogued as Greek text with Latin introduction/notes; the scan is from a Royal College of Physicians of London copy. Langkavel’s page 69 is the complete entry headed *Περὶ μέλιτος* (“On honey”), with apparatus notes at the foot of the same page. It corresponds to PDF scan page 83 (printed page 69). Catalogue records and scan metadata identify this as the 1868 edition; this task did not inspect its introduction or assess the editor’s manuscript stemma.

I compared that whole-page Greek entry to Noble’s complete English translation (entry 86, printed p. 7). The central recipient contrast is present in Greek: honey is suitable for old people and, generally, cold constitutions; it provokes bile in people in their prime and of hot constitution. The entry also says spring honey is better, winter honey unusable, and yellow bile arises especially in hotter intestines. Preparation is also conditional: diluted honey is boiled until fully skimmed, with the foam continually removed; honey boiled on its own becomes less pungent and more nourishing. The complete entry continues with uses for phlegmatic anorexia and thick sputum, and cautions about difficult digestion, obstruction from poorly prepared honey, and itching after excessive continuous intake.

## Material translation difference

Noble’s sentence says that honey mixed with plenty of water is “left to ferment until it has formed a good head of foam.” The Greek page instead has *ἑψηθῇ* (boiled) and *ἄχρις ὅτου τελέως ἀπαφρισθῇ* (until completely skimmed/cleared of foam), followed by the instruction that the foam is continually removed. I find no fermentation clause in the inspected Greek entry. This is a material processing difference; the previous report’s central recipient and seasonal claims remain supported, but that preparation detail must not be repeated as if confirmed by Langkavel’s Greek text.

The exact effect of the clauses on bowel activity and the anatomical referent of *κόλπων* in the boiled-honey benefit are not resolved here; Noble’s rendering may be retained as a translator’s choice, not as a verified equivalence. The printed page includes variants, so this single-edition check is not a manuscript-level collation.

## Conclusion

The specific historical recipient contrast has stronger textual provenance than the Noble PDF alone supplied: it is directly legible in an identified Greek edition of the named work. Preserve it as a Symeon Seth textual parallel, not as a Laufenberg or southern-German source, and retain its separate provenance. The full Greek passage does not establish target meaning, direct influence, or an experimentally validated mechanism. No target material or whole-draft/result was accessed.
