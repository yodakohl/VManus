# Regional housebook programs: fixed source comparison

2026-09-26 UTC. External-source research, not a Voynich reading or regional
attribution. The [decision](REGIONAL_COMPOSITE_DECISION.md) fixed six discovery
queries, a ceiling of twelve manuscript objects, and at most two complete
source images before selection. Eleven objects entered the roster; slot12
remained unused. One new source image was inspected. No Voynich data, reserve,
contact, semantic executor or current reading draft was accessed.

The regional material supplies several **real but different kinds of connected
program**: season/age diagrams, a planetary figure with zodiac medallions,
body-to-celestial correspondences, and sequences of astronomical and medical
chapters. This bounded comparison did not establish a single new page combining
the entire season/life-age, medical-actor, cosmic-ring and wind/cloud bundle.
That is a coverage result, not evidence that no such page exists.

The most consequential additional caution is physical: two related Swabian
housebooks have displaced illustrations. A planet beside a medical passage
can result from a misplaced or pasted picture. The strongest unviewed exact
comparator is Edinburgh Cr.4.6,121v, whose catalogue describes drawn links from
a human body to planets and zodiac signs. Its photograph was not obtained.

## 2. The one newly viewed complete page

**Cod.Sang.760,p47** was selected in the decision before opening pixels.
The [exact institutional canvas](https://e-codices.ch/metadata/iiif/csg-0760/canvas/csg-0760_047.json)
is labelled47. The [full-region rendition](https://media.e-codices.ch/iip/csg/csg-0760/csg-0760_047.jp2/full/2200,/0/default.jpg)
is2200×2933. No crop, montage, rotation, OCR or generated reconstruction was
used. This is informed native observation, not a blind iconographic test or
specialist palaeographic certification.

- A large naked, curly-haired figure fills the upper field. A star covers the
  genital region. The figure stands above two separately outlined medallions.
- The viewer-left hand supports an open book with red writing. Its left page
  permits the short reading **tota pulcra es amica mea**. Other writing was not
  fully transcribed. A red flower grows on a green stem to the figure's left;
  it is not held in that hand.
- The viewer-right hand suspends two implements or containers on cords: a
  long, narrow reddish-brown object and a smaller brown object. Their exact
  functions remain uncertain; a writing case and ink container are plausible,
  but not established here. No rounded flask raised toward the eyes is visible.
- One lower medallion contains a robed woman; the other has two facing naked
  seated figures whose arms meet. Their celestial names follow catalogue
  context, not newly read labels in this inspection.
- No encompassing year/cosmos annulus, four-stage division, physician examining
  a patient, blowing heads, cloud boundary or wavy band is visible. The page
  supplies the figure, book, flower and two medallions together; it does not
  supply the larger requested conjunction.
- Upper-right47 is visible. A lower red numeration is not securely read and is
  not silently converted to another folio identity. Apart from numbers and
  writing on the book, there is no running explanatory paragraph on this page.

The catalogue proposes a later flower/book-inscription addition and a Marian
interpretation, while explicitly leaving its placement unexplained. Neither
the later hand nor that interpretation is established by the pixels alone.
The source is useful precisely because a flower and book coexist with a
planetary program without entailing a season or an examining physician.
Mercury as a medical/celestial alternative was already a project predecessor;
this is an exact additional witness, not its discovery.

**Pagination limits:** the institution uses154 pages; KdiH's codicological
description says154 leaves while citing pages. KdiH's enema references vary
between119 and120; the institution uses120. Those discrepancies are not
reconciled here, and no enema-page pixels were inspected.

## 3. Why physical co-location needs a written or codicological link

Cgm28's catalogue describes red cross-references correcting a shifted picture
program. Thus a reader could be instructed to associate a picture with text
away from its present position. Munich578 is a stronger material warning:
the catalogue identifies both the pasted planets and the medical picture
spaces they now occupy. These are source-specific catalogue claims, not a
diagnosis of displaced Voynich text or pictures.

This creates a serious alternative to direct diagrammatic ownership: an
adjacent picture may illustrate its present text, another referenced text,
or a displaced original context. It does **not** justify choosing whichever
ownership makes a target reading succeed. A new hypothesis would need the
actual cross-reference, physical displacement or comparable written evidence.
None is supplied for the target by this research. For that reason this useful
constraint is retained in the source report rather than registered as another
unbound RAW mechanism.

## 4. What the comparison changes, and the next bounded opportunity

The principal gains are exact regional witnesses, differentiated program
boundaries, one native source-page receipt and two material counterexamples
to assuming current image/text adjacency. The source collection now includes
more than English comparators. It still does not explain why one particular
target must have a ring plus its surrounding passages, nor assign those
passages to seasons, ages, planets or medical roles.

A further catalogue-level limit is concrete in C54: the introduction to the
bloodletting figure48v explicitly points to it, yet the intended numbers on its
red body-lines were not painted, although subsequent prose refers to them.
This supplies written image ownership together with an incomplete linking
apparatus. It does not license inventing missing labels. No48v image was opened
in this run. [KdiH C54](https://kdih.badw.de/datenbank/handschrift/87/1/18).

**Best next source inspection, if separately authorized:** obtain a verified
institutional photograph of Edinburgh Cr.4.6,121v and its complete local
writing, within a short access budget. The catalogue's body-point lines supply
an actual relationship to inspect, unlike another generic circle. Record
whether the endpoints are body parts, named planets or signs; whether any
annular text states a global proposition; and whether a physician or life
phase is actually present. A body-to-sign allocation would favor a distinct
correspondence architecture; an unlabeled generic person or incomplete image
would not provide a full content contract. Do not conflate this with104v's
medical Mercury or87v's physician, or import their roles into121v.

For this run, exact folio searches and one UNC catalogue API attempt produced
no verified121v image identity. The API returned an HTML application shell.
No guessed image ID, second source image or serial endpoint search followed.
This is an access gap, not absence of a photograph in every repository.

No new RAW was added. Existing mixed-section, planetary-role and body/cosmos
routes remain available; the new evidence constrains their ownership claims
