## 1. The final GRIND and its carrier

Contract6 says without a type qualification that CRUSH/GRIND create a prepared
solid bulk containing coarse and powder submaterial. Literally, this includes
the final `chy` on f32v.11. V1 contains no written exception for that occurrence.

Contract12 makes KNEAD consume the powder into a paste. Contract13 explicitly
requires the water already contained in that paste as the medium of the final
GRIND. The written-consequence section preserves ancestry and tracked plant
solid through that operation. These fix the input, medium and plant-solid
amount. They do **not** say how the universal solid-bulk output of contract6
relates to the paste carrier, water and oil afterward. It is therefore not
licensed to claim that V1 already specifies an unchanged paste containing all
three components after grinding. Nor is it licensed to discard the liquids.

Two omissions must be corrected openly in a new model: distinguish the
physical aggregate from its plant-solid constituent, and define GRIND on
that constituent while retaining a separately specified carrier. Replacing
universal production of coarse-plus-powder by a general non-coarsening size
change is also a substantive effect-rule change. Keeping the English word
GRIND does not make this merely an editorial clarification of its denotation.
No rule mentioning only f32v.11 would be adequate.

The local coarse-Q versus two-Q-products contradiction does not depend on
this final operation. The claimed fully specified terminal paste state does.
No executed successful model is being rescued: V1 was a raw development offer.

## 2. What V1 says about POWDER compatibility

There are three different statements:

* Contract7 makes the coarse and powder inventories disjoint parts of one
  prepared bulk. This is object/mass disjointness, not a complete type lattice.
* Contract8 assigns the coarse names to the coarse branch and FINE_PART/FINE
  to its powder branch. Contract9 calls FIBRES and GRANULES distinct coarse
  product kinds and explicitly says they are not further powder aliases.
* Contracts11–12 require an available compatible material and prohibit
  inventing missing powder. The consequence section intends P, not F/G, as
  the .10 powder reference.

Thus **P-only was the intended local reference**, supported by these explicit
branch assignments and exclusions. However, V1 has no closed table specifying
all kind/subtype matches, containment matches and role-sensitive access. Mere
inequality of C and P does not prove that no coarse object can also satisfy a
generic powder predicate. Nor does the phrase “not aliases” alone implement
every possible subtype exclusion. The intended local reference should not be
presented as a theorem of a complete existing compatibility matrix.

A V2 must explicitly make powder and coarse-grist reference domains disjoint
in this finite model, retain the F/G exclusions, distinguish a free powder
stock from powder contained in paste, and state when a medium-reference can
access a contained water component. Those are newly completed model rules;
they are not observations about the historical words or powder taxonomy.
