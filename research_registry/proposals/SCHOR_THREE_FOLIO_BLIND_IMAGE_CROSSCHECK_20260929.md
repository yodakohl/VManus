# `schor`: three-folio check against existing word-blind organ inventories

29 September 2026. **Positive compatibility, no translated word.** GDT765 proposed exact whole `schor` ≈ *Blütenstand* (C1) before the two later Herbal images f32r and f42v were admitted; it enumerated all three exact all-reader occurrences at f22r.4, f32r.4 and f42v.10. GDT1089 subsequently froze two independently written **word-blind** six-code image inventories for 38 Herbal folios, including these three. This review selected the comparison **after** those codes and f22r's morphology were visible to root. It is a transparent retrospective cross-check, not a registered prediction test or independent semantic confirmation.

The old noun is interpreted at a broad pictured-organ level as a conspicuous compound or pointed reproductive head, operationally `MULTI_UNIT_SPIKE OR SPINY_ROUND_HEAD` in **both** frozen image inventories. This union is a review choice, not GDT1089's registered organ-match gate. It preserves the different actual geometries instead of relabelling f42v's star-like pointed organ an elongated spike.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| f22r.4 | internal H2 field before `daiin` | `MULTI_UNIT_SPIKE=YES` | `MULTI_UNIT_SPIKE=YES` | Many red/pale bead-bearing upright axes; three unlike dark cups also occur on this one plant. |
| f32r.4 | line-initial item head | `MULTI_UNIT_SPIKE=YES` | `MULTI_UNIT_SPIKE=YES` | Several terminal and axillary compact clusters of many tiny pale/blue units. |
| f42v.10 | line-initial item head before `okchey` | `SPINY_ROUND_HEAD=YES` | `SPINY_ROUND_HEAD=YES` | One upper pointed star-like organ with a dark centre; flower versus fruit remains uncertain. |

For denominator visibility, the same broad union is YES in **12/38** GDT1089 folios in both observers, including the three `schor` folios; it is YES in **9/35** other folios. The wider union also including radiate heads and broad-petal flowers is YES in 20/38 and is too permissive for the proposed specific organ. GDT1089's 38 folios were selected for its different first-head edit-neighbour experiment, not as a random herbal image sample. `schor` was one of many earlier semantic candidates. Therefore these 3/3 matches cannot receive a search-wide p-value or a promotion to confirmed meaning. All three Voynich readings concern one manuscript, and page co-presence does not point the exact word to the drawn organ. In f22r the paired `ofchy daiin` / `schor daiin` fields lack an authorial organ-to-text pointer; the three dark cups and the many red axes must not be assigned to either field by convenience.

The concrete gain is that the old `schor` reproductive-head candidate survives **all of its exact occurrence pages** now available in a word-blind visual coding, including two leaves not visually admitted when GDT765 was formulated. The picture supports the broad class but does not distinguish *Blütenstand*, *Samenstand*, flower, fruit, a general plant-part item, or a same-page subject marker. GDT765's C1 working gloss is retained as a practical hypothesis without raising its confidence; confirmed word meanings remain zero. A future discriminator must either find a singular word-to-organ owner on a new eligible folio or freeze a complete, search-controlled image-text prediction before inspecting its image. No new target data were admitted here; f84/f84r and reserves remained closed.
