# f22r: two named arum entries checked against the paired upper forms

29 September 2026. Exploratory, target-exposed source screen. The already admitted [Yale f22r image](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006116/full/1500,/0/default.jpg), SHA-256 `9982030dc91881139804bcab671927443e84aada50c4a45289632ae6c8eeacd0`, has three dark blue cup/bell terminals and many red/pale bead-bearing upright axes connected to one pictured stem. A new candidate was arum: a spathe and a later upright berry spike can occur in one plant's life cycle. This supplies a potentially nontrivial *stage* explanation for the two pictured forms, but it was not a Voynich textual reading.

The named primary images were selected by their historical entries, not by visual similarity to the target. The [British Library Egerton MS 747 manuscript record](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001983805) dates the *Tractatus de herbis* witness to about 1280–1350. Its [f.48v `Iarus` image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x000068/full/1500,/0/default.jpg), SHA-256 `53b964872be1dfabdb595ec8a059bc8f2243c6dab861e7f9ca714f526666728f`, depicts **two red beaded upright fruit axes** on an arum with large arrow-like leaves and one tuber. The blue flowers on the right of this same folio belong to a **separate pictured plant and separate entry**. They must not be counted as arum's blue terminals. The folio's label reads `Iarus` beneath the left plant.

The same manuscript's [f.93v `Serpentaria` image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100058663074.0x0000c2/full/1500,/0/default.jpg), SHA-256 `4fd3cc6a56f7647fa5847193143a56710dbd235872c42a3586269cf0b74ea48b`, depicts a **single tall spathe with a dark inner spadix**, sharply divided leaves, and a snake around the stem; no red berry-spike array is depicted under that entry. Its adjacent left plant is again separate. The [Kew species description](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:86067-1/general-information) supports the botanical possibility of a spathe/spadix and a later fruiting spike in *Arum maculatum*, but it does not identify either manuscript drawing as that modern species or prove that f22r combines those stages.

Thus these two named historical drawings each match **one broad organ class**, but neither shows f22r's paired three-blue-cup/many-red-axis architecture under one entry. The neighboring blue plant on f.48v is an especially concrete false co-presence control. `Iarus` and `Serpentaria` are different historical entries; combining their features would invent a source picture. No source-written stage relation has been aligned to an independently owned Voynich text field. Arum remains a C0 botanical idea, with less direct visual support from these witnesses than its generic morphology suggests. No plant name or word meaning is confirmed, and f84/f84r and reserves stayed closed.
