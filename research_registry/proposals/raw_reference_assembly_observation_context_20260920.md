Complete proposition content: construct an equilateral triangle on a given finite
line AB. Declare AB given and repeat the required construction. Draw BCD centered
at A with radius AB, and ACE centered at B with radius BA. Their meeting point is
C; join CA and CB. Since A centers CDB, AC equals AB; since B centers CAE, BC
equals BA. Recall CA equals AB. Both CA and CB therefore equal AB. Invoke the
common-notion rule that things equal to one thing equal one another; conclude
CA equals CB and all three sides are equal. Thus ABC is equilateral on the given
AB, completing the required construction. The edition points to Postulates1/3,
Definition15 and Common Notion1. These cited rules are proof dependencies, not
newly constructed geometric objects.

Attested compact reference mechanism: the same A/B/C identifiers recur inside
AB/BA, AC/CA, BC/CB and ABC; circle names BCD/CDB and ACE/CAE are also reused in
different orders. A segment's reversed name denotes the same undirected segment;
that convention is different from silently deleting or replacing a whole word.
A prospective writer could therefore bind several groups through common endpoint
components, rather than assigning each a free object name. No EVA character or
substring is here identified with a Greek/Latin letter. The entire source proof,
not only its drawing operations, is part of the proposed reading.

Concrete consequence: the common-side argument requires the AB in both radius
claims to be the same segment, and C to belong to both circles. Merely repeating
an equality operator is insufficient. With distinct A/B and a selected circle
intersection, the triangle is equilateral; replacing a circle's radius by an
unrelated length invalidates the inference. Uniform point-renaming and reflection
of C preserve the geometry and remain symmetries. A diagram-free equality story
with all quantities freely declared equal does not establish this construction.

Scope conflicts: IDEA125 already proposes construction programs. The present
addition is a whole proof/reference reading on a nominated complete paragraph,
not a wider JOIN/CIRCLE code. I.10's omitted helper circles remain a countercase
to universal helper retention. GDT214's failed general label/prose-reuse prediction
and GDT947's failed ol reference rule remain; no ol prefix is assigned here.
No corresponding equilateral diagram or point labels have been identified on the
target, and the nominated passage may simply not describe geometry.

Complete worked content: on12March1391 the narrator wants the time. On the back
of the instrument the Sun's altitude is25degrees30minutes. Turning it over,
and because it is before midday, he turns the rete and puts the Sun's degree,
first degree of Aries, on the right side at25degrees30minutes among the
almucantars. He puts the label over the Sun's degree, reads its point at capital
X on the border, counts capital letters from midnight to X and obtains9o'clock.
On the eastern horizon he reads20degrees of Gemini ascending and adopts that
as ascendant. He concludes that he has learned how to find both time and
ascendant. “Label” is the instrument pointer here; it is not a written noun label.

Attested reference reuse includes the same altitude on the back and front, the
same solar degree for rete-setting and pointer-setting, and the same capital X
first read and then used as the endpoint of counting. In the proposed whole
reading these are shared typed values/references, not separate free numbers or
independent assignments. The two reported outputs belong to the same instrument
setting. Before/after midday chooses the side in the prerequisite general rule.

Concrete rival: changing only the transferred altitude or the later solar degree
breaks the observation/setup identity while leaving a plausible sequence of
commands. Independently choosing one rete position for the clock and another
for the ascendant violates the worked account. Numerically deriving9o'clock
and20Gemini requires the source instrument's plate/calendar calibration, which
this packet does not own as a full computational model. Consequently simultaneous
