# GDT213 — Ashmole 399 source audit

## Institutional object

Bodleian Libraries, MS Ashmole 399 is catalogued as a Latin composite of
medical and arithmetical treatises and recipes, made in England.  The public
IIIF manifest identifies fols. 13v and 22v and binds the official image
services.  The exact manifest and resized-image hashes used for inspection are
recorded in `gdt213_ashmole_source_manifest.tsv`.

## Scholarly claims admitted

Karl Whittington's 2008 article, DOI `10.61302/GLRT6998`, identifies fol. 13v
as female reproductive anatomy and fol. 22v as an eyes-and-brain diagram.  It
distinguishes original diagram labels from later surrounding texts concerning
conception, childbirth, treatment, and herbal medicine, and treats the two
pages as unusually conceptual or process-oriented medical diagrams.

Sarah Griffin's 2018 Oxford project essay independently describes fol. 13v as
schematic and architectural, says that individual components are labelled,
and describes the longer surrounding texts as later practical remedies in
multiple hands.

These are source interpretations.  GDT213 does not independently translate or
read the Latin.

## Direct visual observations

Direct inspection of the official images establishes only visible geometry:

- fol. 13v has a dominant bilateral connected system, paired curved paths,
  round and teardrop forms, column-like lower forms, and a central channel;
- a small humanlike figure appears within an upper oval;
- some short inscriptions visibly lie within or against components;
- dense longer text occupies open spaces inside and around the system;
- fol. 22v uses another branching symmetrical system, but continuous prose
  dominates the page rather than a dense inventory of isolated component
  captions.

The image inspection did not use OCR, object detection, image similarity,
embeddings, segmentation, or automated captioning.

## Independence and limits

Whittington and Griffin discuss the same manuscript and are not independent
physical samples.  The two Bodleian folios are likewise pages of one codex.
Their value is architectural: they demonstrate that a medieval medical
compendium can vary between component-labelled and prose-dominated schematic
systems.

They do not establish an exact q13 homolog.  The readable subject is anatomy
and generation, not a bath-site record.  Ashmole 399 lacks q13's repeated
arrays of figures in pools and its long page-spanning hydraulic networks.
