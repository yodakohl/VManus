## Human-source page structure

The public catalogue records 45 loci: 32 prose lines in three paragraphs and
13 graphical labels.  Its neutral geometry includes two upper marginal figures
connected by two lines/tubes and a joining element, a second pair with a
different connection, a lower green bounded region containing several figures,
and a separate blue bounded region containing one figure.  These are quoted
human descriptions, not interpreted flows, treatments, patients, or materials.

All 13 exact graphical labels are joined.  Twelve have proximity-only evidence;
only f82r.10 is described as a label **on** a cross-shaped component.

## Prose-field scaffold and its coverage limit

The available GDT229 selected-record lattice contributes 26 fields on only
eight distinct prose loci—**8/32 = 25%** of the human prose-line census.  It is
therefore a partial scaffold, not a full-page parse.  Those 26 fields comprise:

- 16 short-argument-like fields;
- 10 instruction-clause-like fields;
- 19 DY-ended fields and seven physical-line-ended fields.

The strongest working paraphrase at this granularity is:

> a densely chained technical record alternating compact component/parameter/
> state-like entries with longer setting/procedure/description-like entries.

That sentence describes the hypothesized **information architecture**, not the
Voynich plaintext.  Every compact field also retains material, case, condition,
location, quantity, and degree alternatives; every long field retains practical
description, indication, setting, and procedure alternatives.

## Best current page theory

The leading abductive page model is an illustrated therapeutic-or-hydraulic
practical record with component/state arguments.  Two mandatory alternatives
remain equally live at the source-unit level:

1. an illustrated case/indication record;
2. an apparatus/setting key accompanied by prose;
3. a nonsemantic record renderer.

No field is securely owned by a pictured object, and the field lattice covers
only one quarter of the catalogue prose loci.  Consequently none of these
worlds yields a clause translation.
