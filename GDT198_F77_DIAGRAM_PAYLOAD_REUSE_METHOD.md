# GDT198 — f77 diagram-internal payload reuse

## Purpose

Test a local consequence of the hybrid compiler after GDT196 found no prose
dictionary bridge.  The same opaque HPR2 payload may be rendered differently
in tube-state and figure-associated labels on the same diagram.

This is an exposed YOLO synthesis.  The payload abstraction was noticed after
the f77 labels were visible and is therefore tested against a complete local
assignment null, not presented as a preregistered discovery.

## Inventory

The six tube labels are inherited unchanged from GDT180.  Four figure-label
rows are transcribed in `gdt198_f77_figure_label_manifest.tsv` from the cached
human exact-locus/source-alignment layer.  Ownership remains `PROXIMITY_ONLY`.
The first three figure labels are single-group and stable in ZL3b/IT2a/RF1b.
The fourth is `otolaiin | o` in ZL3b but joined `otolaiino` in IT2a/RF1b; it is
excluded from the exact primary null and retained as a reading-sensitive
substring lead.

## Frozen local comparison

Parse the nine stable single-group labels with the frozen GDT196/HPR2 parser.
Define the deliberately renderer-stripped payload as:

```text
PAGE_HOST | RIGHT_FAMILY | DY | B3
```

`WRAPPER`, `INNER_D`, and `O/OT_FRAME` are excluded because the hypothesis is
that they render the same local address differently.  Count figure labels
whose payload occurs among the six tube labels.

The exact null enumerates all `C(9,3)=84` assignments of three rows to the
figure-label class, keeping the nine parsed payloads fixed.  The inclusive tail
is the fraction with at least the observed number of cross-class payload
matches.  No alternative payload definition is scored.

## Claim ceiling

At most this supplies a local example of opaque payload reuse across visual
label classes under different compiler renderers.  It does not establish
ownership, quality, figure identity, tube function, word, sound, language,
plaintext, meaning, or translation.  `f84r` and every `f84*` row are excluded.
