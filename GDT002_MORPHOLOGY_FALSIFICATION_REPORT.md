# GDT002 falsification-first morphology report

Status: **FORMAL REUSE SUPPORTED; SEMANTIC SLOT SYSTEM NOT SUPPORTED**.

This exploratory checkpoint tests the fixed strings `AR`, `OL`, `DAL`, `DAR`,
`SY`, `TE`, `TEE`, and `DY`, plus `d-/s-/q-/o-/ot-` contrasts. It inventories
manual source groups in ZL3b, IT2a, and RF1b, which are alternate readings of
one manuscript rather than replications. The displayed nearest-basic-EVA form
is a lossy projection of the source-native STA alignment. Cleaner-created
fragment boundaries are never treated as spaces. The sealed f84r formal
payload is excluded before formal records are retained or scored.

## Label density versus running text

The comparison uses only physical source groups with the same surface and
group count in all three readings.

| Register | Groups | Candidate hits / 100 symbols | Multi-candidate groups | Standalone candidate groups |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Labels | 630 | 13.519 | 100 / 630 = 15.87% | 8 / 630 = 1.27% |
| Running text | 17,828 | 10.586 | 1,295 / 17,828 = 7.26% | 814 / 17,828 = 4.57% |

Labels therefore have 1.28 times as many candidate hits per symbol and 2.19
times the multi-candidate-group rate, while standalone candidates are much
less common in labels. This supports denser concatenation in labels. It does
not by itself identify morphology: register, source-group length, selection of
the fixed strings, and label ecology remain confounds.

The excess is not caused solely by nested `TE`/`TEE`: after excluding both,
labels still have 11.969 hits per 100 symbols versus 9.396 in prose, and an
11.43% versus 3.99% multi-candidate-group rate. Conversely, the four proposed
right-edge candidates alone have a slightly *lower* hit rate in labels (4.558
versus 4.846 per 100 symbols), although multiple right-edge candidates co-occur
more often in labels (1.11% versus 0.19%). The evidence is therefore for dense
formal combination in general, not a clean right-morphology slot by itself.

## Visual-role tests

No fixed module produces a convincing page-controlled visual association.
The smallest page-conditioned one-sided value is 0.151 (`DAL`, human-described
upper versus lower positions). Other leading results are `OL` in explicit
flow/duct descriptions versus figure descriptions (effect 0.218, p=0.307),
and `AR` in apparatus-exclusive versus figure-exclusive descriptions (effect
0.035, p=0.347). Human descriptions provide position/proximity evidence, not
author-visible ownership; unmentioned roles remain unknown rather than
negative.

The f82v spatial sequence illustrates why meaning cannot be read from a neat
surface pattern:

## Conclusion

**Partly, at the descriptive level; no, at the decipherment-model level.**
Whole-word similarity alone is inadequate because fixed pieces recur freely,
bound, in exact transformation families, and across manual split/join
boundaries. Labels are also measurably richer in concatenated candidates.

But the proposed fixed semantic slots do not yet explain the manuscript better
than the strongest whole-source model. Slot boundaries overlap (`DAR` versus
`d + AR`, `TE` inside `TEE`), visual roles do not survive page control, and the
most tempting object interpretation is directly contradicted by plant labels.
The supported claim is reusable **formal composition**, not recovered
morphology in a historical language and not translation.
