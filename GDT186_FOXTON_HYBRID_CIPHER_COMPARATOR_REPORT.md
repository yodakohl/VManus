# GDT186 — a real 1408 hybrid cipher sharpens the Voynich theory

## Result

John de Foxton's *Liber Cosmographiae* supplies a strong historical positive
control for a **hybrid technical concealment system**, but not a viable direct
Voynich decoder.

The important mechanism is more specific than “medieval writers used
ciphers.”  In Foxton, 135 operative or rubric-like words are selectively
enciphered inside otherwise readable Latin.  Diagram keys can supply a noun
that subsequent descriptive paragraphs omit.  In one chapter, only the first
syllable of the recurrent headword is ciphered while the remainder stays in
Latin, and normal Latin abbreviation is used inside the cipher.  Enciphered
vocabulary concentrates in physiognomy, palmistry, phlebotomy, obstetrics, and
astrological prognostication.  A contemporary Fontana manuscript shows that a
technical work could instead be almost wholly written in an artificial
alphabet.

This gives the leading GDT181 theory a historically attested analogue for its
most unusual combination: opaque address/headword material plus separately
rendered record structure.  It also generates a concrete new route: test
whether diagram labels behave as page-local **keys for omitted or distributed
head material**, rather than expecting a label to be an ordinary noun repeated
verbatim in prose.

## What it does not explain

The direct Foxton mechanism fails as a whole-manuscript account.  Foxton is a
Latin text with selective substitution-alphabet concealment; most of its substrate is
readable.  Voynich has no recovered readable substrate, and its confirmed
page-conditioned inventory, line reset, wrapper/right-edge ecology, DY field
chaining, and B3 closure are not consequences of a letter substitution.
Existing historical-language, injective, homophonic, abbreviation, and
word-code models also remain far behind the best nonsemantic source model.

The analogy therefore licenses neither a Latin plaintext nor a key.  It only
raises one mechanism above generic speculation: **diagram/rubric keys plus a
technical record compiler** is historically real; **simple selective
substitution** is insufficient for Voynich.

## Decision

Status: **FOXTON_SIMPLE_SUBSTITUTION_INSUFFICIENT_HYBRID_RUBRIC_POINTER_ROUTE_OPEN**.

The next experiment is a source-native, semantics-free keyed-omission test:
on nonsealed pages with independently inventoried labels and prose, compare
whether label PAGE_HOST inventories predict page-local paragraph/field
structure while exact label repetition is absent or suppressed.  It must be
compared with matched same-page label permutations and ordinary page/register
effects.  This is a route to locating content addresses, not a license to
gloss them.

No Voynich word, sound, language, alphabet, plaintext, or translation is
established.  f84r was not accessed.
