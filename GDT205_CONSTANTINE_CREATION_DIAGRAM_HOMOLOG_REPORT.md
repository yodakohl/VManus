# GDT205 — a real six-day metal system, but not a six-cell f77 key

Status: **READABLE_SIX_DAY_METAL_CREATION_ANALOGY_FOUND_EXACT_F77_HOMOLOG_ABSENT**.

The Constantine comparator is more structurally relevant than a generic
furnace drawing.  A scholarly source describes a 1257 medical student's
alchemy compilation in which diagrams and tables explicitly connect planets,
metals, lunations, and artificial transformation.  Its creation argument says
that six metals were brought forth in the six days of creation.

The decisive falsifier is the rendered architecture.  The 1361 witness does
not present six process cells: it uses a vertical sequence of seven
planet/metal circle segments, followed by lower cosmological segments for
earth, air, and the Dead Sea.  The later Flemish adaptation adds a creator
circle, personifications, animals, and waters.  Neither witness documents the
f77 conjunction of six ordered states, five adjacent relations, and four
active outputs plus one hold.

## What this changes

The historical prior becomes sharper.  Medieval alchemical manuscripts could
compress a technical theory into ordered labelled diagrams and tables whose
labels named planets, metals, elements, and lunar conditions.  That is a good
architectural analogue for an anonymous page-conditioned technical compiler.

It is not a translation key.  The attractive number six belongs to the
theological explanatory layer, while the visible diagram has a different
cardinality.  Mapping f77's six labels to six metals or six days would therefore
be cardinality cherry-picking.
