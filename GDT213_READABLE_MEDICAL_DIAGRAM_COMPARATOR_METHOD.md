# GDT213 — readable labelled-medical-diagram comparator

## Question

Can a readable medieval medical manuscript supply the document architecture
currently missing from the q13 theory: an abstract connected system containing
short local component labels while longer practical text occupies the same
page?

This is not a search for a pictorial double of q13.  It is a bounded
system-level comparator.  It cannot identify a Voynich object or transfer a
readable label.

## External source

The primary source is Bodleian Libraries, MS Ashmole 399, fols. 13v and 22v,
from the official institutional IIIF service.  Fol. 13v is identified by the
scholarly sources as a schematic female reproductive-system diagram.  Fol.
22v is identified as an eyes-and-brain diagram.  The manuscript is an English
Latin medical compendium from the late thirteenth century; the Bodleian-linked
catalogue range is 1250–1310.

The source inventory freezes exact URLs and retrieved hashes.  The images are
not redistributed.  Direct image observations are explicitly marked
`AI_DIRECT_VISUAL_OBSERVATION`; anatomy, textual content, layer chronology, and
component identities come only from the cited scholarship.

## Fixed comparison

Eight axes are declared without fitting:

1. schematic connected system;
2. short local labels plus longer prose;
3. singular component ownership;
4. embedded humanlike figure;
5. multiple chronological text layers;
6. visual recovery of text content;
7. balneological specificity;
8. exact topological homology.

The q13 side is limited to already registered f80r/f82r facts and the published
GDT210/GDT212 conclusions.  Across those two pilot pages, the frozen GDT002
inventory contains 23 text-linked rows: 22 `PROXIMITY_ONLY` and one
`CONNECTED_COMPONENT` (f82r.10).  These counts are inherited public facts, not
recomputed from an inventory containing the sealed holdout.

No Voynich transcription, source group, image, statistical feature, or f84
artifact is read by the runner.

## Interpretation rule

An architectural match may establish only that the mixed diagram/label/prose
format has a readable medieval medical precedent.  It does not establish
anatomy, generation, baths, disease, treatment, label ownership, textual
chronology, language, or translation in Voynich.

The highest-value discriminator remains absent: a readable bath or hydraulic
diagram with independently singular component labels and q13-comparable
topology.
