## Result

**MINORITY_EXACT_LABEL_TO_PROSE_CODE_INTERFACE_DESCRIPTIVE_NOT_SEMANTIC**

Across 23 label groups aligned in all three readings, three exact source-native
member identities recur as complete internal prose groups:

| source surface | graphical label | neutral human geometry | prose occurrence | paragraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `okaly` | f80r.3 | above figure 3 | f80r.31 group 6/8 | P2 |
| `olky` | f80r.7 | above figure 7 | f80r.38 group 2/13 | P3 |
| `okal` | f82r.36 | between figure 2 and a vertical feature | f82r.6 group 6/9 | P1 |

All three equalities hold in ZL3b, IT2a, and RF1b. They are stronger than
family-shape similarity: the complete ordered member vectors and visible
source forms agree. All prose occurrences are line-internal, whereas the
graphical occurrences stand alone.

## Generative implication

A small set of graphical labels can be generated by selecting an otherwise
prose-compatible opaque group and rendering it alone near a figure. This is a
better fit than a model with wholly disjoint prose and label vocabularies. It
also corrects an overly literal reading of the transferred label-prefix layer:
all three matches carry a GDT233 label-enriched prefix, and `olky`/`okal` are
entirely consumed by that prefix, yet the same forms occur in prose. The prefix
is therefore a rendering propensity or construction family, not an exclusive
"this is a label" morpheme.

## Counterweight

The interface is sparse: only 3/23 aligned label groups match same-page prose,
and all three human ownership grades are `PROXIMITY_ONLY`. The remaining 20
labels do not have an exact local prose counterpart. Family-level matching is
broader than member-level identity, and ordinary prose itself contains many
recurrent short groups. This is a candidate code interface, not evidence for a
dictionary or a named figure.

## Translation consequence

`okaly`, `olky`, and `okal` are now the best exact cross-scope content-address
candidates on the two discovery pages because they exist both as isolated
graphical groups and as internal prose groups. Their referents remain unknown;
the visible associations are positional rather than ownership evidence.
