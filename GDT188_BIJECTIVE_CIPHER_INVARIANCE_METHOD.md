# GDT188 — bijective scientific-cipher invariance audit

## Question

Can a Foxton- or Fontana-style one-to-one artificial alphabet turn an
otherwise ordinary abbreviated Latin technical text into the residual Voynich
surface algebra measured by GDT159/GDT160?

This is a mathematical reuse of frozen results, not a new decoder search.

## Invariance

For a bijection `f` on graphematic symbols applied consistently while keeping
source-group and physical-line boundaries fixed:

- group equality, length, recurrence, and host support are unchanged;
- every prefix/suffix replacement operation is carried to an isomorphic
  operation under `f`;
- every complete transformation rectangle remains complete;
- the LEFT×RIGHT compatibility graph is graph-isomorphic;
- operation counts, compatible-pair density, left/right support totals, and
  the GDT160 degree/frequency-preserving excess are numerically unchanged;
- line/record reset statistics are unchanged.

Named glyph identities and language-model scores are not invariant.  Standard
abbreviation, homophony, nulls, nomenclators, context-dependent replacement,
and a separate record compiler are outside this theorem.

## Frozen numerical comparison

Reuse the published GDT159 matched fingerprints and GDT160 exact pairing null.
The primary comparators are `LATIN_15C_GRAPHEMATIC` and
`LATIN_MEDICAL_GRAPHEMATIC`; no corpus, transformation, threshold, or metric is
reselected.  Ratios are deterministic arithmetic from the published rows.

## Decision

A direct bijective scientific cipher is insufficient for a frozen comparator
when its identity-invariant compatible-pair density remains below half the
Voynich value.  This rejects only that corpus-plus-bijection model.  It does
not reject a more complex hybrid compiler, an unknown plaintext distribution,
or a nonbijective/context-dependent cipher.

No f84r source row or image is read; only previously published aggregate
results are used.
