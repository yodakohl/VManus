# Public-prior stop for another cross-boundary increment test

Status: **STOP_PUBLIC_PRIOR_ALREADY_TESTS_BROADER_ESTIMAND**

The proposed next experiment would ask whether the penultimate STA family of
one source group predicts the second family of the next after conditioning on
the immediately adjacent terminal/initial pair, group lengths, position, and
Currier. No manuscript score was run.

That route is not sufficiently new. Reddy and Knight (2011), “What We Know
About the Voynich Manuscript,” already used the last `m=1,2,3` transcribed
characters of one word to predict the first `n=1,2` characters of the next.
Their Table 5 reports measurable but modest Voynich improvements, including
the exact broader `m=2, n=2` case containing the proposed penultimate-to-second
increment:

<https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf>

Smith and Ponzi (2018/2019) directly quantified last-to-first glyph
combinations across word breaks:

<https://agnosticvoynich.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/glyph-combinations-across-word-breaks-in-the-voynich-manuscript-preprint.pdf>

Parisel (2026) additionally describes word-to-word transitions as a
left-to-right boundary dependency and explicitly frames cross-boundary mutual
information as flowing between suffix and prefix graphemes:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19762>

The exact source-synchronized, held-folio, confound-controlled implementation
would be cleaner than those public analyses, but it would refine an existing
constraint rather than discover one. It is therefore stopped before capacity,
calibration, or target scoring. The already completed `NONE` versus literal
`qo` adjacent-context target is also distinct but nonconfirming, so it supplies
no reason to reopen this family.

Decision: **DO_NOT_RUN_ANOTHER_SUFFIX_TO_PREFIX_BOUNDARY_MODEL**. A future
boundary experiment must introduce genuinely external ownership, graphical
slot structure, or a mechanism not reducible to suffix/prefix prediction.
Nothing here establishes authorial wordhood, syntax, sound, language, cipher,
meaning, plaintext, or translation.
