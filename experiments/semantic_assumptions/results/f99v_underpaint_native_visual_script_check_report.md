# f99v under-paint native visual script check

Decision: **THE MARK IS VISIBLE, BUT ITS SCRIPT IS UNRESOLVED; COL001 DOES NOT REOPEN**.

This was a worthwhile near-miss to inspect because a genuine second
Voynich-script note beneath a different paint colour would materially change
the colour-note route. The target was selected before viewing from the hedged
human annotation at `f99v.45`, which describes apparent tiny letters under the
reddish paint on the west tuber of plant `[3,4]`.

The official 2014 Yale image and the public tile from the 2004 Beinecke scan
both show a small, low-contrast dark trace inside that painted tuber. It is
much less sharply bounded than the ordinary Voynich writing around the row.
Across the two witnesses it does not produce a stable character count or
segmentation. It cannot be classified independently as Voynich-style or
plain-alphabet writing, and neither a plain `p`/`r` nor the older `qo???`
suggestion is recoverable without prompting.

Ordinary-light pixels also cannot decide whether the trace is deliberate ink
beneath translucent paint, pigment variation, abrasion, parchment texture, or
another incidental mark. A future multispectral or qualified palaeographic
reading could revisit that physical question. The current images cannot.

Therefore f99v.45 is not authenticated as a second Voynich-script under-paint
record. `f2r.15` remains the sole Voynich-script colour-note candidate, and
the existing `COL001` lexical stop remains unchanged. This result assigns no
character, colour, word, sound, language, cipher, plaintext, meaning, or
translation.

This is source-bound native AI visual inspection, not literal human
annotation. No OCR, automated transcription, multispectral inference, CLIP,
embedding, image-similarity score, automated glyph classifier,
enhancement-derived reading, or external decoder claim was used.
