# f76r key-like sequence source and ownership audit

Date: 2026-08-08  
Decision: **STOP_NO_DOCUMENTED_FUNCTION_PRESERVE_VERTICAL-SEQUENCE /
LINE-MARKER / SUBENTRY AMBIGUITY**

## Why this audit was required

F76M001 treated the nine f76r margin marks as an aligned-line view plus a
hypothesized interval/block view. After its negative score, the ownership
premise was checked against the complete human sources and public discussion
before any new interpretation was attempted.

## What the sources actually say

All manual transcriptions agree on the top-to-bottom sequence:

`s, d, q, s, o, l, k, r, s`.

The detailed Stolfi interlinear says each item is a label for a corresponding
line. The current human page catalogue instead describes 47 text lines in one
long and three short paragraphs and says the single characters run across the
height of the first paragraph. Jorge Stolfi's 1998 Note 034 calls the same
column a possible vertical “title,” while stating that its marks are
irregularly spaced and only seem aligned with main-text lines. A 2020 public
discussion explicitly leaves open one long paragraph versus several
symbol-marked paragraphs and notes that some marks do not align cleanly with
lines or proposed paragraph starts.

The permitted Yale image is compatible with all three descriptions. Direct
inspection can confirm the column and its approximate alignment but cannot
choose its logical ownership. Pixels were used only for this quality check.

No located source supplies an authorial gloss, index mapping, reading
direction, object mapping, or statement that the marks are initials, bullets,
numbers, title letters, or record-class labels.

## Relevant prior observations

Note 034 inventories other key-like sequences on f49v, f57v, f66r, f69r, and
f75v. It uses them primarily as evidence that several shapes can stand alone;
it explicitly warns that special contexts can mix letters, digits,
punctuation, bullets, section signs, and mathematical symbols. This is not a
semantic key. The f76r glyph set overlaps other isolated-glyph contexts, but
those contexts have different topology and unresolved functions.

The current page description also says that f76r may be a section opener
because of its ornate first character. Public proposals that it is a preface,
index, or summary remain hypotheses. Quire-13 reordering and possible missing
folios prevent a unique nine-mark-to-nine-page mapping.

## Disposition

Retain the physical facts only:

- nine stable one-glyph marks form a vertical column;
- their top-to-bottom order is fixed;
- they lie beside selected lines in the 29-line first paragraph;
- `s` recurs three times;
- f76r is a plausible section-opening page.

Do not call the nine following intervals records, concatenate the column into
a word, assign it to nine later pages/folios, or infer title, index, number,
operation, initial, glyph meaning, plaintext, or translation without a new
authorial mapping. A future test may use `ALIGNED_LINE` ownership alone or test
the two complete vertical orientations as formal sequences, but cannot assume
either interpretation as truth.
