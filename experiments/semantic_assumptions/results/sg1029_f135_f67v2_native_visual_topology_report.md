# Sainte-Geneviève 1029 f135 / f67v2 native visual topology

Decision: **SOURCE 4x3 POSITIONAL MAP PASSES; f67v2 TRANSFER STOPS**.

Native inspection confirms that Sainte-Geneviève MS 1029 f135 is more
structured than its catalogue prose alone established. Twelve bust medallions
form one annulus. Four larger crowned busts occupy the principal top, right,
bottom, and left axes; exactly two smaller busts occur between each consecutive
pair. The source is therefore an exact four-sector-by-three-person system.

The four name strings published by Initiale occupy four distinct axes inside
the central disk. Each string is radially aligned with one crowned bust. This
upgrades the source-internal relation from a collective four-name register to
a positional cardinal-name-to-crowned-bust map. The eight smaller busts are
not individually named in this diagram.

The official Yale f67v image confirms a close iconographic family resemblance
but not the same topology. Its detached corner face clusters contain 3, 4, 4,
and 3 faces clockwise from northwest: fourteen faces, not twelve. They are not
a continuous annulus and show no four-crown hierarchy. The page also contains
four strings on the principal axes around a central rosette and four separate
diagonal blue-accented, text-bearing motifs. The principal-axis strings are
the closest visual analogue to the source's central cardinal register, but
they do not visibly connect to the corner face clusters.

Accordingly, the four principal-axis strings may be retained only as a
`FOUR_AXIS_PRINCIPAL_WIND_OR_DIRECTION_REGISTER_CANDIDATE`. The role is not
confirmed: the source and target do not share unit cardinality, annular
ownership, crowns, or an independently fixed start and orientation. Copying
`oriente`, `occidente`, `miechiorn`, or `aquilo` onto any Voynich locus would
be an unsupported source transfer.
