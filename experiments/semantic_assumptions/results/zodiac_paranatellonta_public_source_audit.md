# Public-source audit of the f67--f73 circle block and the 30-degree prior

Status: **PUBLIC_PROVENANCE_CONFIRMED; POSITIONAL_TARGET_NOT_IDENTIFIED**

## What is public evidence

The f67--f73 scope is not a user-created grouping. René Zandbergen's public
illustration catalogue classifies f67--f68, with named exceptions, as
astronomical; f67v2, f68v3, f69, f70r1, and f70r2 as cosmological; and f70v2,
f70v1, f71, f72, and f73 as astrological/zodiac pages. It describes the zodiac
panels as concentric arrangements of usually thirty nymphs and explicitly
records the two 15-figure Aries panels, two 15-figure Taurus panels, and the
missing Capricorn and Aquarius pages. The already validated local public-data
atlas expands those folios to 26 page panels: seven astronomical, seven
cosmological, and twelve zodiac.

Public sources:

- [Voynich MS illustration catalogue](https://www.voynich.nu/illustr.html)
- [Stolfi/Grove public label catalogue and field definitions](https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/EXPORT/00-EXPORT/98-02-01-lotsa-labels/)
- [Public zodiac-label analysis](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/labels.html)

The Stolfi/Grove catalogue supplies 300 expected zodiac slot records. One
f72r2 record is explicitly `Not labeled`, leaving the publicly reported 299
actual labels. These records, ring descriptions, and item numbers are public
human annotations. No statement by the user is treated as manuscript data.

## What the historical comparison actually predicts

The public Voynich catalogue says that the presentation may be related to
*paranatellonta*, because each zodiac sign contains thirty degrees. This is a
document-family prior, not a decipherment. Public descriptions of Alfonso X's
*Libro de astromagia* (Vatican Reg. lat. 1283) say that each extant sign has
thirty degree images with accompanying descriptions. The images derive from
fixed stars or constellations, while the associated text describes the fate of
someone born under that degree. A related *Astrolabium planum* tradition also
assigns a distinct drawing and sentence to each degree.

Sources:

- [Fiorello, public overview of the Reg. lat. 1283 Book of Paranatellonta](https://heavenastrolabe.wordpress.com/2008/08/08/hermes-book-of-paranatellonta/)
- [Forshaw, description of the image and fate-description rings](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110604337/pdf)
- [ORAEDES catalogue of the Astrolabium planum degree series](https://www.oraedes.fr/Medias/Astrolabe-plat)

This makes one common proposed test unfair: the historical model does **not**
predict that degree 1, degree 2, and so on should be repeated number words
across signs. It predicts degree-specific image/constellation/fate records,
which may naturally have a flat vocabulary. The 80% zodiac-label hapax rate is
therefore compatible with this model but does not identify it.

## Remaining blocker

No checked public source fixes which Voynich slot is degree 1, the direction of
travel, or how separate inner, middle, outer, and off-circle bands continue one
another. Stolfi/Grove's item numbers are a public editorial inventory; they are
not an authorial degree key. The public historical analogues also do not give a
one-to-one mapping from their distinctive degree images or descriptions to the
mostly repeated Voynich nymph--star slots.

Therefore do not score a universal degree-number stem, concatenate bands into
an invented 1--30 order, or infer DAY/DEGREE/NUMBER from the shared cardinality.
The route may reopen only with a provenance-clean human-transcribed degree list
and an independently justified Voynich start/direction/band mapping, or with a
distinctive one-to-one labelled sequence that determines that mapping. No OCR,
automated vision, Voynich string score, lexeme, plaintext, or translation was
used or produced in this audit.
