# PHF001 pharmaceutical flower-count and exact-recurrence screen

This bounded screen asks whether the public human pharmaceutical-label
catalogue supplies a new, directly owned semantic contrast after the failed
root-colour and broad label-decoder routes.

1. Select only unhedged pharmaceutical records whose object is `plant` or
   `root` and whose human comment contains exactly one of the literal English
   forms `flower` or `flowers`. The wording is only a human-described drawing
   state.
2. Require the current-locus crosswalk's unique primary mapping.
3. Reuse every prior source-bound native-visual ownership grade unchanged.
   Before inspection, select every as-yet-ungraded primary flower-count record
   on f88, f101, or f102. Inspect only whitespace, row structure, and overlap
   on exact official Yale witnesses; do not transcribe labels or identify
   plants.
4. A contrast is worth a text test only if both states have at least four
   clear owned records, both span four physical folios, and three folios mix
   the states.
5. Separately count complete literal label recurrence among all clear owned
   pharmaceutical cells. Retain only rows present in ZL3b, IT2a, and RF1b with
   identical compact literal surfaces. A candidate must recur on two folios.

The direct observations are machine-authored and source-bound, not human
annotations. A development diagnostic exposed the complete literal surfaces
after ownership selection, so any later use of these exact rows is exploratory
rather than analyst-blind. No OCR, CLIP, embedding, automated image analysis,
plant naming, formal parser root/role, or translation is used.
