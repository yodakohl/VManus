# FDTW f68r2 Sun--Moon ring metadata worth check

## Question

Does the already validated 263-record human-curated FDTW metadata projection
contain a source worth escalating as an exact readable homologue of f68r2?

This is a cheap text-only worth check.  It may read the validated FDTW TSV and
the cached human-authored voynich.nu description of f68r2.  It may not open a
manuscript image, thumbnail, canvas, paper, PDF, OCR output, automated visual
output, decoder claim, or historical word list.

## Fixed source-side screen

Search the FDTW label, description, archetype, and theme fields.  A broad row
must contain all four independently frozen term families:

- circle: `wheel`, `rota`, `circular`, `circle`, `circles`, `roundel`, or
  `concentric`;
- `sun` or `sunlight`;
- `moon` or `moonlight`;
- `star`, `stars`, `constellation`, or `constellations`.

Keep source order.  Do not select or remove a row after inspecting an image.

## Exact-topology escalation bar

The cached human description of f68r2 identifies five simultaneous features:

1. a moon face near the top;
2. a sun face near the bottom;
3. a large circle of stars connecting the two;
4. stars inside the circle, most with labels;
5. separate circular text around both the moon and the sun.

An FDTW metadata row may escalate only if its own human-authored text explicitly
describes that paired-medallion, connecting-star-ring, internally labelled-star,
owned-circular-text topology.  A generic cosmogram, planetary order, computus
rota, elemental scheme, creation scene, or any row that merely contains the
four broad terms must stop before image or paper review.

For reproducibility, search normalized text within a single description and
require all five relationship predicates: a top/upper Moon followed by a
bottom/lower Sun; a circle/ring of stars explicitly connecting or joining both
Sun and Moon; stars explicitly said to be inside/interior and labelled; circular
text/writing/inscription explicitly associated with both Moon and Sun; and an
explicit readable/transcribed/identified/named label or inscription relation.

that no matching manuscript exists.  It cannot identify a celestial object,
