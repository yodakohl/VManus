Date: 2026-08-12

## Fixed target and new source

The target is the already registered locus `f68r2.31`, the circular writing
around the lower face medallion on Yale canvas `68r`. Public/manual source work
and direct inspection of the official ordinary-light image already stop at an
ambiguous ending: most of the ring is ordinary Voynich-style writing, while
the final weak marks cannot be independently segmented as a plain-script word.

This follow-up opens exactly one genuinely new rendering: manifest item 122,
`image.123.jpg`, in the public December-2024 `oshfdk` full-manuscript
paint-removal gallery. It compares that rendering with Yale canvas `1006196`.
The exact URLs, hashes, dimensions, and prior-result hashes are frozen in
`f68r2_processed_sun_ring_worth_screen_observations.tsv`.

## Question and stop rule

Ask only whether the processed rendering exposes either:

1. a stable multi-character plain-alphabet segmentation in the disputed
   ending; or
2. a visibly separate inscription or equivalence relation rather than a
   continuation of the same circular register.

Inspect the bounded medallion crop in multiple orientations. Stop if neither
condition is met. Do not test `SUN`, `Suna`, `koiin`, another word guess, a
decoder, or a language fit. Paint removal is an algorithmic display transform,
not physical-layer or multispectral imaging.

This is source-bound native AI visual inspection recorded as machine-authored,
not human palaeography. It uses no OCR, automated transcription, glyph
classifier, CLIP, embedding, or similarity score. A pass could only identify a
new readable script register or explicit relation. A stop supplies no letter,
word, sound, language, cipher, plaintext, meaning, or translation.
