## Question

Do the complete human-authored transcription comments that explicitly allege
an addition above a baseline preserve source-visible evidence for a distinct
later insertion?

This is a physical writing-process question. It is not a character-reading,
language, cipher, or translation test.

## Source and outcomes

Use only the official Yale IIIF canvases bound by the official manifest. View
source-native pixels without enhancement, OCR, CLIP, embeddings, automated
segmentation, or automated image recognition.

Classify each target once as:

- `SECURE_VISIBLE_SUPRALINEAR_INSERTION`: all five gates below pass;
- `VISIBLE_SUPRALINEAR_MARK_CHRONOLOGY_UNRESOLVED`: gates 1--3 pass but
  separate/later physical state is not established; or
- `NO_SEPARATE_MARK_OR_UNRESOLVED`: the mark or its anchor is not secure.

Frozen per-target gates:

1. the registered locus and target word are securely localized;
2. a bounded mark exists materially above the ordinary baseline writing;
3. the mark has one unique author-visible baseline host or gap;
4. a separable ink-density, stroke-edge, or noncontinuous-placement boundary
   distinguishes the mark from the baseline writing; and
5. the baseline form remains physically coherent without the supralinear mark,
   so the observation is an addition rather than an inseparable complex glyph.

## Panel decision

`PASS_RECURRENT_VISIBLE_SUPRALINEAR_INSERTION_PRACTICE` requires both of the
two independently selected physical loci to pass all five gates. One secure
locus may be retained descriptively, but does not establish a recurrent
practice. Otherwise stop the panel as
`STOP_NO_RECURRENT_VISIBLE_SUPRALINEAR_INSERTION_PRACTICE`.

## Claim ceiling

A panel pass would establish only that two manuscript loci preserve visible
supralinear insertions anchored to baseline writing. It would not establish
the correct identity of either mark, correction intent, phonetic value,
morpheme, word, language, cipher, plaintext, meaning, or translation.
