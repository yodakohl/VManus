# EBA001 mechanistic constraints after cached-route failures

The negative experiments do not imply that the manuscript is meaningless.
They constrain what a viable encoding mechanism must do.

| Registered observation | Mechanistic constraint |
|---|---|
| Strong grammar/adjacency structure transfers across held Currier strata and folios. | The production system has reusable sequential and construction-level regularities. |
| Exact labels do not behave as stable object-name keys. | Meaning cannot normally be recovered by a global one-surface/one-object lexicon. |
| Simple visible binary attributes do not map to reusable formal affixes. | Diagram semantics, if present, are not a small fixed visible-attribute code at current transcription resolution. |
| RTA001 found no held-out topology-specific edit-operator transfer. | Author-visible relations are not expressed by a small reusable discrete text-edit algebra at the tested levels. |
| LTG/TGC current three-reading channel routes fail or lack fresh capacity. | Alternate readings cannot presently be treated as independent samples or as a validated latent physical segmentation channel. |
| IGR002 coarse shape prototypes fail mapping-invariant held transfer. | Current STA members are not stable visual glyph identities under a six-field prototype. |
| EBA001 shows that the registered f17r/f116v marginal traces persist as dark features in three source TIFF captures; the response mechanism remains unresolved. The prior ordinary-image relation audit, separately, found no gloss device. | A physical mixed-script context remains possible, but EBA001 does not prove ink, fixed-relief exclusion, hand, date, or lexical equivalence. |

Four live illustrative mechanism classes satisfy the constraints (they may
overlap and are not proved exhaustive):

1. **Position-conditioned generative notation.**  Forms encode structural role
   or construction state, while lexical identity is diffuse or context-bound.
   Risky prediction: genuinely independent readable anchors should align with a
   construction or sequence state more reliably than with exact token identity.
2. **Multiple-choice or abbreviation-heavy technical notation.**  A stable
   grammar combines reusable components whose local realization varies by hand,
   domain, or abbreviation practice.  Risky prediction: physical corrections
   should preserve construction role while changing several surface units, and
   recovered before/after states should be interpretable through an independent
   readable technical value.
3. **Homophonic/polygraphic encipherment with strong formatting grammar.**  Many
   surfaces can encode the same value and the same surface can be conditioned by
   position.  Risky prediction: a true bilingual anchor will collapse several
   formally distinct forms onto one readable value, but a second contrasting
   anchor will restrict the permutation rather than support arbitrary remapping.
4. **Text-generation or scribal-exercise system with local semantics only.**
   Grammar is real, exact object labels and diagram edits need not be.  Risky
   prediction: new physical layers will establish production chronology and
   repeated construction practice but repeatedly fail to yield explicit
   authorial equivalences, even where later readers mix scripts.

These are mechanism classes, not language assignments.  The current physical
acquisitions are upstream constraints on whether a purported mark, layer, or
relation is real; most do not yet discriminate these four text mechanisms.
Only a newly authenticated explicit equivalence or contrasting readable value
would force divergent semantic predictions among them.
