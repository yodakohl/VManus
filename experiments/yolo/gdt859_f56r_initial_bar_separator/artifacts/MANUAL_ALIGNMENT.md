# Root post-extraction visual/transcription alignment

This manual alignment was made after A was sealed, B returned and the fixed
three raw source lines were opened. It is not a preregistered software match
or an independent second alignment. It uses the same complete original native
view from the discovery, without a crop, recognition model or image change.

The initial small low oval before A, the following low run, the next low run
ending near B, and the next low oval plus the taller paired form C provide a
consistent alignment of A with source group1, B with group2 and C with group3.
The literal first three source groups are ZL/RF `o@167;chal`, `chchs@168;y`,
`oty`; IT `otchal`, `chchsty`, `oty`. In particular, the third `oty` group is
the first common three-character source anchor following both special forms.

At this gross group level root judges the alignment confident. On that manual
alignment, the native AB upper link crosses the definite first/second stored
group seam. B independently confirmed the visible connection, not this raw
alignment. Software proves neither correspondence nor paleographic truth.
The result is not evidence that an entire glyph or linguistic word necessarily
crosses the seam: a decorative or other supra-group upper stroke remains a
possible interpretation. A visible contact also cannot prove one pen movement.

The first two ZL/RF groups retain @167; and @168;, while IT has literal t at
those respective string positions. This is exact raw reading correspondence,
not our replacement operation. No historical character-definition table was
consulted and the numeric entities are not assigned new identities or meanings.
The canonical significance of those transcriber codes remains outside this
packet. There is no glyph inventory correction or forced choice of reading.
