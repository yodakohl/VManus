**Registered decision: UNSCORABLE.** The preregistered all-reader gate cannot
be evaluated because RF1b's source metadata marks **zero of 3,768** running
line starts as paragraph starts. ZL3b has 665/3,768 and IT2a has 697/3,767.
The runner's literal `DIRECTION_NOT_UNIVERSAL` flag reflects RF1b's artificial
ties, not observed counterexamples; it is preserved in `RESULT.json` and not
promoted to a scientific negative decision.

There is nevertheless a concrete, broader **descriptive pattern** in the two
channels with paragraph metadata. The exhaustive census found 47 exact
line-initial `pX`/`yX` pair bases. Seven have at least three occurrences of
each complete form across at least two physical folios per form in ZL3b; all
seven have a higher paragraph-start fraction for pX than yX. IT2a has eight
supported bases, all eight in the same direction. The supported bases include
`aiin`, `chedy`, `cheol`, `cheor`, `chey` and `chor`; `cho` is supported only in
ZL3b, while `chol` and `daiin` pass only in IT2a. The complete 141-row table

paragraph contrast is **not unique to `chor`** in the observed channels. A
position-sensitive rendering of related complete forms is a live alternative
to assigning those two spellings unrelated historical words. It does not prove
that `p` and `y` are morphemes, or that either means an opening/continuation
operation. The all-reader requirement did not pass, and neither reader is an
independent manuscript. No significance or semantic confirmation is claimed.
