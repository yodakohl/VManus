# GDT625 — `cthy` is Blattgut; state order alone is not an operation

## Result

The pass began by looking for "moist -> dry" as a coded drying operation. The
best opened Herbal candidate instead exposed a concrete plant-part word:

```text
cth- = Blatt-/Krautteil-Familie
cthy = Blattgut, Blattdroge (folium)
```

This is now the preferred working default:

It never starts a line in the safe panel. It can stand on either side of a
quality form:

```text
f18r.12  qokchy cthy  -> hot-dry leaf drug
f29v.4   cthy oltchy  -> leaf drug: cold-dry
```

The direct f18r line is identical in all three readings. On the eleven opened
Herbal images, `cthy` occurs fifteen times across six folios. Seven of the
fifteen are within two tokens of `chor/shor`; f15v alone contains four `cthy`
and is visually dominated by four large leaves. "Oberirdisches Kraut/herba"
remains the strongest rival, so the German default is the material term
"Blattgut" rather than a forced singular "Blatt."

## The corrected f29v reading

Surface:

```text
ysho otshy okaiin cthy oltchy s shot sho okaiin
```

The first pass noticed `otshy ... oltchy` and read a possible cold-moist to
cold-dry transition. The local attachment is better:

```text
[otshy okaiin] [cthy oltchy]
[cold-moist <okaiin carrier>] [leaf drug: cold-dry]
```

Current German working line:

```text

```

The drawing contains one plant but visibly different drug parts: flower,
buds/fruiting bodies, two large leaves and rootstock. That makes two parallel
part-state phrases more economical than a time sequence. Drying remains a
secondary reading, not the preferred one.

The preceding f29v.1 now has a more useful partial translation:


This is the first current line that names all three major visible drug-part
classes in one local entry: underground drug, reproductive head and leaf drug.

## What ordered states do say

The extended terminal family contains 1,162 occurrences, 200 surfaces, 168
pages and 912 loci. The registered GDT624 grid contributes exactly 829 of
those; 333 retain an additional external prefix.

Successive local occurrences yield 535 pairs:

| locality | dry -> moist | moist -> dry |
|---|---:|---:|
| same line, all terminal forms | 17 | 17 |
| next line, all terminal forms | 15 | 14 |
| local total, exact same prefix/e/d frame | 7 | 1 |

The all-form direction is essentially balanced. The exact-frame imbalance is
created mostly by adjacent dry/moist contrasts and cannot itself mean
"moisten." Written order therefore supplies a conditional relation, not an
automatic verb:

```text
same carrier + dry -> moist = befeuchten/einweichen
same carrier + moist -> dry = trocknen/abtrocknen lassen
different or unknown carrier = part contrast remains primary
```

Six successive three-state paths exist, and all are dry -> moist -> dry: two
KCH-KSH-KCH and four TCH-TSH-TCH. This is the shape expected for "soak, then
dry," but only f29v lies on an already opened image and that case splits more
cleanly into two carriers. The path remains a productive recipe template to
test, not a translation already won.

An independent exact-grid census gives the same practical boundary. Strict
same-shell CH/SH pairs occur in both directions; no current named carrier sits
inside a three-reading-stable minimal flip. `shor` remains a useful head and
`dair` a root carrier, but neither proves an operation here.

## The next possible process word

Intervening-token counts rule out the tempting mediator `cthy`: it bridges two
moist-to-dry pairs, but its 90/92 Herbal restriction, part contacts and visual
fit identify the processed object, not the operation.

`otar` also bridges two moist-to-dry pairs and no dry-to-moist pair. It has 123
safe occurrences, 110 conservative stable witnesses, sits mid-line 116/123
times, and is only 24/123 Herbal. Current low-confidence default:

```text
otar = danach / bis zum nächsten Zustand / separates Prozesswort
```

The compact f85r1.4 sequence is the best text-only candidate:

```text
doltshdy otar otchdy
moist-state ... otar ... dry-state
```

It is not enough to choose among "then," "until," or a concrete drying verb.
The distinction is now located in the correct slot.

## Historical architecture

Wellcome MS 542, early fifteenth century, puts learned drug/part names beside
full or abbreviated humoral qualities in its simple-medicine glossary. In its
recipes, reached states and operations are separate: `calidum` is the hot
application state, while heating is expressed by placing the preparation over
coals; washing, pounding, putting in wine and distilling are explicit verbs.

Wellcome MS 541, mid fifteenth century, is an even closer mixed book: recipes,
Saladinus' apothecary compendium, a compact hot/cold dry/moist degree list,
quid-pro-quo substitutions and an Arabic-Latin herb-name glossary coexist in
one codex. This supports the overall working model:

```text
productive quality abbreviations
+ learned whole drug/part words
+ separate recipe-operation words
```

It predicts that the next search should target a repeated token in the linear
slot between a known material carrier and a changed target state.
