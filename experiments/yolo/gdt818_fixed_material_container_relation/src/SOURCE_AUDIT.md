## Four seed readings, one genuine difference

The all-reader seed core remains `solkeey okaiin chedy qokain`.
Conditional core readings are: vapour whose becomes water; vapour whose
contains water; basin whose becomes water; basin whose contains water.
The proposed owner is still the written source otedy at f77r.25, not an
identified relationship. The rest of .35 has not been translated.

Contains gives the basin proposal a physically intelligible content relation
without turning becomes into becomes-filled. That is its genuine local gain.
It does not independently identify the noun, predicate, subject role or owner.
The transformation and containment accounts therefore remain separate models,
not interchangeable expressions for the same decoded event. This text audit
does not resolve whether vapour contains water in the intended historical
material sense; that is a substantive question, not a change of definition.

## Both predicates must carry the awkward written constructions

At f76r.23 ZL/IT write `sheedy qokedy chedy chedy qokain ...`.
The fixed displays therefore contain either “wird wird Wasser” or
“enthält enthält Wasser”. Containment does not repair the adjacent-predicate
problem. A second unexpressed subject or result is additional syntax to find;
deleting a predicate or treating one as an auxiliary is not allowed.
The f72r3.26 doublet adds the same obligation in a complete C record.

f75r.16 ends with reader-exact `qokain chedy`; .22 has
`odar shey qokain chedy or shey kar chedy sar` in ZL/IT, with later RF
splits. Both belong to the complete .1–26 paragraph. A fixed contains account
must also consider WATER as the written preceding possible subject, not
always water as a following container content. Becomes faces the analogous
change-of-state subject question. Neither is refuted merely because .16
ends at the predicate: .17 follows within the same paragraph and may continue
the construction. The unknown next words do not identify its complement.

The whole f83r.9–17 paragraph contains neither exact solkeey nor exact otedy.
Thus `... chldaiin chedy qokain ...` at .16 has no already named basin,
vapour or source supplied by those words. Choosing a concrete preceding
subject or a larger topic is still necessary under either predicate model.
The later solkeey .52 begins another paragraph and is not silently borrowed.

The only exactly-one-intervening-word chedy/qokain construction found by this
rule is `chedy chedy qokain` itself. It is not a distinct clean complement
frame that supports contains or becomes independently of the doublet.

## The four-word tail has no external exact-pair bridge in this scope

The searched pairs `sheedy qokaiin`, `qokaiin chedaiin`, and `chedaiin chealy`
all occur only at f77r.35 in ZL/IT within these 39 selectors. They provide no
second exact continuation under this declared rule. This does not say that
their individual words are unique or their structure is meaningless.
RF instead writes the FIVE-token tail `sheedy qotaiin che aiin chealy`.
Its qotaiin is not qokaiin; its che aiin is not chedaiin. All five stay unknown.
No tail is replaced by while-cooling, after-heating or another unstated process.

The useful present conclusion is narrow: contains fixes the basin-to-water
semantic mismatch at the seed, but not the repeated predicates, subject
selection, owner relation or untranslated continuation. Neither relation
is promoted by the same pleasant German clause it was chosen to produce.
