# GDT627 report — one written value slot, two concrete semantic uses

## Result

GDT626 found a bounded four-cell ending but could only call it “value I–IV.”
GDT627 supplies the missing head semantics. The same written slot is reused
productively:

```text
quality head + a + value  = quality in degree I/II/III/IV
d            + a + value  = free measure or grade I/II/III/IV
```

In a plant-part list, the practical default for the second construction is a
local amount or dose. Thus `daiin` no longer means a generic operation and no
longer remains the opaque “d-value III.” It reads **three local measures or
portions**, with the kind of unit still open.

This produces the first reusable current-sidequest strings that express both a
named property and its intensity:

```text
qokan chan       heiß ersten Grades; trocken ersten Grades
chain qokain     trocken zweiten Grades; heiß zweiten Grades
qokaiin shaiin   heiß dritten Grades; feucht dritten Grades
okaiin chaiin    im o-Rahmen heiß dritten Grades; trocken dritten Grades
```

## The quality paradigms are complete

The inherited quality atoms themselves carry GDT626's four values:

| axis head | I | II | III | IV | working reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `k` | `kan` 2 | `kain` 43 | `kaiin` 74 | `kaiiin` 4 | hot, degree I–IV |
| `t` | `tan` 1 | `tain` 9 | `taiin` 45 | `taiiin` 1 | cold, degree I–IV |
| `ch` | `chan` 12 | `chain` 19 | `chaiin` 48 | `chaiiin` 1 | dry, degree I–IV |
| `sh` | `shan` 6 | `shain` 9 | `shaiin` 19 | `shaiiin` 1 | moist, degree I–IV |

Every one of these sixteen cells is occupied. With the productive `o/qo`
scopes included, 31 of 32 registered cells occur; only `otaiiin` is absent.
The atlas contains 1,561 occurrences on 143 pages, 1,399 stable in all three
alternate readings.

This matters because the degree is not being inferred from one compound. The
same suffix predicts every member of four independently inherited quality
axes. A pure grammatical-grade rival can still explain the form, but it now
has to explain why the entire paradigm lands precisely on the working
hot/cold/dry/moist layer.

## Separate quality words agree locally

Thirty-three lines place a thermal and moisture degree within three token
positions; thirty pairs are stable across all readings. Twenty use the same
degree, including nine of thirteen directly adjacent pairs. Representative
witnesses are:

| locus | surface | reading |
|---|---|---|
| f111v.33 | `qokan chan` | hot degree I; dry degree I |
| f107v.7 | `chain qokain` | dry degree II; hot degree II |
| f106r.20 | `qokaiin shaiin` | hot degree III; moist degree III |
| f112v.39 | `okaiin chaiin` | o-scope hot degree III; dry degree III |
| f4r.3 | `chaiin qotaiin` | dry degree III; cold degree III |
| f55v.5 | `qokaiin chaiin` | hot degree III; dry degree III |

The syntax has a close period analogue. Early-fifteenth-century materia medica
describes a drug as paired hot/dry qualities in a shared second or third degree.
Pal.lat.1234 also arranges substance names under hot-degree rubrics I–IV. These
comparators establish that the proposed semantic construction is historically
ordinary; they do not identify Voynich spellings by themselves.

## What `a` contributes

The surface split is not `daiin = one learned whole word`. Across 545 heads the
same bounded tail follows an `a` edge:

```text
-an     I
-ain    II
-aiin   III
-aiiin  IV
```

GDT627 therefore assigns `a` the practical role **open a value slot after this
head**. In a fluent translation it is normally silent or rendered as a colon.
This is more economical than giving `a` a substance meaning, and it predicts
the quality and measure series without one gloss per whole word. It is a
functional segmentation, not a phonetic claim.

## Why `d` is now a measure head

The free series is large and complete:

| form | value | occurrences |
|---|---:|---:|
| `dan` | I | 17 |
| `dain` | II | 193 |
| `daiin` | III | 721 |
| `daiiin` | IV | 17 |

It occurs 948 times on 176 pages. More importantly, exact-frame comparison
finds only seven heads that vary while both immediate neighbours remain fixed.
`d` supplies 27 of the 72 frames and is the **only** head with all four values
in one identical frame:

```text
chol __ <line end>
```

The thirteen witnesses contain `dan`, `dain`, `daiin`, and `daiiin`; examples
include:

```text
f2r.7    shor ckhy daiiny chol dan
f47v.9   qotcheey chety chol chol dain
f37v.23  yto chol daiin
f17r.11  otchol cthar okaiin chol daiiin
```

That is the distribution expected of a reusable value carrier, not an
invariant word such as “and,” “item,” “take,” or “perform.” Real recipe lists
also place an ingredient before a measure sign and Roman quantity. This makes
`d = measure/unit carrier` the most useful concrete default, though no evidence
yet licenses a specific name such as drachm or scruple.

## Attachment in plant-part lists

There are 46 immediate contacts between `d` values and inherited part terms:
23 part→`d`, 21 `d`→part, and two part→`d`→part. Forty-one use value III and
five value II. Ten wider contexts bracket `d` between nearby part terms.

The strongest local example is f45v.2:

```text
okol | chor | daiin | cthy | okchol ...
       part    d+III   part

Blüten-/Pflanzenteil: drei Portionen/Maße; Blattgut ...
```

The measure binds left by default and the next part starts a new entry. The
drawing has three flowering shoots, so “three pictured shoots” remains an
interesting local alternative. It does not generalize. On f15r the identical
`cthy` is followed once by `daiin` and once by `dain`; f15v presents the same
III contact beside four conspicuous leaves; f2v carries D-IV and D-III in one
line. The values therefore behave more like local quantities or grades than a
page-wide count of pictured organs.

`cth/chor + a + value` remains less resolved than free `d`:

```text
cthaiin   Blatt-/oberirdisches Drogengut, Menge/Grad/Klasse III
chorain   Blüten-/Pflanzenteil, Menge/Grad/Klasse II
choraiin  Blüten-/Pflanzenteil, Menge/Grad/Klasse III
```

Those compounds may encode amount, pharmacological strength, size, maturity,
or another material class. They are not silently converted into literal organ
counts.

## Concrete translation gain

The gain over the rejected generic prose is categorical. We no longer render
a line as “take material, do work, pass onward.” We can now recover bounded
claims with explicit values:

```text
qokaiin shaiin
hot, degree III; moist, degree III

chor daiin cthy
reproductive plant part: three measures; leaf material
```

The unknowns are visible rather than hidden: the exact plant term behind
`chor`, the unit selected by `d`, and the wider syntax around these clauses.
The next useful target is consequently `chol`, because it is the fixed
predecessor of every value in the only identical I–IV `d` frame. Resolving that
head can turn the current local amount phrase into a fuller ingredient or
record clause without reopening the already established value architecture.
