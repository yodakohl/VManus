# GDT821 — physician's content reading of fixed physical ascent

Working reader: a physician teaching natural philosophy around 1420, keeping
water, air and aqueous vapour distinct and separating material change from
movement. This perspective offers possible arguments, not an attribution of
the manuscript to that profession or language.

The inherited proposal is exact whole `raiin = steigt?`, physical upward motion.
It is not increasing amount, rising temperature, evaporating, being, falling or
causatively lifting another object. An attractive German clause cannot change
that sense in a less accommodating occurrence. Whole source groups govern:
RF `@206;aiin` receives no automatic raiin gloss.

## Decision and useful next content question

Keep the physical-ascent reading as the same C0 trial, without promoting it
because it was not immediately refuted. No admitted exact occurrence forced a
known material to perform demonstrably impossible motion. More importantly,
the new paragraphs did not produce an independently identified upward-moving
subject or a read causal/conditional connection. The additional f77r air contact
is the strongest reason to keep exploring it; it is not enough to prefer it as
the dictionary meaning across the seven selectors.

The most useful specific follow-on content question is the shared written
`sal.raiin` at f76r.51 and f82r.24 alongside f82r's second
`qokeey.lkeey.qokeey.raiin`. A fixed motion account must explain what those
subject/attachment candidates contribute; a rival must handle the same complete
groups, not win by deleting them or inheriting old dose prose. This note does
not propose a new sal, qokeey, lkeey or raiin meaning.

The GDT820 medieval examples establish only that noun reprise and upward air
or vapour movement can occur in historical argument. They are not counted again
as a new finding or lexical support in GDT821. No additional historical search
was needed to adjudicate these still-unmapped subjects; no new primary-source
quotation, dictionary adoption or confirmed clause is reported.
