The useful rival is physical lightness, not a synonym of upward motion. It can
describe a substance even when no movement occurs. Conversely, an upward
motion statement needs a moving thing but does not assert that it is light.
This contrast survives the historical natural-philosophy comparison; no modern
physical theory, air/water comparison or obstacle is inserted into Voynich.

Physical lightness has a local economy advantage under a nominal sal reading:
`sal raiin` could be a qualified thing without a separate motion event. That
advantage is conditional on an unknown word and unknown grammar, not evidence
that sal means drug, salt, wood or anything else. It also gives up the finite
predicate whose absence originally motivated rises. Do not silently install
checkhy=is or collapse its spelling variants to cover that cost.

Hence no global replacement winner. Keep rises and light as separate C0 models,
not a combined "light/rising" gloss. The wider context did not make rises
impossible, but it did not turn the original attractive air sentence into a
transferable translation. Unknown contexts cannot count as positive successes.
The next useful content step must resolve a concrete carrier or attachment;
another inventory of the same raiin occurrences would add nothing.
