# Historical analogues for the GDT664 working register

These comparisons calibrate the *type* of working dictionary. They do not
identify a Voynich glyph, language, ingredient, or plaintext.

- Wellcome MS 683 is a mid-fifteenth-century north-east Italian collection of
  Latin medical recipes. Its catalogue records oils, ointments, powders,
  pills, pessaries, soaking in strong vinegar, and a recipe ending with equal
  quantities measured in two grains. This makes a mixed register of ingredient
  words, process words, preparations, and compact measures historically
  ordinary near the manuscript's date.
  <https://wellcomecollection.org/works/w6ne7k4t>
- Wellcome MS 5262 is an English medical recipe collection from the first
  quarter of the fifteenth century, with 129 recipes in its contemporary
  contents list. It is a useful period control for terse repeated recipe
  language, not a proposed donor text.
  <https://wellcomecollection.org/works/hkxxeu85>
- A published table of manuscript medical weights and measures renders Latin
  *manipulus* as “handful” and lists `man`, `manp`, `manip`, and rarely `m` as
  abbreviations. This makes the GDT664 card `m=eine Handvoll` a plausible
  siglum type. It does not show that EVA `m` is the same historical glyph.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK608570/?report=printable>

The three GDT664 forms ending in `-g` are therefore allowed one deliberately
low-confidence shared “grain” reading. The period MS 683 catalogue contains
*grani ii*, but it does not establish the abbreviation or its identity with
EVA `g`. GDT664 exports this reading only to the exact whole cards `chkag`,
`kcharg`, and `chokolg`; it does not rewrite unrelated `-sg`, `-lg`, or `-odg`
families.
