## A human-readable source rule

The proposed source domain is a plan divided into regions. A finite active-leaf
frontier records the current nonoverlapping parts. One word updates one addressed
region: split an active part into its immediate children, or merge exactly its
active immediate children back into that part. Every requested update is retained.
The current state chooses split versus merge, so identical words need not express
the same verb. This content construction is coherent on its own, as RAW1072shows.

The address property being tested is an explicit hypothesis: when one complete
word extends another as a literal prefix, it names a descendant of the SAMEroot;
each added working unit contributes at least one further tree edge. No final mark
or unused sign is exempt. We do not infer that property or actual address values
from the manuscript. No unrecorded update/reset may intervene between the words.

## The obstruction, independent of the starting plan

After splitting U, only U's immediate children are active below U. A descendant
V two or more levels deeper is not active, and neither are V's own immediate
children. After merging U, U itself is active and no proper descendant is active.
Thus V cannot be split or merged immediately after U in EITHERcase. Other roots
and unrelated parts do not change the proof. A node with no children permits no
vacuous merge. The argument works for any nonempty branching structure, not just
the binary finite fixtures; the tested family specifies branching at least2.

The longer word `ordy` is `or` plus the two working units d,y. Under the candidate
address law it must therefore be at least two edges deeper. The recorded adjacency
`or ordy` requires exactly the impossible two-update sequence.
