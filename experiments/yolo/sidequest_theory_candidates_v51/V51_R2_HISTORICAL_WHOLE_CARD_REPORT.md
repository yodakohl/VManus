# V51 R2 Historical Whole-Card Pressure Report

## Status and scope

This is a deliberately non-canonical pressure test, not a decipherment claim. I treated each of the nine selected tuples as one opaque **whole card**. In particular, `OKY` was not read as `OK+Y`, `OKE` not as `OK+E`, and none of `LCHE`, `CTHY`, `OKEEY`, `CKHY`, or `OLOR` was visibly decomposed. The different diplomatic surfaces attached to one joint tuple were not used as sounds or etymologies.

The audit covers all 70 V49 events belonging to the nine exact `joint_tuple_id` values (66 distinct loci). It uses only the fixed V49 card dictionary and event interlinear. An occurrence was counted by exact tuple ID, never by substring. No `f84`/`f84r`, unlisted page, or sibling V51 artifact was opened.

The inherited V49 German defaults are hypotheses under test, not evidence. Likewise, the surrounding V49 interlinear is useful only for testing internal consistency: it cannot independently ground a card because it was generated inside the same speculative system.

## Historical scale controls

Late-medieval abbreviation practice permits a whole sign to stand for a compact operation, cross-reference, or measure, but does not license an arbitrary compressed sentence. The Ad fontes palaeography tutorial stresses both writer-specific abbreviation systems and conventional signs for measures and weights. That makes a whole-card `MASS` more historically ordinary than a card silently containing “take a measured quantity of the preceding liquid.” [Ad fontes, “Abbreviations”](https://www.adfontes.uzh.ch/en/tutorium/schriften-lesen/abkuerzungen)

The historical word checks below establish only the **size and genre plausibility of the German mnemonic**. FWB-online normalizes lemma spellings and covers the 14th through 17th centuries; its entries cannot identify a Voynich form. [FWB usage notes](https://www.fwb-online.de/content/hinweise-zur-benutzung-des-woerterbuchs)

- The FWB `mes` entry includes a small hollow measure used for substances such as medicines, honey, oil, or wine; this supports a one-unit measure mnemonic, not the identification of `AIIN`. [FWB, “mes”](https://www.fwb-online.de/lemma/mes.s.0mn)
- `nutzen` has a specifically medical/pharmaceutical sense of applying or taking something as a remedy, including use in a plaster. The attested examples displayed there are later than 1420, so they support genre continuity rather than an exact date. [FWB, “nutzen”](https://www.fwb-online.de/lemma/nutzen.s.3v)
- `ablassen` includes letting liquid out, tapping/decanting, transferring liquid, and emptying a vessel. It is a better atomic operation than the event noun `ABLASS`. [FWB, “ablassen”](https://www.fwb-online.de/lemma/ablassen.s.3v)
- `lau` continues Middle High German usage and means moderately or lukewarm; the entry includes a 1436 vocabulary glossing the temperature range. `LAU` is therefore smaller and historically sharper than `LAUWARM`. [FWB, “lau”](https://www.fwb-online.de/lemma/lau.s.4adj)
- The `spül-` family denotes washing/rinsing, although the readily displayed FWB examples for `ausspülen` are substantially later. It supports the operation class but leaves the 1420 dating and the exact card assignment weak. [FWB, “ausspülen”](https://www.fwb-online.de/lemma/aussp%C3%BClen.s.3v)

A close but later German recipe comparator (ca. 1463) writes operations and their objects explicitly and uses compact sequencing expressions such as `dar nach`, `Item`, and `vncz`/`byss`. That is a useful brake on overloading any whole card with an invisible object or complete clause. [Herzog August Bibliothek, recipe transcription](https://diglib.hab.de/edoc/ed000270/texts/tei-transcription.html)

## All-occurrence pressure by card

### `AIIN` → MASS

All 20 events were checked. Its seven-page spread and repeated proximity to allotting, taking, dipping, and measured-batch contexts are compatible with a generic measure or dose mark. A measure/weight siglum is also an economical scribal unit.

The strongest contradiction is at f10r.8, where the bare card lies between continuation of a previous preparation and the next “from it” step; several other occurrences stand at record ends. Neither an amount nor a measured substance is independently explicit. `MENGE` fits the quantificational contexts but is less siglum-like; `GABE` fits dosing but over-specializes occurrences outside administration. I retain **MASS**, with no implied number, unit, or object.

### `EY` → UNKNOWN

The four events do not share a stable completion position. At f82r.23 the card opens a record before a same-setting continuation, while at f83r.39 it lies between one measured share and another. At f11r.1 it follows a second sieving but precedes an open cooling step.

Those are direct all-occurrence contradictions to `FERTIG`. `KLAR` is possible after processing, and `FERTIG` remains the inherited rival, but neither survives all four positions. Decision: **UNKNOWN**.

### `OKY` → NUTZEN

Across ten events, the card most often occupies an application/use transition, and `NUTZEN` has the right one-word medical-recipe scale. Its strongest contradiction is f83r.8, where application at a marked site has already occurred and further stirring follows; f10r.2 likewise places the card after drinking and before another measure. Thus it cannot uniformly mean a punctual “apply now.”

The broader operator **NUTZEN** survives better: it can mark entry into or continuation of use without specifying route or object. `BRAUCHEN` is historically broad but semantically looser; `ANWENDEN` is more explicit than the evidence warrants. Retention is tentative.

### `LCHE` → ABLASSEN

All eight events carry the formal `CLOSE` wrapper. Most follow a wash, bath, or run and precede a lower basin or new batch, which makes liquid release/transfer a coherent recipe operation. The infinitive **ABLASSEN** is a cleaner atomic mnemonic than the inherited noun `ABLASS`.

The strongest contradiction is f83r.15: the card follows equal-part mixing at record end, where the context does not independently separate drainage from generic closure. Indeed, because every occurrence is wrapped by `CLOSE`, the evidence cannot yet distinguish the lexical operation from the formal wrapper. `ABGIESSEN` would require pouring off; `AUSLASSEN` is broader and less specifically vessel-oriented. Decision: **ABLASSEN**, low-medium confidence.

### `OKE` → SPÜLEN

All eight events also carry `CLOSE`. Repeated wash/rinse environments, including two consecutive occurrences at f81v.18 and a f83r.20 context explicitly glossed as following a first rinse, make **SPÜLEN** the smallest useful mnemonic.

The strongest contradiction is f81v.2, where the card begins a record without an explicit object; f83r.28 follows an opening rather than an independently marked wash. As with `LCHE`, no open contrast proves that the whole card contributes more than a terminal class. `WASCHEN` is the stronger concrete rival; `REINIGEN` is broader and more resultative. Retention remains provisional.

### `CTHY` → UNKNOWN

Seven occurrences refute a uniform ready-state reading. At f10r.6 the card follows a habitat statement but precedes preparation of the working liquid. At f83r.14 it appears immediately after beginning the next measured batch, and at f83r.24 further processing follows a “clear” stage.

`BEREIT` therefore predicts completion where several records are still starting or continuing. `REIF` would be a historically plausible process-state rival but is even more substance-specific. Neither covers all events. Decision: **UNKNOWN**.

### `OKEEY` → LAU

Seven occurrences often cluster with warming, tempering, bathing, rinsing, or immersion. A whole temperature-state sign is historically compact, and **LAU** is the smallest normalized German mnemonic with direct late-medieval support.

The strongest contradiction is f83r.20, where the card follows binding at the site without an explicit heat or liquid cue; f82r.3 also begins a record before straining. `WARM` fits the broad temperature class but loses the moderate-temperature distinction; `MÄSSIG` is too general. Replacing `LAUWARM` with **LAU** reduces semantic load but does not remove the contextual exception.

### `CKHY` → UNKNOWN

Only four events occur, confined to f81v and f82r. The strongest contradiction to `VERBINDUNG` is f82r.3, where the card lies between passing material through a cloth and starting the next batch; no pair of things being joined is overt. The remaining events do not supply an invariant linked pair either.

`VERBINDUNG` and `DURCHGANG` are both recipe-sized one-word rivals, but the former silently supplies two relata and the latter overfits the filtering occurrence. Decision: **UNKNOWN**.

### `OLOR` → ZUVOR

The two cross-register events both sit near continuation of a previous preparation: f10r.8 lies between two such continuation statements, and f81v.7 precedes one. A compact anterior-reference operator is historically plausible, but an inflected **VORIGES** silently nominalizes an unknown thing. I therefore select the simpler adverb **ZUVOR**.

The strongest contradiction is f81v.7: at record start there is no within-record antecedent, so the reading requires cross-record scope. `VORIGES` and `GLEICHES` are plausible cross-reference rivals but both conceal a contextual head noun. With only two events, **ZUVOR** remains very low-confidence.
