# Post-publication native-scale near miss: Egerton f77v–f78r

The first GDT1093 report omitted the two Plantago pages from its native-scale
shortlist. That made its statement about all *plausible* near misses too strong.
This correction was written **after** GDT1093 was committed and published;
the preregistered rule, original `RESULT.json`, and negative decision are
preserved. No new Voynich folio or text was opened. The already admitted Yale
f25v image was rechecked at its fixed byte hash.

This is a **real iconographic near miss**: the Egerton Plantago fan and fibrous
root architecture are closer to f25v than the report's Rabarbarum entry. It is
not a strict three-feature match. The historical Plantago leaves make an upper
semicircular fan rather than f25v's more complete star; f77v lacks an animal;
f78r has different animals in a separate margin, not one touching quadruped;
the root placement and color differ. Treating the f78r snake or scorpion as
f25v's little quadruped would be a post-hoc substitution.

The f25v–Plantago comparison was already proposed in historical Voynich
identification lists, so this observation is **not an independent new naming
prediction**. It supplies a more concrete primary-source image and written
historical context, but neither a species identification nor a translated
Voynich word. The next useful question is whether a second, independently
written Plantago witness preserves the *same distinctive fan and animal
relation*; another generic rosette alone would not promote this lead.
