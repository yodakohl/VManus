## The hand rule and why no plaintext fit is needed

The source alphabet consists of the21working-unit labels other than ckh.
Scan a source word left-to-right, replacing the chosen adjacent pair by one
ckh, and otherwise copy the literal primitive. Preserve spaces. Decode by
expanding ckh to that pair. The disjoint scan has an exact inverse for all
finite source words. No primitive receives a sound or English meaning.

However, canonical outputs cannot contain the compulsory pair as two literal
units. The two decisive roundtrips are:

- forward: `[ch,k,y] → decode → [ch,k,y] → encode → [ckh,y]`;
- reverse: `[k,ch,y] → decode → [k,ch,y] → encode → [ckh,y]`.

Thus a key search or large source-statistics run cannot repair the stipulated
literal/shortcut binding. Analogous compulsory rules involving t,p,f cannot
change these three-unit witnesses, which contain none of those primitives.
No new glyph, escape, hidden morpheme boundary or exception is inserted.
