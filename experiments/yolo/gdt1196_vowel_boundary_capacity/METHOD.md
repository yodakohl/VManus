# Coarsening and alias bounds

If the printed form is a deterministic function of a finite group label, its frequency vector is a coarsening of the label-frequency vector. Coarsening cannot increase its number of positive cells and cannot decrease the sum of its ten largest cells. The code may depend on the already decoded previous fragment through the declared state; that does not alter the argument. If an encoder additionally branches on position, a growing dictionary or another state, it is outside this finite contract. No inference that the bound covers all languages or abbreviation follows.

With at most A aliases per base label of frequency n, there are at most min(n,A)positive output cells. Among integer partitions into at most A positive cells, the balanced partition (quotient/remainder by A with zero cells omitted) is majorized by every other partition. Pooling independent balanced partitions minimizes all top-k sums; merging aliases from different labels cannot improve diversity or reduce top-k concentration. This is an optimistic capacity bound. Implementing its individual partitions could require a forbidden message-specific assignment and is NOT a proposed writer.

The source projection and 8,000-group sampling are inherited. Full recipe reconstruction checks semantic-text preservation of the grouping only; no glyph rendering exists. In the independent validator maximal vowel runs are found by explicit character-state transitions, rather than the runner's regular expression. A tiny exhaustive partition check validates the balanced-allocation claim separately.
