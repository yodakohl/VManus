# Sidequest pass 666: SOLK collect grid

This fast workshop pass gives **SOLK** one short reusable value:
**AUFFANGEN**. It covers five exact cards and seven occurrences on the fixed
pages. The root does not itself name a basin, pipe, filter, or body part; those
belong to the pictured/local work situation.

## The small paradigm

| Card | Composition | Workshop reading |
|---|---|---|
| `qolky` | SOLK+Y | catch/collect the current item and leave it active |
| `solkey` | SOLK+E+Y | catch briefly and leave active |
| `solkeey` | SOLK+EE+Y | catch for longer and leave active |
| `olkeedy` / `solkeedy` | SOLK+EE+DY | catch for longer and close the step |
| `solkaiin` | SOLK+AIIN | catch to the prescribed measure |

The three observed closed occurrences are all statement-final. The active and
closed endpoints therefore behave like the already useful OK grid: `Y` keeps
the working item available, while the licensed `DY` construction closes the
step.

## Missing but now predictable cells

The compact teaching table has eight duration-by-endpoint cells. Four are
present and four are absent on these pages:

- `SOLK+DY`: collect without a stated duration and close;
- `SOLK+E+DY`: collect briefly and close;
- `SOLK+EEE+Y`: collect fully and keep active;
- `SOLK+EEE+DY`: collect fully and close.

A fifth useful prediction is `SOLK+AIN`, “collect one portion”. These are
workshop predictions, not claims that the guessed EVA spellings must occur.

## Result

SOLK is no longer a sentence-sized gloss such as “hold at a local collection
station”. The shortest common contribution is the verb **AUFFANGEN**. Grade,
quantity, active-item, and closure pieces supply the rest. With SOLK included,
the current compositional dictionary covers 80 of 173 exact cards and 196 of
381 prose events.
