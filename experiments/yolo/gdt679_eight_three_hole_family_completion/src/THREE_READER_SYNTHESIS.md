# GDT679 three-reader synthesis

Three readers worked independently on the same 24 surfaces, 57 exact ZL3b
positions and eight V52 source lines. None edited repository files.

## Fixed reader backgrounds

### Workshop master

The workshop master treats every entry as something a late-medieval pharmacy
could select, measure, heat, cool, soak, dry or finish. Productive blocks and
memorized wholes may coexist. A card must keep one practical core at every
exact position, although a result noun and the corresponding instruction may
alternate. Generic work-item language is forbidden.

### Travelling practitioner

The practitioner asks whether a person could use the line to choose material
and perform a preparation. Concrete ingredients, amounts, fractions, states
and actions outrank abstract structural labels. Elliptical registers and
result nouns are allowed. A creative default remains until a context makes it
impossible or a more useful reading replaces it.

### Scribe and glossator around 1420

The scribe expects recipe commands, drug-name wholes, degree ladders, measures,
abbreviations and unstable word boundaries in one manuscript. Historical
sources constrain the information architecture, not Voynich phonetics. Exact
IT2a/RF1b joins and splits may repair a ZL3b boundary, but may not license an
arbitrary segmentation. A concrete reading is preferred to empty method prose.

## Shared findings

All three independently recovered the same census: 24 surfaces, 57 exact
positions, 41 loci and 35 already admitted pages. The guarded reader query
selected 41 rows, rejected 98 `f84*` rows before materialization and skipped
5,247 non-allowed rows. Reader support is 44 both-exact, four IT2a-only exact,
two RF1b-only exact and seven neither-exact.

The readers agreed without substantive qualification on these high-value
cards:

- `ckhol = ckh+ol`: Drogenstoff aus Arzneikompositum, 17 positions and 16
  both-reader exact positions;
- `orchey`: one portion of drug dried to the middle stage, 3/3 both-exact;
- `olkaiir`: third heated wood-drug fraction in the preparation;
- `ltain`: wood drug cooled to grade II;
- `osheey / orsheey`: fully soaked preparation / one portion of fully soaked
  drug;
- `cholkain`: dry material heated to grade II;
- `cthororaiin`: three herb-drug portions, with both readers explicitly
  splitting `cthor oraiin`;
- `kc → kchs` and `daiiiry → daiiirchy`: two bilateral reader repairs, yielding
  a hot-dry drug charge and a measured fourth dry fraction.

They also agreed that every selected source line can receive a complete,
concrete reading and that no exact context forces a wholesale change from
material to unrelated process or vice versa.

## Decisions where readers differed

### `qeeey`

The workshop master and practitioner preferred a final-portion command by
analogy with `qeey`. The scribe noticed a stronger distributional fact: all
three occurrences follow immediately after a moist, cold or dry process card.
The synthesis therefore uses “carry the preceding treatment to the last stage
and close it.” The final-portion reading remains the named rival.

### `tolg`

All readers rank this last. The master and practitioner prefer ZL3b
`tol+g = one grain of cold drug material`; the scribe prefers IT2a `tolm = one
handful` because the frozen stem model restricts productive `g`. The synthesis
keeps “one grain” because ZL3b has terminal `g`, RF1b also ends its joined form
in `g`, and the exact learned `chokolg` already denotes a grain of hot dry
decoction. `tolm` remains an equally visible unit rival; no general `g` rule is
exported.

### `saraiin`

The default is three parts of the first seed fraction from `sar+aiin`. Two
contexts preserve this directly. At f87r.9, IT2a `soraiin` still preserves seed
material and a three-unit amount, while RF1b `raiin` supplies the explicit root
rival. The card remains medium and replaceable, not flattened to “material.”

### Action versus result

`kchod`, `keeod`, `ochdy`, `shotchey`, `oteed` and `cholkain` are stored as
concrete results because their 57-position context circuit is mostly nominal.
Their corresponding operations remain explicit rivals and may be used in
natural prose where the line already supplies an action head. This avoids
inventing imperatives merely to make a register resemble a modern recipe.

## Historical architecture retained

The source atlas records Royal MS 12 G IV, Harley MSS 2378, 2374 and 2558,
Wellcome MSS 683 and 541, and the Nottingham palaeography guide. Together they
show that recipes, simples glossaries, degree texts, substitutions, Roman or
grain quantities and compressed reader-dependent spellings coexisted in
fourteenth- and fifteenth-century practical books. They support the mixed
card architecture only. They do not identify a Voynich sign, sound, language,
ingredient or codebook.

## Synthesis outcome

The executable basis is the 24-card table, thirteen explicit non-both-reader
decisions and eight natural source-line readings. It assigns 57 exact positions
without a new page, closes eight V52 lines, and leaves `tolg`, `saraiin` and
single-context `qey` as the three weakest replaceable cards.
