# GDT796 — Outer-ring mirror and status/facies bridge

Status: **PARTIAL — f71 has a narrow relative-reflection texture; neither a general
visible-status code nor a fortunate-facies reading transfers**.

## Outcome

GDT796 asks what the smaller local graphical field selected by GDT795 could
actually mean. It tests three deliberately different bridges over the same 101
already admitted Kluge-A labels:

1. a relative reflection in the f71 outer ten;
2. complete label families naming visible barrel, clothing or facing states;
3. complete label families naming a historical planetary facies status.

The first reproduces an uneven formal clue. The second produces three
concrete rivals below the selection gate but no portable dictionary. The third rejects the precise
`AQABAC = favorable/benefic facies` reading that GDT795 left open. The selected
working architecture therefore remains:

```text
learned individual entry/designation
+ locally copied graphical or status field whose concrete value is still open
```

This is not a return to generic process prose. The surviving candidate rows below
have concrete readings and explicit counterexamples, while the large opaque
remainder stays opaque.

## 1. The f71 outer ten show relative reflection

The f70v1, f71v and f72r1 outer members A06–A15 were compared under all 400
pairs of rotations/reflections, with f70 fixed. Inner-five members were not
used. For the source-native boundary-family strings the best joint arrangement
is:

- f71: reflection `F9`;
- f72: unchanged `R0`;
- raw optimized-null `p=.003905`;
- fixed-missing-mask normalized-null `p=.034415`.

These are descriptive calibrations, not a fresh confirmatory discovery: F9 was
already the exploratory rival inherited from GDT795 and is being retested on
the same 101 labels. ZL3b/IT2a/RF1b and the family views are nested readings of
one dataset, not independent replications. The permutation also preserves the
missing mask in its stricter version but not GDT795's known local serial
structure, so a later run-preserving null remains necessary.

The same F9/R0 optimum appears in all three alternate
member-sequence readings, with fixed-mask `p` values from .0242 to .0339. It
does **not** appear as exact member identity, a transferred-prefix match or a
residual match. This makes a relative f71 reflection a worthwhile layout-order
rival, not a recovered ten-member code. The statistic does not identify the
cause or an absolute direction: f71 may reverse a copying order or catalogue
start, but f70/f72 may instead share the opposite convention.

Its weakness is internal stability. When one parity of positions selects the
transform and the other tests it, boundary-family F9 ranks 122/400 in one
direction and 7/400 in the other. The normalized joint ranks fall to 203/400
and 264/400. Raw f71 reflection remains comparatively stable while the paired
f72 transform changes; normalization removes even that apparent stability.
An independent arithmetic decomposition confirms that the fixed-mask effect is
mainly f70–f71 (`p=.0305`); f71–f72 is suggestive (`p=.0781`) and f70–f72 is
null (`p=.8213`). Seven position deltas favor F9 over identity, one reverses and
two tie, but F9 creates no exact f71 family match. Thus the effect is
pair-driven rather than a common three-ring code. GDT796 retains
`F71_OUTER10_F9_MIRROR` at C0 and forbids using it as a global decoder key.

## 2. Visible attributes give only bounded cards

The guarded GDT360 acquisition selected 554 allowed annotation rows and
materialized no sealed row. After removing channels with no usable variation,
174 unique locus-state records remain:

| channel | usable states | events | decisive limitation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| barrel | present/absent | 77 | all 10 page×ring blocks are state-pure |
| clothing | dressed/undressed/uncertain | 30 | genuine variation, poor held-folio transfer |
| facing | profile/non-directional | 67 | all 7 blocks are state-pure |

Local prediction is consequently misleading: barrel and facing can be guessed
perfectly simply from the page×ring block. Across physical folios, no complete
family, compact family, prefix or residual supplies a general status code
beyond its block-aware control. Clothing is the least confounded channel but
also the least coherent.

Three complete-family readings remain useful as explicit concrete rivals until
an independent occurrence contradicts them:

| complete family | context-bound reading | support | confidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `AQABAG` | figure shown with barrel/container | 3/3 events, 2 folios | below gate, block-confounded |
| `AQKA\|ACA` | undressed figure | 2/2 events | below gate, one physical folio |
| `AQACAB` | profile figure | 2/2 events | below gate, one hedged visual block |

These are not thrown away merely because they are unproved. They remain the
best concrete guesses presently available for those complete families in
those contexts. None passes the preregistered cross-folio-plus-null gate, so
there are **zero selected visual status cards**. They cannot yet be substituted
on another page, in running text or into a component such as `AG`, because recurrent families also
supply direct counterexamples: `AQABAB` and `AQABAC` cross barrel states;
`AQABA` crosses clothing states within one ring; `AQABAB`, `AQABAC` and
`AQABAG` cross facing states.

## 3. Historical facies bridge

Two genuine historical candidate systems were encoded as complete 12-sign by
three-part matrices rather than as loose word associations:

- the Indian `adorugen`/facies rulers listed in the Latin *Picatrix*;
- the conventional seven-planet sequence represented in the medieval
  Alkabitius tradition and used here as the Chaldean control.

Each sign was divided into three ten-degree fields. Pisces, Taurus and Gemini
were mapped only through already admitted page/sign associations. Taurus has
two unavoidable half-page phases, H0 and H1. For every matrix and phase all 60
global direction×offset transforms were scored; the same transform had to
serve every sign and family.

The historically tempting nominal result is real: under Picatrix H0 with no
offset, the three `AQABAC` events fall under Jupiter, Venus and Venus, hence all
three are benefic. But that arrangement is selected by the target itself.
After withholding `AQABAC`, the best H0 transform is direction +1, offset 9;
AQABAC then becomes Moon/Mercury/Venus, or `OTHER:2|BENEFIC:1`. H1 independently
selects offset 11 and yields Moon/Venus/Saturn, one event in each broad status.
The exact fortunate-facies reading therefore fails.

There is still a small unexplained historical texture. The Picatrix H1
target-masked model reaches family-balanced training purity .8661 against a
block-null mean .7853 (`p=.023976`), with six training families internally
status-consistent. H0 is weaker (`p=.084915`). Only four of nine held families
remain status-consistent under leave-one-family-out in either phase, and the
selected offsets change between H0 and H1. That is not enough for a global
facies decoder, but it justifies retaining a C0 architecture rival for future
cross-sign tests.

The required leave-one-sign-out makes the limitation even clearer. Depending
on the held sign, only 5/5/3 families connect both training signs, falling to
4/4/2 after the AQABAC mask. Only `AQABAB` and target-diagnostic `AQABAC` are
then observable on the held sign; the sole non-target prediction is therefore
AQABAB once per holdout. The complete 24-row audit is retained, but this is far
too little capacity to declare a sign-general code. Across H0 and H1 only
three of the six Picatrix non-target AQABAB rows include the held status among
their training modes; after excluding one tied training case, only two of five
unambiguous predictions are correct.

The precise semantic adjudication is:

- `AQABAC = favorable/benefic facies`: **not selected**;
- `AQABAC = one marked facies/quality class, polarity open`: **retained C0**;
- recurrent families encode one stable benefic/malefic/other code: **not
  selected**;
- Picatrix-like sign-conditioned status texture: **retained C0 as an
  architecture clue only**.

One more concrete rival emerges from the full held-family table. `AQABBA` is
the **only** family that is status-consistent in the intersection of Picatrix
H0 and H1: its two Pisces/Gemini events have benefic rulers in both
target-masked evaluations. It therefore receives the C0 default `Facies mit
benefischem Herrscher`. This is deliberately narrower than “healing time” and
is not installed as plaintext. With only two events and no Taurus occurrence,
its apparent phase robustness is partly automatic, and the Chaldean control
disagrees. It now makes a useful prediction: a genuinely independent third
event or running host should favor a benefic-ruler context.

The Latin *Picatrix* table is a historical candidate generator, not a Voynich
key. Pal. lat. 1382 is especially useful as an architecture comparison because
it places astrological material in the same codex environment as herbs,
medicines, recipes, women's medicine and wine material. This supports a mixed
register model but identifies no label.

## What changes in the working theory

GDT795's “smaller local status/quality field” survives, but it is now narrower:

- it is not one general visible barrel/clothing/facing vocabulary;
- it is not yet one fixed planetary-status vocabulary;
- it may be a copied layout convention whose realization is conditioned by
  sign, page, ring and local template;
- the rest of the label behaves more like a learned entry than a composition
  of freely translatable roots.

The best next bridge is therefore not another global substring dictionary.
It is to take `AQABBA`, the three complete-family visual rivals and recurrent
learned entries into their already admitted running paragraphs, mask every
inherited generic gloss, and ask whether an entire paragraph or record selects
one concrete domain interpretation better than its rivals. The F9 result can
be used as a fixed layout sensitivity, never as a result-chosen semantic key.
