# GDT503 method

## Question

Are the four GDT502 open cells really supported only by their individual
action atoms, or do the older action-chain inventories contain the directed
pairs `CH>CHD` and `CH>OK` inside longer recipes?

## Inputs

- the four open GDT502 cards and all 4,576 exact GDT416 clauses;
- the GDT421 exact-two-head profiles;
- GDT422 repaired long chains and GDT425 direct-component adjacency;
- the complete GDT426 directional-pair table and GDT427 singleton leaveouts;
- the GDT444 focus-separated-pair atlas.

No new page, image or transcription is opened.

## Method

1. Retain the four target cells, current phrases and `COMPOSED_WORKING` labels.
2. Check whether either target occurs as an exact recipe with exactly two
   action heads in GDT421.
3. Independently inspect the broader directional action-chain count in GDT426,
   then join every positive direction to its repaired GDT422 recipe and exact
   GDT416 clause.
4. Distinguish direct component adjacency from mere action-head order. A pair
   is direct only if GDT425 contains the event and ordered component pair;
   otherwise publish all intervening atoms and the GDT422 repair rule.
5. Derive peer pairs with the same typed transition, the reverse-order count,
   and all accepted GDT444 one-focus separator routes. These comparisons add
   context but never promote the target pair.
6. Join the resulting pair-level support back to all four register-specific
   target cards.

## Decision rule and claim ceiling

An old longer chain supports the direction of the two action heads. It does
not demonstrate that the target two-head recipe exists on its own, that it
belongs in either target register, or that a manuscript surface should occur.
`CH>OK` may be called embedded direct adjacency; `CH>CHD` may only be called an
ordered action chain with a visible separator. The four current translations
remain working compositions.
