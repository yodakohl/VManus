# V2 candidate: the evolved codebook

Date: 2026-08-21

Status: **independent speculative reconstruction, not a GDT result and not a
translation**.

Scope: Herbal `f10r`, `f11r`, `f55v`, `f56r`; Biological `f81v`, `f82r`,
`f83r`; circle/astronomical `f67r2`, `f68r1`, `f69v`. No other page is evidence
for this report. In particular, no `f84` or `f84r` image, transcription, formal
row, or metadata was accessed. ZL3b, IT2a, and RF1b are alternative readings of
one object, not replications.

This candidate was developed from the compact current-route and sidequest
snapshots plus the frozen V2 protocol. It did not consult any V1 or V2 candidate
file or the long sidequest archive.

## Claim in one paragraph

The script began as a private stock of aggressively abbreviated **whole
instruction cards** used beside pictures and diagrams. Repeated cards then
invited analogy: scribes learned that some cards could be put into recurring
frames, could accept a small set of entry wrappers, and could be marked as a
completed cell. The result around 1420 was neither a clean cipher alphabet nor
a pure logographic codebook. It was an **evolved codebook with productive
islands**. A senior practitioner could still remember exceptional cards by
sight; a junior could generate routine entries by filling a stencil. Multiple
hands shared card bodies and page recipes, but acquired different wrapper and
joining habits. The system therefore preserves exact whole-card identity,
limited composition, numerous near-families, register-specific decks, and
irregular exceptions at the same time.

The proposed upstream content is practical natural language reduced to a
hybrid of lexical abbreviations and technical notation. The illustrations
supply the subject. What remains on the page is closer to a practitioner's
elliptical prompt—“prepared; with one share; apply at this setting”—than to a
sentence intended to be read aloud.

## Epistemic key

- **FACT** means reported exact or layout evidence in the authorized compact
  snapshot.
- **INFERENCE** means an explanation intended to cover several facts.
- **GUESS** means a provisional functional or source-wording proposal.
- **RISKY PREDICTION** is deliberately allowed to fail.

No proposed English phrase below is a translation. “Card,” “wrapper,” “field,”
and “closure” are formal names. Surface EVA is a convenient transcription, not
phonetic spelling.

## 1. Why a system would evolve this way

### 1.1 Stage zero: knowledge before the script

**INFERENCE.** The workshop begins with ordinary multilingual or dialectal
practice: plant dossiers, preparation and application routines, bathing or
apparatus settings, and calendrical/astral lookup lore. Much of the procedure
is taught orally and by demonstration. A pictured plant, vessel, body or circle
already identifies arguments that ordinary prose would repeat.

The pressure is not secrecy alone. It is fast retrieval, economy on expensive
prepared pages, and the ability to preserve a working routine whose reader is
already initiated. The decisive abbreviation is therefore semantic ellipsis:
omit “take this plant,” “use this bath,” or “for the figure shown” because the
page makes it obvious.

### 1.2 Stage one: personal mnemonic cards

**INFERENCE.** A founding writer gives recurrent source chunks distinctive
compact outlines. A card can stand for a word, a short phrase, a measure, a
relation, or a conventional instruction. Its value is learned by association
with a page type and a routine, not recovered letter by letter.

At this stage the card inventory is untidy. Similar meanings need not have
similar shapes, and similar shapes need not decompose. High-value phrases are
remembered as gestalts. This is the source of the system's “whole-card” pole.

### 1.3 Stage two: entrenchment and family resemblance

The same useful cards recur. Copyists notice common visual bodies and begin to
preserve them more accurately than incidental pen detail. Entry strokes,
joining strokes and line-initial flourishes vary by hand and context. Some
variants become conventional wrappers around a stable body.

**FACT.** On the fixed prose pages the exact AIIN card appears under surface
forms like `AIIN / DAIIN / SAIIN / CHAIIN / TAIIN`; the Y card similarly
appears through several wrappers; Herbal `CHOL` and Biological `OL` can be the
same exact L/O card. Wrapper agreement is not required inside known formulas.

**INFERENCE.** This is a natural result of teaching “copy this central sign”
while allowing the entry gesture to be supplied by line position, preceding
pen motion, register, or house style. The body is mnemonic identity; the
wrapper is production grammar.

### 1.4 Stage three: productive islands

Once a few cards recur often enough, a learner can abstract a frame without
turning the whole inventory into an alphabet. Three islands become productive:

1. **wrapper substitution** around already licensed card bodies;
2. **slot filling** in repeated line/field stencils;
3. **attached completion** for short typed cells.

The distinction is synchronic and diachronic. A senior scribe may know a card
as one sign; a trainee may construct its familiar rendering from a body plus an
entry habit. Both produce the same manuscript ecology.

### 1.5 Stage four: register annexes and specialist hands

Herbal, Biological, and circle work require different local vocabularies.
Rather than redesigning the code, the workshop keeps a small common deck and
adds page-type sheets. Biological B makes especially heavy use of short closed
cells. Herbal A favors longer open runs. The circle pages can use the same
pedagogy while maintaining a local namespace.

**FACT.** Across the fixed prose pages, 17 exact cards cross Herbal/Biological
and account for 44 of 100 covered Herbal events and 92 of 281 Biological
events. This is a meaningful portable core, not a universal dictionary.

**FACT.** `f55v` behaves as a production bridge: half of its exact types also
occur on the fixed Bio-B pages, more than overlap its fixed Herbal-A peers, but
only one of its three exact closers transfers to Bio.

**INFERENCE.** A hand or exemplar shelf traveled more readily than a complete
technical deck. Shared production culture and local subject addenda are enough;
two languages are not required, and one universal vocabulary is not expected.

### 1.6 Stage five: fossilization

The codex freezes several generations of convention at once:

- old unanalyzed whole cards;
- newer wrapper families;
- productive cell closures;
- analogical forms that look regular but remain lexicalized;
- copied errors or uncertain exemplars;
- local cards known only on one page sheet;
- physical reflow around already planned drawings.

This layered history predicts the central awkwardness: the script repeatedly
looks as though one more decomposition will unlock it, yet exact identity and
exceptions continue to win. The irregularity is not noise added to a perfect
grammar. It is the archaeological record of learning.

## 2. What a scribe actually learns

The teaching apparatus can be small and physical:

```text
HOUSE DECK
  common relation, value, state and action cards

HAND RULES
  how this scribe enters a line, joins a card, and leaves/restarts the pen

REGISTER SHEET
  Herbal dossier / Bio cell grid / circle lookup stencil

EXCEPTION STRIP
  rare plant, apparatus, degree, named preparation or unusual value cards

COPY PAGE
  the immediate exemplar, including line fitting around the drawing
```

A novice workflow is executable:

```text
1. Identify the page register and pictured owner.
2. Select a known record stencil.
3. Copy an exact card for each surviving source packet.
4. Use composition only in a licensed high-frequency frame.
5. Attach a close when the stencil requires a resolved cell.
6. At a physical line break, re-enter with the hand's normal wrapper habit.
7. If a packet does not fit the line, carry or repeat it at the next entry.
8. Copy rare material from the page sheet; do not improvise its internals.
```

This procedure explains how a trainee can produce valid-looking material
without possessing a cryptanalyst's key. It also explains why a copyist may
preserve a technical card operationally without being able to expand it into a
spoken clause.

## 3. The synchronic grammar

The mature grammar is deliberately small:

```text
PAGE    := PICTURED_OWNER + REGISTER_TEMPLATE + RECORD+
RECORD  := STENCIL_INSTANCE+
LINE    := ENTRY_RENDER(CARD_OR_FRAME) + INTERIOR* + optional REFLOW
FIELD   := CARD_OR_FRAME+ + optional ATTACHED_CLOSE
FRAME   := licensed sequence of exact card bodies with typed slots
CARD    := exact learned identity, optionally rendered through a licensed wrapper
```

The important asymmetry is:

```text
selection is primarily whole-card;
placement is moderately compositional;
rendering is weakly compositional;
meaning is not freely compositional from visible characters.
```

Consequently `qokaiin` can be copied as one remembered card even if a later
scribe senses an analogy to an AIIN value family. Conversely, `DAIIN` and
`TAIIN` need not have different lexical expansions when exact analysis says
they render one card.

## 4. A provisional codebook that says something

The purpose of this section is to risk useful readings. Confidence is low and
the entries are mutually constrained.

| exact body / construction | evolved-codebook role | nearest source-like wording | confidence |
|---|---|---|---:|
| AIIN | standard quantum/value entry | “one share,” “the stated amount,” or “one degree” | .38 |
| Y | typed argument or side/item tag | “for this item/side” | .34 |
| L/O | accompaniment or inclusion link | “with / in it” | .30 |
| CTHY | prepared/qualified state | “prepared,” “made ready,” or “treated” | .23 |
| attached close | resolved typed cell | “set/done as entered,” usually unspoken | .64 functional, .12 lexical |
| `qokaiin` | current quantity/parameter packet | “the same stated share/setting” | .24 |
| Y–AIIN–Y | symmetric allocation frame | “for each side/item, one equal share/degree” | .27 |

These are not seven independent guesses. They form one risky miniature model:

```text
Y       = argument tag
AIIN    = standardized quantum
Y-AIIN-Y= the same/equal quantum distributed across two tagged arguments
L/O     = introduces accompaniment or medium
CTHY    = says that the addressed material is in a prepared state
CLOSE   = commits the local cell, not the whole utterance
qokaiin = carries the active quantum/setting into another physical line
```

### 4.1 AIIN: not “a number” in the abstract

**FACT.** AIIN occurs 20 times on all seven fixed prose pages: 6 first, 9
middle, 5 last. It is not immediately followed by an attached close in 15
observed opportunities.

**GUESS.** AIIN is the *default quantified share* rather than a numeral with a
fixed modern value. Its upstream expansion may vary by register:

- Herbal: “one portion / the usual quantity”;
- Bio: “one setting / one application unit”;
- circle: if independently found there, “one step / degree.”

The cross-register constant is “instantiate the standard quantum,” not the
unit itself. This accounts for mobility within a field and lack of obligatory
closure. It is concrete enough to lose: if AIIN behaves like a named substance
or process rather than a value-bearing packet, withdraw the reading.

The tempting stronger claim “AIIN = one” is withheld. A standard quantum can
be one, equal, usual, or context-supplied; the present evidence does not select
among them.

### 4.2 Y: a tag, not a noun

**FACT.** Y occurs 18 times on six fixed prose pages, chiefly in the middle;
three of 15 next-event opportunities close.

**GUESS.** Y tells the reader that a neighboring packet applies to an addressed
item, side, outlet, vessel, body zone, or diagram locus. Its best loose
expansion is “for this one/side,” not the name of that object. The picture or
local stencil supplies the referent.

This makes Y portable without requiring “plant,” “water,” and “star” to share
a noun. Its variable wrapper is expected because it is commonly reached from
different pen trajectories.

### 4.3 L/O: the weak link

**FACT.** L/O occurs 19 times but only on three fixed prose pages; it is mostly
middle, and five of 17 next events close. The same exact body can appear as
Herbal `CHOL` and Biological `OL`.

**GUESS.** L/O is an accompaniment/inclusion relation, nearest “with,” “in,” or
“together with.” I choose **WITH/IN-MEDIUM** as the candidate role, not a fixed
preposition. In an elliptical source it can join ingredient to vehicle,
operation to bath, or setting to apparatus.

This is the most language-like surviving proposal, hence especially risky. A
pure class-link interpretation remains live: L/O may mean “member of this
recipe/configuration class” with no spoken equivalent.

### 4.4 CTHY: ready-state packet

**FACT.** CTHY has seven occurrences on three fixed prose pages, six medial and
one final.

**GUESS.** CTHY denotes a material or apparatus that has reached a usable
state: prepared, warmed, mixed, strained, opened, or otherwise qualified. The
common abstraction is **READY/PREPARED**, while the exact spoken participle is
page supplied.

Its medial bias is useful: a state card can qualify an item before a setting or
application rather than terminate a sentence. The guess fails if CTHY instead
clusters at record owners or acts as a portable numeric/value packet.

### 4.5 Closures: commitment marks with lexical ancestors

**FACT.** Biological B has attached closes in 85 of 115 fields, Herbal A in 2
of 16, and Herbal B `f55v` in 3 of 4. The identical Bio stencil on `f81v.17`
and `f82r.7` preserves a `shedy` closer while eight of nine other cards vary.

**INFERENCE.** Attached closes are neither ordinary sentence punctuation nor
fully lexical values. They are **typed commitments**: the field's value has
been supplied and is valid for the local form. Historically, some may descend
from abbreviated words such as “done,” “prepared,” “use,” or a unit name. In
the mature code they behave more like the marked rim of a filled box.

This allows a meaningful distinction:

```text
free DY = ordinary Y card reached through d-wrapper rendering
attached close = constructional resolution licensed by the field type
```

A closed field does not necessarily end a spoken clause, record, or physical
line. It commits the smallest local cell. Different closer cards can retain
value-class information—process complete, material ready, result accepted—so
“closure” does not mean all closers are identical punctuation.

### 4.6 `qokaiin`: carry the active parameter

**FACT.** Within one paragraph on `f82r`, the same exact `qokaiin` card ends
line 3 and begins line 4. It is the only exact boundary repeat among 46
same-record line transitions.

**GUESS.** The whole card means “the current stated share/setting,” perhaps
source-like “of the same amount” or “at the stated measure.” At line 3 it is
announced but cannot be consumed before the margin; line 4 repeats it as the
head of the continued field. The visible AIIN-like tail may have encouraged
scribes to associate it with the value family, but the mature copying unit is
still the entire `qokaiin` card.

Provisional parse:

```text
f82r.3 ... qokaiin  | physical margin
f82r.4 qokaiin ...

... [WITH CURRENT SETTING] |
[CURRENT SETTING, RESUMED] ...
```

This is not a translated RESUME symbol. Ordinary emphatic phrase repetition
is a serious alternative, and one occurrence is painfully little evidence.

### 4.7 Y–AIIN–Y: retain, but narrow

**FACT.** This is the only exact path of length three or four recurring on two
fixed prose pages:

```text
f10r.6  CHY  TAIIN  SHY     Herbal field tail
f83r.3  CHEY DAIIN  CHEY    Bio field head, then LCHE-CLOSE
```

Wrapper mismatch shows that the construction lives at exact-card level, not
as an agreeing surface phrase.

**GUESS.** Retain it as a symmetric allocation construction:

```text
TAGGED ARGUMENT — STANDARD/EQUAL QUANTUM — TAGGED ARGUMENT

nearest source-like expansion:
“for each of the two [context-given items/sides], an equal/usual share.”
```

The historical recipe convention *ana* (“of each/equal quantity”) is a
functional analogy only. No card is assigned the sound or word *ana*. In the
Herbal tail the frame can balance two picture-supplied materials or treatments;
in the Bio head it can set two sides/outlets before a locally closed operation.
This is closer to possible source content than “type–value–type,” but it is
also easier to falsify.

If no independently symmetric two-place context can be found on the two fixed
pages, retreat to “tagged value frame.” If matched fixed-page rechecking shows
that three-card recurrences of comparable frequency arise readily by chance,
withdraw it entirely.

## 5. Parsing the attested page structures

Only parses warranted by the compact evidence are offered. Unseen cards are
left `LOCAL[...]`; they are not silently translated.

### 5.1 Herbal A: open dossier lines

**FACT.** Five fixed Herbal-A lines instantiate a four-card open field (`4O`),
and two instantiate `5O`. Attached close is rare.

Evolved-codebook parse:

```text
PICTURED PLANT = silent record owner

4O := LOCAL[plant/property] — optional READY — optional WITH/IN — LOCAL[value/use]
5O := the same dossier clause with one extra qualification or ingredient
```

The exact slot meanings remain page-local. The important content claim is that
these are **dossier prompts**, not four- or five-word sentences. A plausible
source paraphrase is:

```text
“[This plant:] prepared [in/with X], usual share, [for local use Y].”
```

`f10r.6` ends in Y–AIIN–Y, provisionally:

```text
LOCAL[...] — [FOR EACH OF TWO CONTEXT-GIVEN ITEMS, EQUAL/USUAL SHARE]
```

The open ending is predicted: the dossier line lists a relation/value packet
and need not check off a cell.

### 5.2 Herbal B `f55v`: a production bridge

`f55v` has shortish Herbal content but B-like completion pressure. Three of
four fields close. Its inventory overlaps Bio-B more strongly than the other
fixed Herbal-A pages, yet its closer deck mostly does not transfer.

Parse:

```text
PICTURED SIMPLE = silent owner
FIELD := LOCAL[operation or preparation] — LOCAL[value/state] — CLOSE[class]
```

This page is exactly what an evolved workshop predicts when a B-trained hand
copies a Herbal register sheet. The hand imports the habit “resolve the cell”
while the subject sheet supplies its own closing values. A two-language model
would have to explain why production and topical vocabulary cross-cut so
neatly.

### 5.3 Biological B: checked configuration cells

**FACT.** The fixed Bio pages contain 115 fields at 2.44 cards per field, with
85 attached closes. Recurrent stencils include:

```text
1C | 3C | 1C | 4O    at f81v.17 and f82r.7
2C | 4O              at f82r.3 and f83r.8
```

Parse of a generic closed Bio cell:

```text
LOCAL[target/apparatus] — optional WITH/IN-MEDIUM — optional VALUE/SETTING
— CLOSE[resolved]
```

Parse of the strong nine-card stencil:

```text
[single resolved setting]
| [three-card resolved operation/configuration]
| [single resolved setting]
| [four-card open qualification or continuation]
```

Eight of nine varying cards imply that the stencil, not a memorized sentence,
is repeated. The shared `shedy` closer is the form's stable commitment class.
Nearest source-like wording might be:

```text
“setting: [x], medium/application: [y with z], state: [x], note: [open detail].”
```

That paraphrase is intentionally schematic. It claims a configurable practical
record and rejects a fixed plaintext clause.

On `f83r.3`, Y–AIIN–Y followed by LCHE-CLOSE receives the strongest local
proposal:

```text
[BOTH TAGGED SIDES/ITEMS AT THE SAME STANDARD SETTING]
— [LOCAL OPERATION/RESULT, COMMITTED]
```

### 5.4 Physical lines are not semantic stops

The `qokaiin` carry on `f82r.3–4` and the general line-entry renderer make the
physical margin a re-entry point. A source clause can cross it. Conversely a
single physical line can contain several closed cells. This distinction is
essential to the proposed source wording: line breaks should not be supplied
with modern periods.

## 6. The circle pages: same pedagogy, separate namespace

The circle pages `f67r2`, `f68r1`, and `f69v` have no GDT327 events. No prose
tuple dictionary can therefore be imported into them. This candidate makes no
claim that a surface lookalike there is the exact AIIN, Y, L/O, or CTHY card.

**INFERENCE.** What can transfer is the workshop mechanism:

```text
DIAGRAM = silent owner and coordinate system
SECTOR / RING / LABEL POSITION = typed field address
LOCAL CARD = named state, count, phase, degree class, or lookup instruction
REPETITION = fill analogous diagram cells
```

Astro is thus a **local technical annex**. It can participate in a broad
WHAT/HOW/WHEN miscellany, but the ten pages do not establish a cross-register
pointer. The circle deck may include ordinal/value habits analogous to the
prose deck without sharing exact semantic cards.

Nearest source-content proposal:

```text
Herbal: WHAT material/state is available
Bio:    HOW/WHERE it is configured or applied
Astro:  WHEN/UNDER WHICH cyclic condition a lookup applies
```

The third line is weaker than the first two. A circle may encode calendrical,
astrological, cosmological, mnemonic, or computational material with no direct
treatment scheduling. The model only requires that a workshop accustomed to
picture-addressed cards reuse its notation pedagogy for diagrams.

## 7. Multiple scribes and irregularity

### 7.1 What is shared

- exact central card identities;
- a small common deck;
- a repertoire of stencils;
- the idea of attached commitment;
- expectations about which page registers permit which forms.

### 7.2 What differs

- entry wrapper preference;
- joining versus spacing;
- how a line is restarted;
- which closer exemplar is favored;
- tolerance for analogical invention;
- local register deck and rare copy-sheet forms.

### 7.3 Four mechanisms of irregularity

1. **Lexical entrenchment.** Frequent cards are copied whole even when learners
   can imagine components.
2. **Analogical overreach.** A learner applies a familiar wrapper or close to a
   new card, producing a plausible one-off.
3. **Exemplar contamination.** Two page sheets give slightly different
   versions of the same practical packet.
4. **Reflow repair.** A card is repeated, wrapped differently, compressed, or
   carried when a pre-drawn image leaves insufficient room.

These mechanisms predict structured mess rather than random corruption. Rare
forms should be locally consistent with a page sheet; common bodies should be
more portable; wrappers should be hand/position sensitive; and closures should
be register sensitive.

## 8. Historical plausibility, kept on a short leash

This model uses ordinary late-medieval practices as ingredients, not as proof
of Voynich identity.

- Fifteenth-century practical manuscripts could be heterogeneous household or
  professional miscellanies rather than single-author treatises. A survey of
  88 fifteenth-century vernacular practical manuscripts emphasizes their mixed
  recipes and estate-use knowledge
  ([Journal of British Studies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-british-studies/article/here-is-a-good-boke-to-lerne-practical-books-the-coming-of-the-press-and-the-search-for-knowledge-ca-14001560/8217EBC4F6CE53F1084709587B7C2E12)).
- An early-fifteenth-century Heidelberg student's notebook (Clm 671) combines
  health rules, ritual magic, culinary recipes, and other practical texts,
  illustrating a plausible ecology for a mixed working codex
  ([Fifteenth-Century Studies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/fifteenthcentury-studies/als-ich-dich-vor-gelert-haun-conrad-buitzrusss-recipe-collection-in-manuscript-clm-671-munich/245FEE5D9AE3D18EC3423CF60630AF13)).
- Medieval scribes used brevigraphs for lexical words, while abbreviation
  practice could persist and change over time
  ([English Language & Linguistics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/3296F47F350D4C519DC737A9ED18447D/core-reader)).
- A documented fifteenth-century book project could involve an editor, a
  primary text writer, acquired exemplars, correction, incomplete quires,
  indexes, and coordinated production—enough social machinery for a small
  multi-person code practice
  ([Making Books in Fifteenth-Century Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/making-books-in-fifteenth-century-cambridge/0DC10FDF8170AC41D26008AA85B5B8C5)).
- Quantitative work on fifteenth-century copying warns that scribes could copy
  exemplars closely. Exact recurrence need not imply mechanical encryption
  ([Immaterial Texts in Late Medieval England](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/immaterial-texts-in-late-medieval-england/this-is-the-copy/316C1A3018F584D5D3C78990FAA14805)).

None of these sources documents the Voynich system, its language, its region,
or its purpose. Together they merely remove the need for an anachronistic
computer-like cipher designer. Mixed practical compilation, aggressive
abbreviation, exemplar copying, and coordinated hands were available social
technologies.

## 9. Awkward evidence

### 9.1 Exact identity remains stronger than the story wants

If productive composition were central, one would expect licensed components
to generalize aggressively. The canonical route instead says exact joint
tuples and exact cards remain more trustworthy, while factorization and unseen
edge licensing fail. The candidate survives only by making composition local
and late. A broad morpheme grammar would contradict the evidence.

### 9.2 The semantic dictionary is underdetermined

AIIN as a quantum, Y as a tag, L/O as WITH/IN, and CTHY as READY jointly make a
coherent prompt language—but alternative labelings can fit the same placement.
There is no external referent. The source-like phrases are abductive targets,
not decipherment.

### 9.3 Y–AIIN–Y has only two instances

Two cross-register occurrences are sufficient to nominate a construction, not
to identify equal allocation. Its apparent symmetry is exactly the kind of
pattern humans overinterpret.

### 9.4 `qokaiin` has only one boundary witness

One repeat among 46 transitions is unusual, but the interpretation could be
ordinary repetition, copying, correction, or coincidence. The card cannot
carry the theory by itself.

### 9.5 Astro is barely joined

The circle pages lack the exact prose representation. “Same workshop pedagogy”
is weaker than shared content. The proposed WHEN annex may be false even if the
evolved-codebook architecture is right.

### 9.6 Why so elaborate if it is merely practical?

A visual exercise/pattern book explains abundant formal play without requiring
hidden source clauses. The evolved-codebook answer—mnemonic distinctiveness,
initiated access, and copied tradition—is possible but not compelled.

## 10. Falsifiers and risky predictions

All tests remain restricted to the ten fixed pages unless scope is explicitly
changed.

### 10.1 Whole-card versus composition

**RISKY PREDICTION 1.** Frequent portable bodies will preserve exact identity
across hands better than their wrappers do. Wrapper choice will be predicted
more by line entry, preceding closure, hand, and register than by the proposed
lexical function.

**Failure:** wrapper values independently and consistently change referential
meaning across matched contexts. That would favor true compositional
morphology over renderer variation.

**RISKY PREDICTION 2.** Novel combinations will concentrate in a few recurrent
frames and B closure environments. They will not form a nearly Cartesian
product of apparent subparts.

**Failure:** a compact component grammar predicts held fixed-page cards and
their placements as well as exact identity, including rare cards. Then the
primary remembered unit is compositional, not mixed.

**RISKY PREDICTION 3.** Rare cards will show page-local exemplar consistency
and discontinuous exceptions. They will be worse explained by smooth character
composition than common family members.

**Failure:** rare forms are the most regular and freely generable class.

### 10.2 Closures

**RISKY PREDICTION 4.** Attached closes will align with field completion and B
templates, not consistently with physical-line end or prose-like clause end.
Closer identity will retain some local value/template information.

**Failure:** matched annotation shows one closure system behaves as ordinary
punctuation across Herbal A, Herbal B and Bio B. Then “typed commitment” should
be replaced by punctuation.

### 10.3 Concrete card readings

**RISKY PREDICTION 5.** AIIN will be compatible with scale/value slots across
the fixed prose records, including middles and ends, but will avoid behaving
like a uniquely pictured entity name.

**Failure:** AIIN consistently owns one visible referent or one process step.

**RISKY PREDICTION 6.** L/O should most often bridge two content-bearing cards
or precede a locally resolved value, as expected for WITH/IN-MEDIUM.

**Failure:** it is concentrated at record entry, behaves as an owner label, or
selects no relational neighborhood beyond frequency.

**RISKY PREDICTION 7.** CTHY should co-occur with material/configuration cards
and precede values or applications more often than it occupies owner position.

**Failure:** it patterns as a portable count or generic line-frame tag.

### 10.4 The two fragile constructions

**RISKY PREDICTION 8.** On independent manual annotation of only `f10r.6` and
`f83r.3`, both Y–AIIN–Y occurrences will lie in genuinely dyadic or symmetric
local contexts more often than matched three-card controls.

**Failure:** neither location is dyadic, or matched controls do equally well.
Withdraw equal allocation first; withdraw the whole construction if recurrence
is null-like.

**RISKY PREDICTION 9.** The `f82r.3–4` repeated `qokaiin` will sit at an
unfinished-to-resumed field boundary rather than two complete independent
clauses.

**Failure:** separator/layout review makes both occurrences complete ordinary
uses. Prefer phrase repetition or unresolved status.

### 10.5 Multiple scribes and Astro

**RISKY PREDICTION 10.** The common deck will cross hands more strongly than
rare register cards, while wrapper and joining habits will cluster by hand.

**Failure:** body inventories partition completely by hand, or wrapper behavior
is identical despite claimed hands. Either result weakens the proposed teaching
structure.

**RISKY PREDICTION 11.** The three circle pages will display repeated labels or
positional families aligned with analogous sectors/rings, but any prose-like
surface overlap will not support a universal Herbal/Bio dictionary.

**Failure:** labels ignore diagram coordinates and instead form ordinary linear
paragraph behavior; or externally fixed correspondences demonstrate the same
exact functional cards across all registers. The former favors a separate text
genre, the latter a stronger unified technical language.

## 11. Strongest adversarial alternative

The leading opponent is a **visual pattern/exercise book**: scribes practice or
display systematic transformations around content-rich images, and recurring
cards are graphic motifs rather than compressed instructions. This alternative
explains wrapper families, near-regularity, exact copying, local novelty, and
multiple hands with very little semantic speculation.

The decisive comparison is not “does the manuscript look language-like?” Both
models predict that. It is whether card selection tracks practical record
roles beyond graphic opportunity.

```text
EVOLVED CODEBOOK predicts:
  same exact cards retain constrained field roles when image geometry and
  neighboring pen shapes change; stencils accept semantically typed variation.

PATTERN BOOK predicts:
  motif choice follows visual adjacency, balance, copying sequence, or exercise
  transformations more strongly than practical field role.
```

The evolved codebook should lose if, on blinded fixed-page annotation, geometry
and local stroke continuation explain portable-card placement and recurrence as
well as or better than register-conditioned record roles, especially if the
proposed AIIN/Y/L/O/CTHY distinctions add no held information.

## 12. Mandatory V2 decisions

1. **Remembered unit:** a mixture, asymmetrically weighted toward whole cards.
   Composition is real only for wrappers, closures and a few entrenched frames.
2. **Upstream content:** hybrid—elliptical natural-language prompts compiled
   into lexical and technical packets.
3. **Attached closures:** typed field commitments, perhaps descended from
   lexical abbreviations; not ordinary punctuation.
4. **Y–AIIN–Y:** retained narrowly as “two tagged arguments at an equal/usual
   quantum,” with tagged-value fallback and an explicit withdrawal test.
5. **Repeated `qokaiin`:** copied-forward active parameter is preferred by a
   small margin over ordinary repetition; confidence remains low.
6. **Surviving card readings:** AIIN standard quantum; Y argument/side tag; L/O
   WITH/IN-MEDIUM; CTHY READY/PREPARED.
7. **Astro:** same workshop learning mechanism, separate local namespace. The
   WHAT/HOW/WHEN integration is a prediction, not established linkage.
8. **Loss condition:** a compact freely compositional grammar predicts rare and
   held cards, or the visual pattern model explains placement/recurrence without
   practical record roles.

## 13. Bottom line

The Voynich workshop need not choose between “every group is a word” and
“every sign is an enciphered letter.” Its plausible invention is socially
simpler and analytically messier: memorable signs for useful chunks, copied
until some became families; frames learned by filling them; closures learned by
checking cells; wrappers learned through the moving hand; exceptions preserved
because the exemplar, not an abstract grammar, remained authoritative.

The best concrete source-layer paraphrase on these ten pages is therefore:

```text
[PICTURED OWNER]
prepared / with-in medium / standard share or setting /
for this item or side / local operation / cell committed
```

That is a constrained working codebook, not a translation. Its value is that it
makes exact identity and irregular composition consequences of one learnable
history—and makes sufficiently risky claims about AIIN, Y, L/O, CTHY,
closures, `qokaiin`, and Y–AIIN–Y to be discarded when the fixed pages disagree.
