# Candidate V2 — the workshop formularium

Date: 2026-08-21

Status: independent speculative sidequest theory. This is not a GDT result,
translation, language identification, or claim about manuscript origin.

Scope is fixed to `f10r`, `f11r`, `f55v`, `f56r`, `f81v`, `f82r`, `f83r`,
`f67r2`, `f68r1`, and `f69v`. No f84/f84r material was accessed. The only
Voynich inputs used were `VOYNICH_CURRENT_ROUTE.md` and
`SIDEQUEST_SCRIBE_WORKSHOP_CURRENT.md`; no earlier candidate or sidequest
archive was consulted.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The simplest historically plausible system is a **private practical
formularium written with learned whole-card brevigraphs**. It is neither a
letter-for-letter cipher nor a clean symbolic database. A small workshop first
draws or inherits the picture/table, which supplies the main subject. A master
or knowledgeable compiler reduces ordinary technical notes to a short sequence
of conventional content, relation, quantity and state cards. Copyists learn
those cards from exemplars, insert them into a small number of register-specific
forms, add joining/entry strokes habitual to their hand, and attach a closure
when a local cell is complete. The medical pages are primarily an illustrated
materia-medica and treatment formularium; the circle pages are a distinct
almanac/lookup annex using the same general economy of local labels but not
necessarily the same card dictionary.

The most concrete retained provisional reading is:

```text
Y — AIIN — Y  =  PART/ITEM-SLOT — OF-EQUAL-QUANTITY — PART/ITEM-SLOT
```

Functionally this resembles a compact `ana / partes aequales` instruction,
not because `AIIN` sounds or looks like Latin *ana*, but because the same
abstract equal-quantity device is precisely the kind of highly stable recipe
logogram that crossed languages and scribes. An equally live fallback is
`UNIT — SAME-STATED-VALUE — UNIT`; the two occurrences cannot choose between
amount, setting, degree, or index.

## Historical comparison: four mechanisms

### Mechanism A — ordinary scribal abbreviation

A late-medieval scribe could abbreviate by suspension, contraction,
superscript, brevigraph, and conventional sign. Such systems were learned as
rules plus a repertoire, varied between scribes, and were resolved from
context. The Library of Congress palaeography guide emphasizes both the small
set of reusable rules and variation across scribes and texts. A quantitative
study of aligned Latin and Middle English plague tracts is more directly
relevant: the items abbreviated consistently by all five witnesses are
dominated in recipes by formulaic signs—drachm, *ana*, *recipe*, half, and
ounce—whereas running prose favors common function words. In that study, 62
aligned words were abbreviated identically in all five witnesses; the most
repeated were generic recipe machinery rather than distinctive ingredients.

What this explains:

- compact recurrent surfaces;
- hand-specific wrapper/allographic variation;
- stable technical cards across different lexical material;
- forms that can be joined or detached;
- absence of a simple phoneme substitution.

What it does not explain by itself:

- the extreme exact-card ecology;
- many short Biological cells with attached closure;
- formula stencils surviving while almost every card changes;
- why the same source unit would regularly be wrapped in several ways.

Conclusion: necessary upstream mechanism, insufficient complete model.

### Mechanism B — apothecary prescription / antidotary formula

Recipes already mixed language with a stable technical notation. Common signs
encoded `take`, dram, ounce, half and `of each`; quantity systems could change
from recipe to recipe, and some ingredients had no stated amount. The aligned
plague-tract study explicitly identifies `anᵃ` as “the same amount” and notes
that such characteristic abbreviations could act as visible pragmatic markers
of the recipe text type. A fifteenth-century example published in *Mediaeval
Studies* runs in compressed sequence from `Recipe` through several ingredient
groups and `ana`, then into ordinary preparation instructions: stamp, seethe,
strain, put back into the vessel, add substances in an ordered sequence, and
mix. The Wellcome mid-fifteenth-century north-east Italian MS.683 contains
recipes copied by two principal hands plus additions, uses `Recipe`, and ends
one excerpt with ingredients `ana grani .ij.`. Another Wellcome manuscript has
the explicit phrase `Absinthii mente ana partes equales decoquantur in vino`.
BL Harley MS 1736 likewise preserves `sarcocolle mellis partes equales` in a
medical recipe.

This mechanism is unusually close to the required architecture because a
short card can replace:

- an imperative (`take`, `mix`, `make`);
- a recurrent relation (`of each`, `with`, `in`);
- a measure or quantity;
- a preparation state;
- an entire already-understood recipe formula.

It also naturally permits omitted arguments. “Of each” makes sense only by
recovering a prior ingredient list; “as above” or an unstated quantity depends
on surrounding practice. A drawing can carry the plant or apparatus identity
without repeating it in every line.

Weakness: real prescriptions remain substantially readable language. They do
not normally turn every lexical item into a private opaque card. Voynich would
therefore represent an unusually developed house notation, not ordinary
Latin prescription writing.

### Mechanism C — almanac/table/exemplar practice

Late-medieval practical scientific books joined tables, diagrams, calendars,
astrology and medicine. Wellcome's early-fifteenth-century practical manual of
computational science and astrological medicine contains tables and treatises
in one hand and recipes added by later hands. BL Harley MS 2381 combines a
calendar, lunar calendar, a note on weights and measures, a large medical
recipe collection, an antidotary, waters, and three astrological tables. BL
Harley MS 2558, assembled and augmented by the fifteenth-century physician
Thomas Fayreford, combines botanical synonym dictionaries, herbal, medical and
surgical commonplace books, recipes, alchemy, uroscopy, prognostic diagrams
and annotations. These are strong evidence for the ecology of a multi-register
practical codex.

Table use supplies three further mechanisms:

1. a row/sector's place carries information independently of its label;
2. a learner copies a locally complete exemplar rather than regenerating it
   from ordinary syntax;
3. a pre-ruled or pre-drawn layout constrains entry length and reflow.

This is an excellent account of f67r2/f68r1/f69v and of visible pictures acting
as silent addresses. It is weaker for the long Herbal lines unless combined
with formulaic technical language.

### Mechanism D — ledger/bookkeeping notation

Late-fourteenth- and early-fifteenth-century Italian businesses used journals,
ledgers, classified accounts and compact conventional entries. Datini-company
records preserve a 1395 balance; early-fifteenth-century Venetian ledgers show
routine transfer between books. The 1450–51 Cantelowe accounts mix languages,
merchant marks and unusually extensive Arabic/fractional notation. This proves
that a practical workshop could maintain a learned, multilingual, compressed
entry grammar whose layout and repeated fields matter.

It explains record reset, carry, closed entries and repeated stencils, but it
predicts stronger columns, explicit quantities and transaction symmetry than
the ten-page evidence currently provides. It also has no natural explanation
for elaborate plant, bathing and circular imagery.

Conclusion: useful analogy for entry discipline and carrying, not the leading
genre.

## Chosen mechanism

The selected system combines B and C, with A as the rendering technology:

```text
APOTHECARY/TECHNICAL FORMULARIUM
    content and procedural packets
           +
ALMANAC/EXEMPLAR ORGANIZATION
    picture-, sector- and register-owned arguments
           +
SCRIBAL BREVIATION
    house cards, joining strokes, hand variants, closures
```

Accounting contributes one minor convention: a copied-forward entry or
repeated heading can be carried across a physical-line boundary without being
two semantic events.

Why this wins:

- It covers all three registers without claiming they are one homogeneous
  text genre.
- It lets exact card identity be operationally primary while still allowing
  ordinary language upstream.
- It is teachable by exemplar rather than a giant calculated cipher.
- It predicts stable recipe machinery amid highly variable content.
- It expects multilingual or mixed-source compilation without requiring two
  Voynich languages.
- It explains why image, position, separator and closure can carry grammar.
- It requires no phonetic reading of the visible glyphs.

## The workshop's learnable system

### What the apprentice is taught

The system can be learned from four physical teaching aids.

#### 1. A common card leaf

Perhaps 50–100 frequent cards are copied with a short spoken or ordinary-text
expansion. They cover:

- common relations;
- quantity and equality;
- preparation/state;
- continuation/reference;
- frequent materials or material classes;
- generic operation/result formulas.

The surviving manuscript need not contain this key leaf. The important point
is cognitive economy: a learner memorizes recurring graphic wholes, as a
scribe already memorized brevigraphs, rather than a 1,676-entry cipher.

#### 2. Register exemplar leaves

Each register has a few model rows:

```text
HERBAL:     pictured simple + long descriptive/preparative entry
BIO:        pictured installation + several short checked specification cells
ASTRO:      position-owned label/value repeated around a local array
```

The exemplar fixes which kinds of cards can occupy each construction, but not
a universal semantic field number. This agrees with the failure of universal
field ordinal and with the success of register-conditioned placement.

#### 3. A hand's ductus lesson

The apprentice learns entry strokes and joins:

- line-entry `s` when an already licensed card is selected;
- post-close `q` when an already licensed card begins after DY;
- `ch`, `sh`, `d`, `t` and bare renderings as copied approaches/joins for
  certain cards;
- SPACE/JOIN according to local fit and exemplar habit.

These are not independently meaningful prefixes. They are closer to a
combination of brevigraph allograph, pen approach and construction cue.

#### 4. A checking routine

When a short cell is resolved, the scribe attaches its licensed DY/B3-bearing
close. A long open line can continue physically without one. The closure is a
local `ENTRY COMPLETE` mark, perhaps comparable in function—not shape or
history—to a ledger tick, prescription terminal flourish, or completed table
cell.

### Production algorithm

```text
1. Identify register and picture/diagram owner.
2. Recover the ordinary note or model entry to be copied.
3. Omit the subject and arguments already supplied visually or by the form.
4. Replace recurrent phrases by house cards.
5. Preserve rare content as a learned whole card or a local literal spelling.
6. Copy cards into the appropriate register stencil.
7. Add hand-specific entry/join variants.
8. Attach close to resolved cells.
9. Reflow around the already-drawn image; carry a card if necessary.
```

This is simple enough for several scribes because most decisions are lookup
and copying. The master/compiler must understand the source; a junior copyist
may only need to recognize the register and follow the exemplar.

## A provisional card taxonomy

The taxonomy is deliberately small and functional. It is not a segmentation
of every surface group.

| class | workshop use | expected behavior |
|---|---|---|
| CONTENT | substance, property, action or named local item | topic/register concentrated |
| RELATION | of, with, in, for, from, according to | broad environments, often interior |
| VALUE | amount, equality, degree, count or index | broad registers, compatible with typed frame |
| TYPE | part, item, unit or value-kind | surrounds/qualifies values |
| STATE | prepared, heated, strained, ready, selected | intermediate or result-near |
| CARRY | same heading/value/operation continues | boundary-mobile, deletion may improve parse |
| CLOSE | filled cell committed | attached, register/hand conditioned |
| LOCAL LABEL | plant/star/sector/apparatus-specific address | narrow page or diagram namespace |

The visible cards may be polyfunctional. A historical content card can bleach
into a relation or construction marker; a once meaningful ending can survive
as a fossilized allograph. This is expected in an organically used workshop
notation and avoids the unrealistically clean `prefix + stem + suffix` model.

## Key forms: concrete provisional expansions

### AIIN

Leading expansion:

```text
AIIN = OF-EACH / EQUAL-AMOUNT / SAME-STATED-VALUE
```

Second expansion:

```text
AIIN = VALUE-ENTRY (amount, degree, count, index)
```

Why the first is now preferred, weakly:

- real medieval `ana` is a high-consistency cross-scribe recipe abbreviation;
- equal amounts are commonly expressed after a preceding ingredient group;
- `AIIN` is portable over all seven fixed prose pages;
- it occupies first, middle and last positions rather than behaving as a
  lexical plant name;
- it forms the exact repeated `Y–AIIN–Y` path.

Why it remains provisional:

- no numeral, measure, ingredient pair or repeated visible referent is attached
  independently to either occurrence;
- astronomy could reuse the abstract value-entry function without equal
  quantity;
- the surface resemblance to `aiin/ana` is explicitly given no weight.

### Y

Leading expansion:

```text
Y = PART / ITEM-SLOT / UNIT-TYPE
```

Alternative:

```text
Y = VALUE-FRAME or generic THING/ENTRY tag
```

It is common, portable and mostly medial, and a following event sometimes
closes. The free surface `DY` is merely the ordinary Y card under a `d`
renderer; it must not be confused with attached DY closure. The wrappers in
the two `Y–AIIN–Y` realizations differ, so Y does not show a simple agreement
system.

### L/O

Leading expansion:

```text
L/O = IN / WITH / OF / FOR, generalized RELATION
```

Alternative:

```text
L/O = MEDIUM/CLASS link (“in the medium/class already selected”)
```

Its cross-register recurrence and mostly interior placement suit a low-content
relator. Its preference for a following close in 5/17 eligible instances could
mean that it introduces the final argument of a cell. No specific English or
Latin preposition is selected.

### CTHY

Leading expansion:

```text
CTHY = PREPARED/QUALIFIED STATE
```

Plausible practical expansions include `prepared`, `reduced`, `strained`,
`heated`, `selected`, or a Galenic quality class. None is preferred. Its seven
occurrences on three pages and predominantly medial placement suggest a state
or property card, but this is the weakest of the portable core guesses.

### qokaiin

Leading constructional expansion:

```text
qokaiin = SAME ACTIVE ENTRY / CONTINUE UNDER THIS HEAD
```

This is not a claim that `q` means NEXT. The exact whole card moves from the
end of f82r.3 to the start of f82r.4 within one paragraph record. The simplest
workshop account is dittography caused by line carry or a deliberate catch:
the card is semantically instantiated once but graphically appears on both
sides of the physical break. A weaker alternative is that it repeats the
current material, process or value header at the start of the next fitted
line.

### Attached DY/B3 closures

Leading expansion:

```text
ATTACHED CLOSE = CELL FILLED / ENTRY RESOLVED
```

The closure probably has little or no lexical source expansion. It behaves
like a local formal commitment mark. Distinct closer identities may select
different cell types, but unless pre-close stencil preferences survive page
and hand controls, they should be treated as allographic terminal variants.

### Y–AIIN–Y

Leading expansion:

```text
PART/ITEM — OF-EACH/EQUAL-VALUE — PART/ITEM
```

Natural-language paraphrase:

> take/use the paired items or portions in the same stated quantity.

Technical-notation paraphrase:

```text
TYPE(Y) : VALUE(EQUAL/COPIED) : TYPE(Y)
```

This construction is attractive because historical prescriptions use both
`ana` and `partes equales`, and because equal-value notation is useful in
medical, apparatus and astronomical registers. It does **not** imply that the
two Y cards name two visible objects; they may simply delimit a typed value.

The two occurrences can be provisionally expanded as:

```text
f10r.6  [plant-owned dossier material ...]
        PART — EQUAL-AMOUNT — PART
        ≈ “the paired portions are of one amount”

f83r.3  PART — EQUAL-SETTING — PART | LCHE-CLOSE
        ≈ “set the paired entries alike; commit the following apparatus cell”
```

The shift from `amount` to `setting` is not asserted as polysemy. The common
latent function is `copy/equalize a typed value`; the register supplies what
kind of value it is.

## Ten-page interpretation

### f10r — pictured-simple dosage/property dossier

The plant image is the silent subject. The generally open Herbal-A line form
is best read as a compressed descriptive/preparative entry rather than a list
of separately checked steps. Likely source fields, without claiming fixed
order, include:

- plant part used;
- quality or preparation state;
- medium or manner of use;
- amount/proportion;
- application or indication.

The `Y–AIIN–Y` tail on line 6 is the best candidate for a proportion clause:
`two portions/classes at equal value`. Water can be an omitted or explicit
medium somewhere in the line, especially if the picture or normal practice
makes it recoverable. No card is assigned WATER.

### f11r — second pictured-simple dossier under the same Herbal-A exemplar

Its repeated open 4-card/5-card line stencils suggest the same dossier grammar
with different plant-specific content. This is exactly what a formularium
should produce: stable entry length and relation/value machinery, variable
content cards. The picture gives the plant; lines can represent alternative
preparations, parts, qualities or uses. They need not be sentences or one
recipe each.

### f55v — B-rendered herbal bridge

This is the crucial workshop bridge. Half its exact types overlap the fixed
Bio-B pages, far more than the fixed Herbal-A pages, yet only one of its three
closers transfers. The theory reads this as:

```text
same B-school card shelf and closing habit
+ plant-register addendum
```

not a different language. Its higher closure rate means the B hand/school
renders even a plant dossier through more explicitly committed cells. It may
be closer to a prescription worksheet than the open descriptive Herbal-A
pages.

### f56r — further open Herbal-A dossier

Its recurrence of the same open stencils as f10r/f11r supports an exemplar
copied across pages. The strongest prediction is functional substitution at a
few recurrent construction positions: a relation/value/state card should stay
in a slot while plant-specific content changes. Any water reading must arise
from the pictured habitat, preparation medium or use—not from an assumed
universal `OL/AROL` word.

### f81v — Biological checked specification sheet

The many short closed cells are not best treated as prose sentences. The image
supplies figures, pools, vessels, channels or application sites as silent
arguments. The `1C | 3C | 1C | 4O` stencil is a row in a treatment/configuration
formularium. The recurring closer at the end of the three-card cell marks that
cell as resolved even though the eight preceding/following content cards vary.

Plausible cell classes are:

- material/medium;
- preparation or temperature/state;
- path/application relation;
- duration or repeated amount;
- resulting condition.

They are alternatives, not established universal field meanings.

### f82r — multi-line treatment/configuration record

The exact `qokaiin` boundary repeat is most naturally a physical carry or
repeated local head. The statement therefore crosses the line:

```text
... ACTIVE-CARD  /line fit/
ACTIVE-CARD ...
```

with one semantic state. This is learnable scribal behavior: copy the last
card again to prevent losing place when the diagram forces a new line. The
page's `2C | 4O` and longer stencils are separate cells/clauses in one pictured
record, not independent sentences.

### f83r — paired-setting plus committed apparatus/treatment cell

The `Y–AIIN–Y` sequence begins a field and is followed by an `LCHE` close.
The leading reading is an instruction to copy/equalize one typed setting or
portion before committing a local process/configuration cell. This makes the
same abstract card frame useful in Herbal and Biological contexts without
requiring the same concrete referent.

### f67r2 — layered almanac selector

The human-defined seven-member and twelve-member circular sets are local
lookup namespaces. The historically easiest provisional reading is:

- seven luminary/regent slots;
- twelve zodiacal/monthly/sign slots;
- a central result, owner or controlling value.

This is generic medieval astral architecture, not a Georgian or A-65 key. The
labels are local cards whose position supplies most of their identity. The
workshop operator selects one entry from each ring to obtain a condition used
elsewhere, but no cross-page pointer is known.

### f68r1 — spatial star/entry catalogue

One central and 28 noncentral labelled stars form a spatial catalogue, not a
licensed 28-step cycle. The best provisional use is a roster of named/local
astral entries surrounding a distinguished reference. It could support lunar
stations, star groups, prognostic values or calculation examples, but there is
no authorial sequence with which to choose. The labels should be read by
position and exact local identity, not by importing prose cards.

### f69v — 28-entry alternating schedule

This page uniquely supplies an ordered 28-entry ring with strict LONG/SHORT
alternation. The leading workshop reading is a lunar-day/station lookup
schedule whose alternating graphic capacity is a **reading/checking device**
or two alternating entry templates. It is not evidence that LONG and SHORT
mean odd/even, favorable/unfavorable, red/black, day/night or male/female.
F69LS001 found no reliable textual feature distinguishing the two layouts, so
semantic binary state is not encoded detectably in the text. A practical
scribe may simply alternate radial length to keep neighboring entries apart.

The three circle pages therefore form an annex of local instruments, not one
combined table unless a future external pointer links them.

## What the theory explains at once

1. **Free/bound reuse.** A card can be detached, joined or approached with a
   wrapper without changing its source expansion.
2. **Exact collapse beneath spelling.** `AIIN/DAIIN/SAIIN/CHAIIN/TAIIN` are
   renderer variants of a learned card.
3. **Right-edge DY.** Attached DY is a cell close; free DY is rendered Y.
4. **Productive q+X without morphology.** Post-close `q` selects the next
   licensed card as a constructional entry stroke.
5. **Line reset.** Reflow around earlier drawings activates entry allographs;
   it is not necessarily syntax.
6. **Record continuation.** A dossier or form continues across physical lines;
   the f82r duplicated boundary card protects continuity.
7. **Repeated identical labels/cards.** Exact reuse is expected from a house
   repertoire even where concrete subjects differ.
8. **Currier/register effects.** Schools/hands share a core card leaf but use
   different register exemplars and rendering habits.
9. **No easy language/cipher mapping.** One card can abbreviate a phrase,
   logogram, technical value or local name; omitted arguments come from image
   and form.
10. **Different diagram namespaces.** Astronomical labels can be local because
    table position supplies their address.
11. **Several scribes.** They copy the same cards and templates but vary
    entry/join/closure renderers, just as real abbreviation practice varies.
12. **Predrawn images.** Text naturally reflows after illustration; layout
    effects need not carry content meaning.

## Awkward observations

### The notation is more opaque than ordinary medieval brevigraphy

No cited comparator turns an entire practical miscellany into cards this
thoroughly. The theory requires either a lost key/exemplar, an unusually
private house shorthand, or visible graphs that conceal more readable letter
content than the exact-card model suggests.

### Exact identity still predicts better than proposed components

This fits whole-card learning but prevents us from confidently decomposing
cards into relation, value and state pieces. The taxonomy is a functional
story, not an observed parse.

### AIIN may be merely a frequent formal card

Its equality reading leans on historical fit plus `Y–AIIN–Y`, not an external
quantity anchor. If the two frames do not own paired operands, abandon
equal-value while retaining AIIN as a portable card.

### Biological imagery is polysemous

Bathing, treatment, apparatus, reproductive medicine, cosmology and symbolic
instruction can share vessels, channels and figures. The notation architecture
does not choose among them.

### Astro integration is weak

The codex ecology supports medicine plus astrology, but the fixed pages provide
no cross-register pointer. `WHAT/HOW/WHEN` is therefore a helpful editorial
model, not a decoded program.

### Closure identity may be punctuation-like

If distinct closers do not predict different preceding stencils, they are
merely hand/register terminal variants and contain no cell-type information.

## Specific predictions from this reconstruction

These are new theory consequences, not confirmations.

### P1 — equal-value construction

The two fixed `Y–AIIN–Y` occurrences should each sit next to a construction
with two comparable operands, portions, settings or repeated slot types. If
one occurrence has a uniquely owned single object and no paired structure,
the equal-value expansion fails.

### P2 — AIIN is grammar-heavy, content-light

Within the ten pages, removing AIIN from card bags should damage stencil/value
classification more than page-topic classification. Plant or apparatus
identity should not concentrate on AIIN.

### P3 — closer typing is shallow

Major Biological closer identities may prefer different immediately preceding
length/stencil classes, but that preference should substantially weaken after
hand/page are fixed. If it remains strong, promote typed closure; otherwise
collapse closers to `CELL COMPLETE`.

### P4 — carry is one semantic event

For the f82r paragraph, treating the two boundary `qokaiin` tokens as one
carried card should create a better match to another already repeated stencil
than treating them as two ordinary adjacent events. Failure closes the carry
reading.

### P5 — B is a production school, not a language

The B bridge f55v should share renderer-entry and closure behavior with Bio B
even where its content-card distribution remains Herbal-like. A language split
would predict more uniform lexical separation; a workshop-school split predicts
shared form mechanics plus topical vocabulary.

### P6 — picture is silent address

Repeated visible substructures owned independently by the picture should permit
omission rather than repetition of a corresponding content card. Text should
be more explicit where the visual owner is ambiguous, but this must be tested
only with existing annotations.

### P7 — f69 alternation is layout, not a textual binary

After controlling radial capacity, LONG and SHORT entries should not form two
stable surface-card classes. This retains the existing nonconfirmation as an
expected result of the leading theory.

### P8 — local astro namespaces

The same surface label may recur inside a diagram according to repeated local
role, but the seven-, twelve-, 28-star and 28-radial inventories should not
collapse into one universal dictionary. A genuine explicit pointer would be
needed to overturn this.

## Minimal provisional "translation"

The best whole-register paraphrase is:

```text
HERBAL
  “For the simple shown: record its usable part/class, relevant quality or
   prepared state, medium/application, and any amount or paired proportion.”

BIOLOGICAL
  “For the installation or treatment shown: fill the short material/state/
   relation cells, commit each resolved cell, and continue the current head
   across the available writing space.”

ASTRONOMICAL
  “Use the local ring or roster to select the relevant luminary/sign/
   star-or-lunar entry and obtain the page-local condition or index.”
```

For the strongest formula:

```text
Y–AIIN–Y
  “the two typed portions/settings have the same entered value”
```

This is intentionally one abstraction above English plaintext. The remaining
translation problem is to identify what each local content card expands to and
which value domain—amount, dose, degree, time, index, setting—AIIN copies.

## Bottom line

The best historical evolution of the current theory is not “an encrypted
herbal” and not “a medieval database.” It is a **workshop formulary whose
ordinary-language source has been aggressively conventionalized into copied
technical cards**. The cards behave like an enlarged house repertoire of
recipe brevigraphs and table entries; the pictures and form carry omitted
arguments; B hands favor checked cells; circle pages use local positional
addressing.

If forced to guess the first real source-level phrase, the least bad guess is:

```text
Y–AIIN–Y  →  “in/of equal parts” or “same value for both typed entries.”
```

That guess should live or die by paired-operand evidence on the two fixed
occurrences. It must not be protected by changing `amount` into an unlimited
metaphor after failure.
