# Sidequest Pass 851: the small-workshop surface lesson

A seven-line lesson is enough to choose a registered surface for every one
of the 173 exact cards in each of four hypothetical styles. The apprentice
first learns the shared card and meaning. A one-form card is copied exactly;
only then does a personal bare, CH/CHE, Q/SH or D/T preference apply.

The inventory is strongly mixed: 139 cards are surface-fixed and only 34
have multiple registered forms. Thirty-three cards actually split under the
four rules. Together the profiles select 220 of 230 registered card-surface
pairs (95.7%). Ten additional variants remain tiny memorized spellings rather
than reasons to enlarge the grammar.

This is exactly the kind of economy expected from a small workshop: most
cards are copied, a small common subset admits routine house variation, and
meaning never depends on which renderer was chosen. These profiles still do
not identify any real manuscript hand.

Next, inspect the ten unselected registered variants and reduce them to the
smallest possible supplementary lesson.
