## Historical source and fixed signature

Primary: British Library, Sloane MS 4016 f38r, official [IIIF image](https://bl.digirati.io/images/ark:/81055/vdc_100165751215.0x00004d/full/1500,/0/default.jpg), canvas 84 of the official 235-canvas manifest. The page visibly writes
`Dragontea .i. serpentaria .i. asclepius` (subsequent aliases not needed here)
and depicts one plant with the following **four** attached/associated features:

1. A winged, legged dragon **and** a separate long snake beside the same plant,
   with the snake touching/approaching the root zone.
2. Three large palmately divided leaves, each with roughly five to nine long,
   narrow pointed lobes arising from a common leaf base.
3. One single tall central spathe/spadix-like terminal, a curved brown hood
   beside a green shaft, rather than a many-flowered head.
4. One large pale, knobbly tuber/root at the stem base, not a spray of thin
   roots or a branching brown woody root.

The British Library's public [caption](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/a-dragon-and-a-serpent-in-a-herbal/awGj8n0tlCqOnQ)
calls this snakeroot and discusses snakebite. Only the visible manuscript
heading and drawing count as primary evidence here; the caption's medical
interpretation is not a Voynich meaning binding.
