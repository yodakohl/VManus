## The hand-checkable argument
Assume all legal references cost at most6units, counting every marker. A written
qokeedy has7units (q,o,k,e,e,d,y), so neither occurrence can be a reference.
Both must be full spellings. Their identical forms, under a fixed injective
literal code, mean the same source word was written twice. After the first,
that word has a legal reference costing at most6. A writer who MUST exploit a
strict saving cannot then emit the same7-unit full spelling again.

In general, for any exact doublet XX, a strict-saving writer satisfying the
retention/availability premises requires r>=width(X). No paragraph-start flag
is involved. Literal/reference alphabets may overlap; when width(X)>r, length
already makes both occurrences literal. Ties do not affect the contradiction.

This rule is stronger than “abbreviate only if useful.” It requires “whenever a
strictly shorter legal reference is available, use a shorter reference.” A human
who sometimes spells fully for clarity, emphasis or another reason is outside
that rule. No preference for such an alternative is inferred from this proof.

## What is actually excluded
The proof needs ALL these conditions:
- one source word gives one complete written group;
- fixed globally injective literal spelling;
- immediate retention AND a legal reference for the just-written word;
- total reference width bounded byr, including controls;
- mandatory exploitation of a strict saving.

There is no empty-paragraph-memory assumption and no disjoint-mode requirement.
Immediate availability between these two words is still required. Selective
storage, restrictions making this word unreferencable, different units, noninjective
or contextual literals, additional control groups and freely optional full spelling
change the contract; they are not automatically disproved or selected.
Allowing some references of width7or more only avoids this necessary bound; it
does not establish a viable decoder, historical economy or a fit to the corpus.
The conclusion is about the maximum repertoire width, not a claim that EVERY
reference must have seven signs.
