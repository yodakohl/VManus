# Sidequest rule — contemporary codebook attestation for dictionary words

Frozen: 2026-08-22, during V75 and before V77 dictionary selection. Explicitly
required by the user.

## Rule

A portable Voynich-side dictionary gloss may be printed as a **word** only if
the same lexical/function category is explicitly present in a real surviving
codebook, cipher key, nomenclator or comparable coded sign list dated roughly
1370–1450.

Ordinary prose, a recipe, a Herbal, a modern dictionary, a later shorthand
manual, general historical plausibility and a similar-looking abbreviation do
not satisfy the rule.

Every admitted dictionary row must carry:

1. the proposed minimal editorial gloss;
2. the attested source-language entry exactly as edited or transcribed;
3. manuscript/archive shelfmark or unambiguous historical key identity;
4. date or dated correspondence using that key;
5. folio/page/entry location in a facsimile or scholarly edition;
6. codebook type and the opaque sign/code assigned to the entry, where
   preserved;
7. a citation and confidence;
8. an explicit statement that the attestation licenses only historical
   dictionary granularity, not identity with the Voynich form.

If any required field is absent, the Voynich card must be labelled
`EXEMPLAR_VALUE_UNKNOWN` or `FORMAL_LABEL_NOT_WORD`.

## Immediate effect

The eleven old V60 whole-card mnemonics and four formal prompts are no longer
available as dictionary words merely because they made earlier translations
coherent. Until V77 supplies a qualifying source row, labels such as `MASS?`,
`BEREIT?`, `SPÜLEN?`, `ABLASSEN?`, `ANSATZ?`, `ANWENDEN?`, `ZIEL`, `VORIGES`
and similar terms are only `PROVISIONAL_UNATTESTED_MNEMONIC` or structural
editorial descriptions.

This correction does not delete the complete V73–V75 working translations.
Their water, bath, plant, star and action words are occurrence-bound
master-exemplar expansions, not entries in the Voynich dictionary.

## Contemporary source screen

The V77 audit must start from actual early keys, not from desired Voynich
meanings. Candidate documentary families include:

- the 1379 Gabriel de Lavinde papal/Avignon cipher tradition;
- documented Mantuan diplomatic cipher keys beginning around 1401 and the
  Mantuan nomenclator reported for 1404;
- early fifteenth-century Italian diplomatic and papal keys preserved in
  archival or published facsimile form.

The initial navigation sources are Aloys Meister's editions of early Italian
and papal cryptography and the original archives they identify. Their mere
mention of a key does not attest an entry: V77 must inspect the reproduced key
or entry list and cite the exact row.

Useful navigation references:

- Aloys Meister, *Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift*
  (1902), digitized by Google Books;
- Aloys Meister, *Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie* (1906),
  digitized through Internet Archive/Open Library;
- William F. Friedman's historical survey, which reproduces an early
  fifteenth-century Italian cipher and documents the contemporary homophonic
  transition.

These are source finders, not automatic gloss licenses.

## V71–V80 integration

- V75 local celestial labels remain occurrence-bound exemplar text and are not
  dictionary words.
- V76 may compare historical book purposes but may not add a portable gloss.
- V77 must apply this rule to every reusable card before it can retain a word.
- V78–V80 must distinguish attested portable dictionary words from bracketed
  exemplar expansions and formal nonword labels.

No surface resemblance, sound, language identification or semantic transfer
from the historical codebooks is permitted.
