## The useful failure

Two attractive page-hand rules must not be generalized:

- On f81v, changing medial `ol` to `qol` repairs two late occurrences but
  destroys six earlier `ol` occurrences: net -4.
- On f83r, changing medial `checthy` to `shcthy` repairs two occurrences but
  destroys two others: net 0.

The differences therefore sit below page level. On f81v the q-L form appears
late in the record; on f83r both sh-CTH examples occur together in B3-S021,
while later B3 statements return to che-CTH. A single physical scribe can thus
switch a local entry frame by copied block, exemplar, or writing session.

## Renderer architecture now

The best workshop model has four layers:

1. one exact-card identity and value;
2. global field-position habits;
3. six shared q/e/s/boundary rules;
4. a small local-block frame selector.

This explains why several scribes can read one another without spelling every
card identically. It also prevents frame changes such as `cheol/ol/qol` or
`checthy/cthy/shcthy` from being mistaken for different words.

## Next move

Locate renderer change-points inside records and teach them as short copied
blocks. The target is to explain the 64 remaining surface mismatches with a few
block-level switches, not with sixty-four lexical exceptions.
