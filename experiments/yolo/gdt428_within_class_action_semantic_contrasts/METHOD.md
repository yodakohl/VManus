# GDT428 method

## Question

Do the roots grouped together for GDT427 prediction remain separately readable
as `CH=NEHMEN`, `S=WÄHLEN`, `K=GEBEN`, `OK=SETZEN`, `P=EINSETZEN`,
`SH=HALTEN`, `CHD=BEARBEITEN`, `T=EINSTELLEN`, and `R=MARKIEREN`?

## Inputs

- GDT416's 4,576 complete component recipes;
- GDT425's 5,051 resolved focus attachments;
- GDT425's 639 action-bound closes;
- GDT413's current nineteen-root dictionary.

## Method

For each action occurrence, record its action-chain position, immediate action
neighbours, pages, registers, attached grade/item/relation cores, and explicit
or inherited close count.

Then freeze an exact substitution frame by replacing one action atom with
`@ACTION` while preserving every other atom and its position. Six comparisons
are exhaustive for the still-grouped roots: `CH~S`, `K~OK`, `K~P`, `OK~P`,
`SH~CHD`, and `T~R`. A frame enters the table only when both compared roots
actually occupy that exact slot somewhere in the current edition.

Finally, turn the strongest process asymmetry into a one-sentence apprentice
rule for each pair. The distribution chooses between already named short
rivals; it does not generate a new long translation.

## Decision rule and claim ceiling

Retain two meanings as distinct when they share at least one exact substitution
frame but still show a concrete asymmetry in process order, grade binding, or
argument binding. Do not merge class members and do not change a dictionary
meaning unless its own operational definition fails.

These are creative working meanings in a compositional workshop model, not
confirmed lexemes, plaintext, language, or source identification.
