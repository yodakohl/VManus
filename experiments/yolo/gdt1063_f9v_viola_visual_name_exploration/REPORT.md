# GDT1063 — `fochor` and the f9v wild-pansy candidate

Status: `EXPLORATORY_VIOLA_VISUAL_LEAD__FIRST_WORD_MEANING_OPEN`.

The image on f9v is a notably plausible wild pansy/*Viola tricolor*: individual
flowers show five unequal petals with darker blue uppers and paler, partly
yellow lower portions; the drawing also has lanceolate simple leaves alongside
deeply divided leaflike parts. This fits [Flora of North America's description
of Viola tricolor](https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=250100968)
and the longstanding [Voynich f9v pansy/viola identification](https://mail.voynich.nu/q02/index.html).
It does **not** visually fit the public decoder's `JACEA`/knapweed, whose
diagnostic flower is a [composite thistlelike head](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/328403/centaurea-jacea-var-nemoralis/details).

The first prose group on f9v is exactly **`fochor` in all three current
transcriptions** (GDT1062); it occurs exactly once per reading in the existing
179-selector corpus. In GDT1059's 89 Herbal-A heads, 84 forms are distinct
and 80 occur as a head once, so uniqueness is compatible with an entry name
but does not prove that every first word names the pictured species. The
provisional complete-word hypothesis is therefore **`fochor` ≈ “wild
pansy/Viola tricolor” (C0, replaceable)**, conditional on that head-as-name
assumption. It is a meaning hypothesis, not a phonetic decipherment or a
confirmed translation. The generic-entry-head alternative remains live.

The public decoder itself had already described f9v's picture as “Wild pansy”,
so choosing this candidate after reading its table is **not independent or
blind**. This exploratory review makes f9v a better *illustration owner* for
Viola than for Jacea; it does not demonstrate that `fochor` is the word
*Viola*. A historical labeled parallel, a second securely matched illustration
with a systematic word relation, or a whole-passage consequence separating a
species name from a generic heading is needed before promotion. The present
selection uses no held leaf, and gives no p-value. Confirmed translated words:
**zero**.
