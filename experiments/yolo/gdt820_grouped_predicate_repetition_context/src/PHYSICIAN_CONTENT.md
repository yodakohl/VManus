# GDT820 — f77r: repeated material name, possible proposition seam

## Outcome and reading basis

An actual repeated noun can connect two propositions in medieval natural
philosophy. Consequently, `qokaiin.qokaiin` need not mean an accidental duplicate
or an absurd double dose of air. A result/object-to-next-subject seam is worth
retaining as a **construction hypothesis**. It is not yet a complete reading of
f77r.34: the first proposed proposition already begins with another `qokaiin`,
and the second has no identified finite predicate before the line ends.

Reader perspective: a physician teaching natural philosophy around 1420,
distinguishing elemental change from bodily mixture and a material from its
container. The historical role generates possible arguments; it does not identify
the manuscript's language, author or genre. No Latin initials are inferred from
EVA, and no new word meaning is proposed here.

Read complete f77r.9–24 and f77r.25–37 in all three readings in this experiment's
`artifacts/FULL_READER.md`: 29 loci, not merely the selected doublet. GDT819's
reader was used initially for registration only. Final alignments below use the
GDT820 source-group reader, checked against the separator atlas at .16/.23/.34/.35
with explicit locus allowances and f84/f84r prefix exclusions. The three readings
are alternatives for one manuscript, not three independent witnesses.

## Fixed meanings and notation

The inherited exploratory whole meanings remain fixed: `qokaiin` physical air;
`qokain` physical water; `otedy` literal water source; `okaiin` the possessive
whose/dessen. The alternative `solkeey` meanings are aqueous vapour or an open
basin. The alternative `chedy` meanings are finite becomes or physically contains.
The optional conditional tail retains `sheedy` if, `chedaiin` is, `chealy`
physically cold. None is a confirmed translation.

`qotaiin`, `qotain`, `qoteedy`, `oteedy`, `cheedy`, `lchedy` and other similar
spellings stay distinct. An extended glyph entity is not silently replaced by
an EVA letter. Unknown whole source groups are enclosed in brackets below.
Vertical bars separate aligned groups. `||` marks **our hypothetical seam**, not
punctuation seen on the page; the source dots encode spaces, not full stops.
German word order and the gender of dessen are not claimed as Voynich grammar.

## f77r.34: exact aligned alternatives

Source groups, before any gloss:

```text
ZL: qokaiin | shedy | chedy | qolchedy | qokaiin || qokaiin | checkhy | raiin
IT: qokaiin | shedy | chedy | qol | chedy | qokaiin || qokaiin | checkhy | raiin
RF: qokaiin | shedy | chedy | qolche@152;y | qokaiin || qokaiin | checkhy | @206;aiin
```

There are eight source groups in ZL/RF, nine in IT. The proposed seam lies
between ZL/RF groups 5/6 and IT groups 6/7. Every repeated occurrence survives.
RF's final `@206;aiin` is one opaque group, not a cleaned `aiin` word.

With finite becomes:

```text
ZL: Luft | [shedy] | wird | [qolchedy] | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [raiin]
IT: Luft | [shedy] | wird | [qol] | wird | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [raiin]
RF: Luft | [shedy] | wird | [qolche@152;y] | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [@206;aiin]
```

With finite physically contains:

```text
ZL: Luft | [shedy] | enthält | [qolchedy] | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [raiin]
IT: Luft | [shedy] | enthält | [qol] | enthält | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [raiin]
RF: Luft | [shedy] | enthält | [qolche@152;y] | Luft || Luft | [checkhy] | [@206;aiin]
```

The useful proposed role assignment is precise: the first member of the adjacent
pair ends a statement as its result, with becomes, or content, with contains;
the second member starts a new statement as its subject/topic. The first
`qokaiin` of the line is a separate occurrence and cannot be discarded.

The price of that assignment is also precise:

1. If the initial air is the local subject, clause A is air … becomes … air,
   or air … contains … air. A written distinction of state or constituent could
   make either informative, but `shedy` and `qolchedy` are still unknown. No
   different kind of air, initial water, heating step or action may be inserted
   into the gap. Starting clause A before .34 is possible, but then its actual
   .33 groups must be parsed; the line break does not supply the missing syntax.
2. If clause B is a complete finite proposition within .34, a finite predicate
   must be identified in `checkhy` or `raiin`/`@206;aiin`, or an explicit grammar
   for a verbless proposition supplied. Neither has been established. Calling
   these groups a connector plus a predicate describes the outstanding jobs;
   it does not give them meanings.
3. Letting clause B run into .35 does not automatically furnish a solution.
   The next known `chedy` is preceded by `solkeey.okaiin`. One must explain the
   written relation between the earlier air and this vapour/basin possessive
   group. They cannot be silently equated, and two subjects cannot be inserted
   into a German sentence without accounting for the intervening writing.
4. IT has a second whole `chedy` before the repeated air. The proposed noun seam
   does not resolve its two finite predicates. Nor does it resolve the separate
   inherited f76r.23 predicate doublet. No becomes-to-is or contains-to-fills
   semantic substitution is licensed.

Thus the hypothesis stops the premature rejection of a noun doublet, but does
not yet deliver the claimed two propositions. The missing first-clause contrast
and second-clause predicate are its strongest local failures.

## f77r.35: the concrete content stays conditional

The exact source groups are:

```text
ZL/IT: solkeey | okaiin | chedy | qokain | sheedy | qokaiin | chedaiin | chealy
RF:    solkeey | okaiin | chedy | qokain | sheedy | qotaiin | che@152;aiin | chealy
```

All three have eight groups. With the optional if/air/is/cold tail enabled, the
four ZL/IT ordered trial glosses are:

```text
Vapour/becomes:  Dampf | dessen | wird | Wasser | wenn | Luft | ist | kalt
Vapour/contains: Dampf | dessen | enthält | Wasser | wenn | Luft | ist | kalt
Basin/becomes:   offenes Becken | dessen | wird | Wasser | wenn | Luft | ist | kalt
Basin/contains:  offenes Becken | dessen | enthält | Wasser | wenn | Luft | ist | kalt
```

These are source-ordered candidate glosses, not four decoded sentences. In RF,
replace exactly `Luft | ist` with `[qotaiin] | [che@152;aiin]`; the other groups
retain the same trial glosses. RF cannot independently confirm the whole tail.
The separate with/mit connector rival is not collapsed into if/wenn here.

Vapour becoming water under a cold-air condition supplies an actual material
change, whereas a basin containing water supplies an actual container relation.
Neither explains how the preceding .34 air-chain connects. Vapour containing
water is a distinct mixture/content claim: it must mean actual aqueous material,
not water merely potentially recoverable by transformation. Basin becoming
water is a literal container-to-material transformation and is the most strained
local account; changing it to becoming filled would change the fixed verb.
These content costs do not identify the symbols or establish an overall winner.

The possessive owner also remains an assumption. `otedy` at .25 can be proposed
as the earlier water-source owner of the water at .27 and vapour/basin at .35,
but no decoded reference construction links them. An attractive condensational
paraphrase does not remove that debt. Cold means physical cold throughout this
conditional; the older humoral property trial is not borrowed to support it.

## Medieval primary comparisons: real reprise, not a semantic key

Two verified examples are from Thomas Aquinas, hence thirteenth-century works
available before the role's approximate 1420 horizon. This is one author's
practice in two works, not independent manuscript evidence for Voynich syntax.
The consulted digital texts reproduce modern editions; their sentence punctuation
has not been collated against a medieval codex here.

In *Sentencia libri De anima* II.17.1 [80734], an account of hearing reports a
view identifying apparent empty space with air, then resumes air as the subject:
“vacuum sit aer. Aer autem facit audire sonum”. The first air is a predicate
name, the next a subject; a written connector and finite predicate continue the
argument. This is physiological/natural-philosophical exposition, not an example
of water literally becoming air. Consulted text: Turin 1959 edition as identified
by [Corpus Thomisticum, II.17.1](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/can2.html#80734).

In *Super Meteora* I.4.6 [80091], naming the upper element is followed by an
explanation: “non proprie vocatur ignis. Ignis enim significat excessum calidi”.
The repeated noun connects propositions, again with an explicit connective and
new finite verb. This is classification followed by explanation, not a recovered
transformation formula. Consulted text: Rome Leonine 1886 edition, identified by
[Corpus Thomisticum, I.4.6](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cme1.html#80091).

The same work also supplies a counterexample to a mechanical noun-seam rule:
“in loco ignis ignis” in I.5.2 [80093]. Within one clause, the first identical
spelling is a genitive dependent on place, the second a nominative subject.
Thus adjacent identical noun spellings need not straddle propositions. This is
not evidence that Voynich has Latin cases; it demonstrates the non-uniqueness
of the surface pattern. [Corpus Thomisticum, I.5.2](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cme1.html#80093).

Quoted words aggregate to 8 from the De anima webpage and 13 from the Meteora
webpage. The modern full stops support the cited editors' analysis of those
Latin passages, not a claim of visible punctuation at f77r.34. The bounded
search found the two genuine reprises above, but no verified exact
material-transformation-result-to-next-subject formula that supplies the missing
Voynich predicates. No whole meaning or confidence level is upgraded.
