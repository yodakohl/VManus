## Complete edition

The four machine-readable tables cover 281 events, 115 fields, 97 statements
and six Biological records. Each event receives one concrete operational
default so that no sequence escapes interpretation. The default is explicitly
typed as a local exemplar value and is paired with a medical and a purely
formal rival.

The resulting book is best read as a **local station atlas**: figures, basins,
arches, receptacles and conduits own short cells concerning filling, holding,
washing, transferring, draining, resetting or recording a state. A medical
scribe can expand the same cells as baths, washes, applications and rests. A
bathhouse clerk can expand them as operating or maintenance entries. The
visible packet distinguishes those two source vocabularies poorly.

## Four necessary owner breaks

`B2-S012`, `B3-S016`, `B3-S026` and `B4-S015` preserve the V71/V72 visible
break. A statement ID may cross the break, but its second clause must open a
new local owner. This is the core correction to the old global-water-machine
reading.

## Strongest reading and strongest rival

The strongest conservative content is a mixed bath/application and apparatus
register: actual figures make a purely abstract form book needlessly thin,
while actual receptacles and tubes make a purely anatomical or gynecological
text needlessly narrow. The strongest rival is a pattern/exemplar atlas whose
cards teach station layouts and closure cadences without naming any operation.

## What remains invented

Every narrow activity—water, liquor, warmth, body washing, admission, drain,
rinse, basin, valve, rest or dosage—is supplied from an assumed source
exemplar unless directly visible as geometry. No arrow supplies direction.
No repeated card supplies a translated verb. The complete edition therefore
tests whether one 1420 workshop world can explain every event; it does not
identify the historical world.
