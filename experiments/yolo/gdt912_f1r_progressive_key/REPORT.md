# GDT912: f1r marginal table remains incomplete

The selected public multispectral product does not give either reader a complete,
single-valued table. The registered progressive-key paragraph test therefore was
**not run**. Confirmed Voynich meanings remain **0**. This is a source-capacity
stop, not a failed decryption and not a refutation of progressive ciphers.

[Lisa Fagin Davis's 2024 release](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
presents two Roman marginal columns offset by one entry and a Voynich-like middle
column. Her reconstruction is preliminary; the proposed attribution to Marci is
not an established authorial key. The published display has **24 rows**, including
an initial right-column-only row. Middle entries at m, n and q are question marks;
other forms and the j/k label remain uncertain. An inherited project summary's
“three complete 26-row columns” is not supported by that display. Twenty-four is
an editorial display count, not our independent count of native alphabet units.
The earlier primary audit file named by that inherited summary is missing locally;
we did not claim to have inspected it. Old source bytes remain unchanged.

Both readers inspected the same three article figures and, subsequently, four
full-resolution strips from the sole file named RH_margin in the public f1r
processed folder. They made their final judgments independently, but both had
seen the published reconstruction. They are not two blind rediscoveries, nor an
expert palaeographic consensus. Native marginal signs are visible; several exact
values, boundaries and row alignments remain unresolved. Neither reader certified
a complete correspondence table. Descriptive shape similarities are not accepted
EVA identities or ordinary-language meanings.

The original fixed rightmost-30-percent crop was mislocalized onto calibration
equipment. Both readers identified that input error. A 300-pixel navigation view
located the parchment boundary, after which the same original was cropped at
x3300–4600, y850–7500. No new spectral product, contrast adjustment or image
synthesis was used. The mistaken crop is preserved as a separate receipt and
reader record; it is not evidence of absent ink. Four corrected panels are
published with exact coordinates and source hashes. Incidental body fragments
were visible in released figures but were not transcribed or used.

METHOD.md was written before the new image access. It requires a complete table
before any body query: one fixed +/-1 progression per encoded glyph, all starting
phases, and the first complete admitted f3r paragraph only. Since that checkpoint
was not met, no body paragraph, phase, glyph completion or Latin candidate was
computed. The f1r admission is marginal only. Current visual scope is48keys /
54selectors, with2 discretionary keys remaining; body admission is unchanged.
f84 and f84r remain sealed. The earlier f57v interpolation, GDT896 and GDT906
results are unchanged. No relation evidence was scored.

`src/run.py` reproduces the compact decision from the retained observations.
`src/validate.py` separately checks hashes, crop geometry, all24 retained slots
and the conditional stop. With the original JPEG supplied, it also verified all
four crops pixel for pixel. Software PASS establishes those mechanical claims;
it does not certify palaeographic readings or meanings. Root wrote both scripts;
reader B supplied the independent visual judgment.

Native image credit: **The Lazarus Project and the Chester F. Carlson Center for
Imaging Science at Rochester Institute of Technology**; Beinecke Library,
MS408, f1r. Public original URL/hash: `artifacts/NATIVE_SOURCE.json`. Figure URLs:
`artifacts/SOURCE_MANIFEST.json`. Reproduction and retention: `artifacts/README.md`.

Decision: park this literal-key route until an independently resolved table and
a defensible connection to the main script are available. Do not fill its gaps
from desired Latin, introduce adaptive progression rules, or call this source
correction progress in translation.
