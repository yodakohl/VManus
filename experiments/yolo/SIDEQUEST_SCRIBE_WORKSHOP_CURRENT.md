## Current architecture

The best creative model is a small workshop notation with two concrete
registers and one shared abstract card grammar:

1. **WORKSHOP_PROSE** — 2,010 groups in 1,435 spoken work instructions and 354
   clauses. Cards express actions, source, quantity, target, path, grade,
   current referent and closure.
2. **OWNER_ADDRESS_OR_DIAGRAM** — 501 groups. Cards express image owner, row,
   class, source/target place, subplace, value, grade and continuation.
3. **SHARED CORE** — the same ordered component recipe is retained. The image
   or text placement supplies the concrete domain expansion.

The 56-component dictionary contains 30 productive contrast cores, ten learned
workshop roots, five address signs and eleven local/renderer signs. Forty-four
components are actually visible in both channels. All 6,513 component atoms
and all 1,384 cards are compositionally bound.

## Phrase and recipe composition

Seventeen invariant prose verbs combine into 22 widely reused two-step
handgrips with 549 occurrences. Twenty-eight three-/four-step fragments have
154 occurrences. No fixed five-step action chain recurs on three pages. The
notation therefore behaves as:

`short root → two-step handgrip → three/four-step fragment → local clause`.

Frequent examples are `CH→O` take then execute, `OK→SH` start then hold,
`OK→CHD` start then transfer, `CHD→OK` transfer then restart, and
`O→CTH` execute until ready.

## Forward predictions

The 14 action roots crossed with four grades and two endpoints yield 112

## Apprentice rule

A scribe first identifies the picture owner and channel, then reads the ordered
component recipe, expands it into workshop or image language and finally
chooses the learned positional allograph. Thirty surfaces already demonstrate
this as paired exercises, including `daiin`, `dal`, `chedy`, `okedy`, `shey`
and `okeedy`.
