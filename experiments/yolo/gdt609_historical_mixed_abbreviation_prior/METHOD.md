# GDT609 method

## Question

Which documented late-medieval notation family supplies the closest mechanism
for GDT608's directed component backoff plus learned pair-specific cards?

## Inputs

The target side is restricted to published GDT607 and GDT608 formal profiles:
entry, closure, connector, boundary/standalone, heterogeneous compound and
qok-counterclass behavior. No proposed word or workshop meaning enters the
comparison.

The historical side contains eleven cited university, peer-reviewed,
manuscript-catalogue and primary-corpus sources. The eight candidate families
are preserved in artifacts/candidate_grid.tsv with source references and an
explicit limitation for every row.

## Comparison

Each family receives an ordinal 0--3 score for:

- productive composition and compatibility with a 34-base model;
- C/d-like entry, y-like closure and o-like connector behavior;
- ol-like boundary/standalone behavior;
- ability to avoid the false inference that every isolated qok-family unit is
  a whole word.

The five observed-form dimensions sum to the published 0--15 form-fit. These
scores select a mechanism for the next decoder; they are not statistical proof
of manuscript identity.

## Selected architecture

The core is common late-medieval abbreviation practice. A small medical recipe
layer supplies the best domain overlay. Flat diplomatic nomenclators retain
value only as output-capacity controls, and Latin-Western alchemical signs are
rejected as a dense text-decoder prior.

The exact next-run limits are:

- 34 primitive roles in the 18/4/3/3/2/2/1/1 allocation;
- all 64 frequent merges compositional by default;
- at most eight paid exact-merge overrides, at most four whole forms;
- no more than one null primitive and 3% null token mass;
- no standalone-to-whole-word shortcut for qok-family units.

## Claim ceiling

This experiment selects a historically plausible, executable architecture. It
does not identify a codebook, language, glyph value, plaintext, substance,
operation, patient, disease or treatment. f84 and f84r are absent.
