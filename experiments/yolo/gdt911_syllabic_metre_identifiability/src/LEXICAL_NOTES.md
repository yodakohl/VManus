# Quantity and meaning of the changed forms

Lewis and Short1879, primary dictionary transcribed by Alatius:
[frigidus](https://alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=frigidus),
[fervidus](https://alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=fervidus).
Headwords explicitly frīgĭdus and fervĭdus, each -a, -um adjective. The former
has cold/cool senses; the latter hot/burning senses and a botanical attestation
in Pliny20.11.44 §113. Fervidus also admits metaphorical senses; we do not claim
a dictionary forces a unique physical reading of every use of virtus.

The feminine nominative singular ending -a is short. Thus frī-gĭ-da is LSS;
fer-vĭ-da is LSS because r+v makes fer heavy. Both modify feminine nominative
virtus. Neither grammar nor these quantities select the cold or hot sense.
The proposed hot sentence is constructed, not historically attested as Macer.

General rules: [Allen and Greenough quantity](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/quantity-syllables),
[hexameter §615](https://www.hhhh.org/perseant/libellus/aides/allgre/allgre.615.html).
The complete unchanged lines use the lexical/morphological analyses listed in
SPEC.json plus position; scans are manual philological inputs audited separately,
not independent lexical evidence inferred from an optimizer. Any unsettled
quantity must cap the claim rather than be repaired after the comparison.
