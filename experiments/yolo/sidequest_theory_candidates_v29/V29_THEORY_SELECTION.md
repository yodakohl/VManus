# V29 selection — whole-card formulary source phrases

Date: 2026-08-22

Status: **creative upstream-language reconstruction, not language identification**.

## New working model

The 17 Herbal/Bio bridge cards are best treated not as necessarily single
source words, but as compressed **formulary phrases**. A plausible upstream
layer is abbreviated technical Latin mixed with vernacular or coded payload:

```text
stable Latin-like instruction formula
  → one learned common-deck card

plant/substance/place-specific payload
  → rarer exemplar card
```

This makes the system much easier for a small workshop. An apprentice learns
seventeen recurrent commands as indivisible cards and copies rare content from
the exemplar; no letter-by-letter cipher is required.

## Example reconstructed source style

The f55v instruction need not have sounded like its visible groups. Its source
could have had the style:

> *Recipe ... secundum mensuram consuetam; misce donec aequaliter
> incorporentur; applica loco in figura signato.*

That is: “Take ... in the usual measure; mix until evenly incorporated; apply
at the place indicated in the picture.” The omitted plant is supplied by the
image and rare cards.

Likewise a Bio cell could expand as:

> *De eadem confectione praeparata, misce duas portiones; dum adhuc tepidum
> est, applica loco in figura signato.*

This is the register-level source style we are now testing—not a claim that a
particular Voynich group spells any Latin sequence.

## Why it improves the theory

- It explains why common cards carry broad multiword instructions.
- It explains why the same card survives Herbal and Bio while rare tails differ.
- It fits the five field templates and typed repetition grammar.
- It is learnable by multiple scribes with different render habits.
- It permits vernacular plant names and local nomenclature in the rare tail.

The strongest rival is still a purely notational technical register with no
natural-language source phrases. V29 does not distinguish the two; it supplies
the most historically natural expansion if ordinary recipe language exists
upstream. No sound, language, Latin plaintext or etymology is claimed. f84 and
f84r remained sealed.
