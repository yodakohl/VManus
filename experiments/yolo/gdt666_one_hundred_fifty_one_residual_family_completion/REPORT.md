## The model has become more predictive, not merely larger

The four independent drafts converge on the same architecture: a compact
workshop register with a command head, a material or preparation, an
operation, a stage or measure, and a limited learned vocabulary. The final
151 cards contain:

- 129 exact role-composed cards made from the final 48 scoped roles;
- 22 learned exact wholes where a productive split would require a new or
  contradictory role;
- one new, tightly bounded learned action block and zero unlicensed spelling
  residue.

Among non-short cards, 122 productive compounds cover 526 positions, eleven
learned action wholes cover fourteen, and eight other learned wholes cover
fifteen. Ten short forms cover another 57 positions and are separately checked
for reader-boundary joins. Three of those short forms (`g`, `chm`, `om`) are
learned exact wholes; the other seven use a scoped role.

That distinction matters. The one new role is `OLY_STRAIN_ACTION=abseihen`.
Naked `oly` occurs 53 times, 36 times line-final; `loly` is line-final in four
of five cases, and `olyly` explicitly repeats the action. This licenses
`chololy = CH_DRY + OL_MATERIAL + OLY_STRAIN_ACTION`, “seihe den getrockneten
Drogenstoff ab,” while `choly` remains the nominal “Trockenrückstand” and
`doly` a learned “Dosis Abguss.” In the other direction, `ykeeochody` and
`chokokor` retain all visible repeated preparation and heat blocks rather than
being hidden as wholes.

These forms occupy recognizably different recipe slots. A `qo-` card commands
an operation or taking; `y-` points back to the preceding material; `qol-`
adds material; `-dy` finishes; `d-` measures; `-d` closes; and `aiin` supplies
the third quantity or stage value. The exact whole always decides the final
German reading, so these roles predict compounds without pretending that one
letter has one universal English word.

## Three corrections that came from full passages

First, free `g` occurs at the end of nine of its eleven lines; both medial
occurrences sit exactly between two work blocks. `dain g` and `daiin g` keep
“ein Gran” as a serious rival, but five other lines already end in a heat,
cool, or closing state and would require a quantity to reach backward over the
process. V43 therefore uses the learned whole “vorstehenden Rezeptposten
abschließen.” The historically plausible `G=gutta`, a drop, remains a weaker
rival. The separately scoped grain siglum stays confined to its three named
compound cards and cannot leak into free or arbitrary `g`-ending forms.

Second, `cthody` is nominal: “fertiger Krautansatz.” It lacks a command frame,
whereas `qody` says “stelle die Zubereitung fertig.” This keeps a result card
distinct from an instruction.

Third, repeated middle-stage markers are no longer collapsed. `checthey`
means “Krautdroge mäßig trocknen, nochmals mäßig trocknen und abschließen”; the
longer `ycheckhey` explicitly repeats that moderate drying around the compound
preparation. Every visible stage atom now contributes.

## Historical fit

The historical comparison does not identify Voynich glyphs with Latin
letters. It supplies a useful format analogue: fifteenth-century medical and
craft recipe books mix short command abbreviations, material names, process
verbs, exact and rough measures, dosage-form names, and closing formulae in
one register. Cappelli likewise records highly context-dependent short signs.
That makes a hybrid of productive abbreviations and learned wholes more
plausible than either a one-symbol substitution table or unrestricted prose.
The linked manuscript and abbreviation sources are recorded in
`src/HISTORICAL_CODEBOOK_ANALOG_MEMO.md`.
