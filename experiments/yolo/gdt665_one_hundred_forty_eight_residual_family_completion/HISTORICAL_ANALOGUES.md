# Historical analogues for the GDT665 mixed register

These analogues justify the *shape* of the working model: productive recipe
elements can coexist with abbreviations and memorised whole entries. They do
not identify any EVA glyph or prove the proposed German meanings.

- Wellcome MS 683 is a mid-fifteenth-century north-east Italian collection of
  Latin medical recipes. Its catalogue describes oils, ointments, powders,
  pills, pessaries, soaking in strong vinegar, equal quantities, and grain
  measures. Ingredient names, operations, preparations and compact quantities
  therefore belong naturally in one period recipe register.
  <https://wellcomecollection.org/works/w6ne7k4t>
- Wellcome MS 5262 is an English medical recipe collection from the first
  quarter of the fifteenth century with 129 recipes in its contemporary
  contents list. It provides a period control for terse, repetitive workshop
  language, not a donor text.
  <https://wellcomecollection.org/works/hkxxeu85>
- A published manuscript table renders Latin *manipulus* as “handful” and
  records `man`, `manp`, `manip`, and rarely `m` as abbreviations. This makes a
  learned handful siglum historically ordinary in type, while leaving EVA `m`
  unassigned historically.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK608570/?report=printable>

The useful comparison is not a one-letter substitution cipher. GDT665 instead
models a compact professional register with four coexisting mechanisms:

1. productive process and material components;
2. positional endings such as a terminal charge/species marker;
3. compact quantity sigla and learned abbreviations;
4. memorised whole commands and preparation names whose spelling resembles
   productive compounds but is not safely decomposed everywhere.

This is why `qol-` may function as a learned “add material” command while
initial O+L elsewhere still builds a wood-drug preparation, and why a terminal
`-s` can mark a charge without erasing internal `s-` seed readings.
