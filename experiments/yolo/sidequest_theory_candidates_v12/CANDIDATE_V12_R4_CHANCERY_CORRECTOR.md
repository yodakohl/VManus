## Verdict first

The exact normalized path

```text
Y -> AIIN -> Y
```

is real at the current exact-card level and is not well dismissed as simple
line filling or a one-off copying slip.  It is the only adjacent exact
three-card path that recurs across two fixed prose pages.  It is also the only
one of eight observed adjacent `A-B-A` paths that recurs.  That is good evidence
for a learned or copied formula.

It is not evidence that the two `Y` cards denote two independently known
things, nor that `AIIN` makes their values equal.  In f10r the formula is the
tail of one long open Herbal field and is immediately preceded by a third
exact `Y`.  In f83r it begins the third field after two committed cells and is
followed by an attached closer.  The two settings therefore share the formula
but not a demonstrated antecedent, operand pair, field coordinate, closure or
downstream consequence.

The strongest positive reading is `SHARED_ACTIVE_REFERENCE`: an outer node on
each side of `AIIN` may invoke one currently active standard.  The strongest
period-plausible counterreading is a fixed exemplar formula copied as a unit,
compatible with `ORDINARY_FORMULAIC_PROSE` or an anonymous checklist frame.
Those readings make the same prediction for both observed strings.  With only
these two occurrences, the correct forced choice is therefore
`NOT_DISTINGUISHABLE_WITH_TWO_OCCURRENCES`.

`PAIRED_EQUAL_VALUE` does not pass the protocol's special gate: neither record
supplies two independently supported symmetric operands.

## Correction and copying audit

### Dittography

A direct word-for-word dittography is a poor joint explanation.  The repeated
outer cards are separated by `AIIN`, and the same normalized sandwich appears
on two different pages.  In f10r only, however, the larger sequence is
`Y-Y-AIIN-Y`; the first two `Y`s form one of only five immediate exact-card
duplicates in the 381-event panel.  A copying eye could have resumed on the
wrong similar item there.  That local possibility makes f10r weaker evidence
for a two-operand construction, even though it does not explain f83r.

### Homoeoteleuton and resumption

Two equal outer signs can delimit an omission rather than assert equality.  A
scribe returning to the second occurrence of the same exemplar card could
drop intervening material; alternatively the second sign could be a deliberate
Wiederaufnahme.  There is no external exemplar, cancelled text or surviving
parallel from which to decide.  This is a specifically scribal reason not to
turn `A-B-A` into “A and A receive the same value.”

### Exemplar formula

The strongest null is not random error but faithful copying.  A model sheet,
register heading or commonly memorized formula can preserve
`Y-AIIN-Y` across an Herbal open line and a Biological committed cell while
letting the surrounding stencil differ.  Such copying explains both the
rarity and the recurrence without assigning a referent.  It maps onto either
`ORDINARY_FORMULAIC_PROSE` or `INDEXED_CHECKLIST_FRAME`, depending on the
unknown upstream document.

### Line filling and cadence

Line filling can explain the f10r placement: the formula occupies the last
three groups.  It cannot explain f83r, where the formula is medial and another
field follows.  A learned cadence copied in more than one position remains
possible, but “the scribe merely filled both line ends” is rejected.

### Segmentation and renderer

All three target groups are separately delimited in both source lines.  No
encoded uncertainty or correction marker occurs inside either triple.  The
f10r drawing interruption is immediately before, not inside, the target.
Thus a false split or join is not the leading explanation.

The cross-page identity does, however, exist only after wrapper normalization:

```text
f10r: chy  taiin shy    -> ch-Y  t-AIIN sh-Y
f83r: chey daiin chey  -> che-Y d-AIIN che-Y
```

That is compatible with the established renderer model, but it means the
visible phrase was not copied letter for letter.  A renderer artifact alone
is too strong—the group boundaries and exact hosts are stable—while a copied
formula realized by local wrapper habit remains entirely viable.  Image-level
erasure, expunction and overwriting were not audited here; absence of a
transcription marker is not proof that none exists on the parchment.

### Apprentice errors to look for

A trainee copying from the proposed card shelf could make four diagnostic
errors: double an outer card, skip between two identical outer cards, choose a
wrong local wrapper while preserving the host, or copy the formula into the
wrong field before adding the register's normal closer.  The f10r extra `Y`
fits the first possibility; the wrapper diversity fits ordinary realization
variation; no fixed-page evidence identifies an omission or wrong-field
correction.  None of these errors requires a modern cryptanalytic role.

## Candidate decision

| frozen architecture | assessment |
|---|---|
| `SHARED_ACTIVE_REFERENCE` | strongest pro-reference case; repeated exact formula and mobile AIIN fit reuse of a current standard, but neither record owns the antecedent |
| `PAIRED_EQUAL_VALUE` | rejected as winner; symmetric operands and equality-sensitive consequences are absent |
| `DYADIC_RELATION_FRAME` | possible formal description, but outer repetition alone does not establish two endpoints |
| `INDEXED_CHECKLIST_FRAME` | viable exemplar/null account, weakened because target field position and closure differ |
| `ORDINARY_FORMULAIC_PROSE` | viable, especially for Herbal; cannot be separated from copied notation by these strings |
| `RENDERER_OR_SEGMENTATION_ARTIFACT` | segmentation-only account is weak; renderer normalization contributes but does not manufacture the definite three-group sequences |
| `NOT_DISTINGUISHABLE_WITH_TWO_OCCURRENCES` | **winner**; all semantic alternatives remain observationally equivalent after the formal recurrence is admitted |

The win is not a claim that the formula is meaningless.  It separates the
supported statement—“the same normalized formula was used twice”—from the
unsupported statement—“the scribe asserted reference or equality.”

## Teachable forward rule

```text
IF the chosen exemplar or local stencil calls for FORMULA-1:
    copy three separate cards in the exact order Y, AIIN, Y;
    realize each card with the wrapper licensed by hand/register/local place;
    preserve any preceding repeated Y or drawing break from the exemplar;
    add a close only if the local field stencil calls for one.
```

This rule is simple enough for several hands.  It reproduces both targets
without requiring the apprentice to know whether the source function is
reference, equality, relation, index or wording.

```text
  => outer exact identities are equal
  => middle exact identity is AIIN

  => possible {shared reference, dyadic frame, indexed formula,

```

For f10r the reverse annotation must also retain `preceded by another Y` and
`open line tail`.  For f83r it must retain `field-initial after two committed
fields` and `followed by LCHE-CLOSE`.  Erasing those differences would create
the very equality claim the evidence does not supply.

## Fixed-page falsifiers

1. **Shared reference:** identify, without glyph similarity or imported image
   meaning, the same exact antecedent and a common downstream consequence in
   both complete records.  Failure leaves reference unowned.
2. **Equal value:** independently establish two symmetric operands in both
   records and show a consequence that differs when their values differ.
   Repeated `Y` alone can never satisfy this test.
3. **Indexed checklist:** show that the path occupies one invariant field or
   stencil coordinate in matched records.  The present tail/head and open/
   committed split already counts against a rigid coordinate.
4. **Dittography or eye skip:** find encoded or manual evidence of expunction,
   overwrite, abnormal spacing, omitted exemplar matter, or an otherwise
   matched fixed-page passage with the accidental copy removed.
5. **Renderer/segmentation artifact:** an alternate source reading that fuses,
   splits or removes one of the three definite groups would reopen this
   account.  Wrapper variation by itself does not.
6. **Ordinary formulaic prose:** a source-independent syntactic or readable
   parallel must predict the whole exact triple before inspecting these
   strings.  Surface or phonetic resemblance is excluded.
7. **Copied exemplar formula:** a matched fixed-page stencil that predicts the
   triple but allows different wrappers and closures would favor this account;
   a demonstrated operand-sensitive alternation would falsify it.

Until one of these observations is obtained, the Kanzleischreiber records the
formula in the register, marks its expansion *non liquet*, and does not turn a
clean repetition into a translation.
