## What the candidate actually buys

`qol = daraus?` means from that material. It contributes a source reference,
not the material name, result, causal reason or verb. Keep chedy = becomes.

1. **f75r.32–46.** IT .33 reads `qokeey.lshedy.qol.chedy.qokain...`:
   `Feuer? [lshedy] daraus? wird? Wasser? ...`. A reading “from fire comes
   water” is now expressible, but identifying fire as the referent is an added
   attachment guess. Water is already in .32 and recurs in .35/.40/.43.
   Those recurrences do not tell us whether the source is fire, earlier water,
   or an unknown intervening material. ZL .33 has `qo,l`, NOT whole qol;
   RF has qol but opaque `ched@222;`, not the exact becomes whole. Thus the
   entire short source–becomes–water sequence is exact only in IT here.
   The later qokeedy/qokedy AABBA .38 and dar repetitions remain unexplained.

2. **f81r.16–31.** All three .20 have
   `[qopchedy] daraus? wird? Feuer? [odaiin] [rain] [daly/@152;aly]`.
   None of the current concrete material names occurs earlier in this P.
   Do not invent water as its input. qopchedy could name a source but is
   untranslated; .19's conditional `[qokedy] wenn? wird? [qoteedy] [olam]`
   also has unresolved syntax. Crucially .22 reverses the local order:
   `[qotal] wird? daraus? [ol] viel? [ol] [chedar] [ol] [oly]`.
   A material-source expression can be displayed here without changing its
   sense; this does not establish the manuscript's word order. qol at .25
   and .26 also lacks an identified referent. No fire-to-water cycle is read
   in this P, and nothing licenses translating qokedy as water by proximity.

3. **f82r.20–33.** All three .21 have
   `viel? [cheoky] [lkedy] [salshey/rolshey/solshey] viel? [chey]
   daraus? wird? Feuer? [dal]`.
   Here too neither preceding .20 nor the left part of .21 supplies a decoded
   material identity. `.22` has two air guesses before qol in ZL/IT, with
   unknown words between them; RF joins `qolkeeedy`. Air is a possible source
   candidate only under an added attachment, not a recovered one. The fire
   repetitions and both raiin readings in .24 are unchanged. Water in .31
   (ZL/IT; RF qokeiin) is later, not automatic retrospective proof of a cycle.

## An additional short content contact, not another flexible gloss

All three f77r.33 begin `daiin.chedy.qol`:

```text
viel? wird? daraus? [keedy/kee@152;y] [qoteeedy] [sar] ...
```

“Viel wird daraus?” is intelligible with the same material-source sense and
the inherited quantity word. It does not require “therefore,” “then,” or an
add instruction. But the rest of the line is unknown, and several possible
materials precede it in the P. This is conditional local compatibility, not
a completed clause or identification of the source. In IT .34 the same new
word also yields `... wird? daraus? wird? Luft? Luft? [checkhy] steigt?/leicht?`.
Both becomes and both air tokens remain. No automatic punctuation fixes it.

## Decision and next useful distinction

Retain **one C0 trial**, not a dictionary promotion. No inspected context with
known enough semantics clearly makes material-source reference impossible;
most remain too unknown to support or refute it. Do not call this 37 successes.
The attractive examples were partly chosen from the inherited fire trial, so
their coherence is not independent evidence for the whole system. `qol` could
still be a different kind of word, and the material-change model could fail.

The concrete gain is that one short meaning spans `daraus wird Feuer`,
`daraus wird Wasser`, and `viel wird daraus` without adding a new action or
altering fire/water/becomes. The concrete debt is referent recovery, not merely
better German prose. Repeated qol does not solve that debt.

A sharply bounded next question is whether **whole qolchedy** can represent
the same source–becomes construction, under one explicit composition proposal.
IT's split versus ZL's joined f77r.34 gives a written boundary bridge; the
all-three joined forms before water in f81v.17/f82r.2 offer other contexts.
That is a proposal to investigate, NOT a current split or gloss. Do not extend
it to qolchey/qolsheedy/qolky, and do not treat spelling similarity as evidence
of compositional meaning. Run the duplicate route screen before launching it.
