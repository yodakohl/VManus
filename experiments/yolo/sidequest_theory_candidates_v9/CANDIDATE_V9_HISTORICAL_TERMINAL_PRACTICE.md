# V9 historical terminal practice: recurrent committed values

Date: 2026-08-21

Status: independent speculative sidequest report, not a GDT result and not a
translation.

## Scope and evidence discipline

This pass uses only the fixed ten-page sidequest:

- Herbal: `f10r`, `f11r`, `f55v`, `f56r`;
- Biological: `f81v`, `f82r`, `f83r`;
- circle/astronomical: `f67r2`, `f68r1`, `f69v`.

`f84` and `f84r` remained sealed. The seven prose pages were inspected only
through `./vmanus-exp query-tsv` with an explicit page allow-list, selected
columns, and `--forbid-prefix f84`. The circle pages have no GDT327 events, so
they are discussed only at the already frozen level of topology and surface
layout. No candidate archive was consulted.

The question is narrow: what late-medieval writing practice could produce many
fields whose last visible card is both an exact payload identity and a local
commit, including many fields consisting of that card alone?

All 38 occurrences carry attached DY closure. The labels are deliberately not

source-language word. `VAL-A` is the exact family whose surface realization is
represented by `shedy` in the current compact theory; that spelling is not an
expansion.

Three facts constrain the historical comparison.

1. Sixteen of the 38 occurrences are complete one-card fields. A model that
   requires an overt noun plus a following verbal formula must explain why the
   noun or verb can stand alone so often.
2. The families are page-selective. `VAL-B` and `VAL-C` never occur on f81v;
   `VAL-D` never occurs on f82r. `VAL-A` alone spans all three Biological pages.
3. The same exact family can close a multi-card field or occupy a one-card
   field. That is expected of an answer supplied to a known slot; it is less
   natural for an ordinary lexical ending whose host has disappeared.

## Historical mechanism comparison

### 1. Abbreviated lexical endings, ingredients, measures, or products

This mechanism is historically real. Late-medieval medical writing used a
large inherited abbreviation repertoire, including suspensions, contractions,
superscript letters, brevigraphs, and signs specific to medical practice.
De la Cruz-Cabanillas and Diego-Rodríguez's manuscript survey explicitly lists
special signs for *recipe*, *ana*, handful, drachm, ounce, pound, scruple,
*semis*, and half-pound; it also shows that the same final brevigraph can need
different expansions according to the local lexical item
([article, pp. 166-181, especially Table 2 on p. 179](https://reunido.uniovi.es/index.php/SELIM/article/download/13301/12036/28090)).
The same study's corpus crosses ophthalmology, uroscopy, gynaecology, herbaries,
antidotaries, recipe collections, astrology, and a small alchemical extract.

There is equally good manuscript-ecology support. BL Harley MS 2381 (mid
fifteenth century) combines roughly 600 recipes with an antidotary, medicinal
waters, bloodletting, weights and measures, and astrological tables; its
catalogue even records a chapter list with alphabetic running titles
([British Library catalogue, especially ff. 31-63 and 47-115v](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002048212)).
Utrecht University Library MS 1355, made around 1400 as an 11 x 9 cm medical
vademecum, combines recipes, a herbal, regimen, monthly prescriptions, several
lunaria, bloodletting, and prognostics
([Utrecht Special Collections description](https://www.uu.nl/en/special-collections/collections/manuscripts/medieval-medical/medical-prescriptions)).

What it explains: compact whole-card identities, a learned common deck, rare
local technical cards, and mixed natural language plus notation.

What it does not explain well: sixteen complete one-card fields and four
families that recur as interchangeable-looking field answers. Ordinary
ingredient or product abbreviations should normally retain more lexical
companions, while pure measure signs should be accompanied by numbers or
measured substances. This remains a plausible source layer, but is not the best
specific account of the 12/10/8/8 terminals.

### 2. Table, checklist, or keyed categorical values

This is the best mechanism for the Biological terminals. Late-medieval
practical books regularly compressed a known question or axis into a small
entry cell. The value did not need to restate the heading, body part, month,
planet, or operation because the row, column, diagram, or repeated rubric
supplied it.

The closest secure analogies are calendrical rather than modern checkboxes.
Salzburg, St Peter a.VI.17 (fifteenth century) places John of Gmunden's calendar
beside a table of Golden Numbers and dominical letters, monthly rules,
bloodletting texts, and a plague tract
([vHMML catalogue, items 1-6](https://www.vhmml.org/readingRoom/view/10506)).
In such systems a deliberately small recurrent deck is normal: seven dominical
letters classify dates, while nineteen Golden Numbers locate years in the
lunar cycle. A compact value is meaningful because its axis is supplied by the
table.

Medical lists show the same ellipsis in prose form. A late-medieval uroscopy
tract is described as a user-facing, quasi-bulleted sequence with alternating
rubrics and a repeated frame roughly `urine as [colour] -> [diagnosis]`; its
abbreviated notes were designed for consultation
([QMUL digital-edition description](https://digitalmanuscripts.history.qmul.ac.uk/editions/miscellaneous/urinarium-colores-urinarum-database-by-jessica-louise-reeves/)).
That witness is later fifteenth century, so it is used only to show the mature
form of an older diagnostic-list practice, not to date the Voynich pages.

Bookkeeping supplies an adversarial control. Borromei ledgers of the 1430s use
fixed *dare* and *avere* sides, repeated monetary columns, cross-entries, and
slashes to mark balanced accounts
([QMUL Borromei Bank Research](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/borromei-bank-research/project-background/medieval-accounting/)).
The Cantelowe accounts of 1450-51 use four languages, extensive Arabic and
fractional numerals, and merchant marks identifying wool sacks
([Tiddeman edition and study](https://academic.oup.com/british-academy-scholarship-online/book/46163)).
These comparators show that a small nonverbal value inventory can coexist with
abbreviated prose. They also show why a literal financial-ledger reading is
weak here: the Biological pages lack an established debit/credit opposition,
currency axis, arithmetic balance, or crossed counterpart.

Fit to the guarded evidence: high. A categorical value can be a complete cell
or the last element of a qualified cell; the same value can recur across
different stencils; page-local availability is expected; and one common value
can span all three pages while alternatives remain restricted.

### 3. Recipe stage, product, or result formula

Medieval recipes are a strongly standardized procedural genre. Remedy books
commonly organize entries by ailment, begin the ingredient section with a
formula equivalent to “Take ...,” and may end with an efficacy phrase such as
*probatum est*
([survey prefacing the edition of Wellcome MS 542](https://www.peterlang.com/document/1052987)).
Their recurrent functional stages can be summarized as heading/purpose,
ingredients, preparation, application, and efficacy. The medical-abbreviation
survey also notes a recurrent abbreviated *sanabitur* efficacy formula at
recipe ends.

What it explains: committed cards at field ends could abbreviate `make [a]
syrup/ointment/plaster`, `let it stand`, `strain it`, `it will be healed`, or
`it is proven`. Different exact terminal identities could carry different
products or results while sharing one closure realization.

What it does not explain well: the Biological page has many short cells inside
larger records, not one closure only at the end of each remedy. `VAL-B` and
`VAL-C` are one-card fields in half or more of their occurrences. Reading each
as a complete efficacy clause would make a single record assert “proven/healed”
repeatedly. A better retained version is weaker: a terminal value may name a
stage or product selected by a form slot, but the four families cannot yet be
assigned four particular processes.

### 4. Diagram or route-station codes

Late-medieval astronomical and medical-astrological instruments establish the
general mechanism. Michael of Rhodes's working manuscript gives a Zodiac Man
for bloodletting and then a table for tracking the Moon through the zodiac;
the medical decision depends on the sign occupied by the Moon
([Michael of Rhodes Project, p. 103b](https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/michaelofrhodes/manuscript/page_103b.html)).
Penn LJS 361 contains tables for weekdays, Sunday letters, lunar conjunctions,
planetary hours, lunar effects on activities, body parts governed by planets or
zodiac signs, and appropriate medical activities
([Schoenberg Institute catalogue/facsimile record](https://repository.upenn.edu/handle/20.500.14332/46339)).

What it explains: a recurrent compact card can identify a station, branch,
phase, house, sign, or route outcome without being a spoken word. It is an
excellent general comparator for the 7-, 12-, 19-, and 28-part astronomical
ecology.

What it does not explain for the four terminal families: all 38 are on the
three Biological prose pages, and no independent geometric station is aligned
to their exact identities. Thus route/station codes remain a strong model for
the circle pages but a weaker direct account of the 12/10/8/8 deck.

## Selection

The best historical mechanism is **a keyed categorical-value practice embedded
in abbreviated technical prose**.

In compact form:

```text
PICTURE / repeated stencil supplies the question and most arguments
  + optional qualifier cards
  + one exact value card
  + attached local commit
```

This does not require a modern printed form or a binary checklist. Its closer
historical analogues are calendrical letter/number tables, uroscopic
colour-to-diagnosis lists, rubric-driven remedy books, and working ledgers in
which location supplies part of an entry's meaning. The model is compatible
with abbreviated natural language upstream; it only claims that the terminal
deck is functioning as a set of values at this local layer.

Provisional functional hierarchy, with no English dictionary assignment:

- `VAL-A`: the portable/default member of the deck, because it is the only one
  present on all three Biological pages and most often completes a qualified
  field (9/12).
- `VAL-B`: a f82r/f83r alternative, evenly divided between isolated and
  qualified cells (5/5); its two appearances in separate one-card fields on
  f82r.27 are especially value-like.
- `VAL-C`: the strongest standalone-answer candidate (5/8 one-card fields),
  heavily concentrated on f83r.
- `VAL-D`: an f81v/f83r alternative (3/8 one-card fields), perhaps belonging
  to a route or subtemplate absent on f82r.

“Default,” “alternative,” and “standalone” describe distribution only. They do
not establish ordinal rank, polarity, substance, degree, body part, apparatus,
or operation.

## Fixed-page pseudo-translations

Notation:

- `[opaque ...]` is untranslated exact-card material;
- `=> VAL-X [commit]` is a qualified field whose final exact card is one of
  the four families;
- `[VAL-X; commit]` is a one-card field;
- no implied language, word order, or spoken expansion is asserted.

### Circle pages

No member of the four exact terminal families is available for these pages.
The historical comparison licenses only a separate structural paraphrase:

```text
f67r2  LOOKUP INSTRUMENT: choose within distinct 7-part, 12-part, and central decks.
f68r1  SPATIAL CATALOGUE: central label plus 28 individually placed outer labels.
f69v   ORDERED SCHEDULE: 28 alternating long/short station entries.
```

Dominical letters, Golden Numbers, zodiac signs, lunar stations, and medical
elections show that such compact lookup practices existed. None is an
identified donor, and no label on these three pages is assigned one of those
meanings.

## Discriminating predictions

The selected mechanism makes stronger predictions than a generic “technical
text” analogy.

1. In additional unsealed Biological material, the four values should remain
   enriched at field closure and in one-card fields, not diffuse through long
   open prose like ordinary nouns.
2. Repeated field-shape stencils should predict which subset of the value deck
   is licensed better than neighboring lexical cards do.
3. `VAL-A` should remain the most portable member; `VAL-B/C/D` should show
   stable page-, diagram-, or subtemplate-conditioned availability.
4. If the families are recipe products or efficacy formulae instead, they
   should correlate with larger record endings and preceding preparation
   sequences, not merely local field endings. The present page ecology favors
   the latter.
5. If they are route stations, independently annotated geometry should predict
   their identity or order. No such alignment currently exists.
6. If they are measures, adjacent overt numeral-like or ingredient-like cards
   should form a recurrent pairing. One-card fields are adverse evidence unless
   the quantity axis is independently supplied by the stencil.

## Bottom line

The historically safest expansion of the 12/10/8/8 terminal families is not
four drug names or four verbal endings. It is:

```text
exact categorical value selected for an inherited practical slot, then committed
```

The four values may ultimately denote operations, routes, states, products,
degrees, or answers. The current data distinguish their distribution but not
their English content. Historical evidence supports the writing practice; it
does not identify the Voynich language or translate a card.
