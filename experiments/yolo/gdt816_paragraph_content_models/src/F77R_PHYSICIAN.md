later its vapour is described as warm. The intervening text may describe
their properties or uses, but those uses are not translated. On f75r a source
itself is warm. A source's warmth is a normal metonymic way to characterize
its water, but that is a stated semantic cost, not another word sense.

## Historical bridge: a real discourse pattern, not a word key

The Hildegardian *Physica* river descriptions distinguish a named watercourse,
its water, its sand and uses of that water. The Main and Danube entries use
the explicit possessive expressions “aqua eius” and “arena eius”. This is a
concrete parallel for a named entry followed by different possessed heads;
it neither identifies f77r as river text nor establishes a Voynich pronoun.
The consulted source is a modern edition transcription with editorial
brackets, not a collated circa-1420 manuscript.
[Primary text, river entries](https://monumenta.ch/latein/text.php?domain=&hide_apparatus=&inframe=1&lang=1&level=&links=1&rumpfid=Hildegardis+Abbatissa%2C+Physica&tabelle=Hildegardis_Abbatissa).

Magninus's *Regimen sanitatis*, chapter 4, distinguishes a bath's air and
water and assigns different bodily effects to each; it also separates bath
from stupha. Thus an associated material is not the same thing as its
therapeutic effect. Importantly, its discussion separates actually warm water
from natural coldness and subsequent cooling of the body. Our Q must not
change from temperature to effect merely to fit the context. CELT dates the
work to 1329–1334, but its consulted Latin edition rests on circa-1500 and
1585 prints; the separately listed 1469/1486 Irish manuscripts do not date
these Latin words. [Text and edition metadata, pp.168–173](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/L600009B.html).

Neither source gives the precise source/water/vapour triplet as our Voynich
passage, much less its word order. The historical bridge makes both property
description and possession realistic competitors. It does not choose the
three atoms. A source-and-medium account is worth carrying to other admitted
contexts, but solkeey=Dampf should be the first noun challenged, not defended
with synonyms or an invisible process. Q has simpler local attachment at
f75r.45; R has an explicit but unproved whole-entry relationship on f77r.
