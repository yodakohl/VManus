## Historical source and reading

Fribourg, Bibliotheque cantonale et universitaire, MS L 52, f. 8v,
main-text lines 18-19, counted here from the first written main-text line.
Line numbering is the present reader's coordinate, not an edition's numbering.

Normalized Latin, with abbreviations expanded and editorial punctuation:

> Hoc unguentum conficitur ex oleo / et balsamo. Oleum lucet, balsamum redolet.

The slash marks the physical line break. Translation: "This ointment is made
from oil and balsam. Oil shines; balsam gives off fragrance." The spelling
*unguentum* is normalized; this is not a character-for-character diplomatic
transcription. The abbreviated ending of *lucet* is a palaeographic feature,
not evidence that the word or sentence occurs in Voynich.

The phrase was read directly in the [official f. 8v facsimile](https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/en/bcuf/L0052/8v)
and its [official IIIF detail](https://www.e-codices.ch/loris/bcuf/bcuf-L0052/bcuf-L0052_008v.jp2/pct:20,41,70,25/1400,/0/default.jpg).
This is native AI visual assessment, not independent human annotation or a
critical edition. No OCR or automatic transcription was used. No source image
is redistributed in this follow-up.

[Romain Jurot's catalogue](https://e-codices.unifr.ch/en/description/bcuf/L0052/Jurot),
*Catalogue des manuscrits medievaux de la Bibliotheque cantonale et
universitaire de Fribourg* (Dietikon-Zurich, 2006), pp. 108-116, dates the
manuscript to the beginning of the fifteenth century and locates it in
southern Germany, possibly Zurich. These are copy-date/place assessments,
not a precise composition date. The catalogue identifies ff. 8r-9r as an
Easter sermon by Guibert of Tournai, Schneyer II, p. 285, no. 36, with a
different ending; that attribution was not independently reconstructed here.

The visible surrounding passage invokes David's anointing and develops the
oil/fragrance contrast into moral qualities of conscience and reputation.
Thus the sentence belongs to homiletic discussion of anointing, not an
identified pharmaceutical instruction. The catalogue also reports contemporary
German glosses elsewhere in L 52; these do not make the quoted sentence a
Latin/German equivalence or connect either script to Voynich.
