# Complete f21r and f32v image/text review

Root personally viewed the two official images recorded in IMAGE_SOURCES.tsv
after the four-page admission file was created, and read all55 admitted loci
with all three alternative strings. The extra visual admissions do not make
the already known f21r/f32v text an unseen comparison.

On f21r the three source paragraphs form a text block above a widely branched
plant drawing. Numerous small green leaf-like forms and pale brown clustered
structures alternate along its branches. There is no local inscription naming
a particular depicted part, no written arrow joining a prose token to one,
and no depicted drying or storage procedure.

On f32v two paragraphs wrap around a tall plant. Three large dark-blue,
flower-like heads sit above lobed green leaves and an elongated root/rhizome
drawing. This is not independent identification of a rose or of the exact
number represented by daiin. Repeated daiin elsewhere on the page cannot be
explained merely by counting those three heads. The flowers' relation to the
text is a page-level context, not a gloss for every nearby word.

Both exact chor chol daiin spans remain in the full source: f21r.12 within
the third paragraph, f32v.10 within the second. The first is followed by
chkaiin; the second by cpho l cthol da ar in ZL3b (cphol in the alternatives).
Neither span is a standalone caption. There is no independently read year,
storage predicate, degree label or named substance to select the historical
keeping-time interpretation over the existing degree/amount rivals.

The new images therefore neither identify the rose comparator nor refute
generic storage language for other plants. They leave the word meanings open
and make a direct copy of a particular historical rose entry unsupported.
Do not convert that lack of identification into a universal botanical
rejection or remove the exploratory rival.
