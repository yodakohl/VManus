# GDT812 follow-up: drug identity is not therapeutic effect

Post-result exploration, 2026-09-05; no new experiment or page admission.
This develops the existing dchdy alternatives, without scoring a new relation.

## Concrete historical contrast

In Tadhg O Cuinn's 1415 *Materia Medica*, chapter144 (Galitricum/clary)
assigns hot/dry qualities and describes a lower-body bath promoting urine and
menstruation. The same herb occurs in an eye preparation and a compound
against liver/lung heat. Chapter150 (Genestula/broom) instead assigns cold/dry,
styptic qualities; bathing fumes are used to arrest menstrual flow.
These are reports of historical practice, not present-day medical advice.

Locations: Irish pp.175/179, English pp.552/556 in the
[edited primary text](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600006.html).
The separately assembled Latin comparanda are not a diplomatic1415 Latin
witness. No clary or broom identification of a Voynich drawing follows.

Thus the historical architecture separates a persistent drug name, its
qualities, its preparation and its effect. Similar delivery can have opposing
effects. A drawing of a bath cannot by itself choose a flow predicate.

An independently edited comparator makes the fields explicit: Latin9333,
f34v, separates Saluia from Complexio, Electio and Iuuamentum. Its constitution
is hot in the first degree and dry in the second. The
[MediManus edition](https://medimanus.chs.harvard.edu/app/en/pharmantica/tacuinum_sanitatis)
dates this copy to1445-1450 or earlier, possibly Swabia; it is not a1420
dated witness. This supports field separation, not a Voynich field assignment.

Keep two distinct interpretations available:

- **Named substance:** the same referent can survive different preparations
  and effects. The missing connection is a written reference from that
  particular substance to a prose predication.
- **Property/effect:** different substances could share the inscription, but
  the meaning must remain fixed. Dry, diuretic and arresting menstrual flow
  cannot be substituted for each other to fit successive pictures.

Neither interpretation currently wins. The historical contrast narrows what
an explanation must do; it does not confirm a word. Dictionary unchanged.
