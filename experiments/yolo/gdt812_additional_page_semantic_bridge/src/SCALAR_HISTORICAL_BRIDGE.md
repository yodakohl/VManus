# GDT812: historical scalar-expression bridge

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: source-only construction evidence, C0.
Scope: historical comparators for the fixed nonnumerical daiin trial; no new
Voynich image/text admission, dictionary entry, numerical ranking, or codebook.

## Result and boundaries

Medical texts attest quantity/intensity uses of the same written Latin form,
qualitative intensity alongside an explicit numbered quality degree, and four
ordinal quality grades defined by effects. These are distinct observations.
They do not establish four interchangeable intensity adverbs, an abbreviation
key for dan/dain/daiin/daiiin, adjacent intensifier doubling, or valde = III.

## 1. Quantity and intensity: one dated manuscript, differing constructions

Witness: Paris, BnF, latin 9333, Tacuinum sanitatis, catalogued 1445-1450 or
earlier, possibly Swabian; Latin translation of Ibn Butlan, with German text.
Source type: MediManus manuscript transcription and witness description.
[Transcription and dating](https://medimanus.chs.harvard.edu/app/en/pharmantica/tacuinum_sanitatis).

- f071v, calf meat: `nutrimentum multum` (much nourishment; noun then modifier).
- f077v, testicles: `nutriunt multum` (nourish much; verb then adverb).
- f097r, sleep: `si fuerit multum longus` (if it is very long; adverb before adjective).

The related high-magnitude senses are historically real. But the first multum
agrees with a neuter noun; the others are adverbial. This pair does not prove
an invariant uninflected postfix for arbitrary nouns or a constant word order.
It supplies no Latin identification of a Voynich word, plant, or pictured part.

## 2. Four quality grades are not ingredient quantities

Witnesses: BL Add. 12190 and Sloane 351, both broadly fifteenth-century, not
securely restricted to 1350-1450. Source type: critical edition by Virginia de
Frutos Gonzalez, "Edicion critica del Regimen sanitatis salernitanum transmitido
por los manuscritos Add. 12190 y Sloane 351 de la British Library de Londres,"
Minerva 23 (2010), 143-195; manuscript dating in introduction, text at p.163.
[Edition PDF](https://uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/handle/10324/10581/Minerva-2010-23-regimen-sanitatis-safernitanum.pdf?sequence=1).

Verses 1090-1093 define first through fourth medicinal grades through their
effects: sense predominates in the first, nature enjoys an equal balance in
the second, the third exceeds it with injury, and the fourth destroys sense.
This is an ordinal, effect-defined scale, not an attested slightly/moderately/
very/extremely vocabulary. The following dosage section states at v.1095:

> Si fortis, paucum; si debilis, accipe multum.

Reported historical schema only: stronger medicine receives a smaller amount,
weaker medicine a larger amount. Strength and amount are separate variables;
this is not present-day medical advice. A separate weights section follows;
the editor notes that Sloane 351 supplies graphic weight signs there. Those
signs are not evidence for a four-form intensity abbreviation system.

## 3. Prose confirmation: grade, internal position, and quantity distinguished

Work: Petrus de Sancto Floro's fourteenth-century revision of the Concordanciae.
Witness used by the editor: BnF latin 14734, a fifteenth-century manuscript.
Do not conflate the work's century with the surviving witness's date.
Source type: Julius Leopold Pagel, Neue litterarische Beitrage zur
mittelalterlichen Medicin (Berlin, 1896), edited Latin text, pp.56-57.
[Primary-text edition](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Neue_litterarische_Beitr%C3%A4ge_zur_mittelalterlichen_Medicin_%28IA_39002086313427.med.yale.edu%29.pdf).
[Witness catalogue](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/jordanus/ms/5056).

The prose defines four quality grades by effects on sense/body and divides
each into three internal positions: beginning, middle, end. It then explicitly
leaves whether quantity increases grade for further investigation. Printed
ordinal abbreviations in Pagel are not independently checked manuscript glyphs.
Neither the four grades nor their three subdivisions supplies an EVA-minim key.

## 4. Explicit intensity plus degree for the same qualities

Source type: older medieval Macer verse, reproduced in the Latin comparanda
of An Irish Materia Medica, section 55, p.323, Acidula/Semperviva passage.
[CELT edited source](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600006.html).

> Virtus est illi siccans et frigida valde
> Tertius a medicis datus est gradus huic in utroque.

The same subject is drying and very cold, then assigned third degree in both.
This establishes coexistence, not universal equivalence of intensity and III.
These Latin comparanda are not a diplomatic reading of the dated Irish witness.
This is a different passage from the already-recorded Macer Satureia example.

## Consequence for the trial

A fixed high-magnitude reading abandons exact cardinal/ordinal III; it is not
a rediscovery of GDT686's context-selected degree/amount dispatch. The written
four-member family nevertheless remains unexplained, not permission to assign
four intensities retrospectively. Complete-context grammar, repetition, scope,
and transfer must decide between readings. Historical plausibility alone is
insufficient for a word meaning, named substance, object owner, or translation.
