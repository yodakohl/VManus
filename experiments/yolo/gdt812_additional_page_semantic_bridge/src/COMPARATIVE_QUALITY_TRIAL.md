# Comparative medicinal qualities: an invented reading trial

This memo records a concrete trial, not a decoder, a translation, a selected
dictionary, or a new quality discovery. It was formed from the complete GDT811
four-page reader and GDT809 joint reader before the four GDT812 page payloads
were opened. The repeated source forms were already inspected; this is not an
unseen prediction or a blinded test.

## One subject-matter model

Try f88r as a comparative register of medicinal simples: named substances,
their temperaments, then their usable forms or applications. The tempting
alternative is three compound prescriptions, with the vessel-like drawings
standing for preparations. Neither the drawings nor the current text establish
that grouping. A comparative register has no need to turn repeated qualities
into repeated manufacturing actions, but still needs independently identified
subjects.

These exact-whole meanings are deliberately invented trial assignments:

| Exact whole | Trial meaning |
|---|---|
| qokol | hot, as a medicinal quality |
| chol | dry, as a medicinal quality |
| shol | moist, as a medicinal quality |
| otchol | cold, as a medicinal quality |

The old defaults are not evidence for these meanings. No component rule
follows: okol, okoldy, chokol and qoekol remain untranslated. Hot and dry are
properties in this model, not instructions to heat and dry. Other concrete
property assignments could fit the same written repetitions.

The attractive conjecture at .27 is:

```text
substance A: hot, dry / substance B: hot / substance C: hot, dry
```

It preserves all five source tokens, including adjacent qokol qokol. A teacher
comparing different drugs could repeat the same quality. But the two slashes,
three subject assignments and correspondence to the three inscriptions are
invented. No author-visible boundaries or owners establish those groupings.
The two further qokol occurrences at .29 still have no identified subjects.
The prose ofal at .30 remains separate from the inscription ofaldo; spelling
resemblance cannot silently provide the missing reference or a do suffix.

## Cross-passage pressure and genre rivals

Without changing any assignment, the existing reader gives:

```text
f17r.4    tcho shol qokol ...             -> [tcho] moist hot ...
f17r.2-3  otchol qodcthy ods / chol ...   -> cold [qodcthy ods] dry ...
f77r.9    ... qokol chey ...              -> ... hot [chey] ...
```

These are compatible fragments, not independent corroboration. The complete
four-page text does not identify subjects that select hot over another broad
property. The f17r comparison is local; no obligatory two-interior-word grammar
is restored. Physical line boundaries are not sentence delimiters.

Descriptive materia medica is the least-strained working genre in this trial,
not a selected result. A synonym glossary needs an unsupported equivalence
construction. A prescription needs identified operations and ingredient
references. A correspondence reading needs supported pairings. An inventory
is plausible at page level, but amounts or transactions are not identified.
Absence of an exact label repetition does not itself refute any of those rivals.

The strongest objection is missing subject control. The trial explains the
repetition only by guessing where one drug's description ends and another
begins. Until those subjects can be bound independently, it remains a possible
story rather than an intelligible translated paragraph. No gloss is promoted.
