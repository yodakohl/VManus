# Intensity transfer: a conditional quantity/intensity refinement

Status: POST_RESULT_EXPLORATION_ONLY; no candidate selected. This memo leaves
`INTENSIFIER_TRIAL.md`, its fixed `sehr?` trial, and the dictionary unchanged.
It uses only guarded cached GDT812 text already admitted on f21r, f101r and
f100v, with f32v as the previously explored starting case. No new page, image,
source ownership or independent validation of meaning is claimed.

## One proposed refinement, with a real restriction

Try exact whole `daiin` as **much / high magnitude**, not specifically three.
Keep postfix attachment to the immediately preceding explicit expression
within the same source paragraph. A mass-noun expression would yield much
material; a gradable property would yield a high degree of that property.
English/German can render the latter as very/sehr, but the proposed invariant
is high magnitude, not an unrestricted choice of meanings at each occurrence.

This is a broader selection rule than the original strict `sehr?`, not a
cost-free clarification of it. It is legitimate as a new linguistic hypothesis
only if the preceding whole's category is independently retained or unknown;
one may not keep changing nouns into adjectives to obtain a pleasing result.
Unknown heads remain unknown. The construction supplies no unit, exact
cardinal, ordinal index, action, referent or omitted property. It may not jump
over an inconvenient adjacent whole to a more convenient distant noun.

## Concrete case: complete f21r first paragraph

ZL3b, with the source paragraph running from f21r.1 through f21r.3:

```text
f21r.1  pchor o eeockhy o fychey ypchey qopcheody otaiin chan
f21r.2  saiin chcphy oky sheaiin qotchol oteos sheey cthy daiin
f21r.3  qotol shy o l cheor chy qokchey chey keey dy
```

The entire second line, including `cthy daiin`, agrees in all three cached
readings. Other word-boundary differences remain in the complete admitted
reader; this display does not claim three identical full paragraphs.

Conditionally retain GDT809's `cthy = leaf/herb material?`, explicitly a
mass-noun interpretation, not a newly inferred property:

```text
f21r.1  [pchor o eeockhy o fychey ypchey qopcheody otaiin chan]
f21r.2  [saiin chcphy oky sheaiin qotchol oteos sheey] [herb-material?] [much?]
f21r.3  [qotol shy o l cheor chy qokchey chey keey dy]
```

This gives the conditional phrase "much herb-material?" while preserving all
other words, order and uncertainty. Strict `sehr?` instead produced the
awkward "herb-material very". The refinement does not assign an adjective to
`cthy`, invent a hidden drying property, or reach forward to the following
line's `qotol`. The paragraph remains untranslated except for this trial pair.
If `cthy` is instead a discrete name or a countable single leaf, the mass
construction is not thereby established; the inherited opaque rival survives.

## Historical lexical range, not a Voynich key

Latin 9333 supplies `nutrimentum multum` (f071v: abundant nourishment) and
`si fuerit multum longus` (f097r: if it is very long).
[Edited primary text](https://medimanus.chs.harvard.edu/app/en/pharmantica/tacuinum_sanitatis).
These support the same written form's quantity/intensity range. The noun use
is an agreeing neuter-singular adjective; the intensity use is adverbial.
This is not evidence for an invariant Latin modifier of every noun: feminine
`herba` would ordinarily take `multa`. Nor is it fixed postfix order in both
uses: the intensity example is preposed.
The copy is catalogued around 1445-1450 or earlier, not specifically 1420.
Neither doubling, the four-cell written family nor a Latin identification of
Voynich is established by this comparator.

## Distinction from strict sehr and from axis-open III

| Fixed candidate | Mass `cthy` retained | Property `chol` retained | Additional commitment |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sehr?` | Immediate "herb-material very" is not explained. | Very dry? | Selects a gradable expression. |
| Proposed much/high magnitude | Much herb-material? | Very dry? | Allows amount and intensity, but always high, indefinite magnitude. |
| `III?` with axis open | Three unspecified measures/portions? | Degree III? | Claims an exact cardinal/ordered value, though axis and units remain unproved. |

The refinement is not merely old III renamed: it drops exact numerical
identity and predicts abundance/high degree. But it would become an empty
generic operator if "high" could mean any value or if every unknown head could
be reclassified afterward. No such flexibility is licensed here.

Concrete obligations distinguish the proposals:

- Hold `cthy` mass-noun and `chol` property meanings fixed in this comparison.
  The candidate's local improvement is that both take one high-magnitude
  modifier without changing category. This is a retrospective fit, not a
  successful held test.
- Do not attach across a source paragraph boundary or skip an unresolved
  intervening whole. A genuine paragraph-initial target with no written host
  would defeat this particular postfix construction. Physical-line starts
  alone do not do so.
- A securely identified ordinal position, exact number or nonmeasurable
  identifier at the same construction would pressure high magnitude. An
  independently keyed abundance/intensity contrast would instead distinguish
  it from literal III. No such independent key exists in this small scope.
- The previous `dain daiin` difficulty is not automatically solved: if plain
  `dain` were truly II/two, "two much" would still need explanation. `dain`,
  `cthaiin`, `dairin` and fused `cthodaiin` remain distinct unknown wholes.
- Immediate `daiin daiin` still requires a repetition construction. The new
  historical pair does not validate doubling or explain the recurring written
  four-cell family. Amount/intensity flexibility does not erase those costs.
