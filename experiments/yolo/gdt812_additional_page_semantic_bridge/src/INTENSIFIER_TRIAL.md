# f32v: one fixed `daiin = very` trial

Status: LOW_CONFIDENCE_POST_RESULT_EXPLORATION. This is a constructive rival
within GDT812, not a preregistered new experiment, a successful meaning test,
an adopted dictionary change, or a translation. No new page or image is used.

## The proposal

Give the exact complete whole `daiin` one provisional meaning: **very**.
The construction hypothesis is a postposed intensifier of the preceding
explicit expression. An immediately repeated `daiin daiin` intensifies that
same expression emphatically: "very, very". Both written occurrences remain;
neither is deleted, added to make six, or assigned an invented second subject.

This is a nonnumerical alternative to "III/three", not a second way of
asserting degree III. No specific language, historical spelling, sound value,
or EVA component meaning follows. The proposal does not claim that emphatic
doubling or this modifier order has been established for the manuscript.

Only `daiin` receives that meaning. `dain`, `daiiin`, `cthaiin`, `qotaiin`,
`kcheodaiin` and every other distinct whole remain unknown here. In particular,
`cthaiin` is not silently read as "leaf III", "leaf", or an adjective. The
RF1b whole `cthodaiin` is not split to recover a missing intensifier.

## What this construction explains in JP03

1. `otchol daiin daiin` can refer to one explicitly written intensity-bearing
   expression, with emphatic repetition. It need not distribute two numbers
   over two unwritten properties or subjects. `otchol` is still unknown: the
   hypothesis predicts an intensity-bearing use, not "cold-and-dry".
2. `chocthy daiin cthaiin daiin` can have two distinct expressions, each with
   the same intensity modifier. Neither expression is merged with the other;
   no root meaning or omitted adjective is supplied. Their suitability for
   modification is a prediction of this trial, not observed semantics.
3. `chor chol daiin` gives a concrete conditional reading if two GDT809
   low-confidence meanings are separately retained: `chor = flower-material?`
   and `chol = dry?` would yield "flower-material, very dry?". In source order
   this remains `[flower-material?] [dry?] [very?]`. It could describe a quality
   or a material state; it does not mean "dry the flowers".
4. f32v.7 and f32v.11 remain untranslated. The trial does not fabricate a
   recipe opening, preparation step or concluding action to make the middle
   lines sound fluent.

The first point is a real explanatory difference from a numerical reading,
but it is not an independently tested advantage: the doubled occurrence
motivated this hypothesis. Calling it natural in a hypothetical English
display cannot establish the manuscript's syntax.

## First-paragraph contrasts and costs

The complete page prevents the favorable JP03 example from being the whole
argument. A fixed postposed "very" must also account for these positions:

| Source position | Fixed trial | Remaining cost |
|---|---|---|
| f32v.1-2 `chofchody / daiin` | `[chofchody] very?` across the line break | This attachment is possible because the paragraph continues, but unproved. RF1b also changes the preceding word boundary. |
| f32v.2 `chy daiin` | `[chy] very?` | `chy` must support intensity here; its meaning and scope are unknown. |
| f32v.3 `shor daiin` | `[shor] very?` | Retaining inherited `shor = fruit/material` gives the awkward "fruit very". The trial must suspend that noun assignment here, not insert an unwritten property such as ripe or dry. |
| f32v.3 `dain daiin` | `[dain] very?` | Plain `dain` remains unknown. Retaining `dain = II/two` would create "two very", not an explanation. No comparative/intensity meaning is assigned to `dain` to rescue this. |
| f32v.4 `dcho daiin` | `[dcho] very?` | The preceding expression must be intensity-bearing; RF1b reads `dch`. Neither is translated. |
| f32v.5 `chokchy daiin` | `[chokchy] very?` | Compatible with a property-like head, but that head is not decoded. |

The second paragraph has the same dictionary cost at `cthaiin daiin`.
Keeping the older component-composed "leaf/material III" would produce
"leaf/material III very", which this trial does not call good syntax.
`cthaiin` therefore remains an unknown whole. Its required intensity-bearing
use is a falsifiable obligation, not permission to turn a noun into an
unspecified quality whenever convenient. `ctho` carries the same open
obligation where it is a separate word.

The trial trades precise numbers, units and some inherited part/value
assignments for a single fixed intensity adverb. It is not a free improvement
to the existing dictionary. The improved handling of repetition must be
weighed against these lost assignments and unresolved modifier scopes.

## Comparison with the three numerical models

| Model | `chor chol daiin`, under tentative flower/dry meanings | What the double `daiin` still requires |
|---|---|---|
| Degree | flower-material, dry in degree III? | Two explicit or independently justified degree fields, or another account of repeated III. |
| Dose | dried flower-material, three portions? | Separate amount scopes or an account of repeated three; no automatic sum to six. |
| Keeping time | dried flower-material, keeps three years? | An independently supported keeping/time construction, plus an account of repeated three elsewhere. Neither keeps nor years is decoded here. |
| Intensity trial | flower-material, very dry? | One explicitly hypothesized emphatic-repetition construction; all other `daiin` uses must accept the same nonnumerical modifier. |

The intensity trial does not choose quality versus state. Its gain is narrower:
one repeated modifier can target the same expression without introducing
another subject, counted item or time unit. It loses numerical specificity
and is not yet a successful whole-page parse.

## Duplicate screen and inherited counterevidence

Two route-check searches were run before selecting this proposal: one covering
nonnumerical `daiin` alternatives and repeated `daiin daiin`, and one naming
very/much/strongly/intensity/intensifier. Targeted reading covered GDT626,
GDT686, GDT764, GDT802 and the GDT810 arity report; targeted active-registry
and ledger searches found no previously decisive `daiin = very` test.
This is navigation and claim auditing, not a new corpus search or a formal
novelty verdict.

- GDT626 reports a strongly recurrent four-cell written family, and GDT686
  reports repeated neighbour frames involving different cells. Those are
  important obligations for a nonnumerical account. This local trial does
  not explain away the family as noise or assign meanings to its other forms.
  The reports' Roman-number and degree interpretations are hypotheses rather
  than independent numerical keys; the family alone does not identify the
  English word three. A graded nonnumerical account remains possible but is
  not developed or scored here.
- Those reports reject generic "take/process" and unrestricted conjunction
  substitutions. This proposal supplies no action and does not reopen them.
- GDT764's parallel `X daiin` fields are structurally compatible with repeated
  modified expressions, but their precise heads and inherited nominal
  readings are counterpressure. No new semantic credit is taken from them.
- GDT802 retires a particular `daiin -> m` placement lead, not this meaning.
  No neighbour classifier or line-edge argument is rerun.
- GDT810 already shows that repeated following values do not reliably count
  hypothesized quality components. Its retained `chol daiin daiin` example
  could also accommodate a doubled intensity modifier, but that is a
  retrospective explanation of known evidence, not new support or a reopened
  arity test. No additional source page was queried for this memo.

## Historical compatibility, not identification

Macer, *De viribus herbarum*, XXIII *Satureia*, v.843:
"illi sicca minus vis est, sed fervida valde."
Own translation: "Its power is less dry, but very hot."
This attests postposed intensity, not adjacent `valde valde`.
[CELT](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600006.html), ch.242, pp.417-418,
prints it as Latin comparanda; the 1415 Irish entry instead gives numeric
hot/dry III. [Macer location](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_viribus_herbarum#XXIII._Satureia).
No checked medical example licenses the trial's immediate doubling, and this
literary citation does not date a particular manuscript witness.

## Working conclusion

Retain **`daiin = very?`** as one explicit low-confidence rival worth comparing
with the numerical models. It offers a cleaner local account of the doubled
whole and preserves all source repetition. The whole-page extension exposes
its price: `shor`, `dain`, `ctho` and `cthaiin` cannot keep inherited nominal
or numerical meanings unquestioned, and the modifier attachments remain
undemonstrated. No lexeme or clause is confirmed, no model is selected, and
the current dictionary and renderer remain unchanged.
