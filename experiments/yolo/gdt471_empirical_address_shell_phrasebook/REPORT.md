## Result

The 89 label-derived source forms reduce to three useful levels:

| level | distinct | recurrent groups | source forms in recurrent groups |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| exact surface template | 71 | 14 | 32 |
| component/meaning template | 69 | 15 | 35 |

This is the clearest practical version of the mixed codebook model so far:
many learned entries occupy a small inventory of technical slot arrangements.
The topology count does not translate the names, but it tells the future reader
where an opaque name can sit relative to known function cards.

## Four function frames already cross owner classes

Four exact function templates cover eleven source labels in more than one owner
class:

- `ot{NAME_1}` — five labels, four owner classes, four pages;
- `ar{NAME_1}` — stellar and pharmaceutical labels on two pages;
- `ol{NAME_1}` — stellar and bath labels on two pages;
- `{NAME_1}ar{NAME_2}` — stellar and pharmaceutical labels on two pages.

Three further exact function templates recur across pages inside one owner
class, covering seven forms. Six recur locally, covering twelve. Fifty-seven
are singleton exact function templates. The single whole-name template covers
`oiil` and `cheosdy`, but is deliberately ranked as a generic name default,
not as a strong cross-owner function frame.

`ot{NAME_1}` is especially informative for the working theory. The fixed
`ot=DANACH` card surrounds unrelated learned material beside a plant, stellar
positions, a bath station and pharmaceutical objects. That looks much more
like a portable workshop/address instruction than like one object name.

## Two component templates bridge alternate calibrated renderings

Collapsing surface templates only when the component and slot sequence is
identical adds two small renderer families:

- `{NAME_1} · AIIN` occurs as `{NAME_1}aiin` and `{NAME_1}daiin` in three
  stellar labels;
- `{NAME_1} · O+IIN` occurs as `{NAME_1}oin` and `{NAME_1}oiin` across one
  stellar and one pharmaceutical label.

No other literal template is merged. The exact surface rendering remains in
the output beside the shared component template.

## Family markers belong to the learned layer

All 89 function templates survive the name-core insertion. Owner-family
substrings do not: thirty source probes contain at least one marker, only
nineteen mutations retain any marker, and only fifteen retain the exact marker
trace. Fifteen of 89 traces change. The sole route change is `cheosdy`, whose
broken `cheo` family clue falls from family-plus-name to the same whole drug-name
default; its function template remains unchanged.

Across the eighteen individual family rules there are 48 source occurrences,
32 retained occurrences, sixteen losses and zero gains. This is not a failure
of the mixed model. It locates those substrings inside the variable learned
nomenclator rather than in the productive function frame.
