# V17 R3 — technical register and executable wet-process deck

Date: 2026-08-21

Status: maximally abductive ten-page sidequest candidate. This is a complete
concrete working reading, not a decipherment claim or canonical GDT result.

## Verdict

The 30 recurrent exact cards read most economically as a small wet-process
teaching deck embedded in a broader illustrated medical register. They are not
thirty ordinary independent words. Together they maintain a few active things:

```text
OWNER  pictured plant, body place, vessel or conduit
Q      current measured charge
M      current working liquid
P      current place or flow route
U      stated measure and duration
T      current process condition
```

The workshop procedure is:

```text
take next charge → put it in → combine charges
  → add warmed water → warm / boil → stir
  → stand / cool → strain / decant
  → route through channels → pour, rinse or apply at P
```

This gives every recurrent card a concrete spoken expansion and an executable
register effect. An apprentice learns the complete card face and its stock
phrase; the several EVA renderings attached to one exact tuple do not create
new meanings.

The exhaustive results are in:

- `V17_R3_RECURRENT_CARD_DECISIONS.tsv`: 30 cards, exactly three concrete
  rivals each, five-part scores, chosen reading and revision status;
- `V17_R3_ALL_OCCURRENCE_READINGS.tsv`: all 217 occurrences, two-card context
  on both sides where present, field/line boundaries and rewritten sequence;
- `V17_R3_AFFECTED_LINE_READINGS.tsv`: all 57 affected physical lines;
- `V17_R3_EXECUTABLE_DECK.tsv`: the apprentice's register rule for every card;
- `V17_R3_REWRITTEN_PASSAGES.md`: complete f10r, f56r and f82r readings.

Coverage is 30/30 cards and 217/217 occurrences. No neutral or empty meaning is

## Main V16 decisions

Eight readings are retained essentially unchanged, fifteen are sharpened, and
seven are reversed.

### Strong reversals

1. **`OKY/QOKY` is APPLY/USE, not LESSER PORTION.** Its ten occurrences range
   across five folios and six are field-medial. On f10r it can be followed by
   `AIIN`, yielding “apply in the usual measure”; on f56r it can precede `AL`,
   yielding “apply at the indicated place”; after `CKHY` in Biological it is
   the final use of a routed liquid. “Lesser” requires an unexpressed compared
   quantity too often.

2. **`SHEDY` is LET STAND/SETTLE, not merely SET READY.** All twelve copies are
   field-last or field-only. In `CHEDY – L/O – SHEDY – QOLCHEDY`, “stir, with
   the foregoing liquid, let stand, draw off the clear liquor” is a complete
   technical progression. A generic readiness phrase loses the specific
   recurrence between mixing and decanting.

3. **`OKEEY/QOKEEY` is ADD WARMED WATER, not KEEP WARM.** It is open—three
   field-first and four field-medial copies—and repeatedly precedes rinsing,
   use or another committed process card. Heat itself already has three better
   cards: moderate warming `QOKEEDY`, single boiling `QOKCHDY`, and gentle-fire
   mode `CHEKY`. This division makes the deck simpler and gives water a stable
   card rather than spreading WATER across `L/O` or every channel form.

4. **`CHAR/DAR/SAR` is THEN PROCEED, not OF THE SAME.** The decisive local
   form is f82r.19 `OKAIN – CHAR – OKAIN – QOKEEDY`: “put in, then put in,
   warm”. The same sequential value works line-final as a carry to the next
   physical line.

5. **`QOLCHEDY` is DRAW OFF THE CLEAR LIQUOR, not RETAIN THE MIXTURE.** It
   follows resting or washing and precedes loading a new charge on all three
   folios. Decanting supplies the missing directional operation between
   settling and reuse.

6. **`SCHEDY` is WASH THROUGH ONCE, not FINISH APPLICATION.** Three of four
   copies are one-card fields, and the fourth immediately follows
   `CKHY`. A counted washing pass explains both placements and fits the wet
   channel sequence.

7. **f56r `CHO/SHO` resumes the pictured plant, not merely THEREAFTER.** It is
   confined to f56r and occurs twice at field entry and twice medially in short
   Herbal clauses. A page-owner resumption gives one ordinary reason for the
   register restriction and improves all four plant clauses.

### Important retained distinctions

- `AIIN` remains the measure-reference card. It is portable across all seven
  prose folios and can be first, medial or last because a rubric may inherit
  its quantity rather than state a numeral.
- `OR` remains prepared liquid. Its four f10r occurrences, f55v copy and two
  f83r copies make it the cleanest cross-register material candidate.
- `CKHY` remains a joined-channel route. Three of four copies occur on f81v,
  including one after two successive local rinses; the fourth belongs to an
  f82r conduit sequence.
- `OKAL` remains combination rather than WATER. Assigning WATER here would
  make f82r.2 “warm, water, second opening” but damages f55v and the explicit
  two-charge sequences. `OKEEY` is the cleaner water card.
- `CTHY` remains readiness. Its Herbal and Biological portability makes a
  condition more economical than a named ingredient.

## The four value/commit cards

The strongest mechanical distinction in V17 is not their shared closure but
their four repeatable process outcomes:

```text
VAL-S   SHEDY    let stand and settle
VAL-QE  QOKEEDY  warm to tempered heat
VAL-Q   LCHEDY   cool to ordinary setting
VAL-L   QOKEDY   pour/rinse at the local place
```

All 38 occurrences are field-last or field-only. Thus the visible closure is
part of a completed instruction card; it does not force the four cards to mean
the same thing. The quartet is easy for a workshop to teach because it covers
four common endpoints of wet preparation: rest, warm, cool, and route locally.

## Full-pass pressure points

The 217-row audit reveals where the deck is doing real work and where it still
needs inherited arguments.

- f10r.6 has three `Y` cards around `AIIN`. I read this as three current
  charges, one of them explicitly governed by the standard measure. It is also
  compatible with a small ratio notation; “this charge” is deliberately the
  most literal concrete default.
- f81v.18 has two consecutive `QOKEDY` cards before `CKHY`. “Pour twice, then
  route through the joined channels” is coherent and favors a repeatable rinse
  action over an abstract closure.
- f82r.3 reads “add warmed water; strain clear; continue at the second conduit;
  through cloth; through joined channels; take the next charge.” It is the
  strongest integrated water/filter/channel sequence.
- f82r.7 contains stand/settle, a separately coded keep-warm instruction,
  warmed-water addition, application and a clear-outflow gate. This is the
  clearest whole-line wet-process recipe.
- f82r.19 reads “put in; then; put in; warm; draw off.” It is the strongest
  witness for sequential `CHAR` and vessel-transfer `OKAIN`.
- f82r.27 remains awkward because most of its cards are rare V16 defaults. The
  two `QOKEEDY` copies still divide it into two warmed suboperations, but V17
  cannot repair the surrounding rare cards without exceeding its frozen
  recurrent-card scope.
- f56r.8 remains awkward because its rare closing card still says “leave the
  outlet open”, an inherited V16 Biological default that is poor Herbal prose.
  This is a rare-card defect, not evidence against the recurrent `OKY = apply`
  reading immediately before it.
- f11r.1 supplies the awkward Herbal `CHEEY`: “until the liquid runs clear” is
  acceptable only if the missing preparation is a decoction or expressed
  juice. This is the largest silent-argument cost retained by the selected
  recurrent deck.

## Historical and workshop plausibility

The selected expansions are intentionally ordinary recipe imperatives and
conditions available to a fifteenth-century scribe: take, put in, combine,
warm, bring to the boil, stir, let stand, cool, strain through cloth, draw off,
wash, pour and apply. They require vessels, cloth, water, heat and depicted
places, not modern laboratory equipment or numerical notation.

A master needs six lessons:

1. memorize the thirty whole cards, never parse an unfamiliar card by glyph;
2. keep the current owner, charge, liquid, place, measure and condition in
   memory;
3. copy a closure-bearing operation as one indivisible card;
4. carry active registers across a physical line unless a local close or new
   article owner intervenes;
5. use the register's normal rendered face without changing the spoken card;
6. copy rare cards from the exemplar rather than inventing them from the deck.

This is easy enough for several hands because the common deck is small and
procedural. Its weakness is exactly the expected one: rare article-specific
cards still dominate the lexical substance and must be learned from exemplars.
