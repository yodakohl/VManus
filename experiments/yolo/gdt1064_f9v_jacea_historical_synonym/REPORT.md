# GDT1064 — `Jacea` can name a pansy in historical herbal usage

**Decision: correct the botanical contradiction in GDT1063, without confirming
`fochor`.** In the 1485 *Gart der Gesundheit*, chapter 432, the printed caption
visibly puts `yacea` next to `freyschem krut` beneath a pansy-like plant with
separate flowers and deeply divided leaflike parts. The source is preserved as
[`YACEA_GART_1485.jpg`](src/YACEA_GART_1485.jpg), from the
[public-domain facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yacea_Gart.jpg).
Fuchs's [1543 *New Kreüterbuch*, chapter 313](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/29723145.pdf)
later explicitly groups `Jacea` with Freyschamkraut/Herba
Trinitatis and treats it as a kind of *Viola*. That later testimony supports
the historical polysemy but is too late to show what a 1420 writer used.

| Reading of the public f9v entry | Relation to f9v illustration | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Bare historical `JACEA` | An attested pansy/Freyschamkraut use exists. | **Compatible**, though not evidence that the decoder recovered it. |
| The public table's explicit `JACEA (knapweed)` | Knapweed has a composite flower head, unlike the individual pansy-like five-petal flowers on f9v. | **Visual conflict remains** for that specified expansion. |
| `fochor` ≈ pansy/Viola | f9v owner is plausible, first prose group is exact across ZL3b/IT2a/RF1b (GDT1062). | **C0 working hypothesis only**; generic or page-specific entry address remains equivalent. |

GDT1063 inadvertently treated the *Jacea* name as if it had only the modern
knapweed sense. The source correction removes that overly broad objection to
the literal name. It does **not** validate the public positional cipher:
historical Jacea has multiple plant referents, the public author explicitly
chose “knapweed,” and the pansy-compatible sense was selected here only after
seeing f9v. Nor does this settle the exact species; *Viola arvensis* and
*V. tricolor* share many of the visible traits used in GDT1063. The defensible
visual owner is pansy-like *Viola* rather than securely *V. tricolor*.

The original GDT1063 file and decision remain intact as historical records;
this is the explicit later correction. The two-paragraph f9v text, old project
glosses and historical Viola medicinal entries cannot distinguish plant-name
from entry-address without another independently bound target word or a
complete passage prediction. GDT1059 already establishes high first-head
uniqueness; IL018 found no transferable two-paragraph ordinal profile. No new
target admission, decoder, reserve, or sealed page occurred. The admitted f9v
image was viewed again before the source lead was found. This source-only
compatibility is a concrete positive historical lexical finding, **not** a
confirmed Voynich translation. Confirmed translated words: **0**.

Reconsider the word only after a second independently owned same-taxon
name slot, an authorial bilingual label, or a complete fixed reading that
discriminates `fochor` as a species name from a generic entry address across
multiple physical leaves. The current 51 admitted visual keys contain no
second independently established pansy owner in the selected packet.
