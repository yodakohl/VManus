# Method and data boundary

The signal was discovered in a 2026-09-28 web search after GDT1063 had been
published. This is therefore a registered **retrospective source correction**,
not a blind or prospective test. The exact question is whether the bare
historical name *Yacea/Jacea* can denote a pansy-like herb, even though the
public decoder expands its f9v `JACEA` as “knapweed.” The fixed evidence is the
visible 1485 *Gart der Gesundheit* chapter-432 facsimile, paired with the
already preserved public decoder table and GDT1063 f9v image assessment.

The facsimile is archived in `src/YACEA_GART_1485.jpg`, from
<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yacea_Gart.jpg> (public-domain
historical printed image). Its caption visibly juxtaposes `yacea` and
`freyschem krut` under a pansy-like illustration. The 1543 Fuchs *New
Kreüterbuch*, chapter 313, is a later corroborating witness and not a
demonstration of usage in 1420. Modern Kew also records *Jacea tricolor* as
a later taxonomic synonym of *Viola tricolor*; that name was published in 1852
and is not itself medieval evidence.

The already admitted f9v image was viewed again during this turn before the
historical Yacea lead was found; it is therefore additional informed exposure,
not an independent holdout. No new Voynich selector or image was admitted.
f84/f84r stay sealed and reserves closed. The packet does
not infer a cipher map, first-word function, manuscript language, exact Viola
species, or any confirmed word. The validator checks only byte identity and
the intended interpretation ceiling.
