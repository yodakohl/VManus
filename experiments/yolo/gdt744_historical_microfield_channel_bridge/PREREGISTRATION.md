# GDT744 exploratory working contract

## Working hypothesis

The twelve GDT738 exact wholes are reusable bound level/state fields. Their
local neighbors may place a given occurrence inside one of GDT735's historically
attested descriptive or prescriptive record channels, while the learned content
name or ingredient remains a separate whole.

## Decisions made before interpretation

1. Use all and only the inherited 202 target positions and cached radius-five
   windows.
2. Clip at line edges, strict initial heads, another target and direction-aware
   close markers.
3. Use only screened W2/W3 exact-whole neighbors; do not infer substring
   meanings.
4. Dispatch the seven channel outcomes in the precedence written in
   `src/FIELD_CHANNEL_RULES.tsv`.
5. Require two fully bounded pages and two varying anchor signatures before a
   target-surface/channel template can enter the renderer.
6. Keep target meaning, field channel and unresolved content identity in
   separate columns.
7. Print hot/cold or dry/moist collisions as competing evidence.
8. Show window-only and open countercases in the human reader.

## Correction retained in the final contract

The first manual render exposed that GDT739's `PASS` denotes a processing
passage but was omitted from the initial process rule. The final executable
contract treats `PASS` alongside `PROCESS` while rendering PASS-only cases as a
processing section, not an unsupported command. The same audit found two W3
exact wholes with no axis tag; their already inherited broad whole roles are
listed explicitly in `src/WHOLE_HISTORICAL_ROLE_SUPPLEMENTS.tsv`. Neither
correction grants literal or component credit.

## Useful outcome

A useful outcome is a nonempty recurring field-template deck that adds record
channel information without turning an open content surface into a named
substance. A null outcome remains acceptable and must retain open fields.

## Interpretation ceiling

Recurrence licenses a field template only. Historical similarity does not
identify a Voynich word, and a content-slot candidate is not a drug, plant,
ingredient or plaintext item until separately bridged.
