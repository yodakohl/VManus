# Working theory after reading four complete pages

This is a concrete content hypothesis, not a translation. It preserves the
inherited mixed-lexicon architecture without treating any German gloss as
already decoded. All four complete transcriptions and their alternate readings
are in [the full reader](artifacts/FOUR_PAGES_FULL_TEXT.md).

## The most useful joint interpretation

Try a collection describing **natural substances, their properties and bodily
effects, together with celestial correspondences or timing**. Some entries
may prescribe preparations, but not every line or inscription is an action.
The same written vocabulary can serve different subjects without changing a
property's meaning: a plant, a celestial figure and a bodily condition could
all carry an attribute such as hot or moist. That is a possibility, not an
identification of a particular Voynich form with those attributes.

The important separation is:

| Level | Concrete possible content | What cannot be assumed |
|---|---|---|
| Illustrated referent | a plant, rootstock, celestial entry, bodily configuration | the nearest word is its species or organ name |
| Label | name, category, condition or correspondence | every label is an ingredient; every star-holder is a patient |
| Running explanation | properties, usable parts, effects, timing, or preparation | a universal sequence of take, heat, separate and finish |

This is not a newly discovered subject area. The old pharmaceutical and
astrological comparisons already made it plausible. The new work specifies
which text/image contacts a shared interpretation must actually explain.

## f88r: botanical substances before assumed recipes

The picture contains three different ornate vessel-like figures, several
groups of separate botanical/root objects, labels, and three prose paragraphs.
A further large plant/root object sits below the last paragraph without a
fresh caption. The page therefore does not demonstrate three perfectly closed
ingredient lists or a depicted preparation procedure.

Two whole-page readings remain useful:

1. **Descriptive botanical material register.** The pictured entries distinguish
   useful substances, their forms, properties or applications. The prose can
   discuss a group without repeating every label. This requires fewer implicit
   ingredient-name recoveries than the recipe rival.
2. **Three compound medicines.** Each vessel-like object represents a preparation,
   its adjacent plants are ingredients, and the paragraph explains making or
   using it. This is visually attractive, but the grouping, missing name reuse
   and unlabelled bottom object still need an explanation.

Only the complete label okol is repeated exactly in this page's prose:

    f88r.15  okol
    f88r.19  dchey chokol daiin qoekol qoekol qockhol okol cheol

The same source line has chokol and twice qoekol; the next paragraph contains
okoldy and five qokol. These are **separate complete forms**. A q/ch/dy
construction around a shared referent is a good local candidate, but remains
unproved. The repeated qokol chol qokol qokol chol must not be smoothed into
an invented instruction.

The wider inventory corrects the tempting specific-plant identification:
okol occurs58 times on45 page/group keys and in seven source sections;
qokol occurs90 times on52 keys and in five sections. The released f71v ring
text contains exact okol, and f68r1 has an exact okoldy label. This leaves
a broadly usable category, property, ordinary nominal or reference rival at
least as serious as one particular botanical drug. A widespread drug name
is not logically impossible, and different similar wholes need not share a
meaning. No new gloss follows automatically from those distributions.

**Concrete naming lead retained:** ofaldo at f88r.25 and ofal at f88r.30
share a longer initial string. In the admitted inventory, ofaldo occurs only
at that label and ofal has just one further occurrence, f108r.14. This is a
more narrowly distributed name candidate than okol. It does not identify the
plant, prove a do suffix, or establish that the two words are related.

## f77r: different emissions or different powers

The arch links two human figures. Openings have different descending marks,
including blue bands, thin lines and a red region. Lower drawings accompany
two further paragraphs. No global arrowed flow or single closed circuit is
shown. Water, vapour and physiological influence remain interpretations.

The direct medicinal-water rival is **different applications or emissions**:
pouring, trickling, vapour, collection and discharge. The correspondence rival
is **different bodily states or powers expressed through a common system**.
For otedy, Guss/Rieselwasser? and a flowing/moist state? are concrete trial
meanings, not selected dictionary values. Dampf? versus Abfluss? for dchdy
is similarly image-led and unresolved; RF1b also splits that label.

None of the eight upper labels recurs exactly in the long first paragraph.
The only own-page exact upper-label repetition, otedy, opens the second
paragraph. Therefore a literal eight-caption expansion is not established.
The separate complete qotedy and qoteedy forms each occur nine times, with
reader-boundary differences. They cannot be counted as eighteen exact otedy
references or as permission to assign a q operator.

The fully sourced comparison is in [the f77r reading](src/F77_HISTORICAL_READING.md).
Wellcome MS510 is later fifteenth-century material, with the particular f21r
table in a late-fifteenth/early-sixteenth-century hand; it is not a securely
1420 codebook. Parker MS395 also contains distinguishable historical layers.
Neither comparator supplies a Voynich word identity.

## f72r: members of a diagram, not literal treatment subjects

The foldout places repeated star-bearing figures in rings, with short local
inscriptions and longer circular text bands. The figures' repeated graphical
type does not make all their labels the same name. Nor does their nakedness
identify patients, reproductive anatomy or a bathing procedure.

In f72r2.22, the ring text contains both okaly and okal. Each also labels two
different nearby members. That produces four textual edges but only two
prose tokens: the text cannot be assigned to a unique figure merely because
its spelling matches. GDT794–795 already ruled out turning those exact
strings into unique member/day numbers. Keep the ambiguity visible.

Learned member names and recurring status/property material remain possible.
The common-property reading is useful because it allows repeated labels at
different objects. It does not select hot, cold, a planet, a lunar phase,
an organ or a temporal unit. Repeating the failed coordinate search would
not supply those meanings.

## f17r: consistent scope before another connective guess

The whole page has78 prose tokens, three paragraphs and a separate two-token
local record. Its three blue/white heads do not confirm a numeral: the page
has no exact daiin and only daiiin at .11. Flower versus fruit is not visually
settled; two red root outlines also identify neither a number nor a remedy.

Two complete written spans share this shape, across all alternate readers:

    otchol [qodcthy ods]  chol [or ...]
    otchol [cthar okaiin] chol [daiiin]

The first crosses a physical line. The bracket can be tried as a named
material/form between two property fields, or as successive forward rubrics.
The inherited cold/dry, preparation and IV readings remain hypotheses. Their
German sentence does not establish the two interior words' grammatical roles.

The thirty-page inventory finds no other two-interior-word example. It also
contains exact otchol chol on f4r. Thus **two slots are not an obligatory
general grammar**. Keep this only as a local comparison between f17r's two
passages; the mere recurring endpoints are not sufficient to bracket distant
words on other pages. [Both explicit local readings](src/F17_SCOPE_PROPOSAL.md)
retain the unknown words and the difference between dry quality and dried state.

## What is selected, and what is not

Selected for further interpretation: distinguish referent, property and
reference; retain complete page context, explicit scope and alternate
boundaries; prioritize scarce candidate names separately from broadly shared
forms. This sharpens the search target, not a decoded vocabulary.

The current dictionary is unchanged. No name, ingredient, organ, operation,
number or clause is confirmed. A complete natural-language rendering would
still require many unsupported assignments. Calling it a translation would
hide the very problem the four-page comparison exposes.

The next meaningful gain has to identify **what at least one written contrast
means**, with the same assignment fitting its other occurrences. Further
ranking of these same seven words, re-counting the same f17r bracket, or
creating smoother German from the old defaults is not that gain.

The closed BOTANICAL_TRAITS_AND_NAMES route is not reopened by the ofaldo
lead. Its scarce occurrences are a linguistic reference candidate, not
permission to assign a species by resemblance and recycle medicinal-use
statistics. A genuinely source-bound historical relation or a different
explicit textual prediction would be needed.
