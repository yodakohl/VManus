## What the image contributes, without naming unseen anatomy

The upper structure forms a continuous arch between two human figures standing
in receptacle-like forms. Its openings and associated descending marks are
not identical: broad blue bands, thin strands, interrupted/dotted marks and a
red-coloured region occur. The middle lateral figure accompanies a branching
structure with several open mouths. The lowest figure accompanies a blue
rounded form and a broad patterned discharge-like area.

These are direct visual descriptions. Calling the marks liquid, vapour, blood,
fire, sweat, semen, urine or celestial influence is a further hypothesis. The
drawn marks do not establish flow direction throughout the whole network.
Likewise, the figures might be users, bodily recipients or personifications;
nakedness alone does not decide among them.

GDT790 assigns the eight upper labels to two endpoint figure/port zones and
six numbered internal component zones. Endpoint ownership is explicitly
ambiguous; the middle/lower labels are proximity-only. Those annotation IDs
are not recovered names of organs or uniquely resolved nozzle attachments.

## Rival A: differentiated medicinal-water applications

The page might compare modes of medicinal water delivery and their effects.
The upper arch would summarize connected delivery types; P2 and P3 would
expand selected applications or configurations. The figures would be users
or recipients, not names of ingredients. The different drawn emissions make
**Guss, Rieselwasser, Dampf, Sammelwasser, Abfluss** reasonable candidate
vocabulary for this model.

Under this model, `otedy` can tentatively be tried as **Guss/Rieselwasser?**:
the upper label marks a component and the next paragraph resumes that type.
For `dchdy`, **Dampf?** competes with **Abfluss?**, but the image does not
discriminate them sufficiently and its RF1b boundary is uncertain. These are
deliberately replaceable content proposals, not new token translations.

This model explains bodies plus receptacles and differentiated emissions.
It does not establish a mechanically working pipe layout, a specific bath,
eight separately described sources or a time-ordered treatment recipe. The
absence of exact upper labels from P1 weighs against a simple caption-by-
caption expansion; related whole forms might still bear grammatical relations.

## Rival B: bodily qualities and cross-domain powers

Alternatively, the page might describe how bodily qualities or powers are
distributed and expressed. A person can then embody an effect, while a
channel represents its relation to another recipient or state. The same
property vocabulary could be used with a plant, a celestial diagram and a
bodily configuration without making those three subjects the same object.

Under this model, `otedy` could be tried as a **feuchter/strömender Zustand?**,
not the literal name of one physical nozzle. The P2 recurrence would resume
a state or effect rather than move the upper receptacle into the middle
picture. A narrower **Phlegma**, specific organ or named planet is currently
unsupported. This distinction matters: a quality can describe a planet and
an organ; that does not make the quality word mean either planet or organ.

The repetitive whole-form vocabulary across all three paragraphs is compatible
with such a common descriptive register. It does not prove this register:
repeated recipe language, local reference notation and learned opaque names
remain alternatives. Neither counting four colours nor assigning seven
planets to chosen openings is justified. This proposal does not reinstate the
withdrawn f57/f77 element decoder or GDT796's failed benefic assignment.

## Historical comparators and their actual dates

The following are bounded summaries of directly checked catalogue or owning-
library pages. Their wording is not copied into the Voynich dictionary.

### H1 — named thermal baths, not an identified f77r machine

[Monumenta's Bodmer 135 contents](https://monumenta.ch/latein/codex_content.php?c=119)
date that witness to **1350–1375** and link its contents to edition texts.
The listed sections include *Sudatoria* (1v), *Sulfatara* (2v), *Arcus* (14v),
*Tripergula* (15v) and *Sol et Luna* (23v). Only the catalogue/linked contents
were successfully checked here; no matching multi-outlet illustration or
full interpretation of those chapters was established. The useful comparison
is a differentiated, named thermal-water register. *Arcus* is not a translation
of any Voynich label merely because f77r contains an arch.

[Edinburgh's MS176 catalogue](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/160996)
dates its copy to the **15th century** and describes text sections about
named medicinal baths with large accompanying illustrations. The modern
binding inscription mentioning 1413 is not treated here as a secure scribal
date. This second catalogue supports the existence of the image-plus-bath-
effects format, not an exact iconographic match with f77r.

### H2 — qualities, planets and bodily sites in one practical collection

[Wellcome MS510's official catalogue](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/revt5nqc)
dates the collection **1450–1500?**, written by several hands. Its contents
distinguish planetary properties, bodily sites and complexions. On f7r the
catalogue transcribes Saturn's relation to “the mylte and the mawe”; ff7r–v
combine planets, signs and bodily locations. The table on **f21r** correlates
planets/signs with body parts, but its hand is specifically **late fifteenth/
early sixteenth century**, not automatically 1420 or earlier. The ff22v–23r
description associates the Moon with a female character and cold/moist nature.
These are named correspondences in a documented medical-astrological
collection. They establish neither a f77r planetary layout nor a specific
Voynich quality word. The catalogue is the source of the dating and contents;
the f21r manuscript image was not independently transcribed in this pass.

### H3 — a plant image can participate in a celestial/alchemical explanation

The owning college's [Parker MS395 discussion](https://www.corpus.cam.ac.uk/articles/residue-alchemy-botany)
examines *lunaria* images on ff49v–50r. The **modern Parker Library article**
places the Catalan illustrated material probably in fifteenth-century southern
France and the English-context Latin addition on ff48v–49r in the **second
half of the fifteenth century**. Its proposal that the first illustration was
added to suit that text is the article's inference, not a dated colophon.
The article connects the depicted plant's changing leaves with lunar phases
and quotes an annotation treating first matter metaphorically through a
plant. It distinguishes botanical identification from later alchemical
interpretation, even noting incompatible plant naming between layers. This
is a concrete comparator for cross-domain imagery, not evidence that all
Voynich plants are allegories. The linked Stanford manuscript catalogue
timed out; this pass relies on the owning library's published examination.

## Bounded conclusion

Rival A is the more immediate medicinal-water reading of f77r's visible
receptacles and emissions. Rival B offers the more useful architectural
comparison when plant and celestial pages also reuse related forms. Neither
currently wins a word identity. The practical discriminator is whether a
complete label names **the depicted thing**, **its property/effect**, or a
**reference/category** reused elsewhere.

The strongest exact f77r bridge remains `otedy`, upper label to P2 opener.
Its existence and cross-panel position are useful constraints; they do not
identify a woman, water, organ, planet or transferred physical batch. No smooth
German paragraph is supplied, because the remaining text has not been read
as plaintext.
