# Hundred-ninety-seventh pass: normalized H3 and B3 translations

## H3 now reads as one compact preparation chain

The 17 visible events normalize into four statements:

1. Set the plant material in a liquid preparation, press it out, leave it for
   the stated time, strain again, take the clear extract and add the final
   component; close.
2. Prepare the additional component.
3. From the previous preparation, work this item and bring it to the prescribed
   measure.
4. Move to the following item, process it, check readiness and retain it as the
   current item.

The D-frame in statement 3 is the key repair: `dchol ... dy daiin` is a carried
reference plus item and measure, not a collection of unrelated d-words.

## B3 now reads as a station ledger

B3 contains 86 events, 38 fields and 34 short statements. It is better read as
a series of local work cells around visible vessel/rim owners:

- collect or hold at the upper station;
- move to the next place and warm;
- measure and drain;
- transfer through the middle round station;
- feed, settle, introduce and drain at the lower basket-like station;
- run repeated short hold/settle/transfer cells through the unresolved middle
  owner;
- finish at the visible main pair with measure, continuation, transfer and
  draw-off cells.

B3-S021 is the long exception. Its two fields form a linked S-mode entry: first
measure the item and move it when ready; then set the prescribed measure at the
target, make the short preparation, move the current item, check readiness and
close the target transfer.

## What normalization removed

The old edition repeated a long speculative preamble before every B3 cell and
could make allographs sound like separate actions. The revised edition keeps
the visible owner once, reads each exact card once, and lets the registered
frame allograph contribute mode rather than a new noun or verb.

All 103 events, 42 fields and 38 statements have an explicit normalized form,
literal card reading and fluent German working translation. The next pass
should apply the same treatment to H1/H2 and B1, where CH- and O-mode passages
can sharpen the source-preparation versus continuation distinction.
