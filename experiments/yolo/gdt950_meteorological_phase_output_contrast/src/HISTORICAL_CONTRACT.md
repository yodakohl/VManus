# Historical content used by the fixed test

Primary sources:

- Aristotle, *Meteorology* I.9–12, Webster translation:
  https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/meteorology.1.i.html
- Aquinas, *Sentencia super Meteora* I.13–15, Leonine text (1886) presented by
  Corpus Thomisticum: https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cme1.html

The proposed distinction is condensation into liquid water versus freezing
into a solid. Dew/rain form the liquid pair; frost/snow are frozen forms. Hail
requires freezing water after it has formed. These source concepts motivate
the candidate meanings, not their Voynich spelling or attachment rule.

The full source includes temperature, location, wind and seasonal qualifications,
and rejects a competing high-cloud account of hail. The simple phase contract
does not pretend to translate those clauses or identify a copied passage.
No pictured nozzle, coloured patch, or falling dot is independently named here.
No circa1420 manuscript witness of a particular wording or diagram is established.
The historical theory is not replaced by a modern assertion that all phase
changes follow the source's physical account.
