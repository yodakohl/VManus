# GDT1225 — method

The question is whether the two-metric construction target, measured on a
pooled8000-group sample, is also portable to large single metadata strata.
This differs from1213's pooled page-order variation and1223's stronger
cross-reading conjunction. None of their decisions is replaced.

The source words come only from the unchanged1170guarded cache. A second
selector-first projection requests edition/page/locus/group index and
currier/section/hand metadata, not word contents, from the existing original
source. The179allowed selectors are identical. Both inputs are hash bound.
Every cached prose group must join exactly once with a matching page.

The fixed22working signs and the old definite-boundary conditions determine
eligibility. For each seed0..127, shuffle the same sorted prose page universe;
keep the old lexical locus/integer group ordering. For each reader and single
metadata field value, take the first8000 eligible groups. Insufficient cells
are NO_CAPACITY. Never combine values to obtain a desired result.

Count distinct exact raw whole strings and sum the ten largest exact whole
counts. Compare each to its own old reference with absolute400count bands.
Each cell needs122joint passes. The global portability claim fails if any
scoreable cell misses that fixed threshold. Publish capacities, all counts,
ordered-ID hashes and directional exceptions. Correlated labels/readings and
overlapping seeds do not supply independent replication or inferential p-values.

The entire pooled reconstruction must match all128prior1213samples and the

old metrics: it uses regex unit parsing, page buckets, and direct incremental
frequency accumulation. Same-author computational validation is not independent
manuscript or semantic confirmation. New category-specific bands are not fitted.

The result concerns whether an individual-register comparison can inherit a
mixed reference unchanged. It cannot identify the cause of any difference,
recover a word, explain the manuscript's content or rescue a failed writer.
