## Two composed contexts and frozen rules

1. **Treatment context.** A patient/condition phrase may precede the recurrent plant NP. The same plant can then be the subject or topical antecedent of an effect predicate; spatial and body-part arguments must be overt in the token stream or remain unknown. `daiin` always denotes that same plant. No locus-specific change of referent is permitted.
2. **Naming context.** `qokcho` always means “is called/named.” `dshey` always means “therefore.” A naming rationale must have an expressed animal/condition/plant-benefit operand in the same connected paragraph; the mere presence of `qokcho` does not bind a cause. `daiity` is the provisional epithet. I hypothesize that the hare's summer condition and the plant's relief relation are the operand for this name.

Other frozen construction rules:

- Every `daiin` is the same referential plant NP; doubled `daiin daiin` is a **topic plus resumptive subject** construction in this order, one rule paid once and applicable generally. It does not license deleting either occurrence. The syntax is provisional and counterexample-prone.
- Repeated whole forms keep their value. In particular `qokcho`, `cho`, `dshey`, `daiin`, and `chor/chol/shol` are not changed by position. Unspecified nouns, verbs, patient identity, and clause connections are UNKNOWN, not supplied from source prose.
- Line breaks are not sentence boundaries or semantic operators. I will not use line/locus IDs as keys. The remaining finite core may be extended with priced whole-form C0 guesses in the account, but this frozen portion cannot be repaired.
