## Remaining structure

Seventy-three events remain unmatched in sixty exact residual patterns. The
largest unresolved family is the exact Y-referent card written as `chey`, `chy`,
`dy`, `shy`, `sy` or `y`. The next important clue is page-specific rather than
global:

- the L-card predicted `ol` is usually `chol` on f10r, `qol` on f81v and
  `ol/sol` on f83r;
- `checthy` contracts toward `cthy` on the early plant pages but toward
  `shcthy` on f83r;
- the q-frame is suppressed for some f56r medial `choky` uses but retained in
  later bath records;
- the initial measure card competes between `saiin` and `daiin` by local hand.

These are exactly the kinds of variations expected if several scribes learned
one card inventory but preferred different entry frames. Trying to force one
more global spelling rule would erase that clue.

## Current working hand model

1. The exact card supplies the workshop value.
2. Field position supplies the first renderer layer.
3. A six-rule expansion handles the common q/e/s and boundary habits.
4. Page-hand dialect selects among the remaining registered frames.

The next pass should infer a few page-hand profiles from the residuals and ask
whether three or four profiles explain them economically. No new semantic root
is needed for this renderer problem.
