# GDT1214 — fixed B6 word grammar cannot write the short whole-word square

Status: `NO_SHORT_SQUARE_GRAMMAR_RENAMING`.

None of462 ordered distinct working-sign pairs makes ABAB parseable in the

## Why the short case suffices

The two distinct signs of ol can map to any of22*21 ordered pairs under a
bijection. The other20 assignments do not affect either short word. The old
parser is allowed more freedom than a canonical full text: isolated templates
may leave argument holes, and either word may carry a statement prefix.
Failure in this superset is enough to reject the stricter fixed writer.

The38 readable two-sign forms comprise29 learned roots, four short explicit
references and five statement-marker plus bare-builder combinations. Doubling
a root or reference leaves an unlicensed unmarked continuation after it;
doubling a statement/bare-builder pair introduces an internal statement mark
where the short inner stem cannot be parsed. This explanation is checked by
an independent finite grammar generator, not merely a second call to the old
parser. The complete relaxed language through four signs has2850words; all462
cells agree with the old-parser run. Same author, implementation/proof support.

The packet's original six bindings were rechecked, without a new target query.

No original photograph of these two loci was newly inspected. Working-sign
identity and whole-word correspondence are conditional assumptions; a different
native segmentation or allographic map is not ruled out. Three readings are
alternatives of one manuscript, not independent confirmation. The old paired-

## Decision

Stop considering a mere sign-table renaming of this word grammar as a direct
native writer. Any future proposal must state an actually different word
formation rule and its costs before selection. No automatic root-bank growth,
new spelling, added repeated-word exception or parameter search is performed.
An explicitly meaningful repetition operator in an invented language may be
kept RAW, with no native translation or automatic success implied.
