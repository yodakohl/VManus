# GDT356 external source audit

## Official manuscript attribution

Penn's detailed catalogue assigns fols. 145v–212r to *Commentary on the
Calendar* by Hovhannēs Vardapet, also known as Hovhannēs Sarkawag. It assigns
the following fols. 213r–244r to *On Astronomy* by Anania Shirakatsʻi and the
earlier fols. 3r–54v to Hakob Ghrimetsʻi's calendar commentary.

The narrow eight-band surfaces isolated in GDT355 are current fols. 209r,
209v, and 210r. They therefore lie inside the Hovhannēs commentary, not the
following Anania astronomy item and not Hakob's earlier commentary.

This is a work-range attribution. It does not identify the individual diagram
chapter, values, or designer.

## What scholarship establishes

Galstyan describes Hovhannēs' *Commentary of the Armenian Calendar* as a
composite theoretical and practical calendrical collection. The documented
inventory includes calendar exemplars for multiple peoples, a `Kharnakhoran`,
a lunar indicator, and 532-year tables. The paper discusses the 19-year lunar
cycle, epacts, and calculations of lunar birth/fullness. These facts make
calendar/computistical readings plausible at work level.

They do not identify the three Penn spiral pages. The paper provides no
folio-to-diagram concordance for LJS 443, no eight slot labels, and no reading
start or direction for those pages.

Broutian describes the related `Kharnakhoran` tradition as twelve large tables
that align the days and calendar landmarks of multiple peoples. That is useful
system context, but it is not evidence that an eight-lobed Penn diagram is one
of those tables. The documented lunar system is not an eight-phase key.

## Negative source findings

- The selected pages are not in the catalogued Anania astronomy item.
- No source inspected here calls the selected pages an eight-phase lunar table.
- No source supplies slot values, ownership, start, direction, or a second
  homologous manuscript folio.
- GDT355's twelve-lobe counterexample remains decisive against equating the
  curved-lobe idiom itself with the number eight.
