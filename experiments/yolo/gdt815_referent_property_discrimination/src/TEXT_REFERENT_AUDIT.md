# GDT815 — written carriers and actual label/prose bridges

2026-09-05. Text audit only; no independently named referent and no new
translation. All four GDT814 meanings remain C0. “Carrier” below means the
written word immediately before exact `okaiin`, not an identified noun or
syntactic head. Text recurrence does not establish the pictured owner.

## Answer and bounded method

There are concrete reusable text bridges, chiefly **okol** and **otedy**.
They connect an entire independent label, its occurrence in a complete local
paragraph, and another occurrence immediately before `okaiin`. This permits
a specific comparison between property and possessed-part accounts. It does
not yet choose either account or distinguish warm from cold.

The two raw transcription tables were projected with the existing
GDT812 `family_probe.query` helper. It executes `./vmanus-exp query-tsv` with
each of the GDT813 `SPEC.json`'s 39 selectors supplied as a separate `--allow`,
explicit columns, and forbidden f84/f84r prefixes before payload parsing.
The base columns were page, locus, line_number, kind, paragraph_start,
paragraph_end, eva_clean; the cross-reader columns were page, locus,
zl3b_clean, it2a_clean, rf1b_clean. There were 1062 permitted loci.

The search considered every exact `okaiin` in every reading, with a preceding
word within its source P paragraph or its complete non-P record. Interleaved
L records did not interrupt the P stream and were not inserted into it.
Circular C records were not turned into ordinary P paragraphs. This is a
scoped extraction, not a claim that every one of the 1062 loci was manually
read. The complete relevant paragraphs were retained in GDT814's reader;
the seven shorter paragraphs discussed below were read again with variants.

The 30 distinct immediately preceding forms were:

```text

dchor dsheol eeedy keey okal okol opoeey otalain otchey otedy
oteeey oteey oty qodain qokain qokedy r solkeey y ychey
```

Among these, exactly **okal, okol, otedy, oty, r, y** also occur as entire
non-P records in at least one reading. This is an exhaustive navigation
answer for this scope, not a frequency ranking or semantic score. The last
two include single-character L records, which are not thereby pictured
substance names. The remaining four supply the useful bridge shortlist.

## 1. okol: the strongest local owner candidate, still unnamed

The relevant exact source chain is:

```text

f88r.22 P: ychey okaiin chol cheor ol chorcholsal
f24v.4 P: ydal sh okol okaiin odaiin dlos
```

The whole `okol` in the label, the local prose occurrence, the entire .22,
and the entire f24v.4 agree in all three readings. IT2a reads qockhoy instead
of qockhol in f88r.19; this difference is not relevant to the exact `okol`
identity but must not disappear from the paragraph. f88r.18–22 and
f24v.1–5 are complete source-marked paragraphs, not isolated excerpts.

Conditional R construction: if `okol` in .19 introduces an owner A, then
`ychey okaiin` in .22 can mean “[ychey] of A”. A is now a specific written
candidate, not the wholly unspecified “something previously mentioned”.
Conditional Q construction: .22 applies the candidate property to ychey,
whereas f24v.4 can apply it to the same whole okol that serves as a label.

Missing: no evidence identifies okol's referent or establishes ychey as its
possessed part. No decoded anaphoric link joins .19 to .22. R still has other
possible antecedents in the paragraph, and Q still lacks thermal polarity.
`odaiin` in f24v.4 is NOT `daiin`; its meaning remains unknown.

GDT811 already recorded the label/prose identity. The new use is the fixed
Q/R comparison involving .22 and the independent `okol okaiin` construction;
