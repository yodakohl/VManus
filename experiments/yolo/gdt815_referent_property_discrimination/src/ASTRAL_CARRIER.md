# GDT815: Cancer-like centre does not supply a thermal word key

2026-09-05. Bounded visual and historical review of already admitted f72r3.
No new Voynich page, new image download, dictionary change, or semantic score.

## Finding

The central image is a strong **Cancer-like iconographic candidate**: two
elongated, segmented, clawed crustaceans, one above the other. This is more
specific than merely calling the panel astral. It does **not** make the
peripheral inscription `opoeey okaiin` a caption of Cancer, and Cancer itself
has no context-free thermal value across the relevant medieval literature.

There is an actual pre-1420 Latin counterexample to a tempting shortcut:
Cancer is cold/moist in a complexion table, but warm in a discussion of
weather and sign parts. Thus `Cancer picture -> okaiin = cold` and its
seasonal reverse `summer picture -> okaiin = warm` both omit necessary links.

## Historical counterexamples, with exact carrier and frame

The following are consulted scholarly transcriptions of historical texts,
not independent collations of the historical manuscript images.

| Carrier and predicate frame | What the historical source actually supplies |
|---|---|
| Cancer as a sign's complexion | *Archanum magni Dei*, section 4: “Cancer” — “Frigidus et humidus”. This is a cold/moist whole-sign classification. [PAL text](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/140/210/text/1) |
| Cancer in weather/sign-part description | Hugo of Santalla's *Quadripartitum*, II.13: “Cancer communiter serenus, calidus”. Its first, middle, final, left and right parts then receive differentiated weather descriptions. This is not a replacement complexion table. [PAL Book II](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/243/351/text/2) |
| Cancer and Leo in solar-house reasoning | The same Latin *Quadripartitum*, I.20, explains the luminaries' houses through greater heat when the Sun traverses these northern signs. [PAL Book I](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/243/351/text/1) |
| Individual Cancer stars | Book I.9 gives the stars in the feet Mercury with some Mars, those in the belly Saturn/Mercury, the cloudy breast group Moon/Mars, and the two Asses Mars/Sun. These are differentiated local stellar powers, not one inherited sign adjective. [PAL Book I](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/243/351/text/1) |

Dating matters. *Archanum* is catalogued as translated by Bernard of Gordon
on 22 December 1295; its Paris BnF lat.15353 witness has the relevant section
dated 1295–1315. This is pre-1420 evidence, not a claim that the witness was
copied around 1420. [Work](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/53),
[manuscript](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/140).

Madrid BNE10053, the base witness for the Hugo *Quadripartitum* transcription,
is catalogued as mid-thirteenth century, after 1246. Book I occupies
89ra–96rb, Book II 96rb–102rb. PAL reports other witnesses and its editorial
interventions; the sign name at the II.13 Cancer entry is supplied in the
margin of the base witness, not an unqualified untouched printed heading.
[Catalogue](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/243),
[transcription principles](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/243/351/text/1).

The modern Robbins translation of *Tetrabiblos* I.9/I.17 first helped locate
these contrasts; the retained argument above has a medieval Latin witness.
Its I.9 says eyes where Hugo's Latin says feet. Do not silently harmonize
those body-part readings or treat the translations as independent traditions
confirming a Voynich mapping. [Robbins translation](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/thayer/e/roman/texts/ptolemy/tetrabiblos/1b%2A.html).

An additional consulted Latin comparison, the *Commentum Cracoviense* of
1463, likewise distinguishes Cancer star groups and heat associated with
Cancer/Leo. Its Memmingen witness is **after 1463**, so it is not used as a
circa-1420 anchor. [Text, I.9/I.18](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/335/467/text/1),
[dating](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/335).
No decan identification or decan-ruler list is claimed from this search.

## What would make the comparison discriminating?

Three separate identifications are needed: **depicted subject**, **inscription
owner**, and **the property being predicated**. The first has a useful
Cancer-like candidate; the latter two remain unresolved.

- If the pair described the **whole sign's complexion**, cold/moist would be
  a historically concrete comparison, not a reason to prefer warm. Even
  then, the pair's unknown first word does not tell us which word expresses
  which quality, or whether either is a quality word.
- If it described **a particular star or depicted figure**, the whole-sign
  complexion does not choose its individual quality. The historical mixed
  stellar assignments are positive counterexamples to that inheritance rule.
- If it described **seasonal weather or a solar effect**, warmth is genuinely
  available in a pre-1420 source. That does not turn an adjective into an
  action or prove that the Voynich page is a weather table.

These are bounded rival frames, not permission to choose a different frame
for every difficult occurrence. In particular, `opoeey` is not assigned
“moist”, “star”, “Cancer”, “summer”, or a planet here; `okaiin` is not newly
assigned cold. GDT814's Q warm, R possessive genitive, N powder, and G copula
remain C0 hypotheses. The present panel does not select thermal polarity.

The useful result is a specific limit on the historical bridge: a named
picture alone is insufficient when the historical sources use different
property frames for that same name. A directly bound noun/property contrast
elsewhere is a better immediate target than another ring-order decoder.
