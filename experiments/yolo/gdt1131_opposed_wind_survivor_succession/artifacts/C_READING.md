## Actual whole proposed reading

**N.** Consider winds as kinds according to the regions from which they
blow. Take one kind and another, and retain their pair for comparison. Their
source positions may be diametrically opposed in an observer's horizon.
For distinct winds of those kinds confronting one another, distinguish
equal from unequal strength. Retain that comparison. When strength is
unequal, the original stronger wind continues through the confrontation
and its action causes the weaker wind to cease; when strength is equal,
they obstruct one another. Opposition in source position is distinct from
opposition in qualities: the latter requires reversal on both the hot/cold
and dry/moist axes, allowing mixtures and degrees. Retain that distinction
in the classification of these kinds. Relate the classification to the
observer and local horizon. A kind not locally perceived is not thereby
absent everywhere. Names refer to source regions and may participate in
neighboring classifications. This completes the account of kind position,
opposition, strength conditions, local observation and source naming.

**E.** Consider the rule for neighboring wind kinds under solar movement.
Take a preceding source kind and a distinct neighboring source kind around
the same observer's horizon. The sun's motion supplies a material cause:
approach elevates dry exhalation, whereas withdrawal removes its elevation.
If the sun withdraws from the first source, that supply ends and a wind
which previously blew there ceases. Subsequently the sun approaches the
neighboring source, and dry exhalation rises there. Retain those premises.
Under them a new wind of the neighboring kind is generated and blows
there. It is physically distinct from the first wind; its generation has
the new raised material and solar approach as its cause. The two source
positions retain the same observer-relative frame. The two indexed states
record the first wind's former blowing and cessation, followed by the
ceased first wind and blowing new wind. Withdrawal and first cessation
precede approach and new generation. Continued unbroken blowing of the
first wind would not satisfy this rule, and kind or name comparison cannot
erase the distinct participants or their provenance. The source relation
is neighboring succession, without an imposed opposite direction or four
leg cycle. Collect and universally quantify this implication; retain its
kind-and-relation description, publish it, and close the account.

These are generic predicates and conditional physics. Neither paragraph
asserts that its antecedent occurred. N and E are distinct universal topics,
with explicit opening and closure instructions. Their instance variables
are separately bound; no physical wind is carried invisibly from N into E.
C includes both suppression and succession as generic rules. Its assigned
label is therefore not a claim that these historical mechanisms exclude
one another.
