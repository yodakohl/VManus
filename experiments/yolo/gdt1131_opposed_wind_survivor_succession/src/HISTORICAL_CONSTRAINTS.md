## Strongest fair suppression account

II.12.1 [90864] is a conditional explanation of why spatially opposed winds
cannot jointly blow in the described confrontation. Its two alternatives
are equal strength, yielding mutual obstruction, and unequal strength,
yielding complete elimination of the weaker wind by the stronger. It permits
a generic physics account: no actual storm or previously observed pair must
be invented. II.12.2 [90865] defines incidence as a wind blowing against
another and causing that other to cease; the stronger, nearby winds often
repel weaker ones because their source region supplies abundant material.

If an account asserts a survivor, the fair interpretation retains the
stronger original participant through that encounter and attributes the
weaker participant's cessation to it. This is a reasonable implication of
the explanation, rather than an explicit source narrative with individuated
before/after tokens. The paragraph does not specify the stronger wind's
later lifetime, unchanged properties, numerical identity across seasons,
or destruction of the weaker wind's entire kind. It supplies no successor
generation rule merely from the fact of suppression.

Required when this claim is made: opposed relationship, relative-strength
condition, correct outcome branch, and an explicit distinction between the
original stronger participant and any subsequently generated participant.
An equal-strength survivor is unsupported. Identifying the weaker wind as
the survivor contradicts the unequal-strength explanation. Two opposed
winds persistently blowing together in the same described confrontation
contradict it. The hypothetical causal alternatives are not reports of two
opposed winds successfully coexisting. Conversely, II.12.1 explicitly allows
non-opposed winds to blow together toward the same region from distinct
source signs, with unequal force; different origins alone do not require
mutual destruction.

## Strongest fair succession-after-cessation account

II.12.3 [90866] defines gyration as generation of a nearby wind around the
horizon **after the first wind ceases**. Its example is cessation of one
source wind followed by generation of an adjacent wind. The general cause
is solar movement: approach to different regions raises dry exhalation and
generates winds there; withdrawal removes elevation of material and causes
cessation. The successive positions correspond to the solar cycle.

Required when claiming this mechanism: a preceding wind, its cessation,
subsequent generation, an adjacent source relation, and the specified causal
account if solar gyration is asserted. The generated participant must be
distinguished from the preceding participant; mere renaming, continued
unbroken blowing of the first wind, or kind equality does not establish
this generation event. The text does not settle continuity of constituent
matter or furnish individual identity criteria beyond the events and source
relations. It gives an ordering, not an exact interval, mandatory hiatus,
clock time, uniform speed, or measured angle.

Suppression is not the required cause of the first cessation here. Neither
collision with water nor reflection from water is given as this mechanism.
The ground reflection in II.12.2 [90865] concerns cloud gathering; downward
displacement by another wind in II.12.6 [90869] is another phenomenon.
Treating either as the only route to II.12.3's new adjacent wind conflates
mechanisms. II.12.6 also gives annual-wind sequences with different observer
contexts; it does not impose one universal four-leg cycle.

## Strongest fair kind or relational account

II.11.1 [90855] expressly announces position, opposition, which kinds can
blow together, names, number, and properties. Thus a connected account may
describe kinds, relational predicates and conditional processes, rather
than narrate one actual weather event. II.11.3–5 [90857–90859] distinguish
diametrical spatial opposition from qualitative opposition; the latter
concerns both relevant qualities, with varying mixtures and degrees.
II.11.6–9 [90860–90863] discuss source-based names, intermediate kinds,
regional visibility, participation in neighboring kinds and broadened names.

The strongest fair account can express nontrivial strength conditionals,
source relations, temporal generation rules and qualified effects at the
kind level. It is not restricted to a name list or an empty assertion that
winds are winds. A generic rule does not assert that its antecedent occurred.
If an actual episode is claimed, it needs actual participants and conditions;
kind equality alone cannot establish physical identity. A generic account
that asserts succession still owes its ordered events. A static formulation
with explicit before/after states can represent those obligations; the
historical source does not prefer executable state mutation over an equally
specified relational description.

## Shared controls and retained counterexamples

* **Orientation and locality:** II.11.2 [90856] distinguishes observer
  horizons; II.11.4 [90858] constructs a solar-relative horizon diagram.
  II.11.6 [90860] names winds by places **from which** they blow. Source
  bearing is not destination bearing. II.11.7–8 [90861–90862] distinguish
  winds absent from local observation from winds absent altogether, and
  observer proximity from a separate claim about greater generation.
  II.12.2/5 [90865,90868] explain proximity relative to the northern
  inhabitants. II.12.6 [90869] explicitly varies sequence by western/eastern
  observers. None fixes the manuscript diagram's orientation or owner.
* **Place versus quality:** II.11.5 [90859] gives two types of opposition;
  they must not be silently interchanged. Its listed intermediate qualitative
  assignments should not be repaired into a uniquely uniform table:
  II.11.7 [90861] qualifies which opposed winds are locally perceptible and
  says intermediates have opposition participatively. II.11.9 [90863]
  permits neighboring names and broader quality-based classifications.
* **Common effects:** II.12.4 [90867] allows opposed winds to produce the
  same effect accidentally while having opposed effects intrinsically.
  Cooling may gather heat locally; opposed winds may both moisten or dry.
  Equal effects therefore neither imply participant identity nor refute
  opposition. The final moistening of a usually drying wind is explicitly
  attributed to mixing with humid vapor.
* **Conditional effects:** II.12.2/5 [90865,90868] permit cloud formation by
  cold and clearing by force in related winds; clearing is qualified when
  severe cold thickens vapor. A universal inference from stronger wind to
  clear sky is contradicted. II.12.6 [90869] distinguishes a violent cloud
  outflow from the upward/downward displacement of a wind by another.
* **Coexistence:** suppression, solar succession, generic taxonomy and
  qualified common effects coexist in this source. Combining mechanisms is
  fair if their participants, conditions and transitions are stated. Their
  labels alone do not make them exclusive rivals.

## What the source does not supply

No Voynich lexeme, phonetic value, parser, stem/prefix meaning, exact token
ordering, paragraph correspondence, wind-name identification, manuscript
cardinal assignment, four-part diagram semantics, water identification,
mouth/head identification or independent confirmation is supplied. The page
does contain named winds, cardinal axes, intermediate winds and a solar
cycle; those historical facts cannot be donated as manuscript identifications.
It specifies no mandatory original-only consumer and no semantic advantage
of a dynamic program over a complete static two-time-state account. It
cannot settle alternative-reader equality or choose meanings by successful
typechecking. Modern editorial attribution and paragraph anchors identify
this source, not the manuscript's author, date or exemplar.
