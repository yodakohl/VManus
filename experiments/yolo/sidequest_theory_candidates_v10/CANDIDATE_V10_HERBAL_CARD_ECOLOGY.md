# V10 candidate — opaque Herbal whole-card ecology

Date: 2026-08-21

Status: speculative sidequest theory generation. This is not a GDT result, a
decipherment or a translation.

## Scope and method

The primary pages are `f10r` and `f56r`. `f11r` and `f55v` are comparison
pages only. I used the compact current theory and guarded, explicit-column
slices of the current GDT327 exact-joint-tuple interlinear and its GDT276
surface display. The selector allow-list was exactly:

```text
f10r f11r f55v f56r
```

The raw selector was checked before row parsing and `f84*` was forbidden. No
`f84` or `f84r` image, transcription, row or metadata was accessed. Exact card
identity below means exact GDT327 joint-tuple identity; hashes are abbreviated
only for display. No substring, edit, phonetic, language or glyph-semantic
similarity entered the analysis.

The available projection contains 100 exact-card events on the four pages. The
two target pages contribute 65 events, 12 physical-line/field packets, three
records and 42 exact types. Thirty-two of those types occur once on the two
target pages; ten recur. Thus the central fact to explain is not a small
repeated Herbal vocabulary. It is a small repeated construction deck embedded
in a large local tail.

GDT327 is a formal projection rather than the entire diplomatic transcription.
Consequently, “complete record” below means every GDT327 event in that record,
not every uncertain or unsupported visible group on the manuscript page.

The `H1/H2/H3` labels matter. They are exact cards shared by visually distinct
plants, so they are poor candidates for the unique plant name. They could be
common plant parts, properties, preparations, uses or construction heads. The
fixed four pages do not discriminate among those content classes.

`O56` is different. Its four occurrences are all on f56r, twice field-first
and twice field-medial, under `sh` and `ch` rendering. It precedes different
local tails and crosses the apparent visual text blocks. Its best source-class
reading is therefore not the plant's proper name but a page-active carrier:

```text
O56 ~= CURRENT SIMPLE / OF IT / CURRENT PREPARATION
```

This is deliberately a functional paraphrase. An ordinary short word, an
abbreviation, a notational topic-return or a recurrent content medium could
all realize it. Literal WATER remains an admissible rival for `O56`, especially
if the passage concerns soaking, decoction or a wet habitat, but it is not the
leader: no water is independently shown, `O56` is absent from the three other
Herbal pages, and the card's first/medial mobility is equally natural for an
inherited-topic carrier.

## Role competition

### Plant identity/name

A plant name should normally be page- or article-specific. The 32 singleton
types provide ample capacity, and the unique record-entry cards
`65f320e7`/`7249edc4` on f10r and `b9d7b6d6` on f56r are possible title or
section heads. But one pictured plant can have two f10r records with different
heads, and f56r's single record contains no repeated unique owner token. The
more economical account is that the drawing silently owns the dossier. A
written name or synonym may occur in the unique tail, but no exact card can yet
be selected as THE name.

### Plant part or visible property

This is plausible for `H1/H2/H3`, because all four plants have multiple
visible organs. It is not identified: the same cards do not occupy homologous
positions, and no fixed drawing supplies a repeated independently owned leaf,
root or flower referent. `CTHY` remains the best broad property/state card but
does not license HOT, DRY, LEAF or ROOT.

### Habitat, moisture or water

Historical herbals genuinely record distribution, habitat, gathering and
preparation. A moisture/habitat clause therefore belongs in the allowed source
ontology. It probably resides, if present, in a page-local value rather than a
universal card. The visually striking f56r plant has previously encouraged
wetland/sundew analogies, but the drawing is not a secure botanical
identification. No one whole card gains a water gloss from this ecology.

### Preparation/action and application/use

The second f10r record has repeated `RELATION–ITEM–VALUE` machinery, while
f56r repeatedly resumes a page-local carrier before distinct tails. That is
more compatible with multiple properties, preparations or uses than with a
single plant-name list. Yet whole-card recurrence cannot separate PREPARE from
APPLY or CURE. The exact operations/indications are likely among singletons.

### Amount, quality or classification

`AIIN`, `CTHY` and the compact `H1/H2/H3` deck make this plausible. The
strongest licensed expansion remains `AIIN ~= current/stated standard`, whose
referent may be quantity, degree, duration, class or setting. No numeral or
humoral degree is assigned.

### Index/code versus abbreviated prose

The target pages are structurally unlike the many short committed Biological
cells. Each Herbal line is one long open field and statements continue across
line breaks. This favors compressed clause-like prose over a rigid checklist.
But the exact-card inventory has a common deck plus copied rare tail, so it is
not ordinary uncompressed prose either. The winning mechanism is a mixture.

## Historical fit

The proposed source architecture is ordinary for a late-medieval practical
Herbal even though its encoding is not identified.

- The [British Library description of Add MS
  29301](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002020783), made around
  1420–30, records plant drawings captioned with names, a Middle English
  *Circa instans* beginning with an Aloe quality statement, and adjacent
  recipes and cures. It demonstrates picture, identity, quality and use layers
  in one contemporary medical manuscript.
- The [British Library catalogue for Egerton MS
  747](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001983805) lists the illustrated
  *Tractatus de herbis*, an antidotary, substitution lists, weights and
  measures, and a plant/ingredient synonym section. A workshop user therefore
  had reason to distinguish owner/name, property, replacement, measure and use.
- The [Wellcome overview of typical medieval Herbal
  entries](https://wellcomecollection.org/stories/plant-portraits) summarizes
  synonyms, characteristics, distribution/habitat, gathering, preparation and
  curative recipes. Habitat/water is a legitimate field, but only one among
  several.
- The [Wellcome catalogue of MS.MSL.136](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/md9rpgmh)
  describes a mid-fifteenth-century recipe collection plus plant-name,
  synonym and property glossary, including copying by a scribe who sometimes
  did not understand the longer material. This supports a learnable common
  deck plus faithfully copied local tail without implying a cipher.

These are mechanism comparators, not donors. They do not identify either
Voynich plant, language, card or phrase.

## Selected architecture

The winning V10 candidate is a **picture-owned compressed Herbal article with
a common construction deck and locally copied lexical/value cards**.

```text
PRE-DRAWN PLANT
  -> silently supplies CURRENT SIMPLE
  -> optional unique ARTICLE/SECTION HEAD
  -> one or more clause packets reflowed around the image

CLAUSE PACKET
  := TOPIC/RESUME?
     + CATEGORY / RELATION / CURRENT-STANDARD cards
     + page-local NAME / PROPERTY / HABITAT / PREPARATION / USE values
     + optional attached COMMIT

TEXTUAL LEXICON
  := portable record cards (Y, OR, AIIN)
     + Herbal construction cards (L/O, CTHY, H1/H2/H3)
     + page-local carrier (O56)
     + large singleton lexical/value tail
```

This decision allocates the nine requested possibilities as follows:

| possible content | winning placement |
|---|---|
| plant identity/name | picture ownership plus possible unique article-head/tail card |
| plant part/visible property | page-local values; possibly H1/H2/H3, unresolved |
| habitat/moisture/water | optional page-local value, not universal card |
| preparation/action | recurrent category frame plus local value |
| application/use/condition | recurrent category frame plus local value |
| amount/quality/classification | AIIN/CTHY/H-family frame plus local value |
| nonsemantic index/copied code | renderer and some rare exemplar cards, but not whole article |
| abbreviated ordinary prose | likely upstream clause payload |
| explicit mixture | **selected**: prose-derived article compiled to exact cards |

Why this wins:

1. It explains 32 singleton types as real page-specific payload rather than
   noise.
2. It explains why only four types cross f10r/f56r and why those types do not
   look like unique plant names.
3. It explains `O56` as a current-topic carrier without requiring every line
   to repeat the plant name.
4. It preserves multi-line record continuity and the very low Herbal-A close
   rate.
5. It is learnable in a small workshop: memorize common cards and copy the
   rare botanical/technical tail from an exemplar or local register.
6. It admits water, qualities, preparation and use where medieval entries need
   them without pretending the internal ecology chooses among those glosses.

## Strongest alternatives

### A. Ordinary abbreviated prose

This explains the singleton tail and multi-line continuation very well. It
loses only because exact renderer normalization and the recurrent card deck
make the surviving representation more regimented than unstructured prose.
It remains the probable source layer.

### B. Nonsemantic model-sheet or cipher/code paragraph

This can explain copying, local types and no direct visual alignment. It loses
because the common/local vocabulary split, picture ownership and historically
ordinary Herbal article ecology jointly offer a simpler practical use.
Internal evidence alone cannot eliminate it.

### C. Plant-name plus synonym register

This predicts many page-local types, but it poorly explains the repeated
relation/item/reference machinery and f10r's two records. Names and synonyms
may occupy part of the first article, not the whole dossier.

### D. Water/decoction dossier

This could make `O56` a recurring working medium and fit a wet-habitat reading
of the f56r drawing. It fails as the leader because no water occurrence is
independently owned, the exact card is absent from the control pages, and the
same ecology is explained by generic topic resumption.

### E. Four-quality or fixed checklist

The Bio terminal vectors make this tempting. Herbal-A instead has long open
fields, no stable columns and only one attached close across f10r/f56r. A fixed
quality grid is not supported.

## Counterevidence and failure conditions

1. **No exact content owner.** Neither picture nor record boundary identifies a
   particular name card.
2. **Incomplete formal projection.** Unsupported source groups may alter fluent
   article wording even though the listed GDT327 event ecology is exhaustive.
3. **Four cross-page cards are ambiguous.** Common parts, operations,
   properties and grammar all predict reuse on different plants.
4. **O56 may be mere frequency.** Its four occurrences are all one record and
   one page; no cross-page transfer supports the proposed inherited-topic role.
5. **No order proof.** Identity/constitution before use/preparation is
   historically natural but not established by these strings.
6. **Image identifications are unstable.** Neither a scabious-like f10r nor a
   sundew/wetland-like f56r identification is secure enough to own a card.
7. **Line geometry is not semantics.** Short f56r packets may be forced entirely
   by the pre-drawn image.

The selected architecture should lose if any of the following is found within
the fixed pages:

- a purported local value repeatedly occupying a purely renderer-determined
  position with no content variation;
- an `H1/H2/H3` card shown to be the unique owner of incompatible plant
  identities;
- a complete fixed Herbal stencil whose slots explain the singleton tail
  without clause-like continuity;
- `O56` failing to preserve an active topic under a source-independent
  antecedent test;
- or a historically plausible nonsemantic copying mechanism predicting the
  exact common/local split more economically.

## Fixed-page predictions

1. If `O56` resumes the active simple/preparation, removing it should leave the
   same broad clause frame but make the local tail less explicitly attached to
   the page topic; it should not select one fixed following card.
2. `H1/H2/H3` should recur with different page-local neighbors and should not
   behave as unique page titles.
3. Unique record-entry cards should be more page/record-specific than
   `H1/H2/H3`, even if their exact lexical category remains unknown.
4. A genuine water/medium reading should require a repeated independently
   visible or otherwise owned aqueous context; the current four pages provide
   none.
5. The common technical deck should survive across hands better than the local
   botanical tail, while its exact source meanings may remain register-bound.
6. Continued source recovery should yield clause-like continuation across
   Herbal physical line breaks, not Bio-like independent committed cells.

## Decision

The opaque Herbal payload most plausibly lives in an **explicit mixture**:
picture-supplied plant ownership, a small deck of source-functional category
and relation cards, and a large page-local tail carrying names, descriptions,
qualities, habitat, preparation and use content. The first content-like advance
is therefore not a plant-word guess. It is the localization:

```text
COMMON CARDS  -> construction/category/reference skeleton
LOCAL TAIL    -> most lexical Herbal content
PICTURE       -> current plant owner
```

Within that architecture, `O56 ~= CURRENT SIMPLE / OF IT / CURRENT
PREPARATION` is the strongest new whole-card working hypothesis. It is not a
confirmed pronoun, noun, water term or translation.
