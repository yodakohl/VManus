## Selected architecture

Herbal A on f10r/f56r is best reconstructed as a **strongly abbreviated prose
article inherited from a Herbal or materia-medica exemplar**, written into the
space left by a previously drawn plant:

```text
INHERITED HERBAL ARTICLE
  name/synonym, description, quality, habitat, preparation and/or use clauses
       -> workshop abbreviation and exact learned cards
       -> image-first physical reflow around the pictured page topic
       -> open Herbal-A lines with a small portable deck
          plus a large page-local content tail
```

This revises the manuscript-wide form theory in one important way. The Bio
register can be a committed cell system, while Herbal A remains open compressed
prose. A common workshop can teach both modes. Physical fields made by a plant
stem or available page space are not automatically semantic slots, and a
logical statement may continue across physical lines.

## Water and visible properties

Habitat, moisture, water, decoction or a liquid medium are ordinary possible
contents of a medieval Herbal article. Nothing on f10r/f56r independently owns
a water referent: no secure pool, stream, vessel, irrigation feature or aquatic
organ is localized, and no exact card can be called WATER. Water is therefore
a live clause possibility, not the page key or default gloss.

Likewise no card is assigned LEAF, ROOT, FLOWER, TUBER, HOT, COLD, DRY or WET.
The image establishes the page topic and constrains plausible source content;
it does not map individual cards to depicted parts.

## Historical calibration

The candidates document near-period mechanisms including the illustrated
Herbal LJS 419, Sloane MS 4016, the 1415 Tadhg Ó Cuinn materia-medica text,
Lelamour-type Herbal article ordering and multiple-scribe practical books.
Together they support inherited entries containing names, descriptions,
qualities, habitat and uses, plus image-sensitive reflow and workshop
abbreviation. They do not identify the Voynich source, plant, language or
geography.

## Strongest rivals and loss conditions

The strongest rival is a picture-owned indexed medical form. It would overtake
the prose model if the same opaque cards occupied homologous image-owned slots
across the fixed Herbal pages, or if the open lines decomposed into stable
semantic cells after image interruptions were removed.

The prose model should lose if:

1. a once-per-page exact card is independently localized as the pictured plant
   owner on multiple fixed pages;
2. page-local cards align repeatedly with leaf/root/flower/habitat positions;
3. Herbal-A records reveal the same short attached-commit stencil as Bio;
4. the large singleton tail collapses into a small auditable value table;
5. line/paragraph continuation fails to follow available image space.

A nonsemantic exemplar/pattern-book theory remains possible but explains the
long lexical-looking articles and thematic codex organization poorly.

## Claim ceiling

V10 supports one register-specific source architecture:

> pictured Herbal dossier + abbreviated open article + page-local content tail

It establishes no plant identity, clause order, word, lexical meaning, water
term, language or plaintext. `OWNER-10` and `O56` remain anonymous formal
candidates. `f84` and `f84r` remained sealed throughout all four passes.
