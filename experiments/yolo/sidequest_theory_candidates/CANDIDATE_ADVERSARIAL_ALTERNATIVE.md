# Candidate: visual-indexed workshop pattern book

Updated: 2026-08-21.

Status: adversarial sidequest candidate, not a GDT result and not a
translation. This reconstruction was developed blind from only
`VOYNICH_CURRENT_ROUTE.md` and `SIDEQUEST_SCRIBE_WORKSHOP_CURRENT.md`. The ten
fixed pages alone are in scope. `f84` and `f84r` remain sealed.

## Leading theory

The strongest alternative to the medical-workshop reading is a
**visual-indexed workshop pattern book and advanced copy/form-filling manual**.
Its immediate purpose is not to preserve ten ordinary-language dossiers about
plants, treatments and stars. It preserves reusable page patterns, card
sequences, field shapes, label arrays and rendering conventions with which a
small group learned, demonstrated or transmitted an artificial note-taking
practice.

The pictures are therefore not merely illustrations of prose referents. They
are the stable, memorable addresses of exercise and model classes. A plant
selects a long-description pattern; a vessel/body tableau selects a densely
checked form; a circle selects a spatial labelling or enumeration pattern. The
writing can still carry local information, but much of its observable
regularity comes from **performing the notation correctly**, not from recurring
medicinal propositions.

This is best called a *pattern-book hypothesis*, not a novice's penmanship
book. The material is too structured to reduce to pen trials. It may be a
master's exemplar, a fair compilation of approved exercises, or a teaching
object made by several already competent hands. Nor does “artificial notation”
mean meaningless pseudo-text: it means that the recoverable cards may encode
form slots, options, class markers and completion states more directly than
spoken words.

The core contrast is:

```text
medical hybrid:  picture identifies a technical subject; text records WHAT/HOW/WHEN
pattern book:     picture identifies an exercise/template family; text instantiates HOW TO NOTATE IT
```

Both predict register-conditioned writing. The pattern-book model better
explains why exact form behaviour is currently much stronger than any semantic
anchor, why the same abstract cards cross unlike pictured domains, and why
local diagram namespaces need not translate into one content system.

## Executable generation

```text
MODEL REPERTORY
  plant motifs | figure/vessel compositions | radial/star constructions
        down
CHOOSE VISUAL EXEMPLAR FIRST
  copy or adapt an image that fixes page class and available writing spaces
        down
SELECT EXERCISE/FIELD TEMPLATE
  long open dossier | many checked cells | concentric/spatial label array
        down
INSTANTIATE CARD SLOTS
  portable frame cards + register deck + page-specific variants
        down
MARK COMPLETION
  leave field open or append a licensed DY/B3 close
        down
REFLOW AND RENDER
  fit cards around the drawing; reset line-entry habits; carry one item when needed
        down
WORKSHOP REVIEW/REUSE
  preserve the page as an exemplar for another hand or later exercise
```

An executable prose-page grammar is:

```text
PAGE     := PRE_DRAWN_MODEL + TEMPLATE_INSTANCE+
INSTANCE := RECORD_STYLE + SLOT_PACKET+
SLOT_PACKET := FIELD (FIELD)*
FIELD    := FRAME_CARD* + VARIABLE_CARD+ + optional CHECKED_CLOSE
SURFACE  := render(card, hand, register, line_position, prior_close)
```

For the circle pages, where no joint cards are available:

```text
CIRCLE_PAGE := GEOMETRIC_MODEL + ZONE_LOCAL_LABEL_SET
LABEL_SET   := place(surface_item, indexed_position_or_compartment)
```

This generator makes specific commitments. A new copyist can produce a legal
page by knowing the visual class, one template, its local deck and the renderer;
the copyist need not possess a sentence-by-sentence plaintext. Exact cells
remain licensed exemplars rather than being freely generated by a universal
grammar, matching the failure of sparse-cell and unseen-host factorization.

## How it explains every fixed page class

### Herbal: f10r, f11r, f55v, f56r

These are four **long-form model sheets**. Each plant image supplies an
unmistakable visual index and a complex outline worth preserving; the adjacent
rows demonstrate how the long/open notation style is laid out around such an
object. The five `4O` and two `5O` rows are repeated lesson forms: four or five
open fields populated with different cards, not necessarily four or five
semantic recipe steps.

This allows three progressively stronger readings:

1. conservative: the cards are real descriptors, but the page was selected as
   a reusable description template;
2. intermediate and preferred: some cards are descriptors, while portable
   cards mostly identify slot type, relation or admissible value class;
3. radical: the plant is only a mnemonic/model and most writing is a legal
   formal instantiation with little external denotation.

`f55v` is expected to bridge the Biological B pages because it demonstrates
the B-compatible version of the long-form exercise: B wrappers and closure
habits are being applied to a Herbal layout. Its low transfer of exact closers
then reflects template-local completion marks, not a different language or a
shared medical substance.

### Biological: f81v, f82r, f83r

These are **checked-cell form exemplars**. Their figures, containers, conduits
and outlets create many bounded visual regions and thus furnish a natural
teaching surface for short entries, alternatives and closures. The 115 fields,
short mean field length, and 85 attached closes are precisely what a
form-filling lesson should amplify.

The repeated `1C | 3C | 1C | 4O` and `2C | 4O` shapes are the strongest
positive evidence. In the best repeated stencil, eight of nine cards vary but
the same final `shedy` closer survives. That is easier to describe as a form
with variable boxes and a fixed completion convention than as two nearly
identical medical instructions whose lexical contents almost wholly differ.

The pictures need not be decoded as baths or women's health. They may preserve
apparatus/body motifs useful to a practical workshop, but their immediate
codicological role is to own and spatially cue a form. “Checked-cell exercise”
is a structural function, not a claim that the historical makers called it an
exercise.

### f67r2

This is a **nested-zone selector model**. Its separate 7-, 12- and central
inventories teach that labels belong to zones and that identical geometry does
not imply a universal label dictionary. The central item can function as the
exercise key or title, while outer labels instantiate compartment-local
choices. No planet, weekday, zodiac or astronomical value is assigned.

### f68r1

This is a **spatial catalogue model**: one centre plus 28 noncentral labelled
stars. Its lesson is placement around a field, not necessarily cyclic reading.
The absence of authorial cyclic order is expected if stars are visual loci for
independent labels. Concrete functional parse: `central label = catalogue
owner/key`; `star label = member identifier or permitted local value`. These
are candidate functions, not word meanings.

### f69v

This is an **ordered alternation model** with 28 entries. LONG/SHORT alternation
is the controlling exercise constraint, comparable to alternating ruled slots
or two entry classes. Unlike f68r1, order is part of the template. Concrete
functional parse: `LONG = expanded/member-with-qualifier form`; `SHORT = bare
member/class form`. No day, mansion, star or lunar value is licensed.

The three circle pages thus belong together at the pedagogical level—ways of
organising labelled information in space—while retaining separate local decks.
That explains both their family resemblance and the failure of direct f68-to-
f69 or A-65 identity.

## Concrete card and boundary parses

The following are deliberately functional and falsifiable. None is an English
translation, lexical item or POS assignment.

| observed unit | candidate function in this theory | reason |
|---|---|---|
| AIIN exact card | variable-bearing entry or value token | broad seven-page portability; balanced FIRST/MIDDLE/LAST placement; rarely immediately closed |
| exact Y card | frame/type selector | broad portability; often interior; can flank AIIN |
| exact L/O card | relation or allowed-option marker | shared across unlike registers despite surface `CHOL/OL`; moderate proximity to closure |
| exact CTHY card | qualification/state slot marker | almost entirely medial, compatible with modifying an entry rather than naming the picture |
| attached DY/B3 | checked completion of a local field | attachment and B/Bio enrichment fit resolution of a form cell |
| free DY-like Y rendering | ordinary Y-card realization | must remain distinct from attached closure |
| surface `s` | line-entry rendering cue | enriched at physical-line entry without needing RESUME semantics |
| surface `q` after close | post-check rendering cue | follows an already licensed close; does not mean NEXT |

The sole cross-page exact path:

```text
Y -> AIIN -> Y
```

is parsed as a **framed variable**: `select frame/class — instantiate value —
reassert or balance frame/class`. On f10r it closes a Herbal field; on f83r it
opens a Bio field before an LCHE close. That mobility is awkward for a fixed
phrase but natural for a reusable notation drill. The wrappers (`CHY TAIIN
SHY` versus `CHEY DAIIN CHEY`) belong to rendering context, not to agreement
or translated prefixes.

The `qokaiin` repeat from `f82r.3` field-final to `f82r.4` field-initial is
best read as **copied-forward working value**: the last filled value becomes
the first value available on the next physical line. It could be a scribal
catch/carry device or genuine repeated content; one event among 46 transitions
does not establish a continuation operator. A useful negative prediction is
that future carries should duplicate the full exact card, not merely add a
universal surface “resume” marker.

## Multiple scribes and visual-first production

The theory requires neither a lone inventor nor independent authors:

```text
MASTER MODEL SHEETS      shared motifs and page templates
COMMON CARD LEDGER       portable legal entries
HAND-SPECIFIC EXEMPLARS  wrappers, join/space and closure habits
REGISTER DECKS           long form, checked-cell form, circle labels
COPYISTS                 instantiate and preserve selected models
```

Several hands can therefore share exact cards but differ in wrappers and
closure frequency. Register effects arise because the task changes; hand
effects arise because rendering is learned from local exemplars. `f55v` is the
expected cross-task training sheet where a B hand/deck performs a Herbal-form
model.

Visual-first production is not an incidental accommodation. The drawing must
be selected and placed before the fields because it determines the exercise
class, the compartments and the remaining line space. Text then flows around
the available geometry. Physical lines are reflow opportunities; the field
close, not the line end, marks local completion. A continued dossier can cross
a line without requiring ordinary sentence syntax.

## Comparison with the medical hybrid

| fact | medical WHAT/HOW/WHEN hybrid | visual pattern book |
|---|---|---|
| plants, body/apparatus and circles coexist | heterogeneous practical medicine/astrology | heterogeneous repertory of visual and notation templates |
| portable exact cards cross Herbal/Bio | abstract technical vocabulary or codebook | shared slot/control deck |
| only one longer shared exact path | strong register-local terminology | expected: templates share primitives, not phrases |
| Bio has many short closed cells | terse application/apparatus records | checked-form training surface |
| repeated stencil keeps closer while 8/9 cards vary | same process form with variable contents | direct form-filling exemplar signature |
| f55v bridges B ecology but not most exact closers | shared B production plus topic addendum | B hand practices long-form template with local close rules |
| circle pages have separate namespaces | distinct astronomical instruments | distinct spatial-index exercises |
| no grounded lexeme or clause | missing card-to-source dictionary | predicted if notation function outruns denotation |

The medical model remains stronger on iconographic common sense: medicinal
plants and bathing/apparatus scenes naturally invite medical content, and
medical-astronomical miscellanies are historically attested. The pattern-book
candidate wins only if one weights the exact structural evidence more heavily
than topical resemblance. It is consequently a serious alternative, not yet
the overall winner.

## Awkward facts and failure modes

1. **Expense and finish.** A substantial illustrated parchment codex is an
   expensive place for disposable exercises. The hypothesis therefore needs a
   retained master exemplar or prestige teaching object, not casual practice
   leaves.
2. **No obvious corrections, alphabets or teacher marks are given in the
   fixed evidence.** A literal beginner's copybook is weak. “Advanced pattern
   manual” is safer but less sharply testable.
3. **Iconographic fit still points to content.** Four plants plus bath-like
   figures may genuinely be medical records. The model must not evade this by
   calling every picture arbitrary.
4. **Formal legality is not proof of pedagogy.** Any templated technical text
   can produce fields, closures and register decks. The hypothesis needs a
   distinctive template-copying prediction.
5. **Circle labels may encode real astronomical values.** Spatial exercises do
   not explain why 28 is repeated, only why different page organizations can
   coexist.
6. **The 381 prose events are a small fixed slice.** One carry and one
   cross-page formula cannot identify a curriculum.
7. **“Artificial notation” risks becoming semantics-light unfalsifiability.**
   It must predict exact copying constraints, errors or staged complexity, not
   merely absorb every undecoded form.

These difficulties rule out the strongest claim that the pages are meaningless
practice. The defensible claim is narrower: their *immediate organizing
purpose* may be exemplary/pedagogical even if some cards denote real technical
content.

## Discriminating predictions

Tests must remain inside the ten fixed pages and keep `f84/f84r` sealed.

1. **Slot stability over content stability.** Additional repeated stencils
   should preserve field count, close status and one or more slot-class cards
   while varying most content cards. Medical phrase copying predicts more
   stable exact multi-card content paths.
2. **Complexity staging.** Within a register, early or visually simpler model
   regions should use shorter/fewer card inventories, followed by expansions
   that reuse the same field skeleton. A random semantics-light generator need
   not show staged reuse.
3. **Cross-hand exemplar signatures.** Shared exact cards should retain
   structural slot positions across hands while wrappers and close realizations
   shift. Shared medical lexemes could move more freely with proposition
   structure.
4. **Copy-forward, not operator.** Any second carry candidate should repeat an
   exact full card at adjacent line boundary; there should be no single
   universal surface continuation token.
5. **Error/repair asymmetry.** If visible overwriting or anomalous cells can be
   assessed on already fixed images, deviations should cluster at slot or
   closure boundaries, where a copyist loses place, rather than at particular
   pictured entities.
6. **Herbal/Bio bridge specificity.** `f55v` should match Bio-B more in renderer
   and slot discipline than in long exact content sequences. This is already
   directionally supported by shared types versus closer transfer.
7. **Astro local grammar.** Within each circle page, label length/class should
   correlate with geometric slot type; across pages, surface identity should
   transfer poorly even when cardinality matches.
8. **Visual ownership.** Text density and field breaks should respond to
   available image space more strongly than a sentence-like clause model, but
   exact field closes should remain stable under line reflow.

The highest-value adjudication would preregister stencil positions and compare
three models on held fixed-page instances: (A) exact phrase reuse, (B)
slot-preserving variable fill, and (C) frequency-only generation. The candidate
earns promotion only if B predicts held cards/closures beyond both controls.

## Historical calibration

Historical analogies support the production mechanism, not Voynich identity:

- The Metropolitan Museum's *Pen and Parchment* overview distinguishes
  sketches, model books and unfinished manuscripts; it describes model books
  as repositories of letters, figures and motifs for study and reuse, and
  notes that preliminary drawings reveal production order:
  <https://blog.metmuseum.org/penandparchment/drawings-and-the-artistic-process/>.
- The Morgan Library's late-medieval model book is described as a repertory of
  motifs preserved for study and reuse, probably made by one hand or closely
  cooperating workshop artists:
  <https://www.themorgan.org/collection/model-book>.
- Maria Cristina Rossi reports an uncommon first-half-of-the-fifteenth-century
  Bergamo exercise book containing advanced chancery-script exercises,
  probably supervised by a teacher in a domestic setting. This establishes
  that retained exercise codices and non-monastic instruction are historically
  possible near the proposed period:
  <https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/997378>.
- Peikola and Varila treat late-medieval manuscript tables and diagrams as
  information-organising graphic devices whose verbal cues identify, describe
  and instruct readers in their use. This supports diagram-as-operational-
  template, not any specific Voynich content:
  <https://doi.org/10.1075/jhp.22004.pei>.
- The Rijksmuseum catalogue record for Scheller's *Exemplum* documents the
  medieval model-book tradition across ca. 900–1470:
  <https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/30044869>.

No cited comparator combines plant sheets, checked biological forms, circle
arrays and a Voynich-like card ledger. The historical evidence establishes
ingredients and workshop plausibility only.

### Bottom line

The best adversarial evolution is not “inventory” or “accounting” in the
literal commodity sense; neither explains the pictures or circle geometry
without extra assumptions. Nor is pure ritual notation competitive, because
the repeated variable stencils and renderer conditioning look procedural rather
than invocatory. The strongest rival is a **meaning-bearing but
pedagogy-dominated visual pattern book**: images choose templates, cards fill
typed slots, attached endings check local cells, and multiple hands learn from
a common exemplar ecology.

It should replace the medical hybrid only if slot-preserving variable-fill
prediction beats exact phrase reuse on genuinely held instances. Until then it
is the leading alternative, with the medical practical-miscellany model still
favoured overall.
