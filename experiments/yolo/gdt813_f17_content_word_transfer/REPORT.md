## Fixed alternatives and the actual paragraph

The source line f17r.11 is identical in the three alternate transcriptions:

`otchol cthar okaiin chol daiiin`

| Whole | N | G |
|---|---|---|
| otchol | dieses? | dieses? |
| cthar | Wurzel? | Wurzel? |
| okaiin | Pulver? | ist? |
| chol | trocken? | trocken? |
| daiiin | sehr hohe Ausprägung? | sehr hohe Ausprägung? |

N gives **„Dieses Wurzelpulver: äußerst trocken?“**, G gives
**„Diese Wurzel ist äußerst trocken?“**. N assumes a nominal part/material
composition and nominal predication; the colon is analytical. G assumes an
explicit copula. Both spend three newly proposed word identities. No word
is dropped, but fluency supplies neither their identities nor their grammar.
The old cthar=herb-material and okaiin=hot-III defaults from GDT628 are rival
assignments, not independent support. otchol is not cold-dry inside this trial.

## Manual image and historical comparison

The official f17r image shows a connected plant with narrow pointed leaves,
three terminal heads and marked root-region forms. It does not picture
powder, grinding, boiling or an application recipient. Its roots are possible
subjects, not an identification of cthar. The upper marginal record remains
unlocalised at word-to-object level; the old paired-head naming is not revived.

On the correct Yale1006233 f88v region, both manual reviewers see the target
as a short isolated inscription in the second illustrated band, separate
from the prose above and below. No leader or caption box uniquely assigns
it to a root, leaf, whole specimen or group. The visible fragment has leaves;
it is not powder. An unpictured derived preparation is possible but is an
additional N hypothesis. Exact official URLs, hashes and limits are in
src/SOURCE_REGISTER.tsv; no new manuscript page was admitted.

The [edited Latin comparanda](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600006.html)
provide real part/substance-plus-reference constructions and distinguish
material being dried from a substance drying a named bodily target. Their
Latin source layers are medieval works accessed through the editor's 1486
Avicenna and 1497 Circa instans editions, not diplomatic Latin from the Irish
1415 work. The original incunable pages were not collated here. There is no
five-token alignment, Latin identity or EVA-component key.

At Aqua aluminosa, f087v of the near-contemporary Latin9333 transcription,
a liquid has a dry humoral quality and a separately listed drying harm.
Thus dry quality does not entail dried physical matter or powder.
[Primary transcription and manuscript description](https://medimanus.chs.harvard.edu/app/en/pharmantica/tacuinum_sanitatis).
The historical part-plus-its architecture is a real future rival, not a third
Voynich mapping quietly inserted into N or G. These are historical descriptions,
not treatment recommendations.
