## Concrete content rivals, explicitly not translations

**Morphological description:** a plant with narrow pointed leaves, pale-lobed
dark heads, and fine roots with marked structures. This is an intelligible
description grounded in visible components. A family expression could concern
size, slenderness or abundance, but no Voynich head is identified with those
properties, and no comparative specimen or authored measurement scale is shown.

**Material property or effect:** the herb, flowering tops, leaves or roots
could be described as strongly drying. That conditional account supplies a
concrete subject class for `chol daiiin` if `chol=dry` is retained. But dry
material, a drying effect on a body, and a high numbered dryness degree are
different claims. None follows from pigment, leaf outline or a root marking.

**Preparation or application:** using the roots rather than the leafy tops,
or drying a selected part before preparing it, are concrete alternatives to
whole-plant description. They require a written part reference and an action
or application construction. No image here identifies boiling, powdering,
dosage, a treatment target or an instruction to dry. Those words cannot be
inserted to make the family interpretation fluent.

The useful visual constraint is a real choice of whole plant versus connected
parts, not a word-to-organ key. Keep the complete paragraph and its unknown
heads fixed: proximity cannot turn `cthar` into whichever part a trial needs.
Description, intrinsic quality, bodily effect and application remain distinct
live accounts; no meaning, grammatical role or language is confirmed.
