# GDT354 source audit

## Official manuscript record

University of Pennsylvania LJS 443 is catalogued as a *Collection of texts on
the calendar*. The [official Penn record](https://find.library.upenn.edu/catalog/9951496233503681)
and [OPenn TEI](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0001/ljs443/data/ljs443_TEI.xml)
describe a collection of calendar commentaries, treatises, tables, and diagrams
written in Armenia after 1416. The named authors include Anania Shirakatsʻi,
but the catalogue does not attribute each late diagram to a particular author
or identify the values of its compartments.

The official TEI maps:

- modern folio `209r = 210r` to `0088_0422_web.jpg`;
- modern folio `209v = 210v` to `0088_0423_web.jpg`; and
- modern folio `210r = 211r` to `0088_0424_web.jpg`.

It calls each a diagram and places them inside a much larger run of diagrams
on ff.192r–211v. The exact remote byte hashes are in
`artifacts/gdt354_external_sources.tsv`.

## Direct external visual observations

Native visual inspection of those three official facsimiles found a repeated
family with a central circle and eight curved radial compartments carrying
text. Repeated crescent-like and circular marks appear in the compartments;
the exact neutral descriptions are preserved in
`artifacts/gdt354_external_visual_observations.tsv`.

These are three leaves in one manuscript and one local diagram series. They
are not independent witnesses.

## Human secondary interpretation

[Voynich.nu's illustration survey](https://www.voynich.nu/illustr.html)
already noted that several LJS 443 diagrams are visually similar to f68v3,
with varying numbers and directions of spirals. It explicitly warns that
illustration interpretation is speculative. Wikimedia reproduces one related
image under the title “Phases of the Moon,” sourced to the *Soviet Armenian
Encyclopedia*, but this is not a folio-specific scholarly edition and is not
used to assign compartment values.

The recent scholarly study by Stephanie Pambakian,
[*The Cosmology attributed to Anania Širakac‘i*](https://doi.org/10.17630/sta/1372)
(PhD thesis, University of St Andrews, 2022), establishes the wider Anania
cosmology/computus context. It does not, in the accessible catalogue metadata,
bind these LJS 443 compartments to an ordered eight-phase list.

## Voynich target provenance

The existing human f68v3 page annotation states that the large circular drawing
has eight bands containing lines of writing spiralling from the outer circle
toward the central T-O-like structure. The audit does not add a new target
description and does not treat the annotation's tentative identifications as
role evidence.

## Audit conclusion

LJS 443 supplies a strong same-period system-family/topology witness: repeated
eight-curved-compartment diagrams existed in an Armenian calendar/astronomy
compilation close to the Voynich manuscript's date. It does **not** yet supply
the readable slot key needed for a semantic experiment. The source values,
start, and direction are unresolved; the target has no fixed phase and no
independent held folio.
