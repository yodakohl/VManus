# GDT355 source audit

## Official source

University of Pennsylvania LJS 443 is described by OPenn as an Armenian
collection of calendar commentaries, treatises, tables, and diagrams written
after 1416. The TEI identifies a continuous diagram run on current folios
192r–211v. GDT355 exhausts the 38 surfaces in that run that the TEI marks as
diagrams.

The official metadata and each facsimile URL are preserved in
`artifacts/gdt355_external_sources.tsv`. Remote images are not vendored.

## Direct visual inventory

The series is not one repeated picture. It includes annular grids, concentric
tables, interlaced-circle figures, straight sector wheels, decorated petal-like
wheels, and curved spiral-band wheels.

The narrow f68v3-like spiral-band rendering occurs on three adjacent surfaces:

- current 209r / old 210r (`0088_0422`);
- current 209v / old 210v (`0088_0423`); and
- current 210r / old 211r (`0088_0424`).

Two immediately earlier pages, current 207r–207v (`0088_0418`–`0419`), also
have eight curved text-bearing compartments, but in a decorated petal-like
rather than the narrow spiral-band rendering. The broad family therefore has
five pages.

Current 206v / old 207v (`0088_0417`) is a direct counterexample to treating
curved compartments as an eight-value key: it has twelve curved, text-bearing
lobes around a central circle. Four additional surfaces show exact eightfold
primary divisions in other topologies. Eight is a real recurring cardinality
in the local manuscript series, but neither eight nor curved lobes alone fixes
one semantic system.

## Secondary labels

The public Commons reproduction labelled “Phases of the Moon” remains a
secondary iconographic lead. The complete visual census does not supply a
folio-specific scholarly transcription, compartment meanings, start, or
reading direction. The visible crescent/ring marks remain geometry, not a slot
key.

## Target boundary

No Voynich target material was opened. The only target-side statement carried
forward is GDT354's already published, human-sourced description of f68v3 as
eight inward-spiralling written bands. No f84 material was accessed.
