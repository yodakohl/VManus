# GDT763 — H1 content versus record discriminator

## Result

The best current `pcheey` reading is now narrower and more useful:

> **bound dry-preparation/Form-II record-content field with a following-value
> affinity; substance identity open**

It is not merely a generic list marker. Two of its three occurrences form the
reader-exact sequence `pcheey X daiin`, and all three lead into a recognizable
working content, quality, or amount neighborhood. At the same time, the right
fields are ordinary enough for H1 record ecology that they do not prove a
named ingredient. The local source reading “from/with dry material, Form II”
survives as a concrete C1 rival. The post-moist residue/result reading becomes
the weakest rival.

For `ol`, the best whole role becomes:

> **quantity-bearing preparation/content head with contextual side uses**

This is more specific than “carrier” but still does not identify oil, water,
wine, or a fixed material.

## The three `pcheey` frames

All three targets are medial on paragraph-start lines and each line contains
at least one additional H1 form. Among the 49 other position-matched H1
occurrences, only fifteen occur on multi-H1 lines. The actual frames are:

1. `f8r.9`: `sho pcheey pchey ofchey dsheey`
   — a moist-preparation head followed by Form II, then a related H1 Form-I
   field, an open field, and an exact moist/material/end field.
2. `f10v.1`: `sheo pcheey qoty daiin cthor`
   — Feuchtzubereitung, Form-II field, working cold/beginning quality, value
   III, then an amount/material whole.
3. `f105r.24`: `sheo pcheey dal daiin dam`
   — Feuchtzubereitung, Form-II field, working material/amount I, value III,
   then measure/value I.

The neutral renderer is therefore a record sequence, for example:

```text
sheo pcheey qoty daiin
→ Feuchtzubereitung — Trockenzubereitung/Form II:
  kalt am Anfang des Grades; Wert III
```

The concrete source rival remains:

```text
sheo pcheey
→ Feuchtzubereitung aus Trockengut, Form II
```

The word “aus” is not encoded by an identified relation marker, so this second
version is deliberately C1 rather than the portable default.

Thus `pcheey` has two hits in three occurrences. All other H1 forms together
have three hits in 196 occurrences: `pchaiin ofchy daiin`,

A stricter non-H1 control contains eleven surfaces and 33 occurrences: every

triple, `chopy chofol daiin`. This does not translate `daiin` as a number or

## Why “result” loses

Every `pcheey` has three or five tokens still to its right. None is line-final,
none lies on a paragraph-end line, and none has a reader-exact `PROCESS`,
`PASS`, or `CLOSE` role within three slots on either side. Historical recipe
results are normally accompanied by explicit process or endpoint structure,
such as cooling to a clear oil, straining, or drying. That architecture is
absent here. A residue remains imaginable, but it is no longer a sensible
default.

## `ol`: sixteen amount positions become typed functions

The fixed GDT762 universe contains sixteen positions, seventeen directed
`ol` edges, and thirteen pages. Applying the explicit dispatch gives:

- HEAD: 9;
- OBJECT/PATIENT: 1;
- CONTEXT/SECOND FIELD: 5;
- BILATERAL AMBIGUOUS: 1;
- SOURCE with a positive marker: 0.

The five context cases are not assigned merely because `ol` occurs on the
nonpreferred side. Each also has a reader-exact content candidate in the
preferred slot. Conversely, two nonpreferred cases with a nonexact competitor
remain head candidates. Eighteen amount/position/side-matched GDT760 controls
show why this matters: all nine medial-right controls are working
preparations, while the nine first-right controls mix five materials and four
preparations.

Among unambiguous contacts, `ol` occupies the expected content side 8/15
times. The corresponding profiles are 14/24 for known preparations and 16/17
for known materials. `ol` is placement-compatible with the preparation class
(descriptive odds ratio 0.816), but substantially less directional than the
material class (0.071). This favors “Ansatz/Zubereitung” over a fixed raw
material.

Repeated exact orders include `ol s aiin` four times, `sain ol` four times,
`saiin ol` twice, and `or aiin ol` twice. The related whole `ols` occurs twelve
times and has three exact right-value contacts, but that comparison grants no
free `ol` component meaning.

## Historical bridge

The field-bearing `pcheey` interpretation resembles compact early
fifteenth-century materia entries such as `Aloes lignum … ii gradu` and
`Eleborus radix … iii gradu`: a learned head or part is followed by quality
and degree fields. The source rival resembles explicitly marked phrases such
as `recipe pulveris eufrasiae`; the target, however, has no identified
equivalent of the genitive or source relation. Historical `oleum` confirms
that oil is a plausible domain word, not that EVA `ol` means oil. Every
historical comparison receives zero spelling and target-assignment credit.

## Renderer revision and boundary

`pcheey` advances from “Form-II entry” to a field-bearing record/content head
with value affinity. `ol` advances from a generic carrier to a quantity-bearing
preparation/content head with explicitly dispatched contextual uses. These are
working whole-form meanings. No substring, EVA character, confirmed lexeme,
specific substance, unit, syntax relation, or plaintext clause is established.

No new page, image, or transcription was opened. `f84` and `f84r` remained
forbidden. The validator passes 2,364 checks and byte-identical replay of all
fifteen generated outputs.

## Next route

The useful next discriminator is no longer another global semantic sweep. It
is to follow the five exact `H1 X daiin` triples and the four repeated `ol`
amount orders into their immediately bounded fields, asking whether the
intervening `X` slot consistently separates quality, material, and preparation
subtypes. That can sharpen `daiin` as a degree, amount, or generic value without
opening any new page.
