# GDT359 method: Shirakatsi *bolorak* category audit

## Question

Does earlier scholarly publication use the same Armenian *bolorak* vocabulary
for a diagram with a topology different from LJS 443 f.209r, and does any
audited source supply a figure-specific function or ordered slot key?

This is an external-only source audit following GDT358. It does not inspect or
score Voynich material. All `f84*` material remains forbidden.

## Procedure

1. Audit the complete public scan of R. A. Abrahamyan, B. E. Tumanian,
   T. Kh. Hakobyan and S. T. Melik-Bakhshyan, *Anania Shirakatsi* (Yerevan,
   1958): 75 scan surfaces containing 132 printed pages.
2. Use a complete thumbnail census only to find printed figures, then inspect
   the relevant pages directly at full resolution. No OCR, automated image
   matching, or computer-vision classification is used.
3. Transcribe only the short printed caption whose letters are directly
   readable. Keep the diagram geometry separate from interpretation.
4. Compare that caption vocabulary with the exact-surface encyclopedia caption
   already provenance-bound in GDT358.
5. Audit the public bibliographic record for Abrahamyan's 1962 lunar-cycle
   edition and Tumanian's 1971 attribution study. Do not treat a catalogue
   record as a visually inspected edition.
6. Re-evaluate only external key capacity: exact function, slot values, start,
   direction/order, and an independent readable witness.

## Fixed inference rule

If the same *bolorak* expression labels visibly different diagram topologies,
the word is evidence for a broad circular/calendar-table category, not a key to
the number or meaning of the Penn compartments. An attribution dispute about
other works in the 1962/1970 editorial corpus is an attribution warning only;
it does not by itself disprove the Penn caption.

## Claim ceiling

GDT359 may establish that published Shirakatsi scholarship applies *bolorak*
vocabulary to more than one topology. It cannot identify the function of
LJS 443 f.209r, assign eight values or phases, align a Voynich target, establish
Armenian origin or authorship, language, plaintext, meaning, or translation.
