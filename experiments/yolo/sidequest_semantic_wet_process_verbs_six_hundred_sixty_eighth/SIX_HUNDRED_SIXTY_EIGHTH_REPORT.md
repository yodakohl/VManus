# Sidequest pass 668: three compact wet-process verbs

Three small roots now receive short, reusable workshop meanings:

- **P = EINFÜLLEN** — put the active material into a receiver;
- **LSH = WASCHEN** — perform a wash/rinse step;
- **CFH = AUSWRINGEN** — squeeze liquid from the active material.

Together they cover six exact cards and seven events. P combines with Y, with
CHD+DY, and with CHD+AL. It therefore behaves like a true directional verb,
not like a one-off word for “after-strain”. LSH has an open work-cycle form and
a short closed form. CFH is still a learned one-card operation, but its compact
meaning fits the surrounding process without naming an invisible filter.

## The repaired f11r chain

`tshol schoal cfhy shfydaiin cphy shey tchody`

> Keep the preparation going at the target; wring it out, hold it to the
> prescribed measure, fill it into a receiver, hold it longer, then draw it off
> and close the step.

This is simpler than the previous “wring—stand—strain again—clear extract”
story. The visible cards license wringing, measured holding, filling, long
holding, and final removal. A cloth, wine, clarity test, and second filtration
are no longer smuggled into the card meanings.

## The f81v wash cycle

`lsho qokey lshedy` reads:

> Wash the working item, set it briefly, wash briefly once more, and close.

The following one-card statement repeats `lshedy`: **wash briefly; close**.
This makes LSH a portable wash verb rather than the name of a bath or pool.

The three roots add four previously uncovered cards and five events to the
current compositional layer. Total coverage is now 97 of 173 cards and 217 of
381 prose events.
