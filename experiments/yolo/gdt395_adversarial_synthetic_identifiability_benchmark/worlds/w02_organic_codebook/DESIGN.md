# W02: The Three-School Apothecary Codebook

## Hidden setting

W02 records the inventory and preparation work of an apothecary tradition.  The
writing is a professional codebook, not a spelling system: entries began as
mnemonic labels used by storekeepers, then passed through several generations
of copying in three schools.  The modern corpus mixes stock counts, receipts,
preparation sequences, dispensations, transfers, and spoilage notices.

The fourteen visible symbols are
`ɓ ɕ ɗ ɣ ɦ ƙ ɬ ɱ ŋ ƥ ʂ ƭ ʋ ʐ`.  They form conventional written groups; they do
not encode an alphabet or a character-for-character cipher.  A group normally
names one codebook entry, but productive clitic fusion, fossilized recipe
components, and ambiguous detachment mean that neither group boundaries nor
visible substrings reliably reveal semantic components.

## Organic history

The sealed genealogy has eleven ordered stages.  An early warehouse mnemonic
stock was expanded by recipe writers.  Frequent operations and particles were
clipped; two pairs of commodities merged in counter usage; a single old liquid
entry split into oil and syrup readings by record frame.  A location particle
bleached into three functions.  Several preparation names fossilized whole
older action-plus-ingredient expressions.  Measure signs were analogically
remodeled into a family even though their histories differ.  The full-copy,
counter, and bench schools then diverged.  Two hands generalized different
final shapes.  Later suppletive ledger forms and lexeme-specific exceptions
were retained.  Only clitic attachment, final-position clipping, and a small
amount of hand-conditioned reshaping remain productive.

The result deliberately contains:

- homographs that are resolved by construction and register;
- cognate entries that no longer look related;
- recurrent fragments with no current compositional meaning;
- one polyfunctional particle used for topic, completion, and destination;
- preparation names whose historical components are fully fossilized;
- optional joined or detached clitics and ambiguous observed boundaries;
- high-frequency abbreviations alongside rare conservative exceptions;
- register-specific merger and hand-specific alternation.

## Corpus organization and confounds

Records contain 6--14 observed groups and are laid out on physical lines of
4--9 groups.  Pages contain several paragraphs, and records are assigned one
of three registers and one of two hands with skewed frequencies.  Inventory
entities and common operations recur heavily, while rarer ingredients,
preparations, states, sources, and containers form a long tail.

Semantic relations connect amounts, units, states, ingredients, destinations,
containers, recipients, and cross-record references to their targets.
Preparation and dispensing records also carry exact construction scope.
Inventory state changes are tracked in the oracle.  These dependencies make
the corpus semantically rich, but the carrier remains superficially compatible
with a semantics-light code stream: short recurrent groups, fixed record
frames, skewed types, register-conditioned variants, and noisy boundaries.

The intended adversarial confound is that distributional regularity can be
explained either as a matched formal code carrier or as a historically grown
professional vocabulary.  Successful recovery therefore requires identifying
relations, constructional scope, reference, and the non-tree-like genealogy,
not merely segmenting repeated substrings.
