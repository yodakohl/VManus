# W06: The Namar Collection Index

## Hidden domain

W06 is the writing system of a long-lived taxonomic collection catalogue.  Its
entities are physical specimens, taxa, collecting localities, habitats,
collectors, schools, and shelf loci.  Records do not state isolated labels:
they perform accession, determination, revision, synonymy, habitat indexing,
loan, and explicit cross-reference operations.  A specimen can recur under an
old and a current determination, a taxon can open a scope covering several
entries, and index records point to earlier physical records.

The carrier is a bound index.  Pages contain paragraphs, records, fields, and
physical lines.  Record templates recur but admit omitted fields, alternatives,
parenthetical authorities, and moved cross-references.  Initial/final forms,
line-end compression, joined operator phrases, and ambiguous segmentation make
the surface grouping an imperfect reflection of the hidden components.

## Organic history

The current system is the result of nine ordered stages.

1. An older catalogue used independent class words and entity stems.
2. Frequent specimen, taxon, and cross-reference expressions shortened.
3. Analogy spread two record-frame patterns, but only within productive
   accession and determination records.
4. Several final stem classes merged; an originally single index particle
   split by position and school.
5. Class words bleached into record and field operators.
6. Former class material fossilized on a subset of specimen and locality
   forms, with no current compositional meaning.
7. One relational form became polyfunctional as cross-reference, alternative,
   and scope-continuation marking.
8. High-frequency taxa and two catalogue operators acquired suppletive forms
   and lexical exceptions.
9. Formal, field, and school registers diverged, followed by two hand-specific
   graphic traditions.

Consequently this is not a substitution cipher or a clean affix table.  Some
identical surface forms are historical mergers, some related meanings are
suppletive, fossil material is opaque, and productive morphology coexists with
lexically stored frames.

## Intended confounds

Frequency shortening makes high-frequency relational material resemble layout
punctuation.  The polyfunctional continuation/cross-reference form creates
false equivalence among relation types.  Scope and record boundaries sometimes
coincide and sometimes cross a physical line.  Register-specific suppletion can
look like distinct vocabulary, while hand variants can look like morphological
alternation.  Taxon alternatives and revisions deliberately reuse entities in
different roles, so adjacency alone does not identify a relation's direction.
