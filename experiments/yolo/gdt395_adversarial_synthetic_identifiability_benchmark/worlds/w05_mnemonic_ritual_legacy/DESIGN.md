# W05 — Mnemonic Ritual Legacy

## Hidden setting

W05 is the writing tradition of a conservative ritual household that maintains
a six-season, thirty-day reckoning.  Its records are practical performance
aids, not prose: they tell a celebrant which observance is due, which phase of
the procedure is active, which materials and officiants participate, when a
conditional block applies, which earlier performance is to be copied, and
which alternative is licensed when a gate fails.

The corpus mixes five recurring record schemas: ordinary lamp observances,
full-phase offerings, season-opening renewal, nine-day memorial recurrence,
and exceptional calendar repair.  A record advances a real state machine
through calendrical anchoring, preparation, admission, ordered acts, an
optional or conditional branch, recurrence/reference, and closure.  Scope
openers and closers delimit executable spans.  Argument, sequence,
alternative, recurrence, and cross-record reference relations point to actual
events.  The same participant or object can recur in several schemas without
having the same procedural role.

## Carrier and layout

The carrier is mnemonic.  A visible group is normally a cue sufficient for a
trained performer, and is often neither a word nor a fully segmentable phrase.
Pages contain six records in three paragraphs.  Records occupy several short
physical lines, with field boundaries, joins, detached cues, line resets, and
occasionally uncertain boundaries.  `r0` is the conservative house-book
register, `r1` a compact officiant checklist, and `r2` a teaching/correction
register.  `h0`, `h1`, and `h2` have different ligature and final-mark habits.
These labels are intentionally opaque in observations.

## Historical depth

The observable system is the result of eight ordered stages.

1. A spoken procedural lexicon was first represented by fairly full cues.
2. Frequent phase, sequence, and closure cues underwent irregular shortening.
3. Copyists extended a few common frames by analogy, while preserving common
   exceptions.
4. Several cues merged in compact writing; a high-frequency action split into
   object-conditioned allomorphs.
5. Former motion and completion words bleached into reference, recurrence,
   and scope operators.
6. Obsolete classifiers survived inside calendrical and offering cues as
   fossilized material.
7. Rare numerals, the repair rite, and some closures acquired suppletive forms;
   several forms became polyfunctional.
8. House-book, checklist, and school traditions diverged, followed by
   hand-specific ligatures and boundary reinterpretation.

The final renderer therefore uses lexical whole-form replacements,
construction-conditioned truncation, mergers, split allomorphy, fossil pieces,
register variants, and hand/layout alternations.  Productive affixes exist,
but they are limited and masked by inherited fused forms.

## Intended identifiability confounds

Recurring page positions correlate with calendrical anchors, but calendar
phase, schema, and register also affect form.  The compact register merges the
sequence and recurrence cues; the formal register preserves distinctions.
The action historically meaning *pour* has different visible descendants with
water, oil, and grain, while unrelated closure and phase cues converge in one
hand.  A bleached “turn” cue can mean physical reversal, recurrence, or
reference depending on construction.  Joins sometimes mark a live relation
and sometimes merely a hand habit.  Fossil components resemble productive
class marks but no longer contribute current meaning.  Consequently frequency,
position, and glyph overlap are useful but individually misleading.
