# W08 — The Divided Casebook Hands

## Hidden history

W08 represents a medical teaching notation descended from one temple-infirmary
casebook hand.  The ancestral system mixed mnemonic word signs, rebus spellings,
and compact compounds used while an instructor demonstrated examination and
treatment.  It was never a compositional cipher.  High-frequency signs shortened
at different rates, some compounds retained unread fossil pieces, and common
commands acquired suppletive classroom forms.

The tradition passed through nine modeled stages.  A first round of cursive
shortening was followed by lexicalized case compounds, analogical leveling of a
procedure frame, bleaching of an old HAND sign into an action cue, and
fossilization of obsolete WATER and EYE components.  When teaching centers
separated, four schools retained a common core but changed it incompatibly:

- the archive school preserves fuller roots but keeps old abbreviations;
- the ward school merges several symptom and procedure signs and makes the
  bleached action cue productive;
- the littoral school adds local determinatives, splits PAIN by severity, and
  rebinds an inherited location sign as an evidential;
- the itinerant school uses suppletive commands, productive repetition, and a
  locally bleached state ending.

Late copying added regional allographs, line-initial lead strokes, line-final
tails, and hand-specific joins.  Thus similar strings can be genealogical
cognates, accidental mergers, or locally rebound forms, while related meanings
can be written by unrelated suppletive forms.

## Corpus model

The generated corpus consists of pages, paragraphs, completed case or lesson
records, and reset physical lines.  Records contain actual patients, symptoms,
materials, findings, procedures, state transitions, relations, anaphoric
references, conditions, scope markers, warnings, and teaching assertions.
Several recurring schemas cover case demonstration, procedural drill,
comparison lesson, warning lesson, and follow-up review.  Events may target
earlier events, and conditional markers delimit explicit scopes.

Four opaque register IDs and three hands are visible because schools and hands
are carrier properties, not translations.  Layout roles are likewise opaque.
Joined, detached, and uncertain group boundaries coexist.  Rendering depends on
school, hand, lexical history, construction, state context, and position on the
line.  The same school therefore does not reduce to a substitution table.

## Intended identifiability confounds

Frequency-driven abbreviation makes the most common forms least internally
transparent.  Ward mergers create homographs across both semantic and
grammatical categories.  Littoral determinatives look regular but contain
lexical exceptions and a context-sensitive split.  Itinerant suppletion hides
cognacy in frequent commands.  Fossil pieces resemble productive morphology,
while genuinely productive cues are frequently omitted or fused.  Polyfunctional
scope, reference, relation, and evidential signs further frustrate a clean
form-to-function analysis.  Ambiguous boundaries and positional allography can
make a single inherited form appear as several tokens or the reverse.
