# W01 — Technical Scribal Shorthand

## Hidden cultural setting

W01 is the mature record hand of a distributed guild of maintainers.  Its
texts are short natural-language inspection notes, repair narratives,
calibration records, workshop procedures, and incident reports concerning
hydraulic, thermal, mechanical, and building systems.  The script began as an
ordinary syllabic notation used for full prose.  Repeated copying of the same
technical collocations turned it into a mixed shorthand: conventional lexical
radicals, residual class strokes, grammatical marks, and whole-collocation
ligatures coexist.

The semantic generator models sites, equipment, materials, measurable
properties, states, actions, quantities and units.  Records contain
conditionals, temporal scopes, causal and result relations, anaphoric
references, and actions whose before/after states differ.  Five record schemas
produce recurring discourse shapes without fixing a token slot to a semantic
class.

## Genealogy

The exported genealogy describes ten ordered stages.  In outline, an early
syllabary acquired determinatives; frequent technical words then lost medial
strokes, adjacent words developed phrase ligatures, and two sound changes
merged formerly distinct radicals.  A later school split some merged forms by
domain.  Old material and tool determinatives fossilized on several words
after their productive meanings had been lost.  A locative sign bleached into
a field divider and a result sign became both a clause connective and a
completed-aspect mark.  Analogy regularized only part of the action vocabulary,
leaving high-frequency suppletive signs.  Finally three scribal schools and
three hands diverged.  Thus the final inventory has merger, conditioned split,
syncretism, polyfunctionality, fossil material, and exceptions.

## Visible realization

A visible group is assembled from several interacting conventions:

- most lexical entries have a two-stroke inherited radical, but common items
  can use an unrelated one-stroke sign;
- classifiers may precede, follow, fuse into, or disappear from a radical,
  depending on register and lexical history;
- action groups can incorporate a reduced sign for their patient, while other
  actions remain separate words;
- conditional, connective, reference, and aspect signs are polyfunctional;
- line-final groups contract and some function/content boundaries are joined,
  so the observed group boundary is occasionally ambiguous;
- register and hand transformations affect strokes independently of meaning.

Consequently the system is neither a substitution cipher nor a clean
prefix–stem–suffix table.  A single visible sign can reflect a word, an old
classifier, a current state morpheme, or half of a phrase ligature; conversely,
one lexical item can have unrelated frequent, formal, split, and hand-specific
forms.

## Registers, hands, and layout

`r0` is a compact routine ledger hand, `r1` is a hurried field-note hand, and
`r2` is a conservative formal-copy register.  The observation data expose only
these opaque identifiers.  Hands `h0`, `h1`, and `h2` impose additional stroke
variants.  Pages contain paragraphs, records, fields and physical lines.
Headings, labels, narrative matter, readings and marginal references occupy
distinct opaque layout roles; line and record positions affect shortening.

## Intended identifiability confounds

Skewed recurrence makes common actions resemble grammatical particles.
Classifier fossils create false semantic clusters.  Incorporated patients make
some action groups resemble compounds but leave synonymous unincorporated
phrases elsewhere.  Register-specific splitting partially reverses historical
mergers.  Polyfunctional result/reference signs and ambiguous joined boundaries
confound word segmentation with syntactic analysis.  Nevertheless the oracle,
codebook, and ordered genealogy preserve a complete audit trail.
