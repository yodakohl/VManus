# Hundred-ninety-sixth pass: the reader's normalization sheet

## Compact inventory

The current 173-card dictionary contains 230 registered written surfaces:

- 173 master forms;
- 57 additional allographs;
- zero surface strings assigned to two different exact cards.

Thus a workshop reader does not need to solve an ambiguous alphabet on these
pages. They need a direct 230→173 normalization list, which compresses into ten
teaching families. The largest are fourteen q-framed aliases, four AIIN frames,
five AL frames, five Y frames, four OL frames, three OR frames, two AR frames,
two CTH frames, four learned CHEDY-close aliases and fourteen smaller paired

## Readback

The normalization recovers all 381 observed prose events to their exact cards.
It also collapses both newly written 25-token hands—harmonized and mixed—onto
the same 25-card sequence with no disagreement.

The most important reader caution is negative: visible `dy` is not a universal
ending. It can be the Y/current-item surface. Only the exact registered close
card licenses a close. Conversely, `qol`, `sol`, `chol`, `ol` and `cheol` are
not five words here; they normalize to one continuation card.

## What the workshop now has

- a 173-card taught inventory;
- 57 licensed allographs grouped by ten reader rules;
- optional CH/D/O/Q/S field concord;
- positional habits for fast hands;
- exact close-card recognition.

This is simple enough for a few fifteenth-century scribes: one pocket sheet and
practice with the exemplar, rather than a full spoken-language cipher alphabet.
