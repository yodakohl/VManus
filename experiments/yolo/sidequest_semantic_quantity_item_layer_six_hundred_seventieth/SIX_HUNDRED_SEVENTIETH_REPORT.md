# Sidequest pass 670: quantity, ingredient, and active item

Six productive pieces complete almost the entire fixed prose deck:

| Root | Short value | Function |
|---|---|---|
| AIN | PORTION | one bounded charge |
| AIIN | SOLLMASS | the prescribed measure/value |
| IIN | ARBEITSSTUFE | a named stage of the operation |
| K | ZUDOSIEREN | add a quantity into the current process |
| HO | ZUTAT | the locally intended material addition |
| Y | ARBEITSPOSTEN | the currently active item; this/it |

Their union covers 88 cards and 195 events. Combined with the roots already
closed, the component grammar now reaches **167 of 173 exact cards and 374 of
381 prose events**.

## The important separations

- AIN is the portion; AIIN is its prescribed measure. `qokain` therefore means
  “set one portion”, while `qokaiin` means “set according to the prescribed
  measure”.
- IIN is a process stage, not a misspelled amount. `kaiiin` reads “dose to the
  work stage”.
- K is the act of dosing; HO is the ingredient being supplied.
- Y is an anaphoric work item. It does not itself mean open, state, liquid, or
  closure.
- A visible surface `dy` can realize the exact Y card. Closure is therefore a
  licensed whole construction, never a suffix guessed from spelling alone.

## Only six cards remain

Seven events in six exact cards are not reached by the current roots:

- `os` — work compartment;
- `tchody` — enter/remove/work/close whole command;
- `dchol/schol` — resume (two events);
- `ody` — work-cycle close;
- `talam` — store/put away;
- `ches` — briefly remove/divide.

These are small enough to treat as learned whole commands or to close one at a
time. The central result is that the ten-page prose deck no longer needs 173
unrelated sentence-sized dictionary entries: 167 cards participate in the
same compact verb, address, quantity, item, grade, and endpoint machinery.
