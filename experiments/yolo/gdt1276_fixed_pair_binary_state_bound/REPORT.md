# GDT1276: one shared binary input cannot generate every fixed-pair variant

A deterministic writer receiving only an unchanged ordered pair of stem strings
and one binary initial state can emit at most two distinct ending pairs. The old
915evaluation contains three or more cells for4ZL,6IT and6RFfamilies. Thus that
specific fixed-input writer cannot cover all occurrences. This is a simple
necessary-capacity result from previously exposed evidence, not a newly found
mixed ending, a new statistical association, or an exclusion of binary grammar.

## Why different fixed classes do not suffice
With two possible initial inputs, a deterministic rule has only two complete
outputs for a fixed pair. Different spellings for each stem and each slot are
allowed. Even a fixed state update between the two words leaves the complete
trace a function of the original input, so it still offers at most two outputs.
Three observed outputs require some further distinction under this contract.

For the narrower interpretation where each word has exactly two forms for the
same binary feature, inverting the spelling convention for one word merely
makes the pair always mixed instead of always equal. It cannot produce both.
That SAME/MIXED corollary requires bijective individual tables; the more general
two-output bound does not. Constant/syncretic maps can yield one equal and one
mixed output, but still cannot produce three distinct pairs.

This does NOT infer three grammatical cases, three actual writer states, or
independent choice of endings. Additional context, different constructions,
homographic stems, optional spelling or extra per-word input all lie outside
the fixed-input contract. They require their own evidence; they are not repairs
silently admitted to this tested rule. In particular, adjacency does not prove
that all examples instantiate one grammatical construction (949).
