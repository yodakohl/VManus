# Sidequest pass 676: the apprentice manual

The complete prose system can be taught in nine lessons and sixteen rules.
The apprentice first learns the silent pictured owner and current item, then
source/material, quantity, nine core operations, helper actions, path/sequence,
grade/state, endpoint, and finally three indivisible commands.

The central writing rule is deliberately medieval and practical:

1. decide the intended source instruction from picture and workshop context;
2. compose its short semantic recipe;
3. look up the exact whole card in the master tablet;
4. copy its local visible form;
5. read every component back before speaking the fluent instruction.

This is therefore neither a normal alphabet nor a freely spelled
agglutinative language. It is a mixed system of productive semantic slots and
learned exact cards. The master exemplar remains necessary for surface choice.

Twelve missing-but-readable recipes are now predicted, including full active
OK, zero/short/full SOLK closures, zero/full SH endpoints, portion filling,
long washing, wring-close, and long active transfer. The prediction concerns
the composition and meaning only; it does not invent a Voynich spelling.
