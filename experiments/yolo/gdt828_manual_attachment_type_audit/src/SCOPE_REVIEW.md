`sol chedy qokedy olkedy dol qokchedy` and
`sal chedy qokedy olkedy dol qokchedy`. RF instead has opaque
`s@221;l che@152;y qoke@152;y olkedy dol qokchedy`, so it does not supply

The contradiction follows only from the conjunction:

1. `chedy` retains physical on/contact relation, with a nominal physical ground.
2. Its ground must be its immediately following source group, in the same

3. `qokedy` is exclusively the proposed imperative physical press, not a ground
   nominal; coercion to pressure, pressing, a press-device or quoted command is
   forbidden.
4. The ZL/IT group identification is accepted for this conditional test.

Then the required ground is `qokedy`, which has the wrong hypothesized type.
This is a real contradiction *inside that grammar*. The packet has not established
condition 2. German `auf druecke` order alone cannot establish it. An image of

## Uniform alternative construction exists, conditionally

A finite countermodel to the purported unavoidable lexical contradiction is:

`CONTACT := chedy ACTION* GROUND`.

Here ACTION is the fixed hypothesized imperative category
{qokeedy=rub, qokedy=press, shedy=stroke}. Every chedy takes the first following
group outside that category as its proposed ground; an intervening command is
retained as a command whose action has the contact modifier. This describes a
discontinuous relation construction, not nominalization of a command. The
marker may precede the command while the ground follows it. If the candidate
ground has an unknown type, the construction is unresolved, not passed.
Every opaque or alternate whole remains a single unknown; no prefix expansion,
hidden translated words or clause punctuation is supplied.

The same rule applies at every exact chedy, with no reference to a page or locus.
At .27 it gives `chedy [qokedy] olkedy`: physical press with an on-relation
whose proposed ground is unknown `olkedy`. Both translated tokens survive;
press never becomes pressure. `sol/sal`, `dol`, and `qokchedy` remain unknown
source groups. At .19 it gives `chedy qoteedy` with zero intervening actions;
the earlier press and unknown sheedy remain present. Whether that earlier
press governs this contact phrase is a further unresolved attachment, not a
decoded relation.

This rule is *not evidence for Voynich grammar*. The ACTION* permission was
chosen after seeing the collision, and it is exercised only at the one
f81r.27 locus (two alternate readings). Serial actions elsewhere do not
demonstrate interruption of a contact construction. Thus it is a logical
counterexample to unavoidable lexical inconsistency, not a demonstrated
successful replacement grammar or a model winner. It would lose if an
independent construction established obligatory adjacency or if olkedy were
independently assigned an incompatible type. In this packet neither is known.
