An occurrence is one exact raw group with source ID, reader, locus, position and literal separators. Its window consists of up to two groups on each side within the same reader and physical line. daldy's union of loci is for finding alternate readings of the same locations, not for adding reader counts or declaring groups at the same ordinal position identical physical spans. Complete same-locus lines retain segmentation disagreements.

The bounded shared-frame census compares complete preceding and following raw groups around daldy/daly/dal in each reader separately, also requiring equal literal left/right separators. Physical line edges are excluded from this two-sided census. This is descriptive retrieval, not a scored text relation, learned paradigm, independent evidence or a test of all possible contexts. A zero or sparse result cannot refute word structure generally.

Manual comparison keeps embedded strings, entities and uncertain separators visible. No raw spelling is normalized to create agreement. Any character-span comparison must explicitly say when it is only an exact concatenated-transcription match and whether outside anchors identify the occurrence uniquely.
