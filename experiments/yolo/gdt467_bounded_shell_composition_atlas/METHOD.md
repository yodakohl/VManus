# GDT467 method

## Question

Do all bounded combinations of the frozen GDT466 prefix, strict-internal and
suffix channels remain predictable under the executable longest-match order,
and which different visible factorizations collapse to the same component
recipe?

## Inputs

GDT466's 44-channel deck and unchanged deterministic intake implementation.
The deck contains twelve prefixes, 23 suffixes and nine strict-internal
channels.

## Method

Enumerate two bounded shell shapes:

- every prefix × suffix pair around one opaque name core: 12 × 23 = 276;
- every prefix × internal × suffix triple around two opaque name-core spans:
  12 × 9 × 23 = 2,484.

Each of the 2,760 shells is executed with three synthetic core markers (`x`,
`zx`, `xvz`) that contain no internal channel. The resulting 8,280 surfaces
must select exactly the intended ordered channel IDs, retain the expected
number of learned characters, and use the shell-plus-name route. A per-channel
table checks that all 44 channels receive their full Cartesian exposure.

The phrasebook retains three parallel identities: visible surface template,
exact ordered channel signature, and flattened component recipe. Repeated flat
recipes are grouped without merging their exact channel or surface traces.

## Decision rule and claim ceiling

Any missing, extra or reordered channel is a precedence collision. Co-valued
flattened recipes are not failures if the exact channel trace stays unique;
they are renderer/factorization families and remain explicit.

The core markers and surface templates are synthetic mechanism probes, not
predicted Voynich spellings. No page, channel, component meaning, occurrence,
individual name, language, plaintext or confirmed lexeme is added.
